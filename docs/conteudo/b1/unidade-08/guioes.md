# B1.2 · Unidade 8 — Money matters · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B1](../unidade-01/guioes.md): tudo em inglês, e o português só nos falsos amigos e na mediação.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2, do B1.1 e das unidades 6 e 7. Novo: a dedução sobre o presente com *must / might / could / can't* (*That can't be right. It must be the exchange rate.*); números grandes, percentagens, fracções e tendências (*rise, fall, increase, decrease, go up, go down, by / to*); o *future continuous* (*This time next week, I'll be presenting the budget. Will you be using the car tomorrow?*); comparativos e superlativos com números (revistos: *much higher than, the most expensive item*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** a dedução sobre o passado (*must have / can't have / might have*), que fica para o B2; o 3.º condicional, *should have* e *could have*; *wish*; a passiva com modais ou noutros tempos; outros verbos de relato além dos da unidade 7.

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mopane Systems, a Morro Azul Energia e a Tsodilo Sensors são fictícias. **Os números do projecto e as taxas de câmbio de 2028 são inventados** (o curso passa-se no futuro); não são previsões.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**; **Sílvia** (directora financeira); **Paulo**; **Lerato**. Novo e fictício: **David Nkosi** (sul-africano, director financeiro da Mopane Systems; o projecto do Lobito é da equipa conjunta, por isso o orçamento é aprovado pelas duas empresas). Na 8E voltam **Esperança**, **Kevin** e **Patrick** (B1 2E) e entra a **Joana** (do Huambo, dona de uma pequena loja; fictícia).

**A história:** Março de 2028. O projecto do Lobito (a rede e as câmaras do terminal da Morro Azul, B1 6) acabou. O orçamento era de 57 milhões de kwanzas; o gasto real foi de 65,5 milhões — cerca de 15% a mais. Há três causas: o kwanza desceu face ao dólar (o equipamento é pago em dólares), o incidente de Janeiro obrigou a duas semanas a mais de trabalho, e os hotéis do Lobito subiram de preço. Na segunda, 6, a Sílvia e o Nuno estranham os números (8A); na terça, 7, a Sílvia explica as tendências à equipa (8B); na quarta, 8, o Nuno organiza a semana seguinte (8C); na quarta, 15, apresenta o desvio ao David e propõe cortes (8F). Entretanto, os sensores da Tsodilo chegam na sexta, 3, e são instalados de 6 a 10 de Março (B1 7G).

## 8A — It must be a mistake

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8A — It must be a mistake        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue fazer deduções sobre o presente com must (tenho quase a
  certeza de que sim), might / could (é possível) e can't (tenho quase a certeza de que não).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: must / might / could / can't + verbo; must be / can't be + adjectivo ou nome; a diferença
  entre must (dedução) e must (obrigação, B1 5D); mustn't não é o contrário de must na dedução (→ can't)
Palavras-alvo: invoice, expense, total, amount, mistake, extra, exchange rate, per night, hotel bill, line
  (de uma tabela), right / wrong
Reciclagem: probabilidade (B1, 4C): might, I doubt; discurso indirecto (B1, 7A)
Pronúncia: must fraco ("It must be" — quase sem "u"); can't forte e longo
Contexto: segunda-feira, 6 de Março de 2028, 9:30; o gabinete da Sílvia; a Sílvia e o Nuno olham para a
  folha de custos do projecto do Lobito
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 8A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 8
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This unit is about money: prices, costs and budgets. Today: how to make a logical guess about the present — "it must be", "it might be", "it can't be".

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 6 de Março, 9:30; o gabinete da Sílvia; no ecrã do portátil, uma folha de custos com o total a vermelho: "65 500 000 Kz"]

**SÍLVIA:** Nuno, come and look at this. The Lobito project. The budget was fifty-seven million kwanzas. We spent sixty-five and a half.
**NUNO:** Sixty-five? That can't be right. We didn't buy anything extra.
**SÍLVIA:** I know. It must be a mistake somewhere. Look at the hotels: eight and a half million. The budget was six.
**NUNO:** Well, the hotel bill must be higher because of January. After the incident, Paulo and I stayed two more weeks.
**SÍLVIA:** True. But two weeks can't cost two and a half million. That's too much.
**NUNO:** Hmm. It might be the price per night. The hotel told us in February that prices were going up.
**SÍLVIA:** Let me check… Yes — fifty-five thousand a night in January, sixty-five thousand in February. OK. And the equipment? Thirty-three million. The budget was thirty.
**NUNO:** That must be the exchange rate. We pay Mopane in dollars, and the kwanza has fallen since we made the budget.
**SÍLVIA:** You could be right. In October it was nine hundred and fifty kwanzas to the dollar. Now it's more than a thousand.
**NUNO:** So it isn't a mistake. It's three different things.
**SÍLVIA:** It looks like it. But David at Mopane is going to ask a lot of questions. You'd better prepare the numbers carefully.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Deduções sobre o presente**

[ECRÃ:
quase certo que SIM — It **must be** the exchange rate.
possível — It **might be** the price per night. / It **could be** the price per night.
quase certo que NÃO — That **can't be** right. / Two weeks **can't cost** two and a half million.]

**PROF:** When we have some information and we make a logical guess, we use "must", "might", "could" or "can't". "Must" — I'm almost sure it's true. "Might" or "could" — it's possible. "Can't" — I'm almost sure it isn't true.
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Can't*, não *mustn't***

[ECRÃ: ✘ That mustn't be right. · ✔ That **can't be** right.]

**PROF:** Careful! The opposite of "must" in a guess is "can't", not "mustn't". "Mustn't" means "it's prohibited" — you know it from unit five.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Dois sentidos de *must***

[ECRÃ: You **must** wear a helmet. (obrigação — B1 5D) · It **must** be the exchange rate. (dedução — tenho quase a certeza)]

**PROF:** "Must" has two meanings: a rule, or a logical guess. The context tells you which one.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: "Deve ser o câmbio." → ✔ It **must be** the exchange rate. · "Não pode ser!" → ✔ It **can't be**! · ✘ It mustn't be!]

**PROF:** In Portuguese, "deve ser" is often a guess — "must be". And "não pode ser" is "can't be".
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In a guess, "must" is quick and weak. "Can't" is strong and long. Listen: It must be the exchange rate. That can't be right.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: It must be the exchange rate.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** That can't be right.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Must, might, or can't?
[ECRÃ: Paulo isn't answering. His flight to Namibe left at nine, and it's half past nine. → He … be on the plane.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: He must be on the plane.]
[ECRÃ: The invoice says the hotel cost 100 dollars for a month. → That … be right.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: That can't be right.]
[ECRÃ: There's a box at reception. Maybe it's the sensors. → It … be the sensors.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: It might be the sensors. / It could be the sensors.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Look around you — or think of your office. Make four guesses: about a colleague who isn't there today, a noise, a price, and a number on a document. Use "must", "might" and "can't". About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — My colleague João isn't here today. He must be ill — he's never late. That noise outside might be the generator; it could be the lorry, too. This printer can't be new — it's so slow! And the electricity bill is double. That can't be right. It must be a mistake.]

**PROF (opinião):** In your opinion, what's the best way to check a strange number on an invoice?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 7A):** What did the hotel tell Nuno in February? Report it.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The hotel told him that prices were going up.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! "Must" for almost sure, "might" or "could" for possible, "can't" for almost impossible. Next lesson: numbers, percentages and trends.

## 8B — Prices have gone up by 10%

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8B — Prices have gone up by 10%        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dizer números grandes, percentagens e fracções e descrever
  tendências (Costs went up by 15%. The kwanza fell from 950 to 1 050.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: rise / go up / increase · fall / go down / decrease (verbos e nomes); by (a diferença) e
  to (o valor final); from … to …; a rise of 10% / an increase of 10%; números grandes (million,
  billion; and antes da última parte); percentagens e fracções (a quarter, a third, half)
Palavras-alvo: rise, fall, increase, decrease, go up, go down, stay the same, exchange rate, budget,
  spend, actual, percent, percentage, a quarter, a third, half, about, almost, more than
Reciclagem: comparativos e superlativos (A2): much higher than, the most expensive; passiva (B1, 5B)
Pronúncia: thirteen / thirty, fifteen / fifty (o acento: no fim em -teen, no princípio em -ty)
Contexto: terça-feira, 7 de Março, 10:00; a reunião semanal da equipa; a Sílvia mostra três gráficos
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 8B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Money talk is full of numbers: how much, how much more, how fast. Today: big numbers, percentages, and how to describe a trend.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 7 de Março, 10:00; a sala de reuniões; a Sílvia ao lado de um ecrã com três gráficos: o câmbio do kwanza, os preços dos hotéis no Lobito e o gasto do projecto por rubrica]

**SÍLVIA:** OK, everyone. Three graphs, three trends. First, the exchange rate. In October, when we made the Lobito budget, a dollar cost nine hundred and fifty kwanzas. Now it costs one thousand and fifty. So the kwanza has fallen by about ten percent against the dollar.
**LERATO:** And we pay for the equipment in dollars.
**SÍLVIA:** Exactly. That's why the equipment went up from thirty million to thirty-three million kwanzas — an increase of ten percent, and we didn't buy one extra cable.
**PAULO:** What about the hotels?
**SÍLVIA:** Second graph. Hotel prices in Lobito rose from fifty-five thousand to sixty-five thousand kwanzas a night in February. That's a rise of almost twenty percent. Add our two extra weeks, and the hotel costs went up by more than forty percent.
**NUNO:** Forty percent! That's the biggest increase in the project.
**SÍLVIA:** In percentage, yes. But in money, the biggest line is still the equipment — it's half of the total.
**LERATO:** And the total?
**SÍLVIA:** The budget was fifty-seven million. We spent sixty-five and a half. That's eight and a half million more — about fifteen percent over budget.
**PAULO:** Did anything go down?
**SÍLVIA:** One thing: the contractor's costs stayed the same, and the damage from the fire was paid by the insurance. So it could be worse!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Subir e descer**

[ECRÃ:
↑ **rise / go up / increase** — Hotel prices **rose**. Costs **went up**. Costs **increased**.
↓ **fall / go down / decrease** — The kwanza **fell**. Prices **went down**.
→ **stay the same** — The contractor's costs **stayed the same**.]

**PROF:** These verbs describe a trend. "Rise" and "fall" are irregular: rise, rose, risen — fall, fell, fallen.
[PAUSA 5 s]

***By* ou *to*?**

[ECRÃ: Costs went up **by** 15%. (a diferença) · Costs went up **to** 65.5 million. (o valor final) · The kwanza fell **from** 950 **to** 1 050 per dollar.]

**PROF:** "By" is the difference. "To" is the final number. "From… to…" gives both.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Nomes**

[ECRÃ: **a rise of** 20% · **an increase of** 10% · **a fall of** 5% · **a decrease of** 3%]

**PROF:** As nouns, use "of" before the number: "an increase of ten percent".
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Números grandes, percentagens e fracções**

[ECRÃ: 65 500 000 → sixty-five **and a half** million / sixty-five point five million · 1 050 → one thousand **and** fifty · 15% → fifteen percent · ½ → half · ⅓ → a third · ¼ → a quarter]

**PROF:** In British English, say "and" before the last part of a number: one thousand and fifty. "Million" and "thousand" have no "s" after a number: sixty-five million, not "millions".
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ sixty-five millions · ✔ sixty-five million · ✔ millions of people (sem número)]
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Numbers ending in "-teen" have the stress at the end; numbers ending in "-ty" have it at the beginning. Listen: thirteen, thirty. Fifteen, fifty.
[ECRÃ: thir**TEEN** · **THIR**ty · fif**TEEN** · **FIF**ty]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: fifteen percent. Fifty percent.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** Thirteen thousand. Thirty thousand.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Describe the trend. Use "by" or "to".
[ECRÃ: Fuel price: 300 Kz → 400 Kz] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: The fuel price went up from three hundred to four hundred kwanzas — an increase of about thirty-three percent, or a third.]
[ECRÃ: Staff: 40 → 30] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: The number of staff fell by ten, from forty to thirty — a quarter.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. How have prices changed where you live in the last year? Talk about three things — food, transport, rent, school — with numbers and "went up by" or "rose to". About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — In my city, prices have gone up a lot this year. A bag of rice went up from five thousand to six thousand kwanzas — a rise of twenty percent. The taxi to my office rose to one thousand kwanzas. Only my rent stayed the same, because I have a two-year contract.]

**PROF (opinião):** In your opinion, which price rise is the hardest for families: food, transport or school? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — A2):** Which was the most expensive line in the project? Compare it with the hotels.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The equipment was the most expensive line. It cost much more than the hotels.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Went up by" for the difference, "to" for the final number, and "and" before the last part of a big number. Next lesson: what you'll be doing next week.

## 8C — This time next week

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8C — This time next week        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar do que estará a acontecer num momento do futuro com o
  future continuous (This time next week, I'll be presenting the budget.) e fazer perguntas educadas
  sobre os planos dos outros (Will you be using the car tomorrow?).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: will be + -ing; won't be + -ing; Will you be + -ing…?; this time next week / tomorrow
  at ten / all day on Monday; a diferença para will (decisão) e going to (intenção)
Palavras-alvo: present, prepare, install, travel, schedule, meeting room, car, available, all day,
  this time next week, at this time tomorrow
Reciclagem: futuros do A2 (will, going to, present continuous); dedução (8A)
Pronúncia: I'll be / we'll be / won't be, ligados e rápidos
Contexto: quarta-feira, 8 de Março, 17:00; o escritório; o Nuno organiza a semana seguinte com a Sílvia;
  o Paulo liga do Namibe, onde está a instalar os sensores com o engenheiro da Tsodilo
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 8C; conversa guiada "That can't be right!"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: how to talk about an action that will be in progress at a moment in the future — and how to ask about other people's plans politely.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 8 de Março, 17:00; o escritório; o Nuno com uma agenda aberta; a Sílvia à porta; o telemóvel do Nuno toca: "Paulo — Namibe"]

**SÍLVIA:** Nuno, when's the meeting with David?
**NUNO:** Next Wednesday at ten. So this time next week, I'll be presenting the Lobito numbers to him — and I'll probably be answering a lot of difficult questions.
**SÍLVIA:** I'll be there too, don't worry. Will you be using the big meeting room?
**NUNO:** Yes, if that's OK. David will be joining us by video from Johannesburg, and we need the big screen.
**SÍLVIA:** Fine. On Monday and Tuesday I'll be working at the bank all day, so let's check the slides together tomorrow.
**NUNO:** Perfect. Oh — it's Paulo. Hi, Paulo! How's Namibe?
**PAULO:** [ao telefone] Hot! The sensors are going in well. We'll be finishing the two rooms here tomorrow, and then Tsodilo's engineer will be flying to Lobito on Friday.
**NUNO:** Great. Will you be coming back to Luanda this weekend?
**PAULO:** On Saturday. Why? Do you need me?
**NUNO:** Can you join the meeting with David on Wednesday? You know the hotel costs better than me.
**PAULO:** Sure. I won't be travelling next week, so I'm free.
**NUNO:** Thanks. One more thing: will you be using the company car on Monday? I need to collect the last invoice from the hotel's office in Luanda.
**PAULO:** No, take it. I'll be at home, sleeping!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**O *future continuous***

[ECRÃ: linha do tempo: quarta, 15 de Março, 10:00 → 11:00 · This time next week, I**'ll be presenting** the numbers. (a acção estará a decorrer nesse momento)]

**PROF:** "Will be" plus "-ing" — the future continuous — is for an action that will be in progress at a moment in the future. At ten next Wednesday, Nuno will be in the middle of his presentation.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: I**'ll be working** at the bank all day on Monday. · I **won't be travelling** next week. · **Will** you **be coming** back this weekend?]

**PROF:** It's also used for plans that are part of a normal schedule: "I'll be working at the bank on Monday". The negative is "won't be".
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Perguntas educadas**

[ECRÃ: **Will you be using** the car on Monday? (educado: só quero saber o plano) · **Will you use** the car on Monday? (pode soar a pedido ou ordem)]

**PROF:** To ask about someone's plans — especially before you ask for something — "Will you be using…?" is softer and more polite. You aren't asking them to do anything; you just want to know.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Os futuros lado a lado**

[ECRÃ: decisão agora — I**'ll** call him now. · intenção — I**'m going to** prepare the slides. · combinação — I**'m meeting** David on Wednesday. · em curso num momento — At ten, I**'ll be presenting**.]

**PROF:** You know three futures from A2. The future continuous adds one more: the action in progress at a future moment.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "I'll be", "we'll be" and "won't be" are fast and joined. Listen: This time next week, I'll be presenting the numbers.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I'll be working at the bank all day.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** Will you be using the car on Monday?
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** What will they be doing?
[ECRÃ: Paulo — sexta, 10:00 — no avião para Luanda] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: At ten on Friday, Paulo will be flying to Luanda.]
[ECRÃ: Ask politely: the meeting room, this afternoon] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Will you be using the meeting room this afternoon?]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Imagine it's this time next week. What will you be doing at three different moments: in the morning, at lunchtime, and in the evening? About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — This time next week, I'll be travelling to Benguela for a training course. At lunchtime, I'll probably be eating fish with my colleagues by the sea. And in the evening, I'll be studying English in my hotel room — or watching football!]

**PROF (opinião):** Do you plan your week in detail, or do you prefer to decide day by day? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — 8A):** Paulo isn't answering his phone. He's installing sensors in Namibe. Make a guess.
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: He must be busy. / He might be in the server room.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Will be" plus "-ing" for an action in progress in the future — and "Will you be…?" to ask politely. Now talk to the tutor: "That can't be right!". Next lesson: the words of budgets and banking.

## 8D — Budgets and banking

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8D — Budgets and banking        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar palavras e colocações de orçamentos e finanças (budget,
  invoice, exchange rate, interest rate, loan, overspend, cut costs, make a profit), os phrasal verbs pay
  back, cut down on e set aside, distinguir os sons de share, change e budget, e evitar os falsos amigos
  tax e commodity.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 4
Gramática-alvo: colocações (cut costs, make a profit / a loss, go over budget, take out a loan, pay an
  invoice); phrasal verbs (pay back, cut down on, set aside)
Palavras-alvo: budget, invoice, receipt, exchange rate, interest rate, loan, overspend, over budget,
  under budget, profit, loss, savings, cost, cash flow, in advance, instalment, tax, fee, rate, commodity
Reciclagem: tendências (8B); dedução (8A)
Pronúncia: os sons "sh" (share, cash), "ch" (change, much) e "j" (budget, charge)
Falsos amigos: tax (≠ taxa → rate, fee; tax = imposto); commodity (≠ comodidade → comfort, convenience;
  commodity = mercadoria, matéria-prima)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 10 min
Verificação: Lab 8D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of budgets, banks and money — and three phrasal verbs for saving and spending.

### 2. Orçamentos e finanças (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| budget | The budget was fifty-seven million kwanzas. (orçamento) |
| over / under budget | The project was fifteen percent over budget. |
| overspend | We overspent on hotels. |
| invoice / receipt | Please send the invoice. (factura) · Keep the receipt. (recibo, talão) |
| exchange rate | The exchange rate went up to one thousand and fifty. (câmbio) |
| interest rate | The bank's interest rate is twenty percent. (taxa de juro) |
| loan | We took out a loan to buy the lorry. (empréstimo) |
| profit / loss | The company made a profit. / It made a loss. (lucro / prejuízo) |
| cash flow | We have good cash flow: clients pay on time. (tesouraria) |
| instalment | We pay in three instalments. (prestação) |

**PROF:** Repeat: budget. Invoice. Exchange rate. Interest rate. Loan. Profit. Loss.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 3. Colocações e *phrasal verbs* (2 min)

[ECRÃ: **cut** costs · **make** a profit / a loss · **go** over budget · **take out** a loan · **pay** an invoice]

**PROF:** These go together. We cut costs. A company makes a profit — or a loss. A project goes over budget. You take out a loan, and then…
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ:]

| *Phrasal verb* | Significado | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| pay back | devolver (dinheiro) | We'll pay back the loan in two years. |
| cut down on | gastar menos em | We need to cut down on hotels. |
| set aside | pôr de lado, reservar | Let's set aside five percent for problems. |

**PROF:** … you pay it back. "Cut down on" — spend less on something. "Set aside" — keep money for later.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: três sons (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: "sh" — **sh**are, ca**sh**, profe**ss**ional · "ch" — **ch**ange, mu**ch**, **ch**eap · "j" — bud**g**et, char**g**e, **j**ob]

**PROF:** Three sounds that Portuguese speakers often mix. "Sh", like the Portuguese "x" in "caixa": share, cash. "Ch" — a "t" before the "sh": change, much. "J" — a "d" before the sound: budget, charge.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: share, change, budget.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** Cash, much, charge.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **tax** ≠ taxa]

**PROF:** "Tax" is money you pay to the government: income tax, VAT.
(PT: "Taxa" diz-se "rate" — taxa de juro, "interest rate"; taxa de câmbio, "exchange rate" — ou "fee" — uma taxa de serviço, "a service fee".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: **commodity** ≠ comodidade]

**PROF:** A "commodity" is a product that is bought and sold in large amounts: oil, coffee, copper.

## 8F — Work & Life: The budget meeting

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8F — The budget meeting        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue explicar à direcção porque um projecto gastou mais do que o
  previsto, com números, tendências e causas, e propor cortes; e explicar em inglês uma tabela de custos
  escrita em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 4 (e 1, 2, 3)
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: números e tendências; dedução; future continuous; comparativos
Palavras-alvo: overspend, over budget, line, item, cause, propose, cut, save, risk, local supplier,
  monthly rate, contingency
Reciclagem: causas (B1, 6C): due to, as a result; 2.º condicional (B1, 4A); discurso indirecto (B1, 7A);
  opinião e acordo (B1, 3B)
Pronúncia: números em contraste (fifteen / fifty) e acento nas palavras que mudam (went up / went down)
Mediação: o Nuno explica ao David, em inglês, a tabela de custos que a equipa da Sílvia fez em português
Contexto: quarta-feira, 15 de Março, 10:00; a sala de reuniões grande; o Nuno, a Sílvia e o Paulo em
  Luanda; o David Nkosi por videochamada, de Joanesburgo

```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! It's Wednesday the fifteenth, ten o'clock — and Nuno is presenting the Lobito numbers to David, the finance director of Mopane Systems. Watch how he explains the overspend and proposes cuts.

### 2. Situação (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 15 de Março, 10:00; a sala de reuniões; no ecrã grande, o David; a tabela de custos em português]

[ECRÃ: a tabela, em português: "Projecto Lobito — Custos (milhões de Kz). Equipamento: orçamento 30,0 · real 33,0 · desvio +10%. Mão-de-obra (engenheiros): 12,0 · 15,0 · +25%. Alojamento e deslocações: 6,0 · 8,5 · +42%. Empreiteiro eléctrico: 9,0 · 9,0 · 0%. Danos do incidente: 0 (pagos pelo seguro). Total: 57,0 · 65,5 · +15%."]

**DAVID:** Morning, everyone. Nuno, I've seen the total. Sixty-five and a half million against fifty-seven. That's a big difference.
**NUNO:** It is — about fifteen percent over budget. The table is in Portuguese, so let me explain it line by line.
**NUNO:** Equipment: the budget was thirty million, and we spent thirty-three. That's ten percent more, and it's all due to the exchange rate. We pay Mopane in dollars, and the kwanza fell from nine hundred and fifty to one thousand and fifty per dollar.
**NUNO:** Labour — the engineers: twelve million in the budget, fifteen in reality — twenty-five percent more. That's because of the incident in January: Paulo and I worked two extra weeks.
**NUNO:** Hotels and travel: six million in the budget, eight and a half in reality — that's the biggest increase, forty-two percent. Two reasons: the two extra weeks, and the hotel raised its prices by almost twenty percent in February.
**NUNO:** The contractor stayed the same — nine million. And the damage from the incident was paid by the insurance, so it's zero.
**DAVID:** Thank you, that's clear. The exchange rate — that can't be the whole story, can it? Ten percent seems high.
**SÍLVIA:** It's the whole story for the equipment, David. The rate went up from nine fifty to one thousand fifty. That's about ten and a half percent.
**DAVID:** OK, I accept that. Now, what are you going to do about it in the next project?
**NUNO:** I have three proposals. First, we pay for the equipment as soon as we sign the contract, so the exchange rate can't change in the middle. Second, for long stays, we book an apartment at a monthly rate — it's about thirty percent cheaper than a hotel. **DAVID:** The first two, yes. The contingency — I'm not sure. If we add ten percent, our price will be higher than our competitors', and we might lose the contract.
**NUNO:** I see your point. What about five percent? It's lower, but it still covers most small problems.

**NUNO:** That's right — with all three changes.
**DAVID:** Excellent. Send me a summary today. Good work, everyone.

### 3. Como se explica um desvio de orçamento (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno fez]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
