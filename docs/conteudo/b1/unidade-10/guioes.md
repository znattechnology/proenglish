# B1.2 · Unidade 10 — Lessons learned · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B1](../unidade-01/guioes.md): tudo em inglês, e o português só nos falsos amigos e na mediação.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2, do B1.1 e das unidades 6 a 9. Novo: o 3.º condicional (*If we had sealed the duct earlier, there wouldn't have been a fire.*); lamentar e criticar com *should have / shouldn't have* e reagir com tacto (*Maybe we could have…*); especular sobre o passado com *must have / might have / could have / can't have*; esperanças e planos (*I'm hoping to…, I'd like to…, I'm planning to…*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** os condicionais mistos (*If we had…, we would be…*); *wish* e *if only*; a passiva com modais no passado (*should have been done*) e no *past perfect*; outros verbos de relato além dos da unidade 7. Estes ficam para o B2.

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mopane Systems, a Morro Azul Energia e a Tsodilo Sensors são fictícias; o inquérito de satisfação da 10F foi escrito por nós.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**; **Paulo**; **Lerato**; **Sílvia**; **Thandi** (por videochamada); **Hannah** (Morro Azul, Namibe); **Ingrid** (directora de operações da Morro Azul); **Pieter** (terminal do Lobito, por videochamada). Na 10E, quatro testemunhos: **Kofi** (B1 1C, 5E), **Neo** (B1 1F, 6F), **Hannah** e **Tomás** (cabo-verdiano, gestor de projectos numa empresa de construção; novo e fictício).

**A história:** Junho de 2028. Um ano depois de o Nuno entrar na equipa conjunta, a Kalandula Tech e a Mopane fazem o balanço de todo o trabalho com a Morro Azul: a ligação de rádio e o satélite no Namibe (B1.1), o projecto do Lobito e o incidente (B1 6), os sensores (B1 7) e o desvio do orçamento (B1 8). Na segunda, 5, o Nuno e o Paulo preparam os diapositivos (10A); na terça, 6, discutem com a Lerato e a Sílvia o que deviam ter feito de outra forma (10B); na quarta, 7, investigam porque é que o satélite do Namibe não ligou durante um corte no domingo (10C); na sexta, 16, a reunião de balanço com o cliente (10F).

## 10A — If we had known…

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10A — If we had known…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue imaginar um passado diferente com o 3.º condicional (If we had
  sealed the duct earlier, there wouldn't have been a fire.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: If + past perfect, would have / wouldn't have + particípio; could have / might have na
  segunda parte; perguntas (What would you have done?); contraste com o 2.º condicional (B1, 4A)
Palavras-alvo: slide, look back, earlier, on time, delay, avoid, decision, result, lucky, otherwise
Reciclagem: past perfect (B1, 6A); 2.º condicional (B1, 4A)
Pronúncia: would have → "would've" (dito "would-uv"); had → 'd ("If we'd known")
Contexto: segunda-feira, 5 de Junho de 2028, 15:00; o escritório; o Nuno e o Paulo preparam os
  diapositivos da reunião de balanço com a Morro Azul
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 10A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 10
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This is the last unit of B1. It's about looking back: what went well, what went wrong, and what we learned. Today: how to imagine a different past — "If we had known…".

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 5 de Junho, 15:00; o escritório; no ecrã do Nuno, um diapositivo: "Morro Azul — one year. What went well? What would we do differently?"]

**PAULO:** OK, slide four: the Lobito incident. What do we write?
**NUNO:** The facts first. Then the lesson. Honestly, if we had sealed the duct before the installation, there wouldn't have been a fire.
**PAULO:** True. And if the drain on the roof hadn't been… sorry — if Pieter's team had cleaned the drain, the water wouldn't have stayed on the roof.
**NUNO:** Both things. But we were lucky too. If Teodoro hadn't done the fire training, the damage would have been much worse.
**PAULO:** Much worse. Next slide: the antennas at customs.
**NUNO:** That one went well, in the end. If we had waited for customs, the antennas would have arrived after the crane left. We'd have lost at least a week.
**PAULO:** And the agent cost two thousand dollars. A week of delay would have cost much more.
**NUNO:** Right. So that was a good decision. And the budget slide?
**PAULO:** If we'd paid for the equipment when we signed, the exchange rate wouldn't have changed the price. We could have saved three million kwanzas.
**NUNO:** Exactly — that's why Sílvia made the new rules. What would you have done differently, if you had been the project leader?
**PAULO:** Me? I'd have booked an apartment from the first day! The hotel food was terrible.
**NUNO:** Ha! Put that on the slide too.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**O 3.º condicional**

[ECRÃ: If we **had sealed** the duct earlier, there **wouldn't have been** a fire.
(Não vedámos a conduta. Houve um incêndio. É imaginário: o passado não muda.)
**if** + *past perfect*, **would have** + particípio]

**PROF:** The third conditional imagines a different past — something that didn't happen, and its result. After "if", the past perfect: "had sealed". In the other part: "would have" plus the past participle.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: If Teodoro **hadn't done** the training, the damage **would have been** much worse. · We **could have saved** three million. · We **might have lost** the contract.]

**PROF:** "Could have" means it was possible. "Might have" means maybe. And notice the negatives: "hadn't done", "wouldn't have been".
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ If we would have sealed the duct, … · ✔ If we **had sealed** the duct, … · "Se tivéssemos vedado…" → If we **had sealed**…]

**PROF:** Careful: no "would" after "if" — just like the second conditional.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**2.º ou 3.º?**

[ECRÃ: 2.º — If I **were** the project leader, I**'d book** an apartment. (agora, imaginário) · 3.º — If I **had been** the project leader, I**'d have booked** an apartment. (no passado, imaginário)]

**PROF:** The second conditional imagines the present or the future. The third imagines the past.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **'d** = had (depois de if) · **'d** = would (na outra parte) · If we**'d** paid earlier, we**'d** have saved money.]

**PROF:** In speech, "'d" can be "had" or "would". After "if", it's "had"; before "have", it's "would".
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "Would have" is very short in speech: "would've". Listen: we would've saved money. We wouldn't have lost a week.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: If we'd known, we would've waited.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** If we had sealed the duct, there wouldn't have been a fire.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Imagine a different past.
[ECRÃ: I didn't set an alarm. I missed the flight. → If I had…] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: If I had set an alarm, I wouldn't have missed the flight.]
[ECRÃ: We checked the forecast. We didn't use the crane on the windy day. → If we hadn't…] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: If we hadn't checked the forecast, we might have used the crane on the windy day.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think of an important decision in your life — a job, a course, a city. What happened? Then imagine: if you had made a different decision, what would have happened? About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Five years ago, I decided to study accounting in Luanda. If I had stayed in Benguela, I wouldn't have met my wife at university. I might have become a teacher, like my father. I'm happy with my decision, but I sometimes think about it.]

**PROF (opinião):** Do you think it's useful to think about decisions in the past? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 4A):** And now, the present: if you were the project leader, what would you change?
[PAUSA 8 s]
[ECRÃ: If I were the project leader, I'd have a meeting with the client every week.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! "If" plus "had done", then "would have done" — for a past that didn't happen. Next lesson: "We should have checked".

## 10B — We should have checked

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10B — We should have checked        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue lamentar e criticar com should have / shouldn't have e reagir
  com tacto, sem culpar ninguém (Maybe we could have… It wasn't only your fault.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 3
Gramática-alvo: should have / shouldn't have + particípio; could have + particípio (sugestão sobre o
  passado); I / we (não you) para criticar com tacto
Palavras-alvo: check, ask, plan, warn, blame, fault, mistake, responsibility, team, together, fair
Reciclagem: 3.º condicional (10A); opinião e acordo (B1, 3B)
Pronúncia: should've (shud-uv), shouldn't have; acento de frase ao lamentar: We should have checked.
Contexto: terça-feira, 6 de Junho, 10:00; o gabinete da Lerato; a Lerato, a Sílvia, o Nuno e o Paulo revêem
  os diapositivos
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 10B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! When we look back, we often say what we should or shouldn't have done. Today: how to say it — and how to say it without blaming people.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 6 de Junho, 10:00; o gabinete da Lerato; os quatro à volta do portátil]

**LERATO:** These slides are good. But slide four is a bit hard on the contractor. "The contractor didn't seal the duct."
**NUNO:** It's true, though.
**LERATO:** It's true. But we were the project leaders. We should have checked the duct before we installed anything.
**NUNO:** You're right. I should have checked the duct myself on the Friday, before we left.
**SÍLVIA:** It wasn't only your fault, Nuno. Maybe we could have planned more time for the installation. The calendar was very tight.
**PAULO:** And we shouldn't have trusted the forecast. In Lobito, it rains a lot in January.
**LERATO:** OK. So let's write it like this: "We should have checked the room before the installation, and we should have planned more time." "We", not "the contractor".
**NUNO:** Good. And the budget slide?
**SÍLVIA:** I should have looked at the exchange rate more carefully in October. Everyone knew the kwanza was falling.
**LERATO:** We all could have done that. But we learned the lesson — that's why we have the new rules.
**PAULO:** What about the customs certificate? Mopane should have sent it with the antennas.
**LERATO:** Yes, but Thandi took responsibility immediately, and Mopane paid half of the agent's fee. Let's say: "The shipping documents should have been complete." Short and fair.
**NUNO:** OK. "We", not "they". Facts, not blame.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

***Should have / shouldn't have***

[ECRÃ: We **should have checked** the duct. (não verificámos — foi um erro) · We **shouldn't have trusted** the forecast. (confiámos — foi um erro) · should (not) have + particípio]

**PROF:** "Should have" plus a past participle is for a past action that was the right thing to do — but we didn't do it. "Shouldn't have" is for something we did, but it was a mistake.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: "Devíamos ter verificado." → ✔ We **should have checked**. · ✘ We should checked. · ✘ We should to have checked.]

**PROF:** The Portuguese "devíamos ter verificado" is "should have checked". Always "have" plus the participle.
[PAUSA 4 s]

***Could have* — uma sugestão com tacto**

[ECRÃ: **Maybe we could have planned** more time. · **We all could have** looked at the exchange rate.]

**PROF:** "Could have" is softer. It says "it was possible", not "it was wrong". Use it to suggest, gently, what was possible.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Criticar sem culpar**

[ECRÃ:
⚠ You should have checked the duct. (pode soar a acusação)
✔ **We** should have checked the duct. · ✔ It wasn't only your fault. · ✔ Maybe we could have…
✔ "The documents should have been complete." (factos, não pessoas)]

**PROF:** In a review meeting, say "we", not "you". Talk about facts, not people. It's more professional — and people learn more when they don't feel attacked.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "Should have" is short: "should've". The stress goes on the main verb. Listen: We should've checked.
[ECRÃ: We should've **checked**. · We shouldn't have **trusted** the forecast.]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: We should have checked the duct.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** We shouldn't have trusted the forecast.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Should have, or shouldn't have?
[ECRÃ: I didn't save the file. I lost two hours of work.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I should have saved the file.]
[ECRÃ: I ate a big lunch before the presentation. I felt sleepy.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I shouldn't have eaten a big lunch.]
[ECRÃ: Your colleague says: "It was my fault." Answer with tact.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: It wasn't only your fault. Maybe we could have planned it better together.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think of a project, a trip or an event that didn't go perfectly. Say two things you should have done, one thing you shouldn't have done, and one thing that went well. About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Last year I organised my sister's wedding party. We should have booked the hall earlier — it was very expensive. And we should have asked for help with the food. I shouldn't have invited so many people from work! But the music was fantastic, and everyone danced until two.]

**PROF (opinião):** In your opinion, why is it important to say "we" and not "you" in a review meeting?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — 10A):** If you had planned more time for the installation, what would have happened?
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: If we had planned more time, we would have checked the duct, and there wouldn't have been a fire.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Should have" and "shouldn't have" for regrets — and "we", "maybe we could have", to say it with tact. Next lesson: guessing what happened in the past.

## 10C — It must have been the cable

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10C — It must have been the cable        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue especular sobre o passado com must have (quase certo que sim),
  might have / could have (possível) e can't have (quase certo que não).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: must / might / could / can't + have + particípio; a diferença para a dedução no presente
  (B1, 8A); can't have (não mustn't have)
Palavras-alvo: power cut, outage, log, switch over, update, cable, loose, socket, cabinet, record, happen, cause
Reciclagem: dedução no presente (B1, 8A); tempos narrativos (B1, 6B)
Pronúncia: must've, might've, can't have (o "t" de can't quase não se ouve, mas o "a" é longo)
Contexto: quarta-feira, 7 de Junho, 9:00; videochamada; o Nuno em Luanda; a Hannah na sala de controlo do
  Namibe; no domingo houve um corte de energia e o satélite de reserva não ligou
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 10C; conversa guiada "If I had known…"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In unit eight you made guesses about the present: "It must be the exchange rate." Today: guesses about the past — "It must have been the cable."

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 7 de Junho, 9:00; videochamada; a Hannah com um ecrã cheio de registos atrás dela]

**HANNAH:** Morning, Nuno. We had a strange problem on Sunday. There was a sandstorm, the radio link went down at three in the afternoon — and the satellite didn't switch on. We were blind for forty minutes.
**NUNO:** Forty minutes? That's strange. The satellite has worked every time since November. Something must have changed.
**HANNAH:** I thought so too. Could it have been the weather? The storm was very strong.
**NUNO:** It can't have been the weather. The satellite worked during much bigger storms in February. What do the logs say?
**HANNAH:** The satellite router says "no power" since Saturday afternoon. The radio link came back at three forty — that's why we were blind for forty minutes.
**NUNO:** Since Saturday? Then the router must have lost its power supply on Saturday. Was anyone working in the room that day?
**HANNAH:** Let me check… Yes — Domingos's team cleaned the control room on Saturday morning.
**NUNO:** Ah. They might have moved the router's power cable when they were cleaning. And nobody noticed, because the radio link was working all Saturday.
**HANNAH:** That makes sense. But they always clean on Saturdays, and nothing happened before.
**NUNO:** True. Then maybe they cleaned behind the cabinet for the first time. Can you look at the cable now?
**HANNAH:** [pausa] You're right. It's half out of the socket. Someone must have kicked it.
**NUNO:** Mystery solved! Let's put a label on it — and a cable lock.
**HANNAH:** And add it to the cleaning checklist. Thanks, Nuno.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Especular sobre o passado**

[ECRÃ:
quase certo que SIM — Someone **must have kicked** it.
possível — They **might have moved** the cable. · The router **could have lost** power on Saturday.
quase certo que NÃO — It **can't have been** the weather.
must / might / could / can't + **have** + particípio]

**PROF:** For guesses about the past, add "have" plus the past participle. "Must have" — almost sure it happened. "Might have" or "could have" — maybe it happened. "Can't have" — almost sure it didn't happen.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Presente ou passado?**

[ECRÃ: presente — The router **must be** off. (agora) · passado — The router **must have lost** power. (no domingo)]

**PROF:** Compare: "must be" for now, "must have been" for the past.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ It mustn't have been the weather. · ✔ It **can't have been** the weather.]

**PROF:** Just like in unit eight: the opposite of "must have" is "can't have", not "mustn't have".
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: "Deve ter sido o cabo." → ✔ It **must have been** the cable. · "Pode ter sido a limpeza." → ✔ It **might have been** the cleaning. · "Não pode ter sido o tempo." → ✔ It **can't have been** the weather.]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In speech: "must've", "might've". "Can't have" is clear, with a long "a". Listen: It must've been the cable. It can't have been the weather.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: Someone must have kicked it.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** They might have moved the cable.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** It can't have been the weather.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make a guess about the past.
[ECRÃ: The office is wet this morning. The window is open. (almost sure)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: It must have rained in the night.]
[ECRÃ: Paulo didn't answer the phone. Maybe he was driving. (possible)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: He might have been driving.]
[ECRÃ: Your colleague says she sent the email, but you didn't get it. She's always careful. (almost sure: no)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: She can't have forgotten. It must have gone to spam.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Something strange happened: you arrived at work, and the door was open, the lights were on, and there were two coffee cups on your desk. Make four guesses about what happened. About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — The cleaner can't have left the door open — she's very careful. The security guard might have come in during the night. Or my manager could have worked late. The two cups mean two people must have been here. Maybe they had a meeting at my desk!]

**PROF (opinião):** In your opinion, is it better to find the cause of a problem, or just to fix it quickly? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 8A):** And now, the present. The router's light is off. Make a guess.
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: It must be unplugged. / It might be broken.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Must have", "might have", "can't have" — guesses about the past. Now talk to the tutor: "If I had known…". Next lesson: the words of reviews and decisions.

## 10D — Reviews and decisions

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10D — Reviews and decisions        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar as palavras e colocações de um balanço de projecto
  (lesson, outcome, feedback, success, learn a lesson, take a risk, meet a deadline), falar de esperanças e
  planos (I'm hoping to, I'd like to, I'm planning to), dizer bem as formas fracas should've, would've,
  might've, e evitar os falsos amigos data e balance.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: colocações (learn a lesson, take a risk, meet / miss a deadline, give / get feedback, make
  a decision, reach a goal); esperanças e planos
Palavras-alvo: review, lesson, outcome, result, feedback, improve, risk, success, failure, goal,
  achievement, recommendation, next steps, data, date, balance, assessment
Reciclagem: 3.º condicional (10A); should have (10B)
Pronúncia: formas fracas: should've, would've, might've, must've, could've
Falsos amigos: data (≠ data → date; data = dados); balance (≠ balanço → review, assessment; balance =
  equilíbrio, saldo)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 10 min
Verificação: Lab 10D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of a project review — lessons, outcomes and feedback — and how to talk about your hopes and plans.

### 2. Balanço de um projecto (2 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| review | The project review is on Friday. (reunião de balanço) |
| outcome | The outcome was positive: the client is happy. (resultado final) |
| feedback | Morro Azul gave us very good feedback. (comentários, opinião) |
| lesson | The main lesson: check the room first. |
| success / failure | The satellite was a success. |
| goal / achievement | Our goal was zero hours offline. It was a big achievement. |
| recommendation / next steps | Our recommendations for the next project… |

**PROF:** Repeat: review. Outcome. Feedback. Lesson. Success. Achievement.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 3. Colocações e planos (2 min)

[ECRÃ: **learn** a lesson · **take** a risk · **meet** a deadline / **miss** a deadline · **give** / **get** feedback · **make** a decision · **reach** a goal]

**PROF:** These go together. We learned a lesson. We took a risk with the customs agent — and it worked. We met the deadline.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: esperanças e planos — **I'm hoping to** lead the next project. · **I'd like to** take the B2 exam. · **I'm planning to** study two evenings a week.]

**PROF:** For the future: "I'm hoping to" — something you want, but you're not sure. "I'd like to" — a wish, politely. "I'm planning to" — you've already decided.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: formas fracas (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: should've · would've · might've · must've · could've]

**PROF:** In fast speech, "have" after these words is just "uv". Listen: should've, would've, might've.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: We should've checked. It would've been worse. It might've been the cable.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ should of · ✔ should have / should've]

**PROF:** Careful when you write: "should've" is "should have", never "should of" — even if it sounds the same.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **data** ≠ data]

**PROF:** "Data" means information, numbers, facts: "The sensors send data every minute."
(PT: "Data" no sentido de dia do calendário diz-se "date": "Qual é a data da reunião?" → "What's the date of the meeting?")
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: **balance** ≠ balanço]

**PROF:** "Balance" means equilibrium — between work and life — or the money in your account: "my bank balance".
(PT: "Balanço" no sentido de avaliação diz-se "review" ou "assessment": "a reunião de balanço" → "the review meeting". O balanço de contas de uma empresa é "the balance sheet".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (2 min)

**PROF:** Complete.
[ECRÃ: We made a mistake, but we … a lesson.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: learned]
[ECRÃ: We finished on time. We … the deadline.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: met]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Look back at the last year — at work or in your studies. What was your biggest achievement, what lesson did you learn, and what are you hoping to do next year? About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — My biggest achievement this year was the new client database. We met every deadline, and the feedback was great. The main lesson: ask the users before you start. Next year, I'm hoping to lead a team, and I'm planning to take a management course.]

**PROF (opinião):** What's more useful for you: positive feedback or negative feedback? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF:** Well done! Next lesson: four people talk about their mistakes — and what they'd do differently.

## 10E — Real English: What I'd do differently

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10E — What I'd do differently        Tipo: Real English
```

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

**PROF:** At a regional conference in Johannesburg, four people talk on a panel called "My best mistake". You know three of them. Listen for three things: the mistake, the lesson, and the plan.

### 2. Primeira escuta: a ideia geral (4 min)

**PROF:** First: whose mistake was about money?

**KOFI:** My best mistake? Three years ago, I chose the cheapest supplier for a big network project in Ghana. The equipment kept failing, and we had to replace half of it after a year. If I had compared quality and not only price, we would have saved money in the end. The lesson: cheap can be expensive. Now I always ask for references from other clients. And next year I'm hoping to write a guide for young engineers about choosing suppliers.

**NEO:** Mine is about people. When I became operations director, I tried to do everything myself. I didn't trust my team. I worked fourteen hours a day, and I got really ill. I should have asked for help much earlier. My team was ready — I just didn't see it. Now I give each manager clear goals and let them decide. And I'm planning to take a long holiday in December — my first in four years!

**HANNAH:** For me, it was the radio link at our solar plant in Namibe. When we built the plant, we didn't think enough about the sandstorms. We should have tested the link in the windy season before we chose it. It might have cost a few weeks more, but we wouldn't have had two years of problems. In the end, a team from Luanda and Johannesburg solved it with a satellite backup. The lesson: test in the worst conditions, not the best. Next, I'd like to use that lesson on our second plant.

**TOMÁS:** My best mistake was in my first year as a project manager in Praia. We were building a school, and I promised the city that it would be ready in nine months. I didn't ask the builders first. It took fourteen months. I shouldn't have promised a date before I had the data. Since then, I never give a deadline without my team. And I'm hoping to bring that rule to a new hospital project next year.

[ECRÃ: Kofi — o fornecedor mais barato → comparar qualidade, pedir referências → um guia para engenheiros · Neo — fazer tudo sozinha → confiar na equipa → umas férias longas · Hannah — não testou a ligação de rádio na época do vento → testar nas piores condições → a segunda central · Tomás — prometeu uma data sem perguntar → nunca dar um prazo sem a equipa → um hospital]

### 3. Segunda escuta: os pormenores (3 min)

1. What happened to Kofi's cheap equipment? (They had to replace half of it after a year. ✓ / It never arrived. / It was stolen.)
2. What does Kofi always do now? (He asks for references from other clients. ✓ / He chooses the cheapest supplier. / He buys from Ghana only.)
3. Why did Neo get ill? (She worked fourteen hours a day. ✓ / She travelled too much. / She ate badly.)
4. What should Hannah's team have done? (tested the radio link in the windy season ✓ / bought a satellite first / built the plant in Luanda)
5. How long did Tomás's school take? (fourteen months ✓ / nine months / four months)
6. What is Tomás's rule now? (He never gives a deadline without his team. ✓ / He never builds schools. / He always adds two months.)

*(Cada resposta repete-se; depois aparecem as perguntas, com 8 s para responder.)*

### 4. Terceira escuta: como disseram (2 min)

**PROF:** Listen and repeat.
**PROF:** If I had compared quality, we would have saved money. [PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** I should have asked for help much earlier. [PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** It might have cost a few weeks more. [PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** I shouldn't have promised a date. [PAUSA 4 s]

**PROF:** Did you notice? Each story has the same shape: the mistake — with "should have" or the third conditional — then the lesson, in one short sentence, and then the plan, with "I'm hoping to", "I'm planning to" or "I'd like to". And nobody blames anyone else.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. A sua vez (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Now you. Tell me about your "best mistake": what happened, what you should have done, the lesson, and your plan. About forty-five seconds.
[PAUSA 45 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — My best mistake was at my first job. I didn't ask questions, because I didn't want to look stupid. I should have asked much more — I made a lot of mistakes in the first month. The lesson: a question takes one minute, a mistake takes one day. Now I'm hoping to help new colleagues feel comfortable asking.]

**PROF (opinião):** Why do you think the panel is called "My best mistake"? Can a mistake be good?
[PAUSA 20 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent listening! Next lesson: the project review with Morro Azul — Nuno leads part of the meeting.

## 10F — Work & Life: Project review

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10F — Project review        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue conduzir parte de uma reunião de balanço: o que correu bem, o
  que se faria de outra forma e as recomendações para o próximo projecto; e resumir em inglês um inquérito
  de satisfação do cliente escrito em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4 (e 1, 2)
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: 3.º condicional; should have; especular sobre o passado; esperanças e
  planos
Palavras-alvo: review, achievement, lesson, recommendation, next steps, survey, score, satisfied,
  response, average
Reciclagem: resumir e citar (B1, 9C); números e percentagens (B1, 8B); discurso indirecto (B1, 7A)
Pronúncia: um balanço em três partes, com pausas e a voz a descer no fim de cada parte
Mediação: o Nuno resume em inglês o inquérito de satisfação que a equipa da Morro Azul respondeu em
  português
Contexto: sexta-feira, 16 de Junho, 10:00; a sala de reuniões; a Lerato, a Sílvia, o Nuno e o Paulo; a
  Ingrid e a Hannah presentes; a Thandi e o Pieter por videochamada
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 10F; conversa livre "Project review"
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This is the last lesson of B1. It's the project review with Morro Azul, and Lerato has asked Nuno to lead one part. Watch how he talks about successes, mistakes and recommendations — and how he ends with his own plans.

### 2. Situação (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 16 de Junho, 10:00; a sala de reuniões; a Ingrid e a Hannah à mesa; a Thandi e o Pieter no ecrã; um diapositivo: "One year with Morro Azul — what went well, what we'd do differently, next steps"]

**LERATO:** Thank you all for coming. Nuno will lead the next part: the year in three steps.
**NUNO:** Thank you, Lerato. First, what went well. In Namibe, the satellite backup has worked perfectly since November — except one Sunday, when a power cable came loose. Before the backup, the plant lost about thirty hours a month; now, almost zero. In Lobito, the network and the cameras work, and the water sensors have already sent two real alerts — both small leaks, both fixed the same day.
**INGRID:** That's a real achievement.
**NUNO:** Thank you. Second, what we'd do differently. We should have checked the server room in Lobito before we installed the equipment. If we had done that, there wouldn't have been a fire. We should have paid for the equipment when we signed, too: if we had, we'd have saved about three million kwanzas. And on the antennas, we should have checked the shipping documents before the shipment left Johannesburg.
**PIETER:** To be fair, the roof was our responsibility. We should have cleaned the drains.
**NUNO:** That's kind, Pieter. Let's say we both learned a lesson.
**HANNAH:** Can I add one? The radio link was our choice, before Kalandula came. If we had tested it in the windy season, we'd never have had those problems.
**NUNO:** Thank you, Hannah. Third, our recommendations for the next project — the plant in Tômbwa. One: test everything in the worst conditions. Two: check the rooms before any installation. Three: a weekly call with Morro Azul, so that small problems don't become big ones.
**INGRID:** I agree with all three. Before we finish, I have the results of our internal survey. It's in Portuguese — Nuno, could you summarise it for Thandi and Pieter?
**NUNO:** Of course.

[ECRÃ: o inquérito, em português (fictício): "Inquérito de satisfação — equipa da Morro Azul (32 respostas). Satisfação geral: 4,3 em 5. O que correu melhor: a rapidez de resposta da equipa conjunta (81% muito satisfeitos); o sistema de reserva por satélite. O que deve melhorar: a comunicação antes das instalações (avisar com mais antecedência); a formação dos técnicos locais. Comentário mais repetido: 'Uma equipa que admite os erros e resolve.'"]

**NUNO:** It says thirty-two people from Morro Azul answered. The general satisfaction was four point three out of five. What went best was how fast the joint team answered — eighty-one percent were very satisfied — and the satellite backup. What should improve: communication before installations — they want more notice — and the training of local technicians. And the most common comment was: "A team that admits its mistakes and fixes them."
**THANDI:** That's the best comment we could get.
**INGRID:** I think so too. For Tômbwa, we'd like the same team.
**LERATO:** Thank you, Ingrid. Nuno, any last words?
**NUNO:** Just my plans. I'm hoping to lead our offer for Tômbwa, and I'm planning to take the B2 English exam next year. A year ago, I was nervous on every call. I'd like to thank everyone in this room for that change.
**LERATO:** Well said. Thank you, everyone.

### 3. Como se conduz um balanço (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno fez]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. O que correu bem — com números | present perfect; números | The satellite has worked perfectly… now, almost zero. |
| 2. O que faríamos de outra forma | should have / shouldn't have; 3.º condicional | We should have checked the server room. If we had done that, there wouldn't have been a fire. |
| 3. Sem culpar ninguém | we; Let's say we both learned a lesson. | That's kind, Pieter. Let's say we both learned a lesson. |
| 4. Recomendações | One… Two… Three… so that… | A weekly call, so that small problems don't become big ones. |
| 5. Planos pessoais | I'm hoping to… I'm planning to… I'd like to… | I'm hoping to lead our offer for Tômbwa. |

**PROF:** Nuno starts with successes — with numbers. Then he talks about mistakes using "we", and when Pieter takes some of the responsibility, Nuno doesn't attack him: "Let's say we both learned a lesson." Then clear recommendations, numbered.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**A mediação**

**PROF:** The survey has numbers, two lists and a quote. Nuno starts with "It says…", gives the main numbers — thirty-two answers, four point three out of five, eighty-one percent — then the best points and the points to improve, and he ends with the quote, because it's the most powerful part.
(PT: Num inquérito, comece pelo número de respostas e pela nota geral; depois, o melhor e o que deve melhorar; e termine com o comentário mais repetido. Lembre-se: "4,3" lê-se "four point three".)
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In a review, pause between the three parts, and let your voice go down at the end of each one. Listen.
**PROF:** First, what went well. [pausa] Second, what we'd do differently. [pausa] Third, our recommendations.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Now you. Say the three parts.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 5. A sua vez (3 min)

**PROF:** Lead the review of a project, an event or a course you know, in about forty-five seconds: one thing that went well, with a number; one thing you should have done differently; and one recommendation for next time. End with your plan.
[PAUSA 45 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — First, what went well: our company's training week had ninety people, and the feedback was very good. Second, what we'd do differently: we should have booked a bigger room — if we had, people wouldn't have stood at the back. Third, my recommendation: register people a month before. Next year, I'm hoping to organise it again.]

**PROF:** Now the mediation. Summarise this survey in English for a foreign colleague.
[ECRÃ: inquérito fictício: "Formação de Março — 45 respostas. Satisfação geral: 4,1 em 5. O melhor: os exemplos práticos (88% satisfeitos). A melhorar: a sala era pequena; o almoço chegou tarde. Comentário mais repetido: 'Quero mais formações assim.'"]
[PAUSA 35 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — It says forty-five people answered, and the general satisfaction was four point one out of five. The best part was the practical examples — eighty-eight percent were satisfied. What should improve: the room was small, and lunch arrived late. The most common comment was: "I want more training like this."]

**PROF (opinião):** Looking back at your English this year: what's your biggest achievement, and what are you hoping to do next?
[PAUSA 25 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Fantastic! You can now look back at a project, say what went well and what you'd do differently — without blaming anyone — and talk about your plans. Go to the tutor for the conversation "Project review". And congratulations: this is the end of the ten units of B1. Next: the review, and the B1.2 oral exam.
