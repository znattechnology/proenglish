# B1.2 · Revisão 2 (unidades 6 a 10) · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B1](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que a revisão pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2 e do B1 (unidades 1 a 10), e nada de novo, com uma excepção, que é uma estratégia de exame: ganhar tempo e organizar uma resposta de opinião (*That's an interesting question. It depends. On the one hand… On the other hand… So, overall…*). As estratégias anteriores mantêm-se (*Let me think. Do you mean…? Sorry, I mean… What I mean is… In other words… There are two reasons. Firstly… Secondly…*).

**Não usar:** os condicionais mistos; *wish* e *if only*; a passiva com modais no passado; verbos de relato fora da unidade 7 (*suggest, claim, deny*). Estes ficam para o B2.

**Personagens:** **Nuno** e **Mei Chen** (de Lusaca; B1 1E e 6F), numa videochamada (R2A); **Lurdes Cassoma**, a aluna-modelo dos exames (contabilista num banco, de Benguela), agora no exame do B1.2 (R2B).

**Quando:** sexta-feira, 30 de Junho de 2028, quase um ano depois de o Nuno ter voado para Joanesburgo (B1 1A).

## R2B — Getting ready for the B1.2 speaking exam

```
Curso: B1.2        Unidade: Revisão 2        Aula: R2B — Getting ready for the B1.2 speaking exam        Tipo: Revisão
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue descrever as quatro partes do exame oral do B1.2, sabe o que é
  avaliado e usa uma estratégia nova: ganhar tempo e organizar uma resposta de opinião (That's an
  interesting question. It depends. On the one hand… On the other hand… So, overall…).
Objectivos de unidade que revê: U6–U10, todos
Gramática revista: toda a do B1
Língua nova (bloco fixo): That's an interesting question. · It depends on… · So, overall…
Situação: a Lurdes faz o exame de demonstração do B1.2
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: conversa de ensaio; exame oral do B1.2
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Soon you take your B1.2 speaking exam — the last exam of B1. Today you see how it works, you learn one more strategy, and you watch a practice exam.

### 2. Como é o exame (2 min)

[ECRÃ: quatro blocos]

| Parte | O quê | Tempo |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Interview | Os seus projectos, uma chamada ou reunião recente e os seus planos (*What are you working on? What did they say? What will you be doing next month?*) | cerca de 2 minutos |
| 2. Long turn | Contar, sozinho, um incidente ou um problema que viveu: o que aconteceu, porquê, o que se fez; depois, duas perguntas do examinador | cerca de 2 minutos |
| 3. Situation | Uma situação sorteada de quatro: relatar uma chamada; explicar um desvio de orçamento; resumir e comentar uma notícia; uma reunião de balanço | cerca de 3 minutos |
| 4. Discussion | Uma pergunta de opinião ligada à situação; o examinador dá outra opinião, e o aluno responde | cerca de 1 a 2 minutos |

**PROF:** The exam is like the B1.1 exam, but a little longer — nine or ten minutes — and the long turn is now a story: something that happened to you.
(PT: É como o exame do B1.1, um pouco mais longo — nove ou dez minutos — e o turno longo é agora uma história: algo que lhe aconteceu.)

**PROF:** The rules are the same: the recording is your choice, the exam opens when you finish most of the B1.2 lessons, and when you don't pass, you can try again a week later.
(PT: A gravação é opcional (com ela, a pronúncia conta), o exame abre com 80% das aulas do B1.2 feitas, e pode repetir uma semana depois.)

### 3. O que é avaliado (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: cinco ícones]

| Competência | No B1.2, quer dizer… |
|---|---|
| Task — a tarefa | Conta a história com ordem e causas; relata, resume e explica com números; responde ao argumento do examinador. |
| Grammar — a gramática | Escolhe entre muitos tempos (narrativos, *future continuous*), relata com o discurso indirecto, faz deduções e usa o 3.º condicional e *should have*. |
| Vocabulary — o vocabulário | As palavras das unidades 6 a 10: incidentes, chamadas, dinheiro, notícias, balanços — e explica o que não sabe. |
| Fluency — a fluência | Turnos longos e organizados, com conectores (*although, therefore, as a result*); gere a conversa. |
| Pronunciation — a pronúncia | Percebe-se sempre; números, datas e formas fracas claros. (Só com a gravação.) |

### 4. Uma estratégia nova: ganhar tempo e organizar a opinião (2 min)

[ECRÃ: **That's an interesting question.** · **It depends on…** · **On the one hand, …** · **On the other hand, …** · **So, overall, …**]

**PROF:** In the discussion, the questions are bigger. If you need a few seconds, say "That's an interesting question" — it's natural, and it gives you time. Then show both sides: "It depends on…", "On the one hand… On the other hand…". And finish with your view: "So, overall, I think…".
[PAUSA 4 s]
[ECRÃ: "Should companies ban phones at work?" — "That's an interesting question. It depends on the job. On the one hand, phones interrupt our concentration. On the other hand, many people use them for work. So, overall, I think clear rules are better than a ban."]
**PROF:** That's an interesting question. It depends on the job. On the one hand, phones interrupt our concentration. On the other hand, many people use them for work. So, overall, I think clear rules are better than a ban.
[PAUSA 4 s]
(PT: Mostrar os dois lados antes de dar a opinião não é fugir à pergunta: no B1, é uma resposta mais forte.)

[ECRÃ: já conhece — **Let me think.** · **Do you mean…?** · **Sorry, I mean…** · **What I mean is…** · **There are two reasons. Firstly… Secondly…**]
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 5. Um exame de demonstração (4 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Now watch Lurdes — at the end of B1.

[ECRÃ: "Practice exam B1.2 — Lurdes"; ícone do examinador]

**EXAMINADOR:** Hello, Lurdes. Welcome to your B1.2 speaking exam. What are you working on at the moment?
**LURDES:** I'm working on the bank's half-year report. I've been preparing it since May, and I'm presenting it to the board next week.
**EXAMINADOR:** Tell me about a call or a meeting you had recently.
**LURDES:** Yesterday my manager called me. She said the board wanted the report two days earlier, and she asked me if I could finish it by Tuesday. I told her it would be difficult, but possible.
**EXAMINADOR:** Thank you. Now tell me about an incident or a problem you lived. What happened, and why?
**LURDES:** OK. Last year, our branch in Benguela lost all its internet for a whole day. At first, we thought it was the provider. But while the technicians were checking the cables, they found that a construction company had cut a fibre cable in the street. As a result, we couldn't open any accounts or make any payments. We sent the clients to another branch, and we wrote the urgent operations on paper. In the end, the connection came back at six in the evening.
**EXAMINADOR:** Could it have been avoided?
**LURDES:** I think so. If we had had a second connection — a 4G backup, for example — we wouldn't have closed for a day.
**EXAMINADOR:** Looking back, what should the bank have done?
**LURDES:** We should have asked for a backup line years ago. After that day, we did — now every branch has one.
**EXAMINADOR:** Now a situation. I'm your manager. Yesterday you had a call with a supplier of office chairs. Please tell me what they said.
**LURDES:** Sure. The supplier said the price was ninety dollars a chair. At first, she told me the delivery would take four weeks, but I explained that we needed them before the new office opens, and in the end she agreed to deliver in three. She asked if we could pay half in advance.
**EXAMINADOR:** Half in advance? That's too much.
**LURDES:** I know. I told her our normal rule is thirty percent, and she promised to check with her manager. She'll call me back on Monday.
**EXAMINADOR:** OK. Now a question for discussion. Is it better to work for a big company or a small one?
**LURDES:** That's an interesting question. It depends on the person. On the one hand, a big company like my bank gives you training and a stable salary. On the other hand, in a small company you do more things, and you learn faster. So, overall, I think a big company is better at the start of your career.
**EXAMINADOR:** But some people say big companies are slow, and nobody listens to young people.
**LURDES:** I see your point. That can be true. But what I mean is: at the start, you need good training and a good manager. My first manager at the bank taught me everything. After some years, a small company can be a great next step.
**EXAMINADOR:** Thank you, Lurdes. The exam is finished. You can press the finish button now.

**PROF:** Look at what Lurdes does well.
[ECRÃ: lista com vistos]
**PROF:** In part one, she reports her manager's call with "she said" and "she asked me if". In the long turn, she tells a story with the three past tenses and "as a result", and she uses the third conditional and "should have". In the situation, she reports a negotiation, and she answers the manager's objection. In the discussion, she uses the new strategy — "That's an interesting question. It depends… On the one hand… On the other hand… So, overall" — and she answers the counter-argument.
(PT: Relata, conta uma história com causas, usa o 3.º condicional e o *should have*, responde à objecção e organiza a opinião com os dois lados.)
[PAUSA 4 s]
