# B1.1 · Unidade 1 — Making connections · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do A2](../../a2/unidade-01/guioes.md), com as regras do B1 ([plano do B1, secção 10](../../04-plano-B1.md#10-guiões-e-revisão-de-cada-aula)): tudo em inglês, e o português só nos falsos amigos e na mediação.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 e do A2. Novo: *question tags* (com *be*, auxiliares e *do / does / did*; *I'm…, aren't I?*; *Let's…, shall we?*) e a entoação que confirma ou que pergunta; perguntas indirectas (*Could you tell me where…? Do you know if…?*); perguntas de sujeito e de objecto (revistas) e perguntas de seguimento; respostas curtas de concordância (*So do I. Neither do I. So did I. So am I.*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** *used to*; o *present perfect continuous*; *too / enough*; *so / such* + adjectivo; o 2.º condicional e *would* hipotético (também "That would be great"); *might / may* de probabilidade; a voz passiva (também "is based in", "was founded"); orações relativas, com ou sem pronome ("the company I work for"); o *past perfect*; o discurso indirecto no passado ("She said it was…"); o *future continuous*; *must / can't* de dedução.

**Personagens:** **Nuno** (Nuno Kapinga, de Lubango, na equipa de apoio conjunta desde 1 de Julho); **Lerato Dlamini** (coordenadora da equipa); **Sipho** (técnico de apoio da Mopane Systems; ajudou o Nuno ao telefone em Março, A2 8F; conhecem-se agora em pessoa). Vozes novas: **Zanele** (recepcionista do centro de formação), **Kofi Asante** (ganês, de Acra, engenheiro de redes numa empresa de energia solar), **Mei Chen** (chinesa, programadora numa empresa de pagamentos móveis em Lusaca), **Carlos Mondlane** (moçambicano, de Maputo, responsável de informática numa empresa do porto), **Neo Molefe** (do Botsuana, de Gaborone, directora de operações numa empresa de logística) e **Hannah Becker** (alemã, engenheira de projecto da Morro Azul Energia, na central solar do Namibe).

**A história:** Julho de 2027. O Nuno voou para Joanesburgo na segunda-feira, 5 de Julho, para a formação de duas semanas da equipa conjunta, na Mopane Systems (de terça, 6, a sexta, 16 de Julho). É Inverno em Joanesburgo: de manhã estão cinco graus (A2, revisão 2).

## 1A — You're Nuno, aren't you?

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1A — You're Nuno, aren't you?        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue confirmar informação com question tags (com be, auxiliares
  e do / does / did; I'm…, aren't I?; Let's…, shall we?) e usar a entoação descendente para confirmar e
  a ascendente para perguntar (You're from Lubango, aren't you?).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: question tags: frase afirmativa + tag negativa, frase negativa + tag afirmativa; o mesmo
  auxiliar (be, do / does / did, have, can, will); I'm…, aren't I?; Let's…, shall we?; respostas curtas
Palavras-alvo: in person, training, joint team, coordinator, degrees, warm coat, half past eight
Reciclagem: past simple (A2, 2); present perfect (A2, 6); short answers (A1)
Pronúncia: entoação da tag: descendente (tenho a certeza) e ascendente (não tenho a certeza)
Contexto: terça-feira, 6 de Julho, 8:20; à entrada do centro de formação da Mopane Systems, em
  Joanesburgo; o Nuno conhece o Sipho em pessoa
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 1A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 1
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello, and welcome to B1! From now on, our lessons are all in English. In this unit, you'll learn to start conversations with new people and keep them going. Today: how to check information with a short question at the end of a sentence.

### 2. Situação (2 min)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 6 de Julho, 8:20; Joanesburgo, cinco graus; a entrada de um centro de formação; letreiro "Mopane Systems — Training Centre"; o Nuno, de casaco quente, à porta]

**SIPHO:** Excuse me — you're Nuno, aren't you? From Kalandula Tech?
**NUNO:** Yes, that's right. And you're… Sipho, aren't you? From the help desk?
**SIPHO:** That's me! We spoke on the phone in March, didn't we? The switch after the power cut.
**NUNO:** Yes! You really helped me that day. It's great to meet you in person.
**SIPHO:** You too. So you're in the new joint team now, aren't you?
**NUNO:** I am. I started last week. Lerato's our coordinator.
**SIPHO:** She's great. Wow, it's cold this morning, isn't it?
**NUNO:** Very cold! In Luanda it's twenty-five degrees today. I bought a warm coat, but I'm still cold.
**SIPHO:** Ha! You arrived yesterday, didn't you?
**NUNO:** Yes, last night. Er… the training starts at nine, doesn't it?
**SIPHO:** No, at half past eight.
**NUNO:** Half past eight? Oh no. I'm late, aren't I?
**SIPHO:** No, no — it's only twenty past. Relax. Let's get a coffee first, shall we?
**NUNO:** Good idea.
**SIPHO:** You like coffee, don't you?
**NUNO:** Of course — I'm Angolan! We grow great coffee.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**O que é uma *question tag***

[ECRÃ: You're Nuno, **aren't you?** · We spoke in March, **didn't we?** · It's cold, **isn't it?**]

**PROF:** A question tag is a short question at the end of a sentence. We use it to check information — when we think it's true — or to ask the other person to agree.
[PAUSA 3 s]

**A regra**

[ECRÃ:
frase afirmativa → tag negativa: You**'re** new, **aren't** you?
frase negativa → tag afirmativa: You **don't** take sugar, **do** you?
o mesmo auxiliar: You**'ve** met Lerato, **haven't** you? · You **can** drive, **can't** you? · It**'ll** be cold, **won't** it?]

**PROF:** If the sentence is positive, the tag is negative. If the sentence is negative, the tag is positive. And use the same auxiliary verb: are — aren't; have — haven't; can — can't; will — won't.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Sem auxiliar: *do, does, did***

[ECRÃ: You **like** coffee, **don't** you? · The training **starts** at nine, **doesn't** it? · You **arrived** yesterday, **didn't** you?]

**PROF:** When there is no auxiliary, use do, does or did — like in a normal question. Present simple: don't, doesn't. Past simple: didn't.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Dois casos especiais**

[ECRÃ: I**'m** late, **aren't I?** (não "amn't I") · **Let's** get a coffee, **shall we?**]

**PROF:** Two special tags. "I'm late, aren't I?" And for suggestions with "let's": "shall we?"
[PAUSA 3 s]

**Como responder**

[ECRÃ: You're from Lubango, aren't you? — **Yes, I am.** / **No, I'm not.** Actually, I'm from Huambo.]

**PROF:** Answer with a short answer. And if the information is wrong, say "Actually…" and correct it — politely.
[PAUSA 3 s]

**O erro mais comum**

[ECRÃ: ✘ You're Nuno, **isn't it**? · ✘ You arrived yesterday, **no**? · ✔ You're Nuno, **aren't you**? · ✔ You arrived yesterday, **didn't you**?]

**PROF:** Careful. In English, the tag changes with the sentence. "Isn't it" and "no" don't work for every sentence. Look at the subject and the verb, and build the tag from them.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: a entoação da tag (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Listen. When you're sure, and you only want the other person to agree, your voice goes down on the tag.
**PROF:** It's cold this morning, isn't it? [voz a descer]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** When you're not sure, and it's a real question, your voice goes up.
**PROF:** The training starts at nine, doesn't it? [voz a subir]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat. Down: You're Nuno, aren't you? [voz a descer]
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Up: The training starts at nine, doesn't it? [voz a subir]
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Down: Let's get a coffee, shall we? [voz a descer]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Complete the tag. Say the whole sentence.
[ECRÃ: You're learning English, … ?] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: … aren't you?]
[ECRÃ: You work in an office, … ?] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: … don't you?]
[ECRÃ: You didn't study English at school, … ?] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: … did you?]
[ECRÃ: You've finished A2, … ?] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: … haven't you?]

**PROF:** Now, a longer turn. Think of a colleague or a friend. Check three things about this person with tags — and use the right intonation. Then imagine the answers.
[PAUSA 30 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — You live in Talatona, don't you? You started in January, didn't you? You can't come to the meeting on Friday, can you?]

**PROF (opinião):** What do you think? Is it a good idea to check information with a tag when you meet someone for the first time? Or is it better to ask a normal question? Why?
[PAUSA 30 s]
[ECRÃ: I think tags are useful because… / I prefer normal questions because…]

**PROF (reciclagem — A2, 6):** And you? Have you ever been on a training course in another city or country? Where? What was it like?
[PAUSA 20 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Now you can check information politely, with the right tag and the right intonation. Next lesson: polite questions for strangers.

## 1B — Could you tell me where room four is?

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1B — Could you tell me where room four is?        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue fazer perguntas educadas a pessoas que não conhece com
  perguntas indirectas, com a ordem de afirmação (Could you tell me where room four is?) e com if /
  whether nas perguntas de sim ou não (Do you know if there's a printer?).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: Could you tell me / Can you tell me / Do you know + palavra interrogativa + sujeito +
  verbo; if / whether; sem do / does / did na pergunta indirecta
Palavras-alvo: reception, floor, lift, printer, code, main entrance, canteen, cash machine, SIM card,
  mall, across the road
Reciclagem: pedidos educados com could (A2, 9); there is / there are (A1, 7); direcções (A1, 7)
Pronúncia: "Could you" ligado; entoação educada, a subir no fim
Contexto: quarta-feira, 7 de Julho, 8:15; na recepção do centro de formação; o Nuno faz perguntas à
  recepcionista, a Zanele
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 1B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! When you speak to a stranger — at a reception, on the phone, at a conference — direct questions can sound a bit strong. Today: polite, indirect questions.

### 2. Situação (2 min)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 7 de Julho, 8:15; a recepção do centro de formação; a Zanele ao balcão; o Nuno com o portátil debaixo do braço]

**ZANELE:** Good morning! How can I help?
**NUNO:** Good morning. Could you tell me where room four is? Yesterday we were in room two.
**ZANELE:** Of course. It's on the second floor, next to the lifts.
**NUNO:** Thanks. And do you know if there's a printer on that floor?
**ZANELE:** Yes, there is. You need a code. Here — two, two, nine, five.
**NUNO:** Two, two, nine, five. Great. One more thing: do you know what time the bus to the data centre leaves tomorrow?
**ZANELE:** Hmm, I'm not sure. Let me check… It leaves at ten, from the main entrance.
**NUNO:** Ten, main entrance. And could you tell me whether the canteen takes cards? I don't have any rand yet.
**ZANELE:** Yes, it takes cards. And there's a cash machine outside, next to the pharmacy.
**NUNO:** Perfect. Sorry, can I ask one last thing? Do you know where I can buy a SIM card near here?
**ZANELE:** There's a phone shop in the mall across the road.
**NUNO:** Thank you very much. You've been very helpful.
**ZANELE:** You're welcome. Enjoy the training!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Pergunta directa e pergunta indirecta**

[ECRÃ:
Where **is room four**? → Could you tell me where **room four is**?
What time **does the bus leave**? → Do you know what time **the bus leaves**?
Where **can I buy** a SIM card? → Do you know where **I can buy** a SIM card?]

**PROF:** An indirect question starts with "Could you tell me…", "Can you tell me…" or "Do you know…". Then the question word. And then — this is the important part — the normal order of a sentence: subject, then verb. Where is room four? Could you tell me where room four is?
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Sem *do, does, did***

[ECRÃ: ✘ Do you know what time **does** the bus leave? · ✔ Do you know what time the bus **leaves**?]

**PROF:** In the indirect question, there's no "do", "does" or "did". The verb changes like in a sentence: the bus leaves.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Perguntas de sim ou não: *if / whether***

[ECRÃ:
**Is there** a printer? → Do you know **if there is** a printer?
**Does** the canteen **take** cards? → Could you tell me **whether** the canteen **takes** cards?]

**PROF:** For a yes / no question, use "if" or "whether". They mean the same here. "Whether" is a little more formal.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Porquê**

[ECRÃ: Where's the toilet? (amigos, colegas) · Could you tell me where the toilet is? (recepção, telefone, desconhecidos)]

**PROF:** With friends, direct questions are fine. With strangers — a receptionist, a client, someone on the phone — indirect questions are more polite.
[PAUSA 3 s]

**Os erros mais comuns**

[ECRÃ: ✘ Could you tell me where is room four? · ✘ Do you know is there a printer? · ✔ Could you tell me where room four is? · ✔ Do you know if there's a printer?]

**PROF:** Two common mistakes: the question order after "Could you tell me", and forgetting "if". Check both.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "Could you" — join the words: could‿you. Could you tell me where room four is?
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** A polite question goes up a little at the end. Do you know if there's a printer? [voz a subir]
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: Do you know what time the bus leaves? [voz a subir]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make the question polite. Start with the words on the screen.
[ECRÃ: Where's the meeting room? → Could you tell me…] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Could you tell me where the meeting room is?]
[ECRÃ: When does the shop open? → Do you know…] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Do you know when the shop opens?]
[ECRÃ: Is the lift working? → Do you know…] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Do you know if the lift is working?]
[ECRÃ: How much did the tickets cost? → Could you tell me…] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Could you tell me how much the tickets cost?]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. You arrive at a hotel in a new city. Ask the receptionist four polite questions: about breakfast, the Wi-Fi, a place to eat, and transport to the airport.
[PAUSA 35 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Could you tell me what time breakfast starts? Do you know if the Wi-Fi is free? Do you know where I can get a good dinner? And could you tell me how I get to the airport?]

**PROF (opinião):** In your culture, is it rude to ask a stranger a direct question? Give an example.
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — A2, 9):** Make a polite request to a colleague. Ask him or her to send you a document.
[PAUSA 8 s]
[ECRÃ: Could you send me the report, please? / Would you mind sending me the report?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! Remember: after "Could you tell me" or "Do you know", use the order of a normal sentence. Next lesson: questions to keep a conversation going.

## 1C — Who invited you?

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1C — Who invited you?        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue distinguir e fazer perguntas de sujeito e de objecto (Who
  invited you? Who did you invite?) e manter uma conversa com perguntas de seguimento (What do you
  mean? How did that happen? What was it like?).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: perguntas de sujeito (Who / What + verbo, sem do / did) e de objecto (Who / What +
  do / did + sujeito + verbo); perguntas de seguimento
Palavras-alvo: seat, solar plant, control centre, by accident, offer (a job), go back, exactly
Reciclagem: perguntas de sujeito (A2, 2B); past simple (A2, 2)
Pronúncia: acento na palavra interrogativa e no verbo principal; "What do you mean?" ligado
Contexto: terça-feira, 6 de Julho, ao almoço do primeiro dia; na cantina do centro de formação; o Nuno
  conhece o Kofi
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 1C; pergunta 5 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! A good conversation is like a game of tennis: a question, an answer, and another question about the answer. Today: who asks, who answers — and how to keep the ball in the game.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 6 de Julho, 12:45; a cantina do centro de formação; o Nuno sozinho numa mesa; o Kofi com um tabuleiro]

**KOFI:** Excuse me, is this seat free?
**NUNO:** Yes, of course. Sit down. I'm Nuno, from Luanda.
**KOFI:** Kofi, from Accra. Nice to meet you. So, Nuno, who invited you to this training?
**NUNO:** Lerato did. I'm in her new joint team. And you? Who did you come with?
**KOFI:** Nobody. I came alone. My company works with Mopane Systems on solar projects in Ghana.
**NUNO:** Really? What do you do there, exactly?
**KOFI:** I'm a network engineer. I connect our solar plants to the control centre. Honestly, the control centre is my second home.
**NUNO:** What do you mean?
**KOFI:** I'm there twelve hours a day! Ha!
**NUNO:** Ha! How did you start in solar energy?
**KOFI:** By accident. I worked for a bank for five years. Then one day a friend offered me a job at a solar company.
**NUNO:** And what happened next?
**KOFI:** I said yes, I left the bank, and I never went back. Best decision of my life.
**NUNO:** What was it like at the beginning?
**KOFI:** Hard! I knew networks, but I didn't know anything about solar panels. What about you? How did you start?
**NUNO:** Oh, my story is shorter. I did an internship at Kalandula Tech, and they offered me a job.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Pergunta de sujeito e pergunta de objecto**

[ECRÃ:
**Lerato** invited Nuno. → **Who invited** Nuno? — Lerato. (sujeito)
Nuno met **Kofi**. → **Who did** Nuno **meet**? — Kofi. (objecto)]

**PROF:** Look at the answer. If the answer is the subject of the sentence, the question has no "did": Who invited you? Lerato did. If the answer is the object, use "did", like a normal question: Who did you meet? I met Kofi.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **What happened** next? (sujeito, sem did) · **What did** you **do** next? (objecto)]

**PROF:** The same with "what". What happened? — no "did". What did you do? — with "did".
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ Who did invite you? · ✘ What did happen? · ✘ Who you met? · ✔ Who invited you? · ✔ What happened? · ✔ Who did you meet?]
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Perguntas de seguimento**

[ECRÃ:
para perceber melhor: **What do you mean? What exactly do you do?**
para saber mais: **How did that happen? What happened next? Why?**
para saber como foi: **What was it like?**
para mostrar interesse: **Really? That's interesting!**]

**PROF:** In a good conversation, you don't change topic after every answer. You ask about the answer. These are follow-up questions.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: Kofi: I worked for a bank. ✘ Nuno: OK. Do you like Johannesburg? · ✔ Nuno: Really? How did you start in solar energy?]

**PROF:** Nuno doesn't jump to a new topic. He asks about Kofi's story, and the conversation grows.
[PAUSA 3 s]

**Respostas mais longas**

[ECRÃ: Who did you come with? ✘ Nobody. · ✔ Nobody. I came alone. My company works with Mopane Systems on solar projects.]

**PROF:** And when you answer, give a little extra information. That gives the other person something to ask about.
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

[ECRÃ: **WHO** in**VIT**ed you? · **WHO** did you **MEET**? · what d'you **MEAN**? · how did that **HAPP**en? · what was it **LIKE**?]

**PROF:** In questions, stress the question word and the main verb. Who invited you?
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Who did you meet?
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** In "What do you mean?", the small words are fast and short. What do you mean?
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: How did that happen? What was it like?
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make a question about the underlined words.
[ECRÃ: __Thandi__ called me last night.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Who called you last night?]
[ECRÃ: I called __Thandi__ last night.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Who did you call last night?]
[ECRÃ: __The printer__ broke this morning.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: What broke this morning?]
[ECRÃ: I bought __a SIM card__.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: What did you buy?]

**PROF:** Now, listen and ask a follow-up question.
**PROF:** I changed jobs last year.
[PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Really? Why? / What happened? / What's your new job like?]
**PROF:** The meeting yesterday was a disaster.
[PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: What do you mean? / What happened?]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Tell me how you started in your job or your studies — and give extra information. Talk for about forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I started at the bank in 2019. Actually, I studied engineering, but I couldn't find a job. Then a friend told me about a job in the IT department…]

**PROF (opinião):** What's the best question to start a conversation with a stranger at a work event? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — A2, 2):** What were you doing at this time yesterday? And what happened after that?
[PAUSA 15 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Great work! Subject or object question, and always a follow-up question. Next lesson: the words and phrases for small talk and networking.

## 1D — Small talk and networking

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1D — Small talk and networking        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue abrir, manter e fechar uma conversa de circunstância num
  evento de trabalho com as expressões certas, concordar com So do I / Neither do I, usar keep in touch,
  get in touch e follow up, e evitar os falsos amigos compromise e comprehensive.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 4
Gramática-alvo: So do I / Neither do I / So did I / Neither did I / So am I / Neither am I
Palavras-alvo: conference, workshop, speaker, delegate, venue, business card, networking, small talk;
  What brings you here? What line of work are you in? How do you know…?; It was great talking to you;
  keep in touch, get in touch, follow up, exchange contacts
Reciclagem: question tags (1A); perguntas indirectas (1B)
Pronúncia: acento em So do I / Neither do I (no I); a entoação das tags, revista
Falsos amigos: compromise (≠ compromisso → appointment, commitment; compromise = cedência, acordo em que
  os dois lados cedem); comprehensive (≠ compreensivo → understanding; comprehensive = completo)
Contexto: a pausa do café de quinta-feira, 8 de Julho; ecrãs com expressões
Duração: 10 min
Verificação: Lab 1D; perguntas 6, 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Small talk is the short, friendly conversation before the "real" conversation. At work events, it opens doors. Today: the words to open, keep and close a conversation.

### 2. As palavras do evento (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: uma sala de conferências com crachás e um programa]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| conference | a big event with talks, for one or more days |
| workshop | a practical session — you work in small groups |
| speaker | he or she gives a talk |
| delegate | a person at a conference |
| venue | the place of the event: a hotel, a centre |
| business card | your name, job, company and contacts on a small card |
| networking | meeting people for your work |

**PROF:** Repeat: conference. Workshop. Speaker. Delegate. Venue. Business card. Networking.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 3. Abrir, manter e fechar (3 min)

[ECRÃ: três colunas]

| Abrir | Manter | Fechar |
|---|---|---|
| Is this seat free? | What line of work are you in? | Well, it was great talking to you. |
| Sorry to interrupt — are you Kofi? | How do you know Lerato? | Let's keep in touch. Here's my card. |
| Hi, I don't think we've met. I'm… | What brings you here? | I'll follow up by email next week. |
| It's a great venue, isn't it? | What did you think of the talk? | Enjoy the rest of the conference! |

[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** To close: say something nice, and talk about the next contact. "Let's keep in touch. I'll follow up by email."

[PAUSA 4 s]

**PROF:** To say "me too" in a conversation, use "so" with the same auxiliary: So do I, so did I, so am I. For a negative sentence: neither do I, neither did I.

**PROF:** In these answers, the stress is on "I". So do I. Neither do I. So did I.
