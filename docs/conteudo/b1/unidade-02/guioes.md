# B1.1 · Unidade 2 — Then and now · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B1](../unidade-01/guioes.md): tudo em inglês, e o português só nos falsos amigos e na mediação.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2 e da unidade 1 do B1. Novo: *used to / didn't use to / Did you use to…?* para hábitos e estados do passado; o *present perfect continuous* (*I've been working… How long have you been…?*) e a escolha entre ele e o *present perfect simple*; adjectivos em *-ed / -ing* (vistos no A2, aqui alargados). *Get used to* só aparece para reconhecer, nunca para produzir.

**Não usar nesta unidade:** *be / get used to* na fala dos modelos (só uma vez, para reconhecer, na 2D); *too / enough*; *so / such* + adjectivo ou quantidade (também "so many"); o 2.º condicional e *would* hipotético; *might / may* de probabilidade; a voz passiva; orações relativas, com ou sem pronome; o *past perfect*; o discurso indirecto no passado; o *future continuous*; *must / can't* de dedução; *would* para hábitos do passado (fica para o B2).

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mopane Systems, a Morro Azul Energia e o café Kiandas são fictícios.

**Personagens:** **Nuno** (nasceu em Lubango em 2003, três irmãs, o pai tem uma pequena loja; licenciou-se em Julho de 2026 e entrou na Kalandula Tech em Outubro de 2026; A2, unidade 2); **Sílvia** (directora financeira desde Fevereiro, A2 7; dançava kizomba, A1); **Thandi** (viveu em Luanda até Fevereiro de 2027, A2 7; volta esta semana para o projecto da Morro Azul); **Lerato** (coordenadora da equipa conjunta, em Luanda); **Paulo** (apoio técnico da Kalandula Tech); **Sr. Baptista** (director da Kalandula Tech; só por escrito, na 2F). Na 2E: **Esperança**, **Kevin** e **Patrick** (A1 e A2 10E, um ano depois das entrevistas) e **Mei** (B1, 1E).

**A história:** Outubro de 2027. Desde Agosto, a equipa conjunta trabalha para a Morro Azul Energia: a rede da central solar do Namibe cai com as tempestades de areia (B1, 1F). A Thandi chega a Luanda na sexta-feira, 1 de Outubro, para uma reunião do projecto. Na sexta-feira, 8, o Nuno faz um ano na empresa e tem a avaliação anual com a Lerato.

## 2A — I used to be the quiet one

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2A — I used to be the quiet one        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue contrastar hábitos e estados do passado com o presente, com
  used to / didn't use to / Did you use to…? e usually (I used to be nervous on the phone, but now I
  call Johannesburg every day.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: used to + verbo (hábitos e estados que já não são verdade); didn't use to; Did you use
  to…?; past simple para uma vez e para durações (I lived there for twenty years); usually para o presente
Palavras-alvo: quiet, nervous, shopping centre, corner, order (a coffee), free time, still, any more
Reciclagem: question tags (B1, 1A): You used to live here, didn't you?; past simple (A2, 2)
Pronúncia: used to: sem som de "d", e o "s" como em "bus"
Contexto: sexta-feira, 1 de Outubro, 18:00; na esplanada do café Kiandas; a Thandi, de volta a Luanda,
  com o Nuno e a Sílvia
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 2A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 2
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This unit is about change: how your life and your work were before, and how they are now. Today: how to talk about past habits — actions again and again in the past, but not now.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 1 de Outubro, 18:00; a esplanada do café Kiandas; a Thandi chega com uma mala pequena; o Nuno e a Sílvia levantam-se]

**SÍLVIA:** Thandi! Welcome back to Luanda!
**THANDI:** Thank you! Oh, it's good to be here. I used to come to this café every Friday, didn't I?
**SÍLVIA:** You did. And you always used to order the same thing: a coffee and a pastel de nata.
**THANDI:** Ha! Some things don't change. One coffee and one pastel, please. But the city has changed. There didn't use to be a shopping centre on that corner.
**NUNO:** It opened in May.
**THANDI:** And you, Nuno — you look different. You didn't use to wear a jacket to work!
**NUNO:** New job, new jacket.
**SÍLVIA:** It's true, Thandi. Nuno used to be the quiet one in the IT team. Now he talks to clients in three countries.
**NUNO:** Well, I used to be really nervous on the phone. Now I call Johannesburg every day.
**THANDI:** Did you use to speak English at university?
**NUNO:** No, never. I used to read in English — computer manuals — but I didn't use to speak it. Now I usually think in English at work!
**THANDI:** Amazing. And you, Sílvia? Do you still dance kizomba on Saturdays?
**SÍLVIA:** Not any more. I used to dance every Saturday night. Now I'm the finance manager, and I usually work on Saturday mornings!
**THANDI:** Ha! We all used to have more free time.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

***Used to***

[ECRÃ: I **used to come** to this café every Friday. (antes, muitas vezes; agora não) · Nuno **used to be** the quiet one. (antes era; agora não é)]

**PROF:** "Used to" plus a verb is for the past: habits — actions again and again — and states — how things were. And the important idea: it isn't true now.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: I **used to** read in English. · I **didn't use to** speak it. · **Did** you **use to** speak English?]

**PROF:** In the negative and in questions, with "did", write "use to" — without "d". I didn't use to speak it. Did you use to speak English? But the sound is the same.
[PAUSA 4 s]

***Used to* ou *past simple*?**

[ECRÃ:
✔ I **used to go** to Benguela every summer. (muitas vezes)
✔ I **went** to Benguela in 2019. (uma vez) · ✘ I used to go to Benguela in 2019.
✔ I **lived** in Lubango for twenty years. (quanto tempo) · ✘ I used to live in Lubango for twenty years.]

**PROF:** For one time, or for "how long", use the past simple, not "used to". "I lived in Lubango for twenty years."
[PAUSA 5 s]

**O presente: *usually*, não *use to***

[ECRÃ: "Costumo beber café de manhã." → ✘ I use to drink coffee in the morning. · ✔ I **usually** drink coffee in the morning.]

**PROF:** Careful! For the present, "used to" doesn't work. A habit now is "usually", with the present simple. "Used to" is only for the past.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Antes e agora**

[ECRÃ: I **used to be** nervous on the phone, **but now** I call Johannesburg every day. · I **don't** dance **any more**. / Do you **still** dance?]

**PROF:** To compare: "used to…, but now…". And two useful words: "still" — it continues; and "not… any more" — it stopped.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Listen: used to. There's no "d" sound, and the "s" is like in "bus". Used to.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I used to be really nervous on the phone.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** I didn't use to speak English.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Used to, or past simple?
[ECRÃ: I … (play) football every weekend when I was a child.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I used to play football every weekend.]
[ECRÃ: I … (start) this job in March.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I started this job in March.]
[ECRÃ: I … (usually / have) breakfast at home now.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I usually have breakfast at home now.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think about your life ten years ago. Tell me about three habits from that time, with "used to", and one with "didn't use to". Then say what's different now.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Ten years ago, I used to live with my parents in Huambo. I used to walk to school, and I used to play basketball every afternoon. I didn't use to drink coffee! Now I live in Luanda, I drive to work, and I usually drink three coffees a day.]

**PROF (opinião):** Do you think life was easier ten years ago? Why, or why not?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 1A):** Check this with a tag: you think Thandi used to live in Luanda.
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: You used to live in Luanda, didn't you?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! "Used to" for the past, "usually" for now. Next lesson: activities from the past until now.

## 2B — I've been working on it since August

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2B — I've been working on it since August        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar de actividades que começaram no passado e continuam
  agora com o present perfect continuous, perguntar há quanto tempo (How long have you been…?) e
  explicar um resultado que se vê (You look tired. Have you been working late?).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: have / has been + -ing; How long have you been…?; for / since; verbos de estado sem
  forma contínua (know, like, have = possuir, understand, believe)
Palavras-alvo: logs, signal, wind, radio link, technical manager, data, recently, lately, all morning
Reciclagem: present perfect com for / since (A2, 6); present continuous (A1, 9)
Pronúncia: forma fraca de "been" em "I've been"
Contexto: segunda-feira, 4 de Outubro, 9:00; no escritório da equipa conjunta, em Luanda; a Lerato e o
  Nuno antes da reunião do projecto da Morro Azul
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 2B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Some activities start in the past and continue now. Today: how to talk about them — and how to ask "how long".

### 2. Situação (2 min)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 4 de Outubro, 9:00; o escritório da equipa conjunta, em Luanda; o Nuno ao computador, com dois ecrãs cheios de números; a Lerato chega com um café]

**LERATO:** Morning, Nuno. You look tired. Have you been working late again?
**NUNO:** A bit. And I've been reading the logs from the Namibe plant since seven o'clock.
**LERATO:** Since seven! How long have you been working on that problem now?
**NUNO:** Since August — almost two months. Hannah's team has been sending us data every day.
**LERATO:** And? What have you found?
**NUNO:** I think it's the radio link. When the wind is strong, the signal goes down. I've been talking to their technical manager about it since last week.
**LERATO:** Good work. Thandi's coming at ten for the project meeting. Can you show her the data?
**NUNO:** Of course.
**LERATO:** By the way, how's your English course going? Are you still going on Saturdays?
**NUNO:** Yes. I've been studying for the B1 exam.
**LERATO:** How long have you been doing the Saturday course?
**NUNO:** Since March — so, about seven months.
**LERATO:** It shows. I've known you since January, and your English is very different now.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**A forma**

[ECRÃ: I**'ve been reading** the logs since seven. · Hannah's team **has been sending** data every day. · **How long have** you **been working** on it?]

**PROF:** The present perfect continuous: have or has, plus "been", plus a verb with "-ing".
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Para que serve (1): começou e continua**

[ECRÃ: linha do tempo: Agosto ───────────► agora → I**'ve been working** on it **since August**. / **for** two months.]

**PROF:** An activity started in the past and it's still happening now. With "for" — a period — or "since" — a starting point.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: "Trabalho nisto há dois meses." → ✘ I work on this for two months. · ✘ I'm working on this for two months. · ✔ I**'ve been working** on this for two months.]

**PROF:** In Portuguese, we use the present. In English, no: for an activity from the past until now, use "have been" plus "-ing".
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Para que serve (2): um resultado que se vê**

[ECRÃ: You look tired. **Have** you **been working** late? · Your hands are dirty! What **have** you **been doing**? — I**'ve been fixing** the generator.]

**PROF:** We also use it when we see the result of a recent activity: you look tired, so you've been working late.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Verbos de estado**

[ECRÃ: ✘ I've been knowing you since February. · ✔ I**'ve known** you since February. · (também: like, have = possuir, understand, believe)]

**PROF:** Some verbs are not activities: know, like, understand, believe, and have when it means "possess". With these, use the normal present perfect: I've known you since February.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In "I've been", the word "been" is short and weak — like "bin". I've been working.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: How long have you been working on it?
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** I've been reading the logs since seven o'clock.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make a sentence with "for" or "since".
[ECRÃ: I started studying English in March. I'm still studying it.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I've been studying English since March.]
[ECRÃ: Rosa started waiting two hours ago. She's still waiting.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Rosa has been waiting for two hours.]
[ECRÃ: I met Thandi in 2026. (know)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I've known Thandi since 2026.]

**PROF:** Answer about you. How long have you been doing your job?
[PAUSA 8 s]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. What have you been doing at work — or in your studies — recently? Talk about two activities, and say how long you've been doing them.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Recently I've been preparing the budget for next year. I've been working on it for three weeks. I've also been training two new colleagues since September. It's tiring, but I'm learning a lot.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it better to work on one big project for a long time, or on many small projects? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 1B):** Ask a new colleague politely how long he or she has been working here.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Could you tell me how long you've been working here?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "I've been working on it since August." Next lesson: when to say "I've been doing" and when to say "I've done".

## 2C — Been doing or done?

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2C — Been doing or done?        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue escolher entre o present perfect continuous (a actividade,
  quanto tempo) e o present perfect simple (o resultado, quantos) ao falar do seu ano de trabalho
  (I've been writing my review all morning. I've written two pages.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 4
Gramática-alvo: present perfect continuous (actividade, duração, ainda não acabou) e present perfect
  simple (resultado, quantos / quanto, acabado); live / work com as duas formas
Palavras-alvo: review, so far, list, weekly report, visit (a plant), exam, practise, slow
Reciclagem: present perfect simple (A2, 6 e 7); used to (2A)
Pronúncia: acento de frase: o número e o resultado (I've written TWO pages)
Contexto: quarta-feira, 6 de Outubro, 15:00; no escritório; o Nuno prepara a avaliação anual de sexta, e
  o Paulo passa por lá
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 2C; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! "I've been writing" or "I've written"? Both are correct — but they say different things. Today: the activity, or the result.

### 2. Situação (2 min)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 6 de Outubro, 15:00; o Nuno ao computador; no ecrã, um documento: "Annual review — Nuno Kapinga"; o Paulo à porta]

**PAULO:** Hey, Nuno. You've been typing for an hour. What are you doing?
**NUNO:** I'm preparing for my annual review with Lerato. It's on Friday.
**PAULO:** One year already! How much have you written?
**NUNO:** I've written two pages so far. I've been working on it since lunch.
**PAULO:** What's in it?
**NUNO:** My projects. I've worked on two big projects this year: Mussulo Energy and now Morro Azul. And I've joined a new team.
**PAULO:** And how long have you been working on Morro Azul?
**NUNO:** Since August. I've visited the plant in Namibe twice, and I've been writing a weekly report for Hannah.
**PAULO:** And your English?
**NUNO:** I've been going to the Saturday course since March. I've done two practice exams, and I've passed both.
**PAULO:** Not bad! What about your weak points?
**NUNO:** Reports. I've been practising, but my writing is still slow.
**PAULO:** Ha! I used to say that about you every week.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**A actividade ou o resultado**

[ECRÃ:
I**'ve been writing** my review all afternoon. → a actividade; quanto tempo; talvez não acabou
I**'ve written** two pages. → o resultado; quantos; acabado]

**PROF:** "I've been writing" — the activity, how long, and maybe it's not finished. "I've written two pages" — the result: how many, and it's done.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Com números: o *simple***

[ECRÃ: ✘ I've been visiting the plant twice. · ✔ I**'ve visited** the plant **twice**. · ✘ I've been writing three reports this week. · ✔ I**'ve written three reports** this week.]

**PROF:** With a number — twice, three reports, four projects — use the present perfect simple. You're counting results.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Com *how long*: o *continuous***

[ECRÃ: **How long** have you **been working** on Morro Azul? · **How many** reports have you **written**?]

**PROF:** "How long" goes well with the continuous. "How many" and "how much" go with the simple.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Os dois são possíveis**

[ECRÃ: I**'ve worked** here since 2026. = I**'ve been working** here since 2026. · I**'ve lived** in Luanda for a year. = I**'ve been living** in Luanda for a year.]

**PROF:** With "work" and "live", and "for" or "since", both forms are fine, and the meaning is almost the same.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** When you give a result, stress the number. I've written two pages.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** I've visited the plant twice.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** And with the activity, stress how long. I've been working on it all afternoon.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Activity or result? Choose.
[ECRÃ: I've (been calling / called) the client three times today.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I've called the client three times today.]
[ECRÃ: She's (been waiting / waited) for an hour.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: She's been waiting for an hour.]
[ECRÃ: How many emails have you (been sending / sent)?] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: How many emails have you sent?]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Talk about your year. Tell me one thing you've been doing for a long time, and two things you've finished or done. Use numbers.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — This year I've been working on a new accounting system. I've been doing it since February, and it isn't finished yet. But I've trained twelve colleagues, and I've written three manuals.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is an annual review with your boss useful? Why, or why not?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — 2A):** Compare your work before and now: one sentence with "used to", and one with "usually".
[PAUSA 12 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Great! The activity — "I've been doing". The result, with a number — "I've done". Next lesson: words for changes and feelings.

## 2D — Changes and feelings

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2D — Changes and feelings        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar de uma mudança e do que sentiu, com verbos de mudança
  e adjectivos em -ed / -ing (I was surprised. It was surprising.), e evitar os falsos amigos retire e
  deception.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: adjectivos em -ed (como a pessoa se sente) e em -ing (o que causa o sentimento)
Palavras-alvo: move, change, improve, become, adapt, grow, give up, take up; surprised / surprising,
  confused / confusing, embarrassed / embarrassing, annoyed / annoying, worried / worrying, amazed /
  amazing, frustrated / frustrating, interested / interesting; get used to (só para reconhecer)
Reciclagem: used to (2A); present perfect continuous (2B)
Pronúncia: formas fracas de "have" e "been" (I've been, She's been, How long have you been)
Falsos amigos: retire (≠ retirar → take out, withdraw; retire = reformar-se); deception (≠ decepção →
  disappointment; deception = engano)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 10 min
Verificação: Lab 2D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! When we talk about changes, we also talk about feelings. Today: the verbs of change, and the adjectives for feelings — "surprised" and "surprising", "confused" and "confusing".

### 2. Verbos de mudança (2 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Verbo | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| move | I moved to Luanda in 2026. |
| change | The city has changed a lot. |
| improve | My English has improved. |
| become | Sílvia became the finance manager in February. |
| adapt | It took me a month to adapt to the new job. |
| grow | Our team has grown from four people to ten. |
| give up | I gave up sugar last year. (deixar) |
| take up | I took up running in March. (começar uma actividade) |

**PROF:** Repeat: move. Change. Improve. Become. Adapt. Grow. Give up. Take up.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: Kevin (2E): "The first month was frustrating… But I'm **getting used to** it." (para reconhecer: habituar-se)]

**PROF:** You'll also hear "get used to" — it means you start to feel normal with something new. Just recognise it for now.
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 3. Sentimentos: *-ed* ou *-ing* (3 min)

[ECRÃ: I was **surprised**. (eu senti) · The news was **surprising**. (a notícia causou)]

**PROF:** You saw this in A2. With "-ed", it's how a person feels. With "-ing", it's the cause of the feeling.

[PAUSA 4 s]

| -ed (a pessoa) | -ing (a causa) |
|---|---|
| I was confused. | The instructions were confusing. |
| I felt embarrassed. | It was an embarrassing mistake. |
| She was annoyed. | The noise was annoying. |
| We were worried. | The results were worrying. |
| I was amazed. | The view was amazing. |
| He was frustrated. | The first month was frustrating. |

[ECRÃ: ✘ I'm boring in meetings. (= eu sou aborrecido!) · ✔ I'm bored in meetings.]

**PROF:** Careful: "I'm boring" means other people find you boring! You mean "I'm bored".
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: formas fracas (1 min)

**PROF:** In fast speech, "have" and "been" are weak. Listen: How long have you been here?
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I've been waiting. She's been working. How long have you been waiting?
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **retire** ≠ retirar]

**PROF:** "Retire" means to stop working because of your age.
[ECRÃ: My father retired last year. (O meu pai reformou-se no ano passado.)]
(PT: "Retirar" diz-se "take out" ou "withdraw": "Retirei dinheiro do multicaixa" → "I took some money out of the cash machine" ou "I withdrew some money".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: **deception** ≠ decepção]

**PROF:** "Deception" means making someone believe something false — a trick.
(PT: "Decepção" diz-se "disappointment": "Foi uma decepção" → "It was a disappointment". E "decepcionado" é "disappointed".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (2 min)

**PROF:** -ed or -ing?
[ECRÃ: The meeting was very (bored / boring).] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: boring]
[ECRÃ: I was (surprised / surprising) by the results.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: surprised]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Describe a big change in your life or your work. What changed? How did you feel at the start, and how do you feel now?
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Two years ago, I moved from Benguela to Luanda for a new job. At the start, I was worried, and the traffic was really frustrating. But I've adapted. Now I'm happy here, and my job is interesting.]

**PROF (opinião):** Are changes at work usually exciting or worrying for you? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — 2B):** How long have you been learning English? Answer with "for" or "since".
[PAUSA 6 s]

**PROF:** Well done! Next lesson: four people tell us how their lives have changed.

## 2E — Real English: How my life has changed

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2E — How my life has changed        Tipo: Real English
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue identificar, em quatro testemunhos à velocidade natural, a
  mudança, a razão e o sentimento de cada pessoa.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 3
```
