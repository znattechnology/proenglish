# B1.1 · Unidade 3 — In the meeting · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B1](../unidade-01/guioes.md): tudo em inglês, e o português só nos falsos amigos e na mediação.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2 e das unidades 1 e 2 do B1. Novo: linguagem de opinião (*I think / I don't think… In my view… Personally… As far as I'm concerned…*) e de pedir opiniões; concordar e discordar, total e parcialmente (*I see your point, but… I'm not sure I agree.*); interromper, retomar e verificar (*Sorry to interrupt, but… Going back to… So, what you're saying is…?*); *too / too much / too many*; *(not) … enough* e *enough* + nome; intensificadores (*a bit, quite, very, really, extremely*); *so* + adjectivo, *such (a)* + adjectivo + nome, *so many / so much*.

**Não usar nesta unidade:** o 2.º condicional e *would* hipotético (nem "That would be great", nem "I'd say", nem "it would cost"); *might / may* de probabilidade e *will probably*; *If I were you*; a voz passiva; orações relativas, com ou sem pronome ("the engineers we have"); o *past perfect*; o discurso indirecto no passado (*She said that…*); o *future continuous*; *must / can't* de dedução; *be / get used to*; *would* para hábitos do passado. *So, what you're saying is…?* entra como expressão fixa.

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mopane Systems e a Morro Azul Energia são fictícias. O 11 de Novembro (Dia da Independência) é feriado nacional em Angola.

**Personagens:** **Nuno** (equipa conjunta; lê os registos da rede da central do Namibe desde Agosto, B1 2B); **Lerato** (coordenadora da equipa conjunta, em Luanda; preside às reuniões); **Thandi** (Mopane Systems; está em Luanda desde 1 de Outubro e fica até ao fim do mês); **Paulo** (apoio técnico da Kalandula Tech); **Sílvia** (directora financeira); **Sipho** (Mopane Systems, Joanesburgo; B1 1A); **Hannah Becker** (engenheira de projecto da Morro Azul Energia; B1 1F). Novos: **Femi Adeyemi** (nigeriano, director financeiro da Morro Azul Energia, em Luanda) e o **Sr. Domingos** (chefe de manutenção da central do Namibe; só fala português).

**A história:** Outubro de 2027. A rede da central solar do Namibe cai com o vento forte e as tempestades de areia, porque depende de uma ligação de rádio (B1 1F e 2B). A equipa estuda três opções: **A** — antenas novas em mastros mais altos; **B** — um cabo de fibra óptica de catorze quilómetros; **C** — uma ligação por satélite, de reserva. Na segunda, 18, a equipa dá opiniões (3A); na terça, 19, a Sílvia discute os custos (3B); na quarta, 20, o Nuno e a Thandi falam por videochamada com o Sipho sobre as antenas (3C); na quinta, 21, o Nuno e o Paulo preparam os diapositivos (3D); na sexta, 22, a Morro Azul decide (3F); na quarta, 27, a reunião do calendário da instalação (3G).

## 3A — In my view…

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3A — In my view…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dar uma opinião com uma razão e um exemplo (In my view,
  option C is important, because… For example,…) e pedir a opinião dos outros (What do you think?
  What's your view? How do you feel about…?).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: I think / I don't think + afirmativa (não "I think it isn't"); In my opinion / In my
  view / Personally / As far as I'm concerned; because / For example / For instance; perguntas de opinião
Palavras-alvo: option, item, antenna, mast, fibre cable, satellite link, backup, offline, the best,
  cheap / the cheapest, long-term, depend on, opinion, view
Reciclagem: present perfect continuous (B1, 2B): You've been reading the logs for weeks; comparativos e
  superlativos (A2)
Pronúncia: a entoação a descer no fim de uma opinião; "think" com acento, "don't" bem claro
Contexto: segunda-feira, 18 de Outubro, 9:00; a sala de reuniões da equipa conjunta, em Luanda; a Lerato,
  a Thandi, o Paulo e o Nuno; no quadro, três opções
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 3A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 3
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This unit is about meetings: how to give your opinion, agree and disagree, interrupt politely, and reach a decision. Today: how to give an opinion — with a reason and an example — and how to ask for other people's opinions.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 18 de Outubro, 9:00; uma sala de reuniões pequena; no quadro: "Namibe plant — network. A: new antennas, higher masts. B: fibre cable, 14 km. C: satellite link (backup)."]

**LERATO:** OK, everyone, let's start. We have one item today: the network at the Namibe plant. We meet Hannah on Friday, and I want your opinions first. Paulo, what do you think?
**PAULO:** In my view, option A is the best: new antennas on higher masts. It's the cheapest option, and we can do it in two or three weeks.
**LERATO:** Thandi? How do you feel about it?
**THANDI:** Personally, I prefer the fibre cable. Radio links and sandstorms are a bad mix. For example, we had the same problem at a mine in Limpopo. The new antennas only helped for a year, and then they put in fibre.
**LERATO:** OK. Nuno, you've been reading the logs for weeks. What's your view?
**NUNO:** As far as I'm concerned, the big problem is this: when the radio link goes down, the plant has no network at all. So I think we need a backup. In my opinion, option C — the satellite link — is important, together with the new antennas.
**LERATO:** Why?
**NUNO:** Because the satellite doesn't depend on the masts or the wind. For example, on Sunday the twelfth of September, there was a sandstorm for six hours, and the plant was offline all afternoon. Hannah's team couldn't see the cameras or the sensors.
**LERATO:** Good point. Thandi, what's your opinion on the satellite?
**THANDI:** I think it's a good idea for now. But I don't think it's the long-term answer. In my view, the long-term answer is fibre.
**LERATO:** Thank you, all. So, three opinions. Let's prepare the three options for Friday.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Dar uma opinião**

[ECRÃ:
I think option C is important.
In my opinion, … · In my view, … · Personally, … · As far as I'm concerned, …]

**PROF:** To give an opinion, start with one of these. "I think" is the most common. "In my view" and "As far as I'm concerned" are a bit more formal — good for meetings. "Personally" shows it's your idea, not a fact.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ In my opinion, I think the cable is better. · ✔ In my opinion, the cable is better. · ✔ I think the cable is better.]

**PROF:** Choose one: "In my opinion" or "I think" — not both.
[PAUSA 3 s]

***I don't think…***

[ECRÃ: "Acho que não é a melhor solução." → ✘ I think it isn't the best answer. · ✔ I **don't think** it's the best answer.]

**PROF:** In English, the negative goes with "think": "I don't think it's the long-term answer." It sounds softer and more natural.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Razão e exemplo**

[ECRÃ: opinião → **because** … (a razão) → **For example,** … / **For instance,** … (um exemplo)
In my opinion, option C is important, **because** the satellite doesn't depend on the wind. **For example,** on the twelfth of September, the plant was offline all afternoon.]

**PROF:** A strong opinion has three parts: the opinion, the reason with "because", and an example. Nuno's opinion is strong because he has an example from the logs.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Pedir opiniões**

[ECRÃ: What do you think? · What's your view? · What's your opinion on the satellite? · How do you feel about it? · Thandi, any thoughts?]

**PROF:** In a meeting, ask other people too. Use their name: "Thandi, what's your view?" Notice: opinion "on" or "about" something.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** An opinion ends with the voice going down — it sounds sure. Listen.
**PROF:** In my view, option A is the best. [voz a descer no fim]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I think we need a backup.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** I don't think it's the long-term answer. Say "don't" clearly.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Say it with "I don't think".
[ECRÃ: Acho que o cabo não é barato.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I don't think the cable is cheap.]
[ECRÃ: Acho que eles não vão terminar em Novembro.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I don't think they're going to finish in November.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Should companies let their staff work from home two days a week? Give your opinion, a reason, and an example. About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — In my view, two days at home is a good idea, because people lose a lot of time in traffic. For example, I spend two hours in the car every day. At home, I can use that time for work. But I don't think five days at home is good: we need to see our colleagues.]

**PROF (opinião):** And meetings at eight in the morning: good idea or bad idea? What's your view?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 1B):** Ask a colleague for their opinion politely, with "Could you tell me…?"
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Could you tell me what you think about the new schedule?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! An opinion, a reason, an example — and then ask the others. Next lesson: how to agree and disagree without being rude.

## 3B — I see your point, but…

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3B — I see your point, but…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue concordar e discordar com cortesia, total ou parcialmente
  (I completely agree. I see your point, but… I'm not sure I agree.), dando uma razão.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: I agree / I don't agree (não "I am agree"); Do you agree? (não "Are you agree?");
  agree with someone / about something; So do I / Neither do I (revistos)
Palavras-alvo: proposal, price, cost, a month, essential, necessary, choose, decide, client, fair,
  a lot of money, point
Reciclagem: So do I / Neither do I (B1, 1C); present perfect continuous (B1, 2B)
Pronúncia: a entoação do "but…": a voz sobe em "point" e faz uma pausa curta antes de "but"
Contexto: terça-feira, 19 de Outubro, 14:00; o gabinete da Sílvia; a Sílvia revê os preços da proposta
  para a Morro Azul com a Lerato e o Nuno
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 3B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In a meeting, people don't always agree. Today: how to agree, how to agree only in part, and how to disagree politely.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 19 de Outubro, 14:00; o gabinete da Sílvia; na mesa, a proposta para a Morro Azul, com três preços]

**SÍLVIA:** Lerato, I've been looking at the proposal for Morro Azul. The satellite backup costs nine hundred dollars a month. That's a lot of money.
**LERATO:** I agree. It isn't cheap. But Nuno has a good reason. Nuno?
**NUNO:** The network goes down two or three times a week. Every time, Hannah's team can't see the panels. For them, a backup is essential.
**SÍLVIA:** I see your point, but they're paying for new antennas and masts too. Is the satellite really necessary?
**NUNO:** I'm not sure I agree. The antennas help, but they don't stop the sandstorms.
**SÍLVIA:** That's true. But nine hundred dollars every month, Nuno…
**LERATO:** You have a point, Sílvia. So let's give Morro Azul three options with three prices, and the client decides.
**SÍLVIA:** I completely agree. That's fair.
**NUNO:** Absolutely. And I don't think Hannah wants another month without a network.
**SÍLVIA:** Neither do I. OK, three options. But I still think the satellite is expensive!
**LERATO:** Up to a point, I agree with you. Let's see what Femi thinks on Friday.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Concordar**

[ECRÃ: I agree. · I completely agree. · Absolutely. · Exactly. · That's true. · You're right. · So do I. / Neither do I.]

**PROF:** These show you agree. "Absolutely" and "Exactly" are strong. "So do I" and "Neither do I" — from unit 1 — are quick ways to agree.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: "Concordo." → ✘ I am agree. · ✔ I agree. · "Não concordo." → ✘ I'm not agree. · ✔ I don't agree. · "Concordas?" → ✘ Are you agree? · ✔ Do you agree?]

**PROF:** Careful! "Agree" is a verb, like "work". So: I agree, I don't agree, Do you agree? Never "I am agree".
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: I agree **with** Sílvia. · I agree **with** you **about** the price.]

**PROF:** Agree "with" a person, "about" a subject.
[PAUSA 3 s]

**Concordar em parte**

[ECRÃ: I see your point, but… · That's true, but… · You have a point, but… · Up to a point. · I partly agree.]

**PROF:** These say: "you're right, a little — but I have a different idea." Start with the other person's point, then say "but" and your reason.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Discordar com cortesia**

[ECRÃ:
✔ I'm not sure I agree. · ✔ I don't really agree. · ✔ I'm not sure about that.
⚠ I totally disagree. · ⚠ That's wrong. (demasiado forte numa reunião)]

**PROF:** In a meeting, disagree softly. "I'm not sure I agree" means "I don't agree" — but it sounds polite. "I totally disagree" or "That's wrong" can sound rude, especially with a client or your boss.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: discordar = frase suave + **a razão**: I'm not sure I agree. The antennas help, **but** they don't stop the sandstorms.]

**PROF:** And always give a reason.
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Listen to "I see your point, but…". The voice goes up on "point", then a short pause before "but".
**PROF:** I see your point, [pausa] but they're paying for new antennas too.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I see your point, but it's a lot of money.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** I'm not sure I agree.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Agree, agree in part, or disagree? Answer with a reason.
[ECRÃ: "Meetings on Friday afternoon are a bad idea."] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: modelo — I completely agree. People are tired on Friday afternoon.]
[ECRÃ: "Email is better than the phone."] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: modelo — I see your point, but for urgent problems the phone is faster.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Your colleague says: "We should stop all paper documents in our company next month." Agree in part, disagree in part, and give your reasons. About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I see your point. Paper is expensive, and digital documents are easy to find. But I'm not sure about next month. That's very fast. For example, some of our clients still send paper contracts. In my view, we need six months.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it difficult for you to disagree with your boss? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 1C):** Your colleague says: "I don't like long meetings." Agree, quickly.
[PAUSA 4 s]
[ECRÃ: Neither do I.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Agree, agree in part, disagree politely — and always give a reason. Next lesson: how to interrupt, and how to check that you understand.

## 3C — Sorry to interrupt

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3C — Sorry to interrupt        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue interromper com cortesia (Sorry to interrupt, but…), pedir
  para terminar (Can I just finish?), voltar ao assunto (Going back to… Anyway, as I was saying…) e
  verificar se percebeu (So, what you're saying is…? Did you say the first or the third?).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: Sorry to interrupt (to + verbo, não "Sorry for interrupt"); Could you repeat that?;
  perguntas de escolha (Did you say X or Y?); Just to check: … Is that right?
Palavras-alvo: interrupt, come in (numa conversa), finish, warehouse, by air, supplier, height, freeze
  / froze (o vídeo), check, repeat, go ahead
Reciclagem: perguntas indirectas (B1, 1B): Could you tell me when…?; números ordinais nas datas (A1, A2)
Pronúncia: interromper com voz suave: "Sorry" com uma pausa curta; datas: the first / the third /
  the fifteenth
Contexto: quarta-feira, 20 de Outubro, 11:00; videochamada; em Luanda, o Nuno e a Thandi; em Joanesburgo,
  o Sipho, no armazém da Mopane Systems
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 3C; conversa guiada "What do you think?"; pergunta 5 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Sometimes in a meeting you need to stop someone — politely. And sometimes you need to check a number or a date. Today: interrupting, going back, and checking.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 20 de Outubro, 11:00; videochamada; o Nuno e a Thandi num ecrã, em Luanda; o Sipho no outro, num armazém com caixas]

**SIPHO:** OK, here's the plan. We have twelve antennas here in our warehouse. We send them by air on Monday the first, they arrive in Luanda on Wednesday the third, and then for the masts we need—
**NUNO:** Sorry to interrupt, Sipho, but did you say twelve antennas? We need fourteen.
**SIPHO:** Fourteen? OK, I can check that. So, for the masts, our supplier in Cape Town needs—
**THANDI:** Sorry, Sipho, can I just come in here? Hannah's team hasn't chosen the height of the masts yet. We can't order them today.
**SIPHO:** Ah, OK. Thanks, Thandi. Anyway, as I was saying, the antennas leave on Monday the first.
**NUNO:** So, what you're saying is the antennas arrive in Luanda on Wednesday the third?
**SIPHO:** Exactly. And the satellite equipment comes from—
[ECRÃ: a imagem do Sipho pára]
**NUNO:** Sipho? Sorry, you froze. Could you repeat that?
**SIPHO:** Sure. The satellite equipment comes from a different supplier, in Nairobi. It takes two weeks.
**THANDI:** Two weeks from today?
**SIPHO:** No, two weeks from the order. Can I just finish? We can only order it after Morro Azul says yes.
**THANDI:** Of course. Go ahead.
**SIPHO:** That's all, really.
**NUNO:** OK. Going back to the antennas — can you send fourteen, not twelve?
**SIPHO:** Let me check… Yes, we have sixteen. Fourteen for you, on the first.
**NUNO:** Great. So, just to check: fourteen antennas, by air, on Monday the first, in Luanda on the third. Is that right?
**SIPHO:** That's right.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Interromper**

[ECRÃ: Sorry to interrupt, but… · Sorry, can I just say something? · Sorry, can I come in here? · Excuse me, can I ask a question?]

**PROF:** To interrupt, start with "Sorry" or "Excuse me", and use "just": it makes the interruption small. Interrupt for a good reason — a wrong number, an important fact.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: "Desculpe interromper." → ✘ Sorry for interrupt. · ✔ Sorry **to interrupt**.]
[PAUSA 3 s]

**Quando alguém o interrompe**

[ECRÃ: Can I just finish? · Just a moment — let me finish. · (para deixar falar) Of course. Go ahead.]

**PROF:** If someone interrupts you, you can say "Can I just finish?" — politely. If you want the other person to speak, say "Go ahead".
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Voltar ao assunto**

[ECRÃ: Anyway, as I was saying, … · Going back to the antennas, … · Where was I? Oh yes, …]

**PROF:** After an interruption, go back with "Anyway, as I was saying" or "Going back to" plus the subject.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Verificar**

[ECRÃ: So, you mean …? · So, what you're saying is …? · Sorry, did you say twelve or fourteen? · Just to check: … Is that right? · Sorry, you froze. Could you repeat that?]

**PROF:** To check, repeat the important information in your words. With numbers and dates, give two options: "Did you say twelve or fourteen?" At the end, check everything: "Just to check… Is that right?"
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ Can you repeat? · ✔ Could you repeat that? / Could you say that again?]

**PROF:** And "repeat" needs "that": "Could you repeat that?"
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** To interrupt politely, use a soft voice, and make a short pause after "Sorry". Listen.
**PROF:** Sorry, [pausa] can I just say something?
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: Sorry to interrupt, but did you say twelve?
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** And dates. Repeat: the first, the third, the fifteenth.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Check the information. Use "Did you say … or …?"
[ECRÃ: "The meeting's on the thirteenth." (não ouviu bem: 13 ou 30?)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Sorry, did you say the thirteenth or the thirtieth?]
[ECRÃ: "It costs fifteen thousand." (15 000 ou 50 000?)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Sorry, did you say fifteen or fifty thousand?]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. I'm your colleague, and I'm explaining a plan. Interrupt me once to correct a number, then check all the information at the end. Listen first.
[ECRÃ: o colega diz: "The training is on Tuesday the ninth, in room four, from nine to five, for twelve people." Na lista do aluno: 14 pessoas.]
[PAUSA 35 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Sorry to interrupt, but did you say twelve people? We're fourteen. … OK. So, just to check: the training is on Tuesday the ninth, in room four, from nine to five, for fourteen people. Is that right?]

**PROF (opinião):** In your culture, is it rude to interrupt in a meeting? When is it OK?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 1B):** Ask Sipho, politely, when the antennas arrive. Start with "Could you tell me…".
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Could you tell me when the antennas arrive?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! Interrupt with "Sorry", go back with "Anyway", and check with "So, what you're saying is…". Now talk to the tutor: "What do you think?". Next lesson: "too expensive", "not big enough".

## 3D — Too expensive, not big enough

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3D — Too expensive, not big enough        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dizer que algo é demasiado ou insuficiente (too expensive,
  too many tasks, not high enough, not enough engineers) e graduar com intensificadores (a bit, quite,
  really, extremely) e com so / such (It's so expensive. It's such a big job.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 4
Gramática-alvo: too + adjectivo (um problema); too much + incontável, too many + contável; not +
  adjectivo + enough; enough + nome; a bit / quite / very / really / extremely; so + adjectivo; such a +
  adjectivo + nome; such + adjectivo + plural; so many / so much
Palavras-alvo: slide, budget, kilometre, metre, high, weak, slow, fast, engineer, task, install,
  useful, expensive, cheap
Reciclagem: comparativos (A2): higher, cheaper; present perfect continuous (B1, 2B)
Pronúncia: acento nos intensificadores e em "too" (It's really expensive. It's too slow.)
Contexto: quinta-feira, 21 de Outubro, 16:00; o escritório da equipa conjunta; o Nuno e o Paulo
  preparam os diapositivos para a reunião de sexta com a Morro Azul
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 3D; perguntas 6, 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: how to say there's a problem with the quantity or the size of something — "too expensive", "not high enough" — and how to make words stronger or softer.

### 2. Situação (2 min)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 21 de Outubro, 16:00; o Nuno e o Paulo ao portátil; no ecrã, um diapositivo: "Option B — fibre cable: 14 km · USD 200 000 · 3 months"]

**PAULO:** OK, slide three: the fibre cable. Fourteen kilometres, two hundred thousand dollars.
**NUNO:** It's too expensive for this year. Hannah says their budget isn't big enough.
**PAULO:** And it's too slow — three months. They need a solution this year.
**NUNO:** OK. Slide one: the antennas. Are the old masts high enough?
**PAULO:** No, they aren't. They're only eighteen metres. In a sandstorm the signal is too weak. We need thirty metres.
**NUNO:** And do we have enough antennas?
**PAULO:** Yes — Sipho has fourteen for us. That's enough. But we don't have enough engineers in November: only two.
**NUNO:** Two engineers for fourteen antennas and four masts? That's too much work for two people.
**PAULO:** I know. There are too many tasks for one week.
**NUNO:** It's such a big job! And slide two, the satellite?
**PAULO:** It's quite cheap to install — about three thousand dollars. But nine hundred dollars a month is a bit expensive.
**NUNO:** It isn't cheap, but it's extremely useful. With the backup, the plant keeps working in a sandstorm.
**PAULO:** True. You've been working so hard on this, Nuno.
**NUNO:** It's such an interesting project. And there are so many people from different countries!

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

***Too* + adjectivo = um problema**

[ECRÃ: It's **too expensive**. (= mais caro do que podemos pagar) · It's **too slow**.]

**PROF:** "Too" plus an adjective means "more than is OK" — it's a problem.
[PAUSA 3 s]

[ECRÃ: "O hotel era muito bom." → ✘ The hotel was too good. · ✔ The hotel was **very** good. / **really** good.]

**PROF:** Careful! "Very" is not "too". "Very" is just strong. "Too" means a problem. "This coffee is very hot" — OK. "This coffee is too hot" — I can't drink it.
[PAUSA 5 s]

***Too much, too many***

[ECRÃ: **too much** + incontável: too much work, too much money, too much noise · **too many** + contável: too many tasks, too many meetings, too many emails]

**PROF:** With a noun: "too much" for things you can't count, "too many" for things you can count.
[PAUSA 4 s]

***Enough***

[ECRÃ: adjectivo + **enough**: The masts aren't **high enough**. · Their budget isn't **big enough**.
**enough** + nome: We have **enough antennas**. · We don't have **enough engineers**.
✘ enough high · ✔ high enough]

**PROF:** "Enough" means "the quantity we need". With an adjective, "enough" comes after: high enough. With a noun, it comes before: enough engineers.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Mais forte, mais suave**

[ECRÃ: a bit expensive · quite cheap · very useful · really interesting · extremely useful]

**PROF:** From soft to strong: a bit, quite, very, really, extremely. Use "a bit" for negative things: "a bit expensive", "a bit slow". "Quite" means "more than a little": "quite cheap".
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: I **really** like the project. (really + verbo)]
[PAUSA 2 s]

***So* e *such***

[ECRÃ: **so** + adjectivo: It's **so expensive**! · You've been working **so hard**.
**such a** + adjectivo + nome: It's **such a big job**! · It's **such an interesting project**.
**such** + adjectivo + plural: They're **such nice people**.
**so many** / **so much**: There are **so many** people! · There's **so much** work!
✘ It's a so big job. · ✔ It's such a big job.]

**PROF:** "So" and "such" make a word stronger, often with emotion. "So" goes with an adjective alone; "such a" goes with an adjective and a noun.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** The intensifier is the strong word. Listen.
[ECRÃ: It's **really** expensive. · It's **too** slow. · It's **such** a big job!]
**PROF:** It's really expensive.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: It's too slow.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** It's such a big job!
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Too, or enough?
[ECRÃ: The room has ten chairs. We're fifteen. → The room isn't … (big)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: The room isn't big enough.]
[ECRÃ: We have eight meetings this week. → We have … (meetings)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: We have too many meetings.]
[ECRÃ: Lunch is fifteen minutes. → We don't have … (time)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: We don't have enough time.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think about your office, or your home. Describe two problems with "too", "too much" or "too many", and one with "not enough". Then say one good thing, with "so" or "such".
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Our office is too small for twelve people, and there's too much noise. We don't have enough meeting rooms, so we have meetings in the kitchen! But my colleagues are such nice people, and the view is so beautiful.]

**PROF (opinião):** Do you think most companies have too many meetings? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 2B):** Your colleague looks tired. Ask how long they've been working on the project.
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: How long have you been working on the project?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Great! "Too" for a problem, "enough" for the quantity we need, and "so" or "such" for emotion. Next lesson: the words of meetings.

## 3E — Meetings

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3E — Meetings        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar as palavras e colocações de uma reunião (agenda, chair,
  minutes, action point, reach an agreement, raise a point), usar o acento enfático e o acento
  contrastivo (I said Tuesday, not Thursday), e evitar os falsos amigos argument e resume.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2, 3
Gramática-alvo: colocações com make, reach, raise, take, set, meet
Palavras-alvo: agenda, item, chair (nome e verbo), minutes, action point, deadline, attend, postpone,
  any other business, on schedule, behind schedule; make a decision, reach an agreement, raise a point,
  take notes, set a deadline, meet a deadline; argument, discussion, summarise, resume
Reciclagem: too / enough (3D); opiniões (3A)
Pronúncia: acento enfático (I really don't think so); acento contrastivo (I said Tuesday, not Thursday)
Falsos amigos: argument (no dia-a-dia = discussão zangada; argumento → point); resume (≠ resumir →
  summarise; resume = retomar, recomeçar)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 10 min
Verificação: Lab 3E; pergunta 9 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of meetings — from the agenda to the minutes. And how to stress a word to make your meaning clear.

### 2. Palavras de reunião (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| agenda | The agenda has three items. |
| item | Item one: the installation dates. |
| chair | Lerato chairs the meeting. She's the chair. |
| attend | Five people are attending the meeting. (assistir, participar) |
| minutes | Lerato sends the minutes after the meeting. (a acta) |
| action point | Action point: Nuno sends the schedule by Friday. |
| deadline | The deadline is Friday the twenty-ninth. |
| postpone | Let's postpone this item to next week. (adiar) |
| any other business | Any other business? (outros assuntos) |
| on / behind schedule | We're on schedule. / We're two weeks behind schedule. |

**PROF:** Repeat: agenda. Item. Chair. Minutes. Action point. Deadline. Postpone.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: "assistir a uma reunião" → ✔ **attend** a meeting · ✘ assist a meeting (assist = ajudar)]

**PROF:** Notice: "minutes" are also the notes of a meeting. And you "attend" a meeting.
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 3. Colocações (2 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Verbo | + nome |
|---|---|
| make | a decision, a proposal |
| reach | an agreement, a decision |
| raise | a point, a question |
| take | notes |
| set / meet | a deadline |

**PROF:** These verbs and nouns go together. We "make" or "reach" a decision. We "reach an agreement". We "raise a point" — we bring a new subject into the meeting. We "set" a deadline, and then we "meet" it — we finish on time.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ do a decision · ✔ make a decision · ✘ arrive at an agreement → ✔ reach an agreement]
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: acento enfático e contrastivo (2 min)

[ECRÃ: I **really** don't think so.]

**PROF:** To show a strong feeling, make one word longer and louder. Listen: I really don't think so.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I really don't think so.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: A: The meeting's on Thursday, then. · B: No — I said **Tuesday**, not **Thursday**.]

**PROF:** To correct someone, stress the different word. Listen: No, I said Tuesday, not Thursday.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I said Tuesday, not Thursday.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: A: Fourteen antennas on the fifth? · B: Fourteen antennas on the **first**.]

**PROF:** Now you. Correct the date: Fourteen antennas on the fifth?
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: No — on the first.]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **argument**: cuidado com "argumento"]

**PROF:** In everyday English, an "argument" is usually an angry discussion between people: "They had an argument, and now they don't talk." A calm conversation about ideas is a "discussion". And for one reason in a discussion, the safest word is "point": "Nuno made a good point."
[ECRÃ: We had a good discussion. · Nuno made a good point. · They had an argument, and now they don't talk.]
(PT: "Argument" também pode querer dizer as razões a favor de uma ideia, mas o sentido mais comum no dia-a-dia é "discussão zangada". "Um bom argumento" diz-se, com segurança, "a good point". "Discutir" um assunto numa reunião é "discuss" — sem "about": "We discussed the price.")
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: **resume** ≠ resumir]

**PROF:** "Resume" means to start again after a pause: "The meeting resumes at two o'clock."
(PT: "Resumir" diz-se "summarise": "Pode resumir as decisões?" → "Could you summarise the decisions?" E "o resumo" é "the summary".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Make, reach, raise, take or set?
[ECRÃ: Let's … a deadline: Friday.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: set]
[ECRÃ: Can I … a point about the budget?] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: raise]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Describe a typical meeting at your work or school: who chairs it, how long it is, who takes notes, and is it usually on schedule?
[PAUSA 35 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Our team meeting is every Monday at nine. My manager chairs it, and we take turns to take notes. It's usually too long — about ninety minutes — and we don't often reach a decision. But we always finish with action points.]

**PROF (opinião):** In your opinion, what makes a meeting good?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF:** Well done! Next lesson: a real meeting, with four people — and they need to decide today.

## 3F — Real English: We need to decide today

**FEMI:** OK. Then I can tell you now: that's too much for this year. Our budget isn't big enough. Maybe next year.

**FEMI:** That's fair. Let's put it in next year's budget discussion.

## 3G — Work & Life: Project meeting

```

Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue participar numa reunião sobre o calendário de um projecto:

  staff, summary, decision, action point

```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today, Nuno is in a project meeting. Watch how he disagrees with the client, interrupts once, helps Mr Domingos with the Portuguese, and summarises the decisions.

### 2. Situação (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 27 de Outubro, 10:00; videochamada; agenda: "1. Installation dates · 2. Any other business"; num ecrã, a Lerato, o Nuno e a Thandi; no outro, a Hannah e o Sr. Domingos, na sala de controlo da central]

**LERATO:** Good morning, everyone. One main item today: the installation dates. Hannah, can you start?

**NUNO:** I see your point, Hannah, but I'm not sure one week is enough. The antennas arrive in Luanda on the third, and the lorry needs two days to get to Namibe. And we only have two engineers. Fourteen antennas and four masts in one week is too much work for two people.
**HANNAH:** But the network keeps going down, Nuno. We can't wait so long.
**NUNO:** I understand. But if we go too fast, we make mistakes. For example, at Mussulo Energy, we installed everything in four days, and we had to go back twice.
**HANNAH:** Hmm. That's a good point. Domingos, what do you think?
**SR. DOMINGOS:** Desculpem, vou falar em português. A semana de 8 não dá. Dia 11 é feriado, é o Dia da Independência, e no dia 12 metade do pessoal faz ponte. Além disso, a grua só está livre a partir de dia 15. E preciso de dois dias para preparar a central antes de desligarmos a rede.
**HANNAH:** Sorry — my Portuguese isn't good enough. Nuno, can you help?
**NUNO:** Of course. Mr Domingos says the week of the eighth isn't possible. The eleventh is a public holiday — Independence Day — and on the twelfth, half of the staff take a long weekend. Also, the crane is only free from the fifteenth. And he needs two days to prepare the plant before we turn off the network.
**HANNAH:** Ah. OK, then the eighth is too early.
**LERATO:** Right. So, let's move to item two, any other business—
**NUNO:** Sorry to interrupt, Lerato, but we haven't agreed on the dates yet.
**LERATO:** You're right, sorry! Going back to the dates. Nuno, what's your proposal?
**NUNO:** Two weeks, from Monday the fifteenth to Friday the twenty-sixth. First the satellite, from the fifteenth to the seventeenth, so the plant has a backup. Then the masts and the antennas, from the eighteenth.
**HANNAH:** Two weeks… I'm not sure. Can we do it in ten days?
**NUNO:** Ten days is possible, but there's no time for problems. In my opinion, two weeks is safer.
**HANNAH:** OK. I can accept that, because the satellite comes first. Domingos?
**SR. DOMINGOS:** Dia 15? Está bem, combinado.
**NUNO:** He says the fifteenth is fine.
**LERATO:** Excellent. Nuno, can you summarise the decisions?
**NUNO:** Sure. So, the installation is from Monday the fifteenth to Friday the twenty-sixth of November. The satellite comes first, then the masts and the antennas. Action points: Hannah books the crane from the fifteenth. Mr Domingos prepares the plant on the fifteenth and the sixteenth. I send the detailed schedule by Friday the twenty-ninth. **LERATO:** Perfect. Such a good meeting! Thank you, everyone.

### 3. Como se participa numa reunião (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno fez]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discordar com razões | I see your point, but… · too / enough | I'm not sure one week is enough… It's too much work for two people. |
