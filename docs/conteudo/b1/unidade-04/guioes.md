# B1.1 · Unidade 4 — What if…? · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B1](../unidade-01/guioes.md): tudo em inglês, e o português só nos falsos amigos e na mediação.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2 e das unidades 1 a 3 do B1. Novo: o 2.º condicional (*If we had more engineers, we'd finish in a week. If I were you, I'd…*) e o contraste com o 1.º (*If customs accepts the document today, we'll…*); a probabilidade com *will definitely, will probably, may, might, might not, probably won't, definitely won't* e *I doubt…*; sugestões (*Why don't we…? What about + -ing? How about…? That might not work, because…*); comparar soluções com comparativos (*faster but more expensive, not as fast as*); os *phrasal verbs run out of, sort out, put off*.

**Não usar nesta unidade:** a voz passiva (nem "the antennas are held", "it was delayed", "the crane is booked": a alfândega *keeps* ou *releases* as antenas); orações relativas, com ou sem pronome ("the document they need"); o *past perfect*; o 3.º condicional e *should have*; *wish*; o discurso indirecto no passado; o *future continuous*; *must / can't* de dedução; *be / get used to*; *would* para hábitos do passado.

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mopane Systems e a Morro Azul Energia são fictícias. O programa de rádio da 4E ("Work Talk") e as pessoas que telefonam são fictícios. O despachante, Sr. Lukoki, é fictício.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**; **Paulo**; **Sílvia** (directora financeira); **Lerato** (coordenadora; não lê português); **Hannah** (Morro Azul, na central do Namibe); **Thandi** (Mopane Systems; de volta a Joanesburgo desde o fim de Outubro, entra por videochamada); **Sipho** (Mopane Systems; só é mencionado).

**A história:** Novembro de 2027. As catorze antenas chegam a Luanda na quarta-feira, 3 (B1 3C), mas a alfândega do aeroporto fica com elas: falta o certificado de homologação do equipamento. A alfândega não aceita cópias: o Sipho envia o original por correio expresso na quinta à noite, e o Nuno entrega-o na sexta, 5. A partir daí, a alfândega pode demorar até dez dias úteis (com o feriado de dia 11, até segunda, 22). Com mais dois dias de camião, as antenas só chegariam ao Namibe na quarta, 24, e a instalação está marcada de 15 a 26 (B1 3G). Na quinta, 4, o Nuno fala do problema ao almoço (4A); na sexta, 5, com a Lerato (4B); na segunda, 8, com a Hannah (4C); na terça, 9, a equipa compara duas soluções e decide (4F).

## 4A — If I had more time…

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4A — If I had more time…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar de situações imaginárias ou pouco prováveis com o
  2.º condicional (If we had more engineers, we'd finish in a week.) e dar conselhos com If I were
  you, I'd…
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 3
Gramática-alvo: If + past simple, would ('d) / wouldn't + verbo; were para todas as pessoas (If I were
  you); perguntas (What would you do if…?); a ordem das duas partes
Palavras-alvo: customs, document, missing, director, hire, change, lorry, holiday, worried, right now
Reciclagem: opiniões e desacordo (B1, 3A e 3B): In my view… I'm not sure I agree; too / enough (B1, 3D)
Pronúncia: "If I were you" ligado, como uma só palavra; "I'd" curto
Contexto: quinta-feira, 4 de Novembro, 13:00; a cantina da Kalandula Tech; o Nuno, o Paulo e a Sílvia
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 4A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 4
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This unit is about problems and solutions — and about imagination. Today: how to talk about situations that aren't real, and how to give advice with "If I were you…".

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 4 de Novembro, 13:00; a cantina; o Nuno com o prato quase cheio; o Paulo e a Sílvia à mesa]

**SÍLVIA:** You look worried, Nuno. What's wrong?
**NUNO:** The antennas arrived yesterday, but customs is keeping them at the airport. One document is missing.
**PAULO:** Oh no. And the installation starts on the fifteenth.
**SÍLVIA:** If I were you, I'd call Sipho right now. He can send the document by email.
**NUNO:** I did. But customs doesn't accept copies, so he's sending the original tonight, by courier. And customs can take ten working days after that.
**PAULO:** Ten days! If we had more engineers, it wouldn't be a problem. We'd install everything in one week.
**NUNO:** And if the antennas were in Namibe today, I'd be a happy man.
**SÍLVIA:** OK, stop. Five minutes without work. Here's a question: if you were the director of Kalandula Tech for one day, what would you change?
**PAULO:** Easy. I'd buy a lorry for our team. We'd never wait for transport again.
**NUNO:** I'd hire two more engineers. And I'd give everyone Friday afternoons off!
**SÍLVIA:** Ha! If you did that, I'd have a big problem with the budget.
**PAULO:** And you, Sílvia? What would you do?
**SÍLVIA:** If I were the director? I wouldn't change anything. I'd just take a long holiday!
**NUNO:** Seriously, if I had more time, I'd study for the B1 exam every day. It's in December.
**SÍLVIA:** Then eat your lunch, Nuno, and stop worrying!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**O 2.º condicional**

[ECRÃ: If we **had** more engineers, we**'d install** everything in one week.
(Não temos mais engenheiros: é imaginário.)]

**PROF:** This is the second conditional. "If" plus the past simple, then "would" plus a verb. The past form doesn't talk about the past here: it shows the situation isn't real, or isn't likely. We don't have more engineers — it's imaginary.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: If you **did** that, I**'d have** a big problem. · I**'d hire** two more engineers. · I **wouldn't change** anything. · What **would** you **do**?]

**PROF:** "Would" is often short: I'd, we'd, you'd. The negative is "wouldn't". And for questions: "What would you do?" "What would you change?"
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: "Se eu tivesse mais tempo, estudava todos os dias."
✘ If I would have more time, … · ✘ If I will have more time, … · ✔ If I **had** more time, I**'d study** every day.]

**PROF:** Careful: no "would" after "if". After "if", use the past simple.
[PAUSA 5 s]

***If I were you***

[ECRÃ: **If I were you**, I'd call Sipho. · If I were the director, I'd buy a lorry. (were com I, he, she, it)]

**PROF:** With "be", we usually say "were" for all people: if I were, if he were. "If I was" is also common in conversation, but "If I were you" is the fixed way to give advice.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**A ordem**

[ECRÃ: If I had more time, I'd study every day. (com vírgula) · I'd study every day if I had more time. (sem vírgula)]

**PROF:** The "if" part can come first or second. When it comes first, use a comma.
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "If I were you" is said quickly, like one word. Listen: If I were you.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: If I were you, I'd call Sipho.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** "I'd" is short — but don't lose the "d". Repeat: I'd hire two more engineers.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make it imaginary.
[ECRÃ: We don't have a lorry. We wait for transport. → If we … , we …] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: If we had a lorry, we wouldn't wait for transport.]
[ECRÃ: I'm not the director. I can't change the rules. → If I … , I …] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: If I were the director, I'd change the rules.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. If you were the director of your company — or your school — for one day, what would you change? Say three things, and why. About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — If I were the director, I'd change three things. First, I'd start work at eight thirty, because the traffic is terrible at eight. Second, I'd buy new computers: ours are too slow. And I'd give everyone a free lunch on Fridays. People would be happier.]

**PROF (opinião):** Would you like to be a manager one day? Why, or why not?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 3B):** Your colleague says: "If we had more meetings, we'd work better." Disagree politely.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: I'm not sure I agree. In my view, we have too many meetings already.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! "If" plus the past, then "would", for things that aren't real. Next lesson: real or imaginary — how to choose between the first and the second conditional.

## 4B — Real or imaginary?

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4B — Real or imaginary?        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue escolher entre o 1.º condicional (situação possível: If
  customs accepts the document today, we'll…) e o 2.º (situação imaginária ou pouco provável: If customs
  accepted documents by email, we'd…).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: If + present, will (possível); If + past, would (imaginário ou pouco provável); não
  misturar as duas formas; unless (só para reconhecer)
Palavras-alvo: working day, accept, release, by email, realistic, option, miss (a date), on time,
  airport, officer
Reciclagem: 1.º condicional (A2, 8); verificar (B1, 3C): Just to check… Is that right?
Pronúncia: will e would em fala rápida: we'll / we'd
Contexto: sexta-feira, 5 de Novembro, 9:30; o escritório da equipa conjunta; a Lerato e o Nuno, antes
  de o Nuno levar o certificado à alfândega
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 4B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! "If we get the antennas…" or "If we got the antennas…"? Both are correct — but they don't mean the same. Today: real possibilities, and imaginary situations.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 5 de Novembro, 9:30; o escritório; o Nuno com uma pasta na mão; a Lerato ao lado]

**LERATO:** Any news, Nuno?
**NUNO:** The courier arrived this morning with the original certificate. I'm taking it to the airport at ten. If customs accepts it today, they'll start the process on Monday.
**LERATO:** And how long does the process take?
**NUNO:** Up to ten working days, they say.
**LERATO:** Ten days! If that happens, we'll miss the installation dates.
**NUNO:** I know. If customs accepted documents by email, we'd have the antennas today!
**LERATO:** If! Let's be realistic. If the antennas aren't in Namibe by the fourteenth, what will we do?
**NUNO:** If they aren't there by the fourteenth, we'll start with the satellite and leave the antennas for the end.
**LERATO:** And Hannah? She has the crane only until the twenty-sixth.
**NUNO:** I know. If I were Hannah, I'd be really annoyed.
**LERATO:** So would I. OK — if you have problems at the airport, call me. I'll speak to Hannah this afternoon.
**NUNO:** Thanks. So, just to check: if customs accepts the certificate, I'll send you a message, and you'll call Hannah. Is that right?
**LERATO:** That's right. Good luck!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Possível ou imaginário?**

[ECRÃ:
1.º condicional — **possível**: If customs **accepts** it today, they**'ll start** on Monday.
2.º condicional — **imaginário ou pouco provável**: If customs **accepted** documents by email, we**'d have** the antennas today.]

**PROF:** The first conditional is for real possibilities: customs can accept the certificate today. The second is for situations that aren't real, or aren't likely: customs doesn't accept documents by email.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: If I **get** the job, I'll move to Luanda. (fiz a entrevista: é possível) · If I **got** a job in Paris, I'd learn French. (não procuro emprego em Paris: é um sonho)]

**PROF:** The situation can be the same — it's your view of the probability that decides. Real chance: first. Dream, or very unlikely: second.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Não misturar**

[ECRÃ: ✘ If customs accepts it, they'd start on Monday. · ✘ If customs accepted it, they'll start on Monday.
✔ accepts … will · ✔ accepted … would]

**PROF:** Keep the pairs together: present with "will", past with "would".
[PAUSA 4 s]

***So would I***

[ECRÃ: "If I were Hannah, I'd be annoyed." — "**So would I.**" · "I wouldn't wait." — "**Neither would I.**"]

**PROF:** Remember "So do I" from unit 1? With "would", it's "So would I" and "Neither would I".
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: para reconhecer: **unless** = if … not · We'll miss the dates **unless** customs is quick. (= if customs isn't quick)]
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In fast speech, "we'll" and "we'd" are very short. Listen to the difference: we'll start — we'd start.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: If customs accepts it, we'll start on Monday.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** If customs accepted emails, we'd have the antennas today.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** First or second? Read the situation, then say the sentence.
[ECRÃ: O Paulo pode chegar a horas amanhã. (arrive on time / start the meeting at nine)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: If Paulo arrives on time, we'll start the meeting at nine.]
[ECRÃ: O Nuno não fala chinês. (speak Chinese / talk to Mei in Chinese)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: If Nuno spoke Chinese, he'd talk to Mei in Chinese.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Talk about the next weekend: two real plans with "if" and "will", and one dream with "if" and "would". About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — If the weather is good on Saturday, I'll go to the beach with my family. If my brother calls, we'll have lunch together. But if I had a boat, I'd go to Mussulo every weekend!]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it better to plan for problems before they happen, or to solve them when they happen? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 3C):** Check the plan with Lerato: Nuno sends a message, and Lerato calls Hannah.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: So, just to check: I send you a message, and you call Hannah. Is that right?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Possible: present and "will". Imaginary: past and "would". Next lesson: how sure are you? "It'll probably…", "It might…", "I doubt it."

## 4C — It might be windy

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4C — It might be windy        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dizer a probabilidade de uma coisa acontecer (will definitely,
  will probably, may, might, might not, probably won't, definitely won't, I doubt…) e reagir (Probably
  not. I doubt it.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: will definitely / will probably / may / might / might not / probably won't /
  definitely won't + verbo; a posição de probably e definitely; I doubt + frase; maybe (no início) ≠
  may be (verbo)
Palavras-alvo: forecast, wind, weather, courier, chance, sure, definitely, probably, maybe, doubt
Reciclagem: present perfect (A2): Customs has had the certificate since Friday; too / enough (B1, 3D)
Pronúncia: "doubt" sem o "b"; might / might not
Contexto: segunda-feira, 8 de Novembro, 15:00; videochamada; o Nuno em Luanda; a Hannah na sala de
  controlo da central do Namibe
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 4C; conversa guiada "If you were the manager…"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! How sure are you? Today: how to say that something will definitely happen, will probably happen, might happen — or probably won't.

### 2. Situação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 8 de Novembro, 15:00; videochamada; o Nuno no escritório; a Hannah na sala de controlo, com ecrãs da central atrás]

**HANNAH:** Hi, Nuno. So, any news about the antennas?
**NUNO:** Customs has had the certificate since Friday. They'll probably release the antennas next week, but it might be the twenty-second.
**HANNAH:** The twenty-second? Then they won't be here before the twenty-fourth.
**NUNO:** Exactly. So we may need to move the antennas to the last days of November.
**HANNAH:** Hmm. The weather might be a problem too. The forecast says strong wind on the twenty-fifth.
**NUNO:** Strong wind? Then the crane probably won't work that day.
**HANNAH:** Probably not. It's too dangerous. And I doubt Domingos can find another crane for the end of the month.
**NUNO:** OK. But there's some good news: customs released the satellite equipment this morning. It came by courier, with all the documents.
**HANNAH:** Great! So the satellite will definitely be ready on the seventeenth?
**NUNO:** Definitely. The engineers leave Luanda on Saturday the thirteenth.
**HANNAH:** And the antennas? Be honest, Nuno. Will they be here on time?
**NUNO:** Honestly? If we just wait, probably not. But I have an idea. I'll explain it at our meeting tomorrow.
**HANNAH:** OK. I hope it's a good idea!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**De certo a impossível**

[ECRÃ:
100% — The satellite **will definitely** be ready.
 80% — They**'ll probably** release the antennas next week.
 50% — It **may** be the twenty-second. / It **might** be the twenty-second.
 50% — It **might not** be windy.
 20% — The crane **probably won't** work. / I **doubt** Domingos can find another crane.
  0% — It **definitely won't** be ready.]

**PROF:** From sure to impossible. "Will definitely", "will probably", "may" or "might", "probably won't", "definitely won't". "May" and "might" mean the same; "may" is a bit more formal.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**A posição de *probably* e *definitely***

[ECRÃ: ✔ They'**ll probably** release them. · ✔ The crane **probably won't** work.
✘ The crane won't probably work. · ⚠ The crane will probably not work. (possível, mas pouco natural)]

**PROF:** Notice the position: after "will", but before "won't".
[PAUSA 4 s]

***Might* + verbo**

[ECRÃ: ✘ It might to rain. · ✔ It **might rain**. · ✘ It mights rain. · ✔ She **might** come.]

**PROF:** After "might" and "may", the verb has no "to". And "might" never takes an "s".
[PAUSA 4 s]

***Maybe* ou *may be*?**

[ECRÃ: **Maybe** it'll be windy. (talvez — normalmente no início da frase) · It **may be** windy. (verbo: pode estar)]

**PROF:** "Maybe", one word, goes at the start. "May be", two words, is the verb.
[PAUSA 4 s]

***I doubt…***

[ECRÃ: **I doubt** Domingos can find another crane. (= acho que não) · "Will it rain?" — "**I doubt it.**" · "Probably not."]

**PROF:** "I doubt" means "I don't think so". For short answers: "I doubt it", "Probably not", "Maybe", "Definitely".
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In "doubt", we don't say the "b". Listen: doubt. I doubt it.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I doubt it.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** It might be the twenty-second.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** The crane probably won't work.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** How sure? Say the sentence.
[ECRÃ: rain tomorrow (50%)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: It might rain tomorrow.]
[ECRÃ: the meeting / finish late (80%)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: The meeting will probably finish late.]
[ECRÃ: Paulo / come to the party (20%)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Paulo probably won't come to the party. / I doubt Paulo will come to the party.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Talk about next year, at work or in your studies. Say two things with "will probably" or "will definitely", one with "might", and one with "I doubt". About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Next year I'll probably start a new project, and I'll definitely take an English exam. I might change departments — my manager asked me last week. But I doubt I'll get a pay rise: the company doesn't have enough money this year.]

**PROF (opinião):** Do you trust the weather forecast? Why, or why not?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 3D):** Why can't the crane work on the twenty-fifth? Use "too".
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: The wind is too strong.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Will probably", "might", "probably won't", "I doubt it". Now talk to the tutor: "If you were the manager…". Next lesson: the words of logistics and delays.

## 4D — Logistics and delays

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4D — Logistics and delays        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar de entregas, atrasos e faltas de material (shipment,
  customs, supplier, shortage), usar colocações com problem e os phrasal verbs run out of, sort out e
  put off, dizer bem as contracções I'd, we'd, it'd, wouldn't, e evitar os falsos amigos assume e
  ordinary.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: colocações (solve / cause / face a problem; place an order; miss / meet a deadline);
  phrasal verbs com pronome (sort it out, put it off)
Palavras-alvo: shipment, delivery, customs, customs agent, warehouse, supplier, shortage, delay,
  storage, fee, certificate, courier, release; run out of, sort out, put off
Reciclagem: 2.º condicional (4A): If we ran out of…, we'd…; probabilidade (4C)
Pronúncia: contracções I'd, we'd, it'd, wouldn't; I'd ≠ I (I'd go / I go)
Falsos amigos: assume (≠ assumir → admit, take responsibility; assume = supor); ordinary (≠ ordinário
  → rude, vulgar, cheap; ordinary = normal, comum)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 10 min
Verificação: Lab 4D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of logistics — shipments, customs, shortages and delays — and three useful phrasal verbs.

### 2. Logística (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| shipment | The shipment arrived in Luanda on the third. (a remessa) |
| delivery | The delivery to Namibe takes two days. |
| customs | Customs is keeping the antennas. (a alfândega) |
| customs agent | A customs agent can help with the documents. (despachante) |
| warehouse | Sipho has sixteen antennas in the warehouse. |
| supplier | Our supplier in Cape Town makes the masts. |
| shortage | There's a shortage of flour this month. (falta, escassez) |
| delay | Sorry for the delay. (o atraso) |
| storage fee | Storage is free for five days; then there's a fee. |
| courier | The satellite equipment came by courier. (empresa de entregas rápidas) |

**PROF:** Repeat: shipment. Delivery. Customs. Warehouse. Supplier. Shortage. Delay.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: **customs** (a alfândega) ≠ **customers** (os clientes)]

**PROF:** Careful: "customs" is the office at the border or the airport; "customers" are your clients.
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 3. Colocações e *phrasal verbs* (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **cause** a problem · **face** a problem · **solve** a problem · **place** an order · **miss** a deadline / **meet** a deadline]

**PROF:** A missing document causes a problem. We face it — we have it in front of us. Then we solve it.
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ:]

| *Phrasal verb* | Significado | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| run out of | ficar sem | The bakery ran out of flour. |
| sort out | resolver | Don't worry — I'll sort it out. |
| put off | adiar | Let's put off the meeting until Friday. / Let's put it off. |

**PROF:** "Run out of" — we have no more. "Sort out" — solve, organise. "Put off" — move to a later time, like "postpone". With a pronoun, it goes in the middle: sort it out, put it off.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ sort out it · ✔ sort it out · ✘ put off it · ✔ put it off]
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: contracções (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** "Would" is almost always short in conversation. Listen: I'd. We'd. It'd. Wouldn't.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I'd call the agent. It'd cost two thousand dollars. We wouldn't wait.
[PAUSA 8 s]
**PROF:** Listen to the difference: I go to the airport. I'd go to the airport.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I'd go to the airport.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **assume** ≠ assumir]

**PROF:** "Assume" usually means to think something is true without checking: "I assumed the documents were complete."
(PT: "Assumir um erro" diz-se "admit a mistake". "Assumir a responsabilidade" diz-se "take responsibility". "Assumir um cargo" diz-se "take on a job". "Assume" é sobretudo "supor": "Presumo que sim" → "I assume so".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: **ordinary** ≠ ordinário]

**PROF:** "Ordinary" means normal, not special: "It was an ordinary day at the office."
(PT: "Ordinário" no sentido de grosseiro ou de má qualidade diz-se "rude", "vulgar" ou "cheap". "Uma pessoa ordinária" não é "an ordinary person" — essa é uma pessoa comum.)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Run out of, sort out, or put off?
[ECRÃ: We have no more paper for the printer. We … paper.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: We ran out of paper.]
[ECRÃ: The director is ill. Let's … the meeting.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: Let's put off the meeting. / Let's put it off.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Describe a delay or a shortage at your work or in your city. What caused it, and how did people sort it out?
[PAUSA 35 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Last month our office ran out of paper for a week, because the supplier had a problem with a delivery. It caused a lot of problems: we couldn't print contracts. In the end, my manager sorted it out: she found a second supplier in Viana.]

**PROF (opinião):** In your opinion, what causes most delays at work: documents, transport, or bad planning?
[PAUSA 20 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — 4A e 4C):** What would happen if your office ran out of paper tomorrow?
[PAUSA 8 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — If we ran out of paper tomorrow, we'd probably print at the copy shop next door.]

**PROF:** Well done! Next lesson: a radio programme — four people with problems at work, and the presenter's advice.

## 4E — Real English: What would you do?

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4E — What would you do?        Tipo: Real English
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue identificar, em quatro chamadas para um programa de rádio, o
  problema de cada pessoa e a solução proposta.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Língua: A1, A2, o B1 até à 4D, sem estruturas novas
Vozes: Marta (angolana, apresentadora), Ricardo (angolano, do Huambo), Amina (senegalesa, no Lobito),
  Joseph (ganês, em Luanda), Fátima (cabo-verdiana, em Benguela)
Velocidade: natural, com hesitações; som de rádio e de telefone
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 4E; pergunta 9 do questionário
```

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

**PROF:** "Work Talk" is a radio programme from Luanda. People call with a problem at work, and the presenter, Marta, gives advice. Listen for two things: each person's problem, and the solution.

### 2. Primeira escuta: a ideia geral (4 min)

**PROF:** First: who has a problem with a supplier?

**MARTA:** Good evening, and welcome to Work Talk. Our first caller is Ricardo, from Huambo. Hello, Ricardo!
**RICARDO:** Hi, Marta. I have a small car workshop. My supplier ran out of brake pads two weeks ago, and I have six cars waiting. My customers are angry.
**MARTA:** Oh dear. If I were you, I'd find a second supplier. Why don't you call workshops in Benguela?
**RICARDO:** That might not work. They're my competitors!
**MARTA:** OK. What about ordering from a supplier in Luanda? It'll probably cost a bit more, but your customers won't wait.
**RICARDO:** That's a good idea. I'll try tomorrow.

**MARTA:** Next, Amina, in Lobito. Hello, Amina.
**AMINA:** Hello. I work at the port, in the office. My colleague left in August, and now I do my job and her job. I finish at eight every night. I'm so tired.
**MARTA:** That's too much work for one person. Have you talked to your boss?
**AMINA:** Not really. I don't want to complain.
**MARTA:** If you talked to her with a list of your tasks, she'd see the problem. And how about asking for a temporary colleague?
**AMINA:** Maybe. I doubt she'll say yes, but I'll try.

**MARTA:** Joseph, in Luanda. Good evening!
**JOSEPH:** Good evening. My team's meetings never start on time. We wait fifteen, twenty minutes every day. It's such a waste of time.
**MARTA:** What would happen if you started without the late people?
**JOSEPH:** Hmm. They'd probably be annoyed the first time. But they'd come on time after that!
**MARTA:** Exactly. And send the agenda the day before, so people know the meeting is important.

**MARTA:** And our last caller, Fátima, in Benguela.
**FÁTIMA:** Hello, Marta. I have a small bakery. The price of flour went up thirty percent this year, and there's a shortage. If I raise my prices, I might lose customers.
**MARTA:** That's difficult. If I were you, I wouldn't raise the prices of everything. Raise the price of the cakes, but not the bread.
**FÁTIMA:** Hmm, that's an interesting idea. And maybe I'll buy flour with two other bakeries — a bigger order is usually cheaper.
**MARTA:** Brilliant! That's all for tonight. Thank you for listening to Work Talk.

[ECRÃ: Ricardo — sem pastilhas de travão → fornecedor em Luanda · Amina — trabalho de duas pessoas → falar com a chefe, com uma lista · Joseph — reuniões atrasadas → começar a horas e enviar a agenda · Fátima — farinha cara → subir só os bolos e comprar com outras padarias]

### 3. Segunda escuta: os pormenores (3 min)

1. How many cars are waiting at Ricardo's workshop? (six ✓ / two / sixteen)
2. Why doesn't Ricardo want to call workshops in Benguela? (They're his competitors. ✓ / They're too far. / They're closed.)
3. Why does Amina do two jobs? (Her colleague left in August. ✓ / Her boss is ill. / The port is busy in August.)
4. How late do Joseph's meetings start? (fifteen or twenty minutes ✓ / five minutes / one hour)
5. How much did the price of flour go up this year? (thirty percent ✓ / thirteen percent / three percent)
6. What is Fátima's own idea? (to buy flour with two other bakeries ✓ / to close the bakery / to stop making cakes)

*(Cada resposta repete-se; depois aparecem as perguntas, com 8 s para responder.)*

### 4. Terceira escuta: como disseram (2 min)

**PROF:** Listen and repeat.
**PROF:** If I were you, I'd find a second supplier. [PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** That might not work. [PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** What about ordering from a supplier in Luanda? [PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** If I raise my prices, I might lose customers. [PAUSA 4 s]

**PROF:** Did you notice? Marta uses three ways to suggest: "If I were you, I'd…", "Why don't you…?" and "What about" plus "-ing". And when an idea is bad, Ricardo doesn't just say no: "That might not work" — and he gives the reason.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. A sua vez (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Now you're the presenter. A caller says: "I start work at seven, but the bus is always late, and my boss is angry." Give two suggestions. About forty seconds.
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — If I were you, I'd talk to your boss and explain the problem. Why don't you ask to start at seven thirty? Or what about sharing a car with a colleague? It'll probably cost a bit more than the bus, but you won't be late.]

**PROF (opinião):** Which of the four problems is the most difficult to solve, in your opinion? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent listening! Next lesson: Nuno's meeting about the antennas — two solutions, and one decision.

## 4F — Work & Life: The shipment is late

```
Curso: B1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4F — The shipment is late        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue analisar um atraso de material com a equipa, propor e
  comparar duas soluções (faster but more expensive, not as fast as) e recomendar uma; e explicar em
  inglês uma nota da alfândega escrita em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4 (e 1, 2)
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: 1.º e 2.º condicional; probabilidade; sugestões; comparativos
Palavras-alvo: option, cost, risk, risky, safe, agent, storage, working day, recommend, share (the
  cost), take responsibility
Reciclagem: opiniões e acordo (B1, 3A e 3B); too / enough (B1, 3D); resumir decisões (B1, 3G)
Pronúncia: recomendar com acento na palavra mais importante e a voz a descer no fim
Mediação: o Nuno explica em inglês, à Lerato e à Thandi, a nota da alfândega
Contexto: terça-feira, 9 de Novembro, 9:00; a sala de reuniões; a Lerato, o Nuno, o Paulo e a Sílvia; a
  Thandi por videochamada, de Joanesburgo
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 4F; conversa livre "Problem solving"; pergunta 9 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today, the antennas are still at the airport, and Nuno has an idea. Watch how he explains a document in Portuguese, compares two solutions, and recommends one.

### 2. Situação (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 9 de Novembro, 9:00; a sala de reuniões; a Thandi no ecrã; na mesa, a nota da alfândega]

**LERATO:** OK, everyone. One item: the antennas. Nuno, you have the note from customs. Can you explain it to Thandi and me? Our Portuguese isn't good enough for official documents.
**NUNO:** Of course.

[ECRÃ: a nota, em português: "Alfândega do Aeroporto de Luanda — Notificação. Mercadoria: 14 antenas de rádio, remetente Mopane Systems (Joanesburgo), destinatário Kalandula Tech. A mercadoria fica retida até à entrega do certificado de homologação do equipamento. Após a entrega, o desalfandegamento pode demorar até 10 dias úteis. Armazenagem gratuita durante 5 dias; a partir do 6.º dia, 50 USD por dia."]

**NUNO:** It says customs is keeping the fourteen antennas from Mopane until we give them the approval certificate for the equipment. We gave it to them on Friday. After that, the process can take up to ten working days — so, with the holiday on Thursday, maybe until Monday the twenty-second. And storage is free for five days; after that, it costs fifty dollars a day.
**THANDI:** The certificate was our mistake. Mopane takes responsibility for that. I'm sorry.
**LERATO:** Thanks, Thandi. So, Nuno. What are our options?
**NUNO:** I see two. Option one: we wait. If customs takes ten days, the antennas will be in Namibe on the twenty-fourth. That's too late: the crane leaves on the twenty-sixth, and the forecast says strong wind on the twenty-fifth. So we'll need a crane again from the twenty-ninth.
**SÍLVIA:** And option two?
**NUNO:** We hire a customs agent. Paulo knows one, Mr Lukoki.
**PAULO:** He's very good. He usually sorts these things out in two or three days.
**NUNO:** If he got the antennas out on Wednesday or Friday, the lorry would leave on Friday the twelfth at the latest, and they'd be at the plant on Sunday the fourteenth. On time.
**SÍLVIA:** How much does he cost?
**PAULO:** Two thousand dollars.
**SÍLVIA:** Two thousand! That's a lot of money.
**NUNO:** I see your point, Sílvia. Option two is faster, but it's more expensive. But option one isn't as safe. If we wait, we'll need another crane, and Hannah doubts Domingos can find one. A week of delay would probably cost more than two thousand dollars: the crane, the engineers' hotel, and Morro Azul's network down for another week.
**LERATO:** I agree. What do you recommend, Nuno?
**NUNO:** I recommend option two. And if Mopane shared the cost, it'd be only a thousand dollars for us.
**THANDI:** That's fair. Mopane will pay half — I'll confirm it today.
**SÍLVIA:** OK. With half, I can accept it.
**LERATO:** Great. So: we hire Mr Lukoki today, Mopane pays half, and I'll tell Hannah. Nuno, can you call him now?
**NUNO:** I'll call him right after the meeting.

### 3. Como se compara e se recomenda (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno fez]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Explicar o documento | It says… · after that… | It says customs is keeping the antennas until we give them the certificate. |
| 2. Apresentar as opções | I see two. Option one… Option two… | Option one: we wait. Option two: we hire a customs agent. |
| 3. Dizer o que acontece | 1.º e 2.º condicional | If he got the antennas out on Wednesday, the lorry would leave on Friday. |
| 4. Comparar | faster but more expensive · not as safe | Option two is faster, but it's more expensive. Option one isn't as safe. |
| 5. Recomendar com razões | I recommend… · probably | I recommend option two. A week of delay would probably cost more. |

**PROF:** Nuno is honest: option two is more expensive — he says it. Then he shows that the delay is more expensive. A good recommendation compares the costs and the risks, not just the price.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**A mediação**

**PROF:** Lerato and Thandi don't read official Portuguese. Nuno starts with "It says…", gives the main facts in order — what, until when, how long, how much — and adds what it means for the project: "maybe until Monday the twenty-second".
(PT: Numa nota oficial, as frases são longas e na passiva ("a mercadoria fica retida"). Em inglês, diga-as de forma simples e activa: "customs is keeping the antennas". E acrescente o que isso significa para o projecto.)
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** When you recommend, stress the important word, and let your voice go down at the end. Listen.
[ECRÃ: I recommend option **two**.]
**PROF:** I recommend option two.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: Option two is faster, but it's more expensive.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. A sua vez (3 min)

**PROF:** Your company needs new laptops for a training on Monday. The supplier is late. Option one: wait for the supplier — free, but the laptops might arrive on Tuesday. Option two: rent laptops for the week — they'll definitely be here, but it costs three hundred dollars. Compare the two options and recommend one. About forty-five seconds.
[PAUSA 45 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Option one is cheaper, but it isn't as safe. The laptops might arrive on Tuesday, and the training starts on Monday. If we waited, twenty people would sit without computers for a day. Option two is more expensive, but the laptops will definitely be here. I recommend option two.]

**PROF:** Now the mediation. A foreign colleague asks about this note from the supplier. Explain it in English.
[ECRÃ: "Informamos que a entrega das cadeiras fica adiada para dia 20, por falta de stock. Pedimos desculpa pelo incómodo. Se preferir, podemos entregar já metade da encomenda."]
[PAUSA 35 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — It says the supplier is putting off the delivery of the chairs until the twentieth, because they don't have enough in stock. They say sorry. And if we want, they can deliver half of the order now.]

**PROF (opinião):** In your opinion, is it better to pay more and be safe, or to save money and take a risk? Why?
[PAUSA 20 s]
