# C1.2 · Unidade 8 — On air · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2, o C1.1 e as unidades 6 e 7. Novo: *fronting* — pôr um elemento no início da frase para dar ênfase ou contraste (*This I can promise you. Much of this we owe to Kalandula. Difficult as it was, … Expensive it may be, but…*); marcadores da fala fluente (*mind you, that said, as it happens, to be fair, if anything, come to think of it*); técnicas de entrevista (mensagem-chave, "ponte" — *What's important here is… The real question is…* —, recusar uma premissa falsa — *I don't accept that premise.* —, retomar a palavra — *If I could just finish…*); moderar um painel (apresentar, dar a palavra, gerir o tempo, sintetizar).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** as expressões idiomáticas e a linguagem vaga informal da unidade 9 (*the ball is in their court, give or take, -ish, or so, kind of, sort of*); a elipse e o futuro no passado aparecem como reciclagem natural.

**Nomes, empresas e factos:** a Radio Kalahari, o programa "Business Hour", o "Windhoek Clean Power Forum" (fictício desde o B2 2F) e o programa da 8E são fictícios. Windhoek existe. O fórum realizou-se em Junho em 2028 (B2 2F); desde 2030 passou para Setembro. A ministra da 8E é fictícia e o país não é nomeado.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**, **Thandi**, **Lerato** (sul-africana, da Mopane, em Luanda desde 2027, na equipa conjunta com a Kalandula — A2 7; falou no fórum de 2028, B2 2F). Do fórum de 2028 (B2 2F) voltam **Tobias Shikongo** (namibiano, engenheiro de manutenção) e **Aïcha Diallo** (senegalesa, directora de uma cooperativa de energia); e **Celeste Kiala** (presidente do conselho de administração da Morro Azul, jurista, B2). Novos e fictícios: **Refilwe Nkuna** (sul-africana, comunicação da Mopane; faz o treino de media); **Hilma Nangolo** (namibiana, jornalista da Radio Kalahari); na 8E, **Chipo** (apresentador) e a **ministra Ruth Kalumba** (fictícia).

**A história:** Setembro de 2030. A Mopane foi convidada para o "Windhoek Clean Power Forum", de 16 a 18 de Setembro: o Nuno vai moderar um painel sobre competências locais, e a Radio Kalahari quer entrevistá-lo sobre os primeiros nove meses da central do Tômbwa (que abriu no fim de 2029, no sul de Angola, perto da fronteira com a Namíbia). Na segunda, 2, a Thandi ajuda-o a preparar as mensagens (8A); na quarta, 4, a Lerato dá-lhe conselhos por vídeo (8B); na sexta, 6, a Refilwe faz-lhe uma entrevista de treino, dura (8C); na quarta, 11, o Nuno ouve uma entrevista difícil a uma ministra (8E); na terça, 17, em Windhoek, dá a entrevista de manhã e modera o painel à tarde (8F). O resultado do concurso do Niassa (unidade 6) só sai em Outubro.

**Os factos do Tômbwa (iguais em todas as aulas):**
- A central abriu no fim de 2029; nove meses depois, a disponibilidade da rede de monitorização foi de 99,6%.
- Tempo médio de resposta a uma avaria: de cerca de dois dias para cerca de três horas (C1 1).
- Os seis técnicos são do Tômbwa e foram formados pela Kalandula (angolana), com a Elsa, antes da abertura; a Mopane ajudou só na parte do satélite (C1 1B).
- Em Março de 2030, uma tempestade de areia danificou uma antena; os técnicos locais repararam-na em quatro horas, de noite.
- O centro de formação da Mopane, em Joanesburgo (piloto desde Abril de 2030), já formou 30 técnicos de seis países em dois cursos.

## 8A — Expensive it may be, but…

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8A — Expensive it may be, but…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dar ênfase e contraste pondo um elemento no início da frase
  (fronting): o objecto (This I can promise you.), o complemento (Much of this we owe to…), e a concessão
  com as / though (Difficult as it was, … · Expensive it may be, but…).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: objecto ou complemento à frente (This I can promise you. What happened next, nobody
  expected. Much of this we owe to Kalandula.); concessão com adjectivo + as / though + sujeito + verbo
  (Difficult as it was, the team… · Small though it is, …); adjectivo + it may be, but… (Expensive it may be,
  but…); quando usar: discursos, entrevistas, escrita cuidada, para contraste
Palavras-alvo: interview, message, key message, politician, emphasis, contrast, local, cost,
  sandstorm, promise
Reciclagem: clivadas (C1, 1); inversão negativa (C1, 4)
Pronúncia: o acento forte no elemento à frente e a pausa depois dele (This [pausa] I can promise you.)
Contexto: segunda-feira, 2 de Setembro de 2030, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; o Nuno com o convite da Radio
  Kalahari e o programa do fórum
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 8A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 8
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to unit 8: On air. When you speak in public, the order of your words can carry emphasis on its own. "This I can promise you." "Difficult as it was, the team fixed it." Today: fronting.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 2 de Setembro, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; no ecrã, um e-mail: "Radio Kalahari — Business Hour. Interview request: Tômbwa, nine months on"; ao lado, o programa do fórum]

**THANDI:** A radio interview and a panel in the same day. Nervous?
**NUNO:** A little. On the radio, I get maybe eight minutes. What do I say?
**THANDI:** Three messages. That's all people remember. Go.
**NUNO:** One: response time. Two days before, about three hours now.
**THANDI:** Good. Two?
**NUNO:** The technicians are local. Much of this we owe to Kalandula — they trained them, not us.
**THANDI:** Say that exactly. "Much of this we owe to Kalandula." It's generous, and it's true. Three?
**NUNO:** The sandstorm in March. An antenna went down at night. Difficult as it was, the local team fixed it in four hours.
**THANDI:** That's your story. People remember stories, not percentages. And if they ask about the cost?
**NUNO:** The training wasn't cheap. Expensive it may be, but it costs less than flying engineers in every month.
**THANDI:** Perfect. And the hard question — "Are foreign companies taking the profits?"
**NUNO:** What happens to the profits, I can't speak for. That's Morro Azul's business. But the jobs are local. This I can promise you.
**THANDI:** [sorri] You're going to be fine. One warning: use these once or twice. Every sentence back to front, and you'll sound like a politician.
**NUNO:** Never have I been compared to a politician.
**THANDI:** Not yet.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**O que vem à frente**

[ECRÃ:]

| Normal | Com *fronting* | Efeito |
|---|---|---|
| I can promise you this. | **This** I can promise you. | ênfase na promessa |
| We owe much of this to Kalandula. | **Much of this** we owe to Kalandula. | ênfase no que se deve |
| Nobody expected what happened next. | **What happened next**, nobody expected. | suspense |
| It was difficult, but the team fixed it. | **Difficult as it was**, the team fixed it. | concessão forte |
| It may be expensive, but it costs less… | **Expensive it may be**, but it costs less… | concede e contra-ataca |

**PROF:** The word order stays normal after the fronted part: "This I can promise you" — not "This can I promise you". There's no inversion here — unlike "Never have I…" in unit 4.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**A concessão com *as* e *though***

[ECRÃ: **Difficult as it was**, … = Although it was difficult, … · **Small though it is**, … = Although it's small, … · **Expensive it may be**, but… = It may be expensive, but…]

**PROF:** "Adjective plus as plus subject plus verb" is a strong, elegant way to concede. It's typical of speeches and good interviews.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Quando usar**

[ECRÃ: ✔ um discurso, uma entrevista, uma conclusão, um contraste forte · ✘ todas as frases (soa a político) · ✘ conversa do dia-a-dia: "Coffee I would like." (soa estranho)]
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ This can I promise you. · ✘ As difficult it was, … · ✔ This I can promise you. · ✔ Difficult as it was, …]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Stress the fronted part, and pause after it. Listen: This [pausa] I can promise you. — Difficult as it was, [pausa] the team fixed it in four hours.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat both.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Bring the highlighted part to the front.
[ECRÃ: I'll never forget **that day**.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: That day I'll never forget.]
[ECRÃ: Although the room was **small**, it was full.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Small as it was, the room was full.]
[ECRÃ: It may be **slow**, but it's reliable.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Slow it may be, but it's reliable.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. You're giving a short speech about a project, a team or a person you're proud of — at work, at school, in your community. Give three key messages, tell one short story, and use fronting twice — once for emphasis, once for concession. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Our community library opened two years ago, in one room behind the church. Small as it was, it had three hundred members in six months. Much of this we owe to two retired teachers who gave every Saturday. Three things I want you to remember: it's free, it's open every day, and it's run by volunteers. Expensive it isn't — but it needs a new roof. That, I hope, is where you come in.]

**PROF (opinião):** Do you trust people who speak very well in public, or do you trust them less? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 1):** Say it with a cleft sentence instead: "Much of this we owe to Kalandula."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: It's Kalandula that we owe much of this to.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! This I can promise you, much of this we owe to…, difficult as it was, expensive it may be — emphasis by word order, once or twice in a speech. Next lesson: the little words that make speech sound fluent.

## 8B — That said…

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8B — That said…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar marcadores da fala fluente para matizar, corrigir, acrescentar
  e reagir (mind you, that said, as it happens, to be fair, if anything, come to think of it).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: that said / having said that — reconhecer (= no entanto, depois de uma afirmação forte); mind
  you (= mas atenção, informal); as it happens (= por acaso; na verdade); to be fair (= para ser justo);
  if anything (= se alguma coisa, até pelo contrário); come to think of it (= agora que penso nisso);
  posição: normalmente no início ou no fim da frase, com pausa
Palavras-alvo: tip, time limit, live, radio, presenter, listener, chairwoman, mistake, advice, panel
Reciclagem: concessão (B2, 7)
Pronúncia: a pausa e o tom descendente depois de "that said", "mind you", "to be fair"
Contexto: quarta-feira, 4 de Setembro, 17:00; videochamada entre o Nuno, em Joanesburgo, e a Lerato, em
  Luanda
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 8B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Fluent speakers use small words to turn, correct and add to what they say: "That said…", "Mind you…", "To be fair…". Without them, speech sounds like a list. Today: the markers of fluent speech.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 4 de Setembro, 17:00; videochamada; a Lerato no escritório da equipa conjunta Mopane–Kalandula, em Luanda; o Nuno em Joanesburgo]

**LERATO:** So, the radio. You'll be great. That said, live radio is different. You can't stop and start again.
**NUNO:** Did you ever make a mistake on air?
**LERATO:** As it happens, yes — in Windhoek, two years ago. I said "megawatts" when I meant "kilowatts". A thousand times too big.
**NUNO:** What did you do?
**LERATO:** I corrected it immediately and laughed. Nobody remembered. Mind you, the engineers in the room did — they still tease me about it.
**NUNO:** [ri-se] And the presenter? Will she be difficult?
**LERATO:** To be fair, most presenters aren't trying to trap you. They're trying to keep the listeners awake. If anything, a hard question is a gift — it gives you a reason to tell your story.
**NUNO:** I'm more worried about the panel. Three speakers, one hour, and I'm the moderator.
**LERATO:** Come to think of it, I know one of your speakers. Tobias Shikongo — he spoke after me in 2028. Lovely man. He talks for ever, though.
**NUNO:** Great.
**LERATO:** Give him a time limit before you start — in front of everyone. Then it's not personal.
**NUNO:** And Celeste Kiala is on the panel too. I've never moderated my former client's chairwoman.
**LERATO:** She'll be the easiest. Lawyers are disciplined about time. That said, she won't like vague questions. Prepare them.
**NUNO:** Any last tip?
**LERATO:** Drink water before, not during. And smile on the radio — people can hear it.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Marcador | Sentido | Exemplo da Lerato |
|---|---|---|
| that said | no entanto (depois de uma afirmação forte) | You'll be great. **That said**, live radio is different. |
| mind you | mas atenção (informal) | Nobody remembered. **Mind you**, the engineers did. |
| as it happens | por acaso; na verdade | **As it happens**, yes — in Windhoek. |
| to be fair | para ser justo | **To be fair**, most presenters aren't trying to trap you. |
| if anything | se alguma coisa, até pelo contrário | **If anything**, a hard question is a gift. |
| come to think of it | agora que penso nisso | **Come to think of it**, I know one of your speakers. |

**PROF:** "That said" is the most useful: you say something positive, then you limit it. "Mind you" does the same, but more informally. "If anything" is clever: it suggests the opposite of what someone expects — "a hard question isn't a problem; if anything, it's a gift".
[PAUSA 7 s]

[ECRÃ: Para reconhecer: **Having said that**, … = That said, …]

**PROF:** You'll also hear "having said that" — it means the same as "that said".
[PAUSA 4 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ That said it, … · ✘ Mind you that… (como verbo) · ✘ If something, … · ✔ That said, … · ✔ Mind you, … · ✔ If anything, …]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** These markers have their own little melody: a falling tone and a short pause. Listen: That said, [pausa] live radio is different. — Mind you, [pausa] the engineers remembered. — To be fair, [pausa] most presenters aren't trying to trap you.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat all three.
[PAUSA 10 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Add a marker.
[ECRÃ: The hotel was beautiful. (But the food was terrible.)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: The hotel was beautiful. That said, the food was terrible.]
[ECRÃ: The new manager is strict. (But she's always fair.)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: The new manager is strict. To be fair, she's always fair.]
[ECRÃ: The traffic isn't worse. (It's probably better.)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: The traffic isn't worse. If anything, it's better.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Give a friend advice before an important event — an interview, a wedding speech, an exam, a first day at work. Use at least four markers: that said, mind you, as it happens, to be fair, if anything, come to think of it. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — You'll be fine at the interview — you know the job better than anyone. That said, prepare one example for each question. As it happens, I had an interview with the same company last year. To be fair, they were very kind. Mind you, they asked me about my weaknesses three times. If anything, being nervous will help you — it shows you care. Come to think of it, wear the blue shirt.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it better to admit a mistake immediately in public, or to correct it later, quietly? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 7):** Concede with "admittedly" and come back with "even so": "Live radio is hard."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Admittedly, live radio is hard. Even so, it's the best way to reach people.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! That said, mind you, as it happens, to be fair, if anything, come to think of it — the small words of fluent speech. Next lesson: a training interview — and some very hard questions.

## 8C — The key message is…

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8C — The key message is…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue responder numa entrevista: passar as mensagens-chave, fazer a
  "ponte" de uma pergunta difícil para o que importa, recusar uma premissa falsa sem agressividade, e
  retomar a palavra quando é interrompido.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: mensagem-chave (What's important here is… The key point is… What people need to know is…);
  ponte (That's a fair question, and the real issue is… · Before I answer that, let me say… · What I can
  tell you is…); recusar a premissa (I don't accept that premise. · That's not quite right. · I'd question
  the idea that…); retomar a palavra (If I could just finish… · Let me answer the first part first.); não
  responder sem fugir (I'm not in a position to… but what I can say is…)
Palavras-alvo: premise, bridge, interrupt, money, soundbite, profit, finances, training, answer, softly
Reciclagem: clivadas (C1, 1); recusas (C1, 2C); fronting e marcadores (8A, 8B)
Pronúncia: calma sob pressão: voz baixa, ritmo regular, sem subir o tom quando é interrompido
Contexto: sexta-feira, 6 de Setembro, 14:00; uma sala pequena com um microfone; a Refilwe Nkuna faz de
  jornalista e grava; a Thandi assiste
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 8C; conversa guiada "Press conference"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In an interview, you don't control the questions — but you can control the answers. Today: key messages, the "bridge" from a hard question to what matters, how to refuse a false premise, and how to keep the floor.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 6 de Setembro, 14:00; uma sala pequena; um microfone de mesa; a Refilwe com um bloco e um sorriso perigoso; a Thandi ao fundo]

**REFILWE:** OK, I'm the journalist. Ready? Mr Kapinga, isn't Tômbwa just a South African company training Angolans to buy more South African equipment?
**NUNO:** I don't accept that premise. The technicians were trained by Kalandula, an Angolan company. Much of this we owe to them. What's important here is that a fault that used to take two days now takes about three hours.
**REFILWE:** But your company makes money from it.
**NUNO:** It does — that's what companies do. The real question is whether the people of Tômbwa are better off. And they are: the jobs are theirs, and so are the skills.
**REFILWE:** Isn't it true that the plant broke down in March and nobody—
**NUNO:** If I could just finish the first point… Yes, in March a sandstorm damaged an antenna at night. Difficult as it was, the local team fixed it in four hours. That's exactly why local skills matter.
**REFILWE:** How much profit did Morro Azul make this year?
**NUNO:** I'm not in a position to talk about Morro Azul's finances — that's their business, not ours. What I can tell you is what the plant does for the people around it.
**REFILWE:** One last thing. Will the electricity be cheaper in Tômbwa?
**NUNO:** That's a fair question, and I'd love to say yes. But prices aren't decided by us. What people can count on is power that comes back on in hours, not days.
**REFILWE:** [pára a gravação] Good. You didn't attack me, and you didn't run away.
**THANDI:** One thing: the first answer. "I don't accept that premise" was right — but say it more softly. "That's not quite right" works as well, and sounds less like a fight.
**NUNO:** "That's not quite right." Noted.
**REFILWE:** And you said "three hours" three times. That's your soundbite. Good — they'll use it.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Técnica | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| Mensagem-chave | What's important here is… · The key point is… · What people need to know is… | What's important here is that a fault… now takes about three hours. |
| Ponte (de uma pergunta difícil para a mensagem) | The real question is… · That's a fair question, and… · What I can tell you is… | The real question is whether the people of Tômbwa are better off. |
| Recusar a premissa | I don't accept that premise. · That's not quite right. · I'd question the idea that… | I don't accept that premise. The technicians were trained by Kalandula. |
| Retomar a palavra | If I could just finish… · Let me answer the first part first. | If I could just finish the first point… |
| Não responder, sem fugir | I'm not in a position to… What I can tell you is… | I'm not in a position to talk about Morro Azul's finances. |

**PROF:** A bridge isn't dodging. Nuno answers the question — "It does, that's what companies do" — and then moves to what matters. Dodging is never answering at all. Listeners notice the difference.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**PROF:** And notice the premise: the question contained a false fact — that a South African company trained the technicians. If you don't correct it, you accept it. Correct the fact first, calmly; then answer.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** When you're interrupted, don't raise your voice. Lower it a little, slow down, and continue. Listen: If I could just finish the first point… [voz calma, mais baixa]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** That's not quite right. The technicians were trained by an Angolan company.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Answer the question: correct the premise, then bridge to your message.
[ECRÃ: "Your hospital is always empty — why should the town pay for it?" (Facts: 3,000 patients a month.)] [PAUSA 9 s] [ECRÃ: That's not quite right — we see about three thousand patients a month. The real question is what the town would do without it.]
[ECRÃ: "Why did your school fail so many students?" (Facts: the pass rate rose from 60% to 72%.)] [PAUSA 9 s] [ECRÃ: I'd question the idea that we failed them — the pass rate rose from sixty to seventy-two percent. What's important here is…]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. A journalist asks you, about your industry or your organisation: "Isn't your industry part of the problem, not the solution?" Answer: correct any false premise, bridge to one key message, tell one short story, and finish with a soundbite. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I'd question the idea that we're part of the problem. Banks have made mistakes — that's true. What's important here is what a bank does for a small business. Last year, we lent money to forty market sellers in Benguela; thirty-eight paid it back, and twelve now employ other people. That, to me, is the solution. Small loans, big change.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should company leaders give interviews when things go wrong, or let the communications team speak? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 2C):** Refuse to answer politely, and offer something else: "How much does the director earn?"
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: I'm afraid I'm not in a position to talk about salaries. What I can tell you is how we decide them.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Key messages, bridges, a false premise corrected calmly, and the floor kept. Try the tutor's "Press conference". Next lesson: the words of media — and two false friends.

## 8D — Media and public speaking

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8D — Media and public speaking        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário dos media e de falar em público (soundbite,
  off the record, on air, live, panel, moderator, Q&A, headline), o ritmo e as pausas do discurso público,
  e evitar os falsos amigos legend e editor.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: colocações (go on air, a live broadcast, give an interview, speak off the record, make
  the headlines, chair / moderate a panel, take questions, open the floor, a keynote speech, a press

```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of radio, television and conferences — "on air", "off the record", "soundbite", "keynote" — the rhythm of public speaking, and two false friends from the world of films and books.

### 2. Media e conferências (3 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Expressão | Exemplo |
|---|---|

## 8E — Real English: The tough interview

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 8        Aula: 8E — The tough interview        Tipo: Real English
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue seguir uma entrevista de rádio dura e identificar as perguntas que
  a entrevistada respondeu, as que contornou com uma ponte, e as que não respondeu de todo.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Língua: A1 ao B2, o C1.1, as unidades 6 e 7 e a unidade 8 até à 8D, sem estruturas novas
Vozes: Chipo (apresentador, firme, rápido, irónico), a ministra Ruth Kalumba (calma, experiente, muito
  controlada); o país não é nomeado; som de estúdio
Velocidade: natural e rápida, com interrupções e sobreposições curtas
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 8E
```

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

**PROF:** On Wednesday, Nuno listens to a famous morning programme: a presenter known for difficult interviews, and a minister of energy — from a country we won't name. Four questions. Listen for three kinds of answer: a real answer, a bridge, and no answer at all.

### 2. Primeira escuta: a entrevista (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 11 de Setembro; um estúdio de rádio; "Morning Edition" (fictício); o apresentador com papéis; a ministra com um copo de água]

**PROF:** First: which question does the minister answer most directly?

**CHIPO:** Minister Kalumba, good morning. Last winter, people in the capital had power cuts of up to eight hours a day. You promised it would end. It didn't. Why?
**KALUMBA:** Good morning, Chipo. To be fair, the cuts were caused by two power stations breaking down at the same time. That said, you're right — eight hours is unacceptable, and I said so at the time.
**CHIPO:** So what's different this winter?
**KALUMBA:** Three things. Two new solar plants with storage are already connected. The old station has been repaired. And this I can promise you: no planned cut will be longer than four hours.
**CHIPO:** Four hours. Last year you said "no more cuts at all".
**KALUMBA:** I said we were working towards no more cuts. If anything, the—
**CHIPO:** [interrompe] That's not what you said, Minister. I have the recording.
**KALUMBA:** If I could just finish… I said we were working towards it. Difficult as it is to admit, we were too optimistic. Four hours is what I can promise today.
**CHIPO:** Third question. Is it true that the contract for the new plants went to a company owned by a relative of a member of your cabinet?
**KALUMBA:** I'd question the idea that there was anything improper. The tender was public, and the results are published. What's important here is that the plants were built on time and on budget.
**CHIPO:** That isn't an answer. Is the owner a relative or not?
**KALUMBA:** The tender was reviewed by the independent procurement authority, and they found no problems. I won't comment on people's families on the radio.
**CHIPO:** Last one. Electricity prices. Will they rise next year?
**KALUMBA:** [pausa] Yes. By about eight percent. I'd rather tell your listeners now than surprise them in January.
**CHIPO:** Well — thank you for that, at least. Minister Ruth Kalumba.

[ECRÃ: Pergunta 1 (os cortes do ano passado): responde — com uma razão e uma admissão ("you're right — eight hours is unacceptable") · Pergunta 2 (a promessa): responde depois de interrompida, e admite que foi optimista · Pergunta 3 (o contrato e o familiar): não responde — faz uma ponte ("What's important here is…") e recusa ("I won't comment…") · Pergunta 4 (os preços): a resposta mais directa — "Yes. By about eight percent."]

### 3. Segunda escuta: os pormenores (3 min)

1. How long were the power cuts in the capital last winter? (up to eight hours a day ✓ / up to four hours a day / two days)
2. What caused the cuts, according to the minister? (two power stations breaking down at the same time ✓ / a storm / the price of diesel)
3. What does the minister promise this winter? (no planned cut longer than four hours ✓ / no more cuts at all / cheaper electricity)
4. What does Chipo say about last year's promise? (He has a recording of it. ✓ / He forgot it. / He agrees with it.)
5. Does the minister say whether the owner is a relative? (No — she refuses to comment. ✓ / Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.)
6. How much will electricity prices rise next year? (by about eight percent ✓ / by four percent / they won't rise)

*(Cada resposta repete-se; depois aparecem as perguntas, com 8 s para responder.)*

### 4. Terceira escuta: como disseram (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Listen and repeat.
**PROF:** To be fair, the cuts were caused by two power stations breaking down at the same time. [PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** Difficult as it is to admit, we were too optimistic. [PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** I'd rather tell your listeners now than surprise them in January. [PAUSA 5 s]

**PROF:** Did you notice? The minister uses every technique from this unit: "to be fair", "that said", "this I can promise you", "difficult as it is", "if I could just finish", and a bridge. Most of the time, they help her. But on question three, the bridge was too visible — and the presenter said so. Techniques work when you also answer.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. A sua vez (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Now you. You're a media trainer. In about one and a half minutes, tell the minister what worked in her interview, what didn't, and what she should have said on question three.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Minister, the best moment was the last answer: "Yes, by about eight percent." Honest, short, memorable. Admitting you were too optimistic was brave, too. What didn't work was question three: the bridge was too obvious, and Chipo caught it. If the owner isn't a relative, say so. If he is, say it, and explain why the tender was still fair. Not answering made it sound worse than it probably is.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should politicians always answer every question, or do they have a right not to answer some?
[PAUSA 25 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent listening! A real answer, a visible bridge, and a refusal — and why honesty beat technique in the end. Next lesson: Nuno on the radio, and in the moderator's chair.
