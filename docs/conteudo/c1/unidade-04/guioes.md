# C1.1 · Unidade 4 — Conflict · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2 e as unidades 1 a 3 do C1. Novo: inversão depois de advérbios e expressões negativas ou restritivas (*Never have I…, Rarely / Seldom do we…, Not once has he…, At no time did I…, Under no circumstances will I…, Not only did she…, but she also…, No sooner had we… than…, Hardly had I… when…, Little did I know…, Only when / Only then did I…*); reformular e separar posições de interesses (*So what you're saying is…, What I'm hearing is…, Correct me if I'm wrong, but…, It sounds as though what really matters to you is…*); propor terreno comum (*What you both seem to want is…, Where you agree is…*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** as orações participiais (*Having analysed…*) e as passivas de relato produzidas (unidade 5); a elipse ensinada (unidade 6); o *fronting* (unidade 8); as expressões idiomáticas da unidade 9 — com uma excepção: *clear the air*, que é o título da 4F e se ensina na 4D como colocação.

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mopane Systems, a Mussiro Energia e o estúdio Fynbos Design (4E) são fictícios, como os acontecimentos. Nampula, Maputo, Joanesburgo e a Cidade do Cabo existem.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**, **Thandi**, **Bongani** (Mopane; desde 18 de Março, o engenheiro da Mopane no local em Nampula, como se prometeu à Mussiro na 2F), **Inês Macuácua** (engenheira, escritório de Maputo; fala com a Mussiro em português e vai a Nampula todas as semanas), **Dr Américo Cumbe** (Mussiro; só é referido). Na 4E, um *podcast* fictício: a apresentadora, **Precious** (sul-africana), e os dois sócios do Fynbos Design são **Ayanda** (sul-africana) e **Marco** (moçambicano, a viver na Cidade do Cabo).

**A história:** Abril de 2030. O projecto da Mussiro avança: nove das doze subestações já estão ligadas e as últimas três devem ficar prontas até 10 de Maio — dentro do pior caso dado à Mussiro (15 de Maio). Mas o Bongani e a Inês deixaram de se falar. Na segunda, 8, o Nuno recebe um *e-mail* furioso da Inês (4A); na quarta, 10, ouve o Bongani por telefone (4B); na quinta, 11, ouve a Inês por vídeo (4C); na segunda, 15, ouve um *podcast* no carro (4E); na terça, 23, depois da Páscoa, junta os dois numa videochamada (4F).

**O conflito (igual em todas as aulas):**
- **Queixas da Inês:** o Bongani muda o calendário da instalação sem a avisar, e ela dá ao cliente datas que já não são verdade; nos relatórios de sexta à Mussiro só aparece o nome dele; numa reunião com o cliente, chamou-lhe "our translator".
- **Queixas do Bongani:** a Inês escreveu ao Dr Cumbe sem o pôr em cópia; prometeu ao cliente a subestação 7 para uma sexta-feira sem confirmar com ele; contradi-lo diante do cliente em português, que ele não percebe.
- **Por trás das posições:** a Inês quer ser respeitada como engenheira e ter informação certa para não passar vergonha com o cliente; o Bongani quer controlar as promessas técnicas, porque é ele quem responde pelo prazo (e pela revisão do David Nkosi).
- **O que os dois querem, sem o saberem:** o cliente satisfeito, a entrega até 10 de Maio, e saber a tempo o que o outro decidiu.
- **O acordo (4F):** uma chamada de 15 minutos todas as segundas e quintas; nenhuma data dada ao cliente sem os dois; a Inês é a única voz da Mopane com a Mussiro em português e põe o Bongani em cópia, em inglês; os relatórios levam os dois nomes; a Inês apresenta a entrega final com o Bongani.

## 4A — Never have I…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4A — Never have I…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dar ênfase formal com inversão depois de advérbios negativos
  (Never have I…, Rarely do we…, Not once has he…, At no time did I…, Under no circumstances will I…).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: advérbio negativo ou restritivo + auxiliar + sujeito + verbo: Never have I seen…; Rarely /
  Seldom do we…; Not once has he told me…; At no time did I say…; Under no circumstances will I…; o
  auxiliar do tempo certo (have / do / did / will / can); com be: Never was I so embarrassed.
Palavras-alvo: embarrassed, schedule, inform, copy (in), complaint, tension, team, respect, client,
  trust
Reciclagem: inversão condicional (C1, 3B); atenuação (C1, 2A)
Pronúncia: o acento forte no advérbio negativo no início (Never, Not once, Under no circumstances)
Contexto: segunda-feira, 8 de Abril de 2030, 8:15; o gabinete do Nuno; a Thandi entra com um café e
  encontra o Nuno a ler um e-mail da Inês
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 4A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 4
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to unit 4: Conflict. When people are upset — or very formal — they sometimes start a sentence with a negative word and turn the order around: "Never have I been so embarrassed." Today: negative inversion — and a team in trouble.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 8 de Abril, 8:15; o gabinete do Nuno; o Nuno de testa franzida diante do portátil; a Thandi à porta, com um café]

**THANDI:** You look like someone who's read an e-mail before breakfast.
**NUNO:** From Inês. Listen to this. "Never have I been so embarrassed in front of a client. Not once in the last three weeks has Bongani told me about a change in the schedule. On Friday, I told Dr Cumbe that substation seven would be ready. It wasn't — Bongani had moved the team to substation nine."
**THANDI:** Ouch.
**NUNO:** There's more. "At no time did I agree to be called 'our translator'. I'm an engineer. Under no circumstances will I keep giving the client dates I can't trust."
**THANDI:** That's a very formal e-mail. When Inês writes "under no circumstances", she's furious.
**NUNO:** And Bongani hasn't said a word.
**THANDI:** Rarely does Bongani complain in writing. He complains on the phone — loudly. Call him. Then call her. Separately.
**NUNO:** Not together?
**THANDI:** Not yet. First, listen to each of them alone. Seldom do people say what they really want in front of the person they're angry with.
**NUNO:** And then?
**THANDI:** Then you put them in the same room — or the same call — and you don't take sides. Never have I seen a conflict like this solved by a manager who decides who's right.
**NUNO:** That's very wise.
**THANDI:** [sorri] It's very painful experience. Call Bongani.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**A inversão negativa**

[ECRÃ:
Normal: I have never been so embarrassed.
Inversão: **Never have I** been so embarrassed.
Normal: Bongani hasn't told me once.
Inversão: **Not once has Bongani** told me.
Normal: I didn't agree at any time.
Inversão: **At no time did I** agree.
Normal: I won't give dates I can't trust under any circumstances.
Inversão: **Under no circumstances will I** give dates I can't trust.]

**PROF:** The negative word goes first, then the auxiliary, then the subject — the same order as a question. If there's no auxiliary, use "do", "does" or "did": "At no time did I agree."
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Advérbios que levam inversão**

[ECRÃ: Never · Rarely · Seldom (raramente) · Not once · At no time · Under no circumstances · On no account (nunca, em caso algum)]

**PROF:** These are negative or almost negative. "Rarely" and "seldom" both mean "not often" — "seldom" is more formal.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Quando soa natural**

[ECRÃ: ✔ cartas e e-mails formais, queixas, discursos, momentos de grande ênfase · ✘ conversa do dia-a-dia: "Never have I eaten such good chicken" (soa teatral, ou irónico)]

**PROF:** Like conditional inversion, this is for strong emphasis or formal writing. In a normal conversation, one inversion shows strong feeling; three sound like a play.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ Never I have seen… · ✘ Not once he told me… · ✘ At no time I agreed… · ✔ Never have I seen… · ✔ Not once did he tell me… · ✔ At no time did I agree…]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Stress the negative word strongly, then continue. Listen: Never have I been so embarrassed. [acento forte em "Never"] Under no circumstances will I do that. [acento em "no"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat both.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make it emphatic with inversion.
[ECRÃ: I have never seen such a well-organised event.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Never have I seen such a well-organised event.]
[ECRÃ: We rarely get complaints about the service.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Rarely do we get complaints about the service.]
[ECRÃ: You must not share this password under any circumstances.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Under no circumstances must you share this password.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Write — and say — a formal complaint to an organisation that treated you badly: a bank, an airline, a shop or a public service. Use at least three negative inversions, and keep the rest calm and precise. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I'm writing about my flight on the third of March. Never have I waited so long for information: we sat at the gate for six hours. Not once did a member of staff explain the delay. At no time were we offered water or food. I'm asking for a full refund of the ticket. Under no circumstances will I fly with you again if this isn't resolved.]

**PROF (opinião):** When you're angry with a colleague, is it better to write or to talk? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 3B):** Make it formal, with conditional inversion: "If I had known about the change, I wouldn't have told the client."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Had I known about the change, I wouldn't have told the client.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! Never have I, not once has he, at no time did I, under no circumstances will I — strong, formal emphasis. Next lesson: Bongani's side of the story — and "Not only… but also…".

## 4B — Not only… but also…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4B — Not only… but also…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue narrar com ênfase uma sequência de acontecimentos com inversão
  depois de not only, no sooner… than, hardly… when, little e only when / only then.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2
Gramática-alvo: Not only did she…, but she also…; No sooner had we… than…; Hardly had I… when…; Little did I
  know (that)…; Only when… did I…; Only then did I… (a inversão vai na oração principal: Only when he called
  did I understand.)
Palavras-alvo: copy in, contradict, promise, field, signal, side of the story, frustrated, find out,
  responsible, deadline
Reciclagem: 4A; tempos narrativos (B2, 2)
Pronúncia: o ritmo de uma narrativa com ênfase; o acento em "only" e "little"
Contexto: quarta-feira, 10 de Abril, 17:00; o Nuno ao telefone com o Bongani, que está numa subestação perto
  de Nampula; ruído de vento e de um gerador
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 4B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! When people tell the story of a conflict, they want you to feel how bad it was: "Not only did she do this — she also did that!" Today: inversion for emphatic storytelling — and the other side of the story.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 10 de Abril, 17:00; o Nuno no gabinete; o Bongani ao telefone, de capacete, ao lado de uma subestação, com vento e um gerador ao fundo]

**NUNO:** Bongani, I've had an e-mail from Inês. I'd like to hear your side.
**BONGANI:** My side? Nuno, not only did she write to Dr Cumbe without copying me, but she also promised him substation seven for last Friday. I never said Friday.
**NUNO:** Why did you move the team to substation nine?
**BONGANI:** Because the road to seven was flooded! No sooner had we fixed the access road than it rained again. So I moved the team to nine. That's my job.
**NUNO:** Did you tell Inês?
**BONGANI:** [pausa] I sent a message to the team group.
**NUNO:** Is Inês in that group?
**BONGANI:** …She's in the Maputo group. [suspira] Fine. Little did I know she was telling the client a different plan. Only when Dr Cumbe called me on Saturday did I find out.
**NUNO:** And the "translator" comment?
**BONGANI:** That was a joke! Hardly had I said it when I saw her face. I should have apologised. I didn't.
**NUNO:** What would you like to change?
**BONGANI:** I'm responsible for the deadline. David will review this project. If she promises dates, I'm the one who breaks them. All I want is: no promises without me.
**NUNO:** That's clear. Thank you for being honest, Bongani.
**BONGANI:** [ri-se, cansado] Never have I been so honest on a windy hill.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Estrutura | Exemplo do Bongani | Sentido |
|---|---|---|
| Not only + inversão, but (also)… | Not only did she write to Dr Cumbe without copying me, but she also promised him substation seven. | não só… como também |
| No sooner had + sujeito + particípio + than… | No sooner had we fixed the road than it rained again. | mal… logo |
| Hardly had + sujeito + particípio + when… | Hardly had I said it when I saw her face. | mal… quando |
| Little did I know (that)… | Little did I know she was telling the client a different plan. | mal sabia eu que… |
| Only when… + inversão | Only when Dr Cumbe called me did I find out. | só quando… é que… |

**PROF:** With "only when", the inversion is in the second part — the main clause: "Only when he called did I find out." With "no sooner", use "than"; with "hardly", use "when". Don't mix them.
[PAUSA 7 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ No sooner had we fixed the road when it rained. · ✘ Only when he called I found out. · ✘ Not only she wrote… · ✔ No sooner had we fixed the road than it rained. · ✔ Only when he called did I find out. · ✔ Not only did she write…]
[PAUSA 5 s]

**PROF:** Notice also how Bongani's story changes when Nuno asks one simple question: "Is Inês in that group?" Each side of a conflict usually has a part of the truth.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Stress "only", "little" and "not only", and let the story move quickly. Listen: Little did I know… [acento em "little"] Only when Dr Cumbe called me did I find out. [acento em "only"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Join the sentences with the structure given.
[ECRÃ: He was late. He also forgot the documents. (Not only)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: Not only was he late, but he also forgot the documents.]
[ECRÃ: We sat down. The lights went out. (No sooner)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: No sooner had we sat down than the lights went out.]
[ECRÃ: I read the contract. Then I understood the problem. (Only when)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: Only when I read the contract did I understand the problem.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Tell the story of a day when everything went wrong — at work, on a trip or at home. Use at least three of these: "Not only…", "No sooner… than…", "Hardly… when…", "Little did I know…", "Only when… did I…". About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Last December, I drove to Lubango for a meeting. No sooner had I left Luanda than the car started making a strange noise. Not only did I have to stop in Sumbe, but I also lost my phone in the garage. Little did I know the meeting had already been cancelled — they'd sent an e-mail. Only when I arrived, eleven hours later, did I find out.]

**PROF (opinião):** In a conflict, is it fair to listen to one side first? What are the risks?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 2):** Tell it with the past perfect: "The team left. Then the client arrived."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: When the client arrived, the team had already left.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Not only, no sooner, hardly, little did I know, only when — stories with force. Next lesson: Inês's side — and how to really listen.

## 4C — So what you're saying is…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4C — So what you're saying is…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue ouvir alguém em conflito, reformular a sua posição, descobrir o
  interesse por trás dela e preparar terreno comum.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 3
Gramática-alvo: reformular: So what you're saying is (that)…; What I'm hearing is…; Correct me if I'm wrong,
  but…; If I've understood you correctly, …; ir ao interesse: What would need to change for…? What matters
  most to you here? It sounds as though what really matters to you is…; validar sem concordar: I can see
  why that upset you. That must have been frustrating.
Palavras-alvo: position, interest, upset, frustrating, respect, recognition, reformulate, validate,
  common ground, side
Reciclagem: clivadas (C1, 1: What really matters is…); reformular (C1, 1C); modais no passado (B2, 6)
Pronúncia: tom calmo, ritmo lento e pausas de escuta
Contexto: quinta-feira, 11 de Abril, 9:00; videochamada entre o Nuno e a Inês, em Maputo
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 4C; conversa guiada "Say it differently"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In a conflict, people say what they want — their position. But behind every position, there's a reason — an interest. Today: how to listen, reformulate, and find the interest.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 11 de Abril, 9:00; o Nuno ao computador; a Inês no ecrã, no escritório de Maputo, com o e-mail impresso à frente]

**INÊS:** I suppose Bongani has told you I'm impossible.
**NUNO:** He told me his side. Now I'd like to hear yours — properly. What happened on Friday?
**INÊS:** I told Dr Cumbe that substation seven would be ready. I believed it. Then he went there, and the team was at nine.
**NUNO:** So what you're saying is that you gave the client information you thought was true — and it wasn't.
**INÊS:** Exactly. And it's not the first time.
**NUNO:** That must have been very frustrating.
**INÊS:** Frustrating? I looked like a fool. In Portuguese, in front of the client.
**NUNO:** Bongani says you wrote to Dr Cumbe without copying him.
**INÊS:** Because Dr Cumbe called me! Bongani wasn't answering his phone. What was I supposed to do?
**NUNO:** I can see why you answered. Correct me if I'm wrong, but it sounds as though the real problem isn't the e-mail — it's that you never know what the plan is.
**INÊS:** [pausa] Yes. That's it.
**NUNO:** And the reports? You said only his name is on them.
**INÊS:** I write half of those reports, Nuno. In two languages.
**NUNO:** What I'm hearing is that you want to be seen as an engineer on this project — not as someone who translates for Bongani.
**INÊS:** [mais baixo] I studied electrical engineering for five years. "Our translator." In front of the client.
**NUNO:** I understand. So what would need to change for you to work well with him again?
**INÊS:** Two things. I want to know the plan before the client does. And I want my name on what I do.
**NUNO:** That's very clear. And would it surprise you to know that Bongani also wants to be told the plan before the client?
**INÊS:** [surpreendida] …He said that?
**NUNO:** In other words. I think you two want more of the same things than you realise.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Posição e interesse**

[ECRÃ: Posição (o que a pessoa diz que quer): "Under no circumstances will I give the client dates." · Interesse (porquê): "I want to know the plan before the client does; I want to be respected as an engineer."]

**PROF:** A position is often impossible to accept: "I won't speak to him." An interest is almost always reasonable: "I want to be informed." A good mediator moves the conversation from positions to interests.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Ferramentas**

[ECRÃ:]

| Para… | Língua |
|---|---|
| reformular | So what you're saying is (that)… · What I'm hearing is… · If I've understood you correctly, … |
| verificar | Correct me if I'm wrong, but… · Is that right? |
| validar (sem concordar) | I can see why that upset you. · That must have been frustrating. · I understand. |
| ir ao interesse | What matters most to you here? · What would need to change for…? · It sounds as though what really matters to you is… |
| preparar terreno comum | Would it surprise you to know that…? · You want more of the same things than you realise. |

**PROF:** "Validating" doesn't mean agreeing: "That must have been frustrating" recognises the feeling, without saying who's right. And notice: Nuno doesn't give his opinion once.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ So you're saying that Bongani is terrible. (exagera e toma partido) · ✔ So what you're saying is that you didn't know about the change.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** When you reformulate, slow down, lower your voice a little, and pause after the question. Listen: Correct me if I'm wrong, but… [pausa] it sounds as though the real problem is that you never know the plan.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Reformulate, then ask for the interest.
[ECRÃ: "I'm never doing the night shift again!"] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: So what you're saying is that the night shift has become too much. What would need to change for it to work?]
[ECRÃ: "He always takes the credit for my work."] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: What I'm hearing is that you want your work to be recognised. Is that right?]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. A friend or colleague complains to you about someone. Imagine what they say, and answer them: reformulate, validate the feeling, find the interest, and suggest one point of common ground — without taking sides. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — (The complaint: "My sister never helps with our mother. I do everything.") — So what you're saying is that you're doing almost all the care, and you're exhausted. That must be very hard. Correct me if I'm wrong, but it sounds as though what really matters to you is not doing it alone. What would need to change? Would it surprise you if your sister felt guilty too? Maybe you both want a plan.]

**PROF (opinião):** Can a manager be a good mediator for his or her own team, or is it better to bring in someone from outside? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 6):** Say what Bongani should have done, with a past modal.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: He should have told Inês about the change. / He shouldn't have called her "our translator".]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Reformulate, validate, find the interest, prepare common ground. Try the tutor's "Say it differently". Next lesson: the words of conflict — and two false friends.

## 4D — Conflict and resolution

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4D — Conflict and resolution        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário do conflito e da mediação (a grievance,
  tension, common ground, de-escalate, clear the air, a fair hearing), o tom das conversas delicadas, e
  evitar os falsos amigos disgust e expert.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 3
Gramática-alvo: colocações (raise a grievance, ease / defuse tension, find common ground, de-escalate a
  situation, clear the air, give someone a fair hearing, take sides, hold a grudge, get off on the wrong
  foot, bury the hatchet — só para reconhecer); adjectivos de emoção com precisão (upset, resentful,
  hurt, defensive, fed up)
Palavras-alvo: grievance, tension, common ground, de-escalate, clear the air, fair hearing, take sides,
  resentful, defensive, hurt
Reciclagem: 4A a 4C
Pronúncia: o tom em conversas delicadas: lento, baixo e neutro; evitar a subida irritada
Falsos amigos: disgust (≠ desgosto → sorrow, grief; disgust = nojo, repugnância); expert (≠ esperto →
  clever, smart; expert = especialista, perito)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 4D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words for conflict and for ending it, the tone of voice that calms people down — and two false friends that can make a sad story sound disgusting.

### 2. Conflito e resolução (3 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Expressão | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| a grievance | Inês has two grievances: the schedule and the reports. (uma queixa, um agravo) |
| tension | There's a lot of tension between them. |
| defuse / ease tension | A joke can defuse the tension — or make it worse. (desarmar, aliviar) |
| de-escalate | Nuno's job is to de-escalate the situation. (fazer baixar a tensão) |
| common ground | They have more common ground than they think. (terreno comum) |
| clear the air | They need a meeting to clear the air. (falar abertamente para desanuviar) |
| a fair hearing | Each of them deserves a fair hearing. (ser ouvido com justiça) |
| take sides | A good mediator doesn't take sides. (tomar partido) |
| hold a grudge | Bongani doesn't hold grudges. (guardar rancor) |

**PROF:** Repeat: a grievance. Tension. De-escalate. Common ground. Clear the air. A fair hearing. Take sides.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: **disgust** ≠ desgosto]

## 4E — Real English: Two sides of the story

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4E — Two sides of the story        Tipo: Real English
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue ouvir duas versões do mesmo conflito, contadas separadamente
  por duas pessoas, e perceber onde discordam, onde concordam sem o saberem, e o que cada uma quer de facto.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 3
Língua: A1 ao B2 e as unidades 1 a 4 do C1 até à 4D, sem estruturas novas
Vozes: Precious (sul-africana, apresentadora de um podcast, calorosa), Ayanda (sul-africana, sócia do Fynbos
  Design, rápida e intensa), Marco (moçambicano, sócio, calmo e irónico, com algumas palavras em português)
Velocidade: natural, com risos, hesitações e pausas emocionais
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 4E
```

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

**PROF:** On Monday morning, in the car, Nuno listens to a podcast called "Two Sides". Each week, two people in a conflict tell their story — separately. This week: the two partners of a small design studio in Cape Town. Listen for what each one wants — and what they both want without knowing it.

### 2. Primeira escuta: as duas versões (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 15 de Abril; "Two Sides" — um podcast (fictício); um estúdio pequeno com dois microfones]

**PROF:** First: what is the conflict about, on the surface?

**PRECIOUS:** Welcome to Two Sides. Today, Ayanda and Marco, who started Fynbos Design together six years ago. They haven't agreed on anything for three months. Ayanda first — Marco can't hear us.
**AYANDA:** So. We have more work than ever. Never have we had so many clients. I want to hire two designers. Marco says no. Not only does he say no, but he also refuses to discuss it. Every Saturday, I'm in the office until eight. I'm exhausted.
**PRECIOUS:** Why do you think he says no?
**AYANDA:** Honestly? He doesn't want to share control. It's his baby. Little did I know, when we started, that "partners" meant "Marco decides".
**PRECIOUS:** Thank you, Ayanda. Marco, your turn.
**MARCO:** [ri-se] I can imagine what she said. Look — I don't want to hire, because two years ago we hired three people, and six months later we had to let two go. Nunca mais. Never again. I was the one who told them. Under no circumstances will I do that again.
**PRECIOUS:** So it's about money?
**MARCO:** It's about risk. We have many clients now, but three of them are on short contracts. If they leave, what do we do with two new designers?
**PRECIOUS:** Ayanda says she works every Saturday.
**MARCO:** [pausa] She does. So do I — at home, at night. My daughter asks why I'm always on the laptop. Only when she drew a picture of me with a computer for a head did I realise how bad it was.
**PRECIOUS:** So you're tired too.
**MARCO:** We're both tired. But she thinks hiring is the answer, and I think hiring is the danger.
**PRECIOUS:** Last question, for both of you separately: what would a good year look like? Ayanda said: "No more Saturdays." Marco?
**MARCO:** [sem hesitar] Free weekends. And no one losing their job.

[ECRÃ: Na superfície: contratar ou não contratar · Ayanda: quer contratar; por trás: está exausta e sente que não tem voz · Marco: não quer contratar; por trás: tem medo de despedir pessoas outra vez (já o fez) · O que os dois querem sem o saberem: fins-de-semana livres; e o Marco também quer que ninguém perca o emprego]

### 3. Segunda escuta: os pormenores (3 min)

1. How long ago did Ayanda and Marco start Fynbos Design? (six years ago ✓ / three months ago / two years ago)
2. How many designers does Ayanda want to hire? (two ✓ / three / six)
3. Why does Ayanda think Marco says no? (He doesn't want to share control. ✓ / He has no money. / He wants to retire.)
4. What happened two years ago? (They hired three people and had to let two go. ✓ / They lost all their clients. / Marco left the company.)
5. When did Marco realise how bad his working hours were? (when his daughter drew him with a computer for a head ✓ / when Ayanda complained / when a client left)
6. What do both partners want for next year? (free weekends ✓ / more clients / a bigger office)

*(Cada resposta repete-se; depois aparecem as perguntas, com 8 s para responder.)*

### 4. Terceira escuta: como disseram (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Listen and repeat.
**PROF:** Not only does he say no, but he also refuses to discuss it. [PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** Under no circumstances will I do that again. [PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** Only when she drew a picture of me did I realise how bad it was. [PAUSA 6 s]

**PROF:** Did you notice? Each one guesses the other's reason — and guesses wrong. Ayanda thinks it's control; it's fear of firing people again. And they agree on the most important thing: neither wants to work at weekends. They just never said it to each other.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. A sua vez (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Now you. You're Precious, and next week Ayanda and Marco will be in the same room. Prepare what you'll say to open the conversation: reformulate each side fairly, name their common ground, and suggest one option that answers both interests. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Ayanda, what I heard is that you're exhausted and you want your voice in decisions. Marco, what I heard is that you never want to let people go again. Would it surprise you both to know that you gave me the same answer: free weekends? So the question isn't "hire or not hire". It's "how do we get our weekends back safely?" **PROF (opinião):** Why do you think people in a conflict so often guess the other person's reason wrongly?
[PAUSA 25 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

## 4F — Work & Life: Clearing the air

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 4        Aula: 4F — Clearing the air        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue mediar um conflito entre duas pessoas até um acordo de trabalho:
  regras da conversa, cada lado ouvido, reformulação, interesses comuns, opções e um acordo concreto; e
  passar a um dos lados, em inglês, o que o outro disse em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2, 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: inversão negativa (com moderação), reformular, validar, interesses,
  terreno comum; propor (What if we…? How would you feel about…?) e fechar (So, to sum up, we've agreed…)
Palavras-alvo: ground rules, interrupt, apologise, agreement, check-in call, in copy, sign off, credit,
  common ground, clear the air
Reciclagem: clivadas (C1, 1); diplomacia (C1, 2); condicionais (C1, 3)
Pronúncia: tom calmo e neutro do mediador; um pedido de desculpas sincero (descendente, sem "but")
Mediação: a Inês, emocionada, passa ao português; o Nuno transmite ao Bongani, em inglês, o que ela disse
Contexto: terça-feira, 23 de Abril, 9:00 (depois da Páscoa); videochamada a três: o Nuno em Joanesburgo,
  a Inês em Maputo, o Bongani num contentor-escritório em Nampula
Duração: 14 min
Verificação: Lab 4F; conversa livre "The mediator"
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! The big conversation of the unit. Nuno has heard both sides. Now Inês and Bongani are in the same call — and the client is waiting for three more substations. Watch how Nuno clears the air without taking sides.

### 2. Situação (6 min)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 23 de Abril, 9:00; três janelas no ecrã: o Nuno em Joanesburgo, a Inês em Maputo, o Bongani em Nampula, num contentor com uma ventoinha]

**NUNO:** Thank you both. Before we start, three ground rules. One: each person speaks without being interrupted. Two: we talk about what happened, not about what kind of person anyone is. Three: we don't finish without an agreement. OK?
**INÊS:** OK.
**BONGANI:** Fine.
**NUNO:** I've spoken to each of you, and I won't decide who's right. Inês, would you start?
**INÊS:** On the fifth, I told the client substation seven would be ready. It wasn't. I didn't know the team had moved. It isn't the first time.
**NUNO:** Thank you. Bongani?
**BONGANI:** The road to seven flooded, so I moved the team. I posted it in the team group. I didn't check that Inês was in it. That was my mistake. But she promised the client a date I never gave.
**NUNO:** So what I'm hearing from both of you is the same thing: each of you found out about a decision after the client did.
**BONGANI:** [pausa] I suppose so.
**NUNO:** Inês, there was something else.
**INÊS:** [respira] The reports. And… [passa ao português, emocionada] Eu não sou tradutora. Estudei engenharia electrotécnica cinco anos. Quando o cliente me pergunta uma data, eu tenho de responder alguma coisa — não posso dizer "não sei, pergunte ao Bongani".
**BONGANI:** Nuno?
**NUNO:** She says she isn't a translator — she studied electrical engineering for five years. And when the client asks her for a date, she has to say something; she can't just say "I don't know, ask Bongani".
**BONGANI:** [longa pausa] Inês… the "translator" thing was a stupid joke. I'm sorry. It was wrong, and I said it in front of the client. I apologise.
**INÊS:** [surpreendida] Thank you. [pausa] And I'm sorry I wrote to Dr Cumbe without copying you. I was angry.
**NUNO:** Thank you both. That took courage. Now — what do you both need? Bongani, you said you want no promises without you.
**BONGANI:** Yes.
**NUNO:** Inês, you want to know the plan before the client, and your name on your work.
**INÊS:** Yes.
**NUNO:** Correct me if I'm wrong, but those aren't opposites. What if the two of you had a fifteen-minute call every Monday and Thursday, before the client calls — and no date goes to the client unless you both agree?
**BONGANI:** Twice a week? From a hill with no signal?
**NUNO:** Fifteen minutes. How would you feel about that, if Inês sends you the client's questions the day before?
**BONGANI:** …I can do that. [pausa] And she should be the one who talks to Mussiro in Portuguese. She's better with them than I am. Much better.
**INÊS:** Then I'll copy you on everything — in English.
**NUNO:** And the Friday reports?
**BONGANI:** Both names. And Inês presents the handover. She knows Dr Cumbe.
**INÊS:** With you. You built the substations.
**NUNO:** So, to sum up, we've agreed: a fifteen-minute call on Mondays and Thursdays; no date to the client without both of you; Inês speaks to Mussiro in Portuguese and copies Bongani in English; both names on the reports; and you present the handover together. I'll send it in writing today. Agreed?
**INÊS e BONGANI:** Agreed.
