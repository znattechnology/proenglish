# C1.2 · Unidade 7 — Science and change · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2, o C1.1 e a unidade 6. Novo: o futuro no passado (*was / were going to, was about to, was to have + particípio, would* — o futuro visto do passado: *We knew it would take time.*; *was supposed to* revisto do B2 6); o futuro próximo e provável com nuance (*be on the verge of, be set to, be due to, be about to; be bound to* revisto do B2 5); explicar ideias complexas a quem não é especialista (*Think of it as… It works a bit like… Put simply, … In other words, … Does that make sense? Let me put it another way.*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** o *fronting* e os marcadores *mind you, that said, as it happens* (unidade 8); as expressões idiomáticas e a linguagem vaga informal (*give or take, -ish, or so, kind of*, unidade 9). Na 7D, as formas reduzidas (*gonna, wanna*) ensinam-se para reconhecer; só se produzem no registo informal.

**Nomes, empresas e factos:** a Marula Power, a aldeia de Mokgalo, a revista "Energia & Rede" e a central com baterias descrita no artigo da 7F são fictícias, como as personagens. A província do Limpopo existe. Os factos técnicos estão simplificados de forma correcta: as baterias de lítio-ferro-fosfato (LFP) são das que menos risco de incêndio têm entre as baterias de lítio; uma instalação de armazenagem guarda energia solar do dia para a usar ao fim da tarde e à noite. A investigação da 7E (baterias de sódio) é um campo real; a descoberta e a investigadora são fictícias.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**, **Thandi**, **Lindiwe** (engenheira da Mopane), **Rajesh**. Novos e fictícios: **Mrs Mmapula Seleka** (presidente do comité da aldeia de Mokgalo), **Mr Tebogo Maake** (agricultor), **Ms Kgomotso Ledwaba** (professora), e dois dos seis técnicos formados, **Karabo** e **Dineo**; na 7E, **Lwazi** (apresentador de rádio) e **Dr Nomvula Khoza** (investigadora numa universidade).

**A história:** Agosto de 2030. A Marula Power, que já tem uma central solar perto da aldeia de Mokgalo, no Limpopo, está a construir ao lado uma instalação de armazenagem em baterias; a Mopane faz o sistema de monitorização e comunicações e forma seis técnicos locais no centro de formação. A instalação devia ter ficado pronta em Junho; está agora prevista para Outubro. Correm rumores na aldeia (incêndios, ruído, contas mais caras), e a reunião com a comunidade, que devia ter sido em Abril, nunca se fez. Na segunda, 5, a Thandi e o Nuno olham para o que estava planeado (7A); na quarta, 7, a Lindiwe fala do que aí vem na tecnologia (7B); na sexta, 9, ensina o Nuno a explicar baterias sem jargão (7C); na segunda, 12, o Nuno ouve um programa de ciência na rádio (7E); na quinta, 22, explica tudo à aldeia (7F). Entretanto, a proposta do Niassa deve ser entregue a 30 de Agosto (unidade 6).

**Os factos da instalação (iguais em todas as aulas):**
- Capacidade: guarda energia para manter as luzes acesas em cerca de 4 000 casas durante quatro horas, ao fim da tarde.
- Tipo: baterias LFP (lítio-ferro-fosfato), em 16 contentores, com sensores de temperatura e sistema de extinção em cada um.
- Distância: a cerca de 2 km das primeiras casas de Mokgalo.
- Ruído: só as ventoinhas de arrefecimento — a 100 metros, mais ou menos como um frigorífico na cozinha.
- Empregos: seis técnicos permanentes da região, formados no centro de formação da Mopane.
- Vida útil: cerca de 15 anos; no fim, o fornecedor recolhe e recicla as baterias (está no contrato).
- O que não muda: o preço da electricidade (decidido pela empresa distribuidora e pelo regulador). O que muda: menos cortes ao fim da tarde.

## 7A — We were going to…

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 7        Aula: 7A — We were going to…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar de planos e previsões do passado que se cumpriram ou não,
  com o futuro no passado (was going to, was about to, was to have + particípio, would).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: was / were going to (plano do passado, muitas vezes não cumprido); was about to (prestes a,
  no passado); was to have + particípio (formal: o que estava previsto e não aconteceu: The facility was to
  have opened in June.); would (o futuro visto do passado: We knew it would take time.); was supposed to
  (revisto do B2 6); contraste com o simple past
Palavras-alvo: facility, battery storage, behind, postpone, rumour, community, village, containers, supplier,
  plan
Reciclagem: was supposed to (B2, 6); passivas de relato (C1, 5B); elipse (C1, 6A)
Pronúncia: as formas reduzidas "was going to" ("wuz gonna", para reconhecer) e o acento em "was" quando o
  plano falhou (It WAS going to… — marcado por indicação)
Contexto: segunda-feira, 5 de Agosto de 2030, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; o calendário do projecto da Marula
  Power no ecrã
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 7A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 7
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to unit 7: Science and change. Projects rarely go exactly as planned — and to talk about them, we need the future seen from the past: "We were going to…", "It was to have opened in June…". Today: the future in the past.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 5 de Agosto, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; no ecrã, o calendário do projecto: "Marula Power — battery storage, Mokgalo, Limpopo"; várias datas riscadas a vermelho]

**THANDI:** The Marula battery project. Where are we?
**NUNO:** Behind. The facility was to have opened in June. It's now October.
**THANDI:** Why?
**NUNO:** The supplier was going to ship the containers in March. They shipped them in May. And our monitoring system was about to be installed when the client changed the control software.
**THANDI:** And the community meeting?
**NUNO:** That's the real problem. We were going to hold a meeting in Mokgalo in April. Marula postponed it — they thought it would be easier to explain once the facility was finished.
**THANDI:** And it wasn't.
**NUNO:** No. And now there are rumours. The batteries are said to catch fire easily. The noise is going to be terrible. Electricity bills are going to double.
**THANDI:** None of which is true.
**NUNO:** None of which is true. But nobody has told them that.
**THANDI:** That meeting was supposed to be Marula's job.
**NUNO:** It was. But they've asked us to do it — we know the technology, and our six trainees are from the area.
**THANDI:** When?
**NUNO:** The twenty-second. I was going to suggest the end of the month, but the longer we wait, the bigger the rumours.
**THANDI:** Good. I knew this project would surprise us. I didn't think it would be the people, not the batteries.
**NUNO:** It usually is.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**O futuro visto do passado**

[ECRÃ:]

| Forma | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| was / were going to | um plano do passado (muitas vezes não cumprido) | The supplier **was going to** ship the containers in March. (mas não enviou) |
| was about to | estava prestes a | Our system **was about to** be installed when the client changed the software. |
| was to have + particípio | formal: estava previsto, mas não aconteceu | The facility **was to have opened** in June. |
| would | o futuro visto do passado | They thought it **would** be easier. · I knew this project **would** surprise us. |
| was supposed to | era suposto (B2) | That meeting **was supposed to** be Marula's job. |

**PROF:** "Was going to" is the everyday form: "I was going to call you" — and I didn't. "Was to have opened" is formal, typical of reports: the plan existed, and it failed. "Would" shows what someone expected at that moment in the past.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Com interrupção**

[ECRÃ: We **were about to** sign **when** the client called. · I **was going to** suggest the end of the month, **but** the rumours were growing.]

**PROF:** These forms often come with "when" or "but": the plan, and what changed it.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ The facility was to open in June, but it didn't. (possível, mas menos claro) · ✔ The facility was to have opened in June. · ✘ I knew it will be difficult. · ✔ I knew it would be difficult.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In fast speech, "was going to" sounds like "wuz gonna". Recognise it — but say the full form in formal situations. Listen: We were going to hold a meeting in April. ["we wuh gonna"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** And when the plan failed, stress "was": It was going to be easy. [acento em "was": "e não foi"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Say what was planned, and what happened.
[ECRÃ: plan: visit Benguela in June · reality: no time] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I was going to visit Benguela in June, but I didn't have time.]
[ECRÃ: plan (formal): new hospital opens in 2029 · reality: 2031] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: The new hospital was to have opened in 2029. It's now expected in 2031.]
[ECRÃ: almost leaving · phone rang] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I was about to leave when the phone rang.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Tell the story of a plan that changed — a project, a trip, a building in your town, a decision in your life. What was going to happen, what was supposed to happen, what you thought would happen — and what actually happened. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — The new road to my village was to have opened in 2027. The company was going to finish it in two years, and everyone thought it would change our lives. Then the money ran out. Work stopped just when they were about to start the bridge. It finally opened last year — four years late. And it did change our lives: my mother's trip to the hospital went from three hours to one.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should a company talk to a community before a project starts, even when it has nothing definite to say? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 5B):** Report the rumour with a passive: "People say the batteries catch fire easily."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The batteries are said to catch fire easily.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! Was going to, was about to, was to have opened, would — the future seen from the past. Next lesson: what's coming — "on the verge of", "set to", "due to".

## 7B — On the verge of…

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 7        Aula: 7B — On the verge of…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar do futuro próximo e provável com nuance (be on the verge
  of, be set to, be due to, be about to; be bound to revisto) e distinguir o que está marcado, o que é
  provável e o que está iminente.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 4
Gramática-alvo: be due to + infinitivo (marcado num calendário: The facility is due to open in October.);
  be set to + infinitivo (muito provável, já decidido ou em curso: Prices are set to fall.); be about to
  (iminente); be on the verge of + nome / -ing (muito perto de algo importante: on the verge of a
  breakthrough); be bound to (quase certo, revisto do B2 5); be likely / unlikely to
Palavras-alvo: breakthrough, research, salt, price, lithium, sodium, trainees, regulator, cheaper,
  on the market
Reciclagem: graus de certeza (C1, 2B); 7A
Pronúncia: o acento em "verge", "set" e "due"
Contexto: quarta-feira, 7 de Agosto, 13:00; a cantina da Mopane; a Lindiwe e o Nuno ao almoço, com um artigo
  de tecnologia no telemóvel
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 7B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! "Will" is a blunt tool for the future. Is something scheduled, very likely, or just about to happen? Today: "due to", "set to", "about to", "on the verge of" — the future with precision.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 7 de Agosto, 13:00; a cantina; a Lindiwe com o telemóvel; o Nuno com um prato de pap e um guisado]

**LINDIWE:** Look at this. Battery prices are set to fall another fifteen percent next year.
**NUNO:** Good for us. When's the Marula facility due to open now?
**LINDIWE:** It's due to open on the fifteenth of October. If the software works.
**NUNO:** "If". And what's this about sodium?
**LINDIWE:** Some labs are on the verge of a breakthrough with sodium batteries. Sodium is everywhere — it's in salt. No lithium needed.
**NUNO:** So should Marula wait for sodium?
**LINDIWE:** No. Nothing on the verge of a breakthrough is on the market. Sodium batteries for big facilities are unlikely to be common for years. Lithium iron phosphate is what works today.
**NUNO:** And the people in Mokgalo — what will they ask me?
**LINDIWE:** They're bound to ask about fire. Everyone does. And they're likely to ask about jobs.
**NUNO:** And the price of electricity.
**LINDIWE:** That one's harder. The facility won't make their bills cheaper — prices are decided by the utility and the regulator. Don't promise what isn't yours to promise.
**NUNO:** [sorri] I've heard that advice before.
**LINDIWE:** And the six trainees? They're about to finish the course. They could come to the meeting.
**NUNO:** That's a great idea. People trust their neighbours more than engineers from Joburg.
**LINDIWE:** Hey! I'm an engineer from Joburg.
**NUNO:** Exactly.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Forma | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| be due to | marcado num calendário | The facility **is due to open** on 15 October. |
| be set to | muito provável; já em curso | Battery prices **are set to fall** another 15%. |
| be about to | iminente (minutos, dias) | The trainees **are about to finish** the course. |
| be on the verge of + nome / -ing | muito perto de algo importante | Labs **are on the verge of a breakthrough**. |
| be bound to | quase certo (B2) | They**'re bound to ask** about fire. |
| be likely / unlikely to | provável / improvável | Sodium batteries **are unlikely to be** common for years. |

**PROF:** Choose by the kind of certainty. "Due to": the calendar says so. "Set to": everything points that way. "About to": any moment now. "On the verge of": something big is very close — but hasn't happened.
[PAUSA 7 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ We are on the verge to finish. · ✘ The plane is due at land at six. · ✔ We are on the verge of finishing. · ✔ The plane is due to land at six.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

**PROF:** Watch the grammar: "on the verge of" takes a noun or "-ing"; the others take the infinitive with "to".
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Stress the key word. Listen: It's due to open in October. [acento em "due"] Prices are set to fall. [acento em "set"] They're on the verge of a breakthrough. [acento em "verge"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat all three.
[PAUSA 10 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Choose the best form.
[ECRÃ: The train / leave at 7:40 (timetable)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: The train is due to leave at 7:40.]
[ECRÃ: Food prices / rise (all the signs say so)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Food prices are set to rise.]
[ECRÃ: Doctors / a new treatment (very close, not ready)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Doctors are on the verge of a new treatment.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Talk about the near future of your field, your town or a technology you care about: one thing that's due to happen, one that's set to happen, one that's about to happen, and one breakthrough people are on the verge of. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — In my town, the new water treatment plant is due to open in March. Water bills are set to rise a little, because of the investment. A new bus company is about to start — the buses arrived last week. And in medicine, researchers are said to be on the verge of a cheaper malaria vaccine. If that happens, it's bound to change life in our province more than anything else on this list.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should we wait for better technology, or use the technology we have now? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 2B):** Say it with a degree of certainty: "Sodium batteries will be common in big facilities soon."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: I'd be surprised if sodium batteries were common in big facilities soon.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Due to, set to, about to, on the verge of — the future with precision. Next lesson: how to explain a complex idea to someone who isn't an expert.

## 7C — Think of it as…

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 7        Aula: 7C — Think of it as…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue explicar uma ideia técnica ou científica a quem não é
  especialista: com uma analogia, uma definição simples, um exemplo e uma verificação.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: analogias (Think of it as… · It works a bit like… · It's similar to…, except that…);
  simplificar (Put simply, … · In other words, … · Basically, …); definir (A battery facility is a place
  where… · What it does is…); verificar (Does that make sense? · Let me put it another way. · What would
  you like me to explain again?); evitar o jargão e explicá-lo quando é preciso (what engineers call…)
Palavras-alvo: analogy, jargon, explain, tank, evening, peak, smoke alarm, sensor, container, expert
Reciclagem: clivadas (C1, 1: What it does is…); 7A e 7B
Pronúncia: o ritmo de quem explica: frases curtas, pausas, acento na palavra nova
Contexto: sexta-feira, 9 de Agosto, 16:00; a sala de reuniões; a Lindiwe treina o Nuno; o Rajesh faz de
  morador
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 7C; conversa guiada "Explain it to me"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Experts often explain things in a way only other experts understand. Today: how to explain a complex idea to anyone — with an analogy, a simple definition, an example, and a check.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 9 de Agosto, 16:00; a sala de reuniões; a Lindiwe ao quadro; o Rajesh sentado, de braços cruzados, a fazer de morador; o Nuno de pé]

**LINDIWE:** OK, Rajesh is a resident of Mokgalo. Nuno, explain what the facility does.
**NUNO:** Right. The facility is a sixteen-container lithium iron phosphate battery energy storage system that charges from the PV plant during off-peak…
**RAJESH:** [interrompe] Sorry — a what? From the what?
**LINDIWE:** Stop. Lost him in ten words. Try again — start with something he knows.
**NUNO:** OK. Think of it as a water tank on your roof. During the day, the sun fills the tank. In the evening, when everyone gets home and switches on the lights and the kettle, you use what's in the tank.
**RAJESH:** Ah. So the "tank" is the batteries.
**NUNO:** Exactly. Put simply, the solar plant makes electricity during the day, and the batteries keep some of it for the evening.
**RAJESH:** Why the evening?
**NUNO:** That's when people use the most — what engineers call "the peak". And that's when the power cuts happen. So: fewer cuts in the evening. Does that make sense?
**RAJESH:** It does. But are they dangerous? I've heard batteries explode.
**NUNO:** Some batteries can. These are a type that's much less likely to catch fire — and each container has sensors, a bit like the smoke alarm in a kitchen, except that it also puts out the fire.
**RAJESH:** And if all sixteen catch fire?
**LINDIWE:** [ri-se] Good question, resident.
**NUNO:** Let me put it another way. Each container is separate, with its own sensors and its own system. A problem in one doesn't spread to the others. And they're two kilometres from the first houses.
**LINDIWE:** Much better. What did you do differently?
**NUNO:** I stopped talking to engineers.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Passo | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Uma analogia com algo conhecido | Think of it as… · It works a bit like… · …except that… | Think of it as a water tank on your roof. |
| 2. Uma definição simples | Put simply, … · Basically, … · What it does is… | Put simply, the batteries keep some of the electricity for the evening. |
| 3. O jargão, só quando é preciso, e explicado | what engineers call… | …what engineers call "the peak". |
| 4. Um exemplo concreto | …when everyone gets home and switches on the kettle | — |
| 5. Verificar | Does that make sense? · Let me put it another way. | — |

**PROF:** Notice Nuno's first attempt: correct, and useless. Fourteen technical words before the verb. The second: one picture — a water tank — and everything else follows from it. And "except that" fixes the limits of the analogy: smoke alarms don't put out fires; these sensors do.
[PAUSA 7 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ Think it as a tank. · ✘ It works a bit like as a tank. · ✔ Think of it as a tank. · ✔ It works a bit like a tank.]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** When you explain, use short sentences and pauses, and stress the new word. Listen: Think of it as a water tank. [pausa] During the day, the sun fills the tank. [pausa] In the evening, you use what's in it.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Explain with an analogy.
[ECRÃ: a vaccine] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: Think of it as a training exercise for your body: it learns to recognise the disease before it meets the real one.]
[ECRÃ: a firewall (computers)] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: It works a bit like a security guard at the door of a building: it checks who comes in and who goes out.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Explain something complex from your job, your studies or your daily life to a twelve-year-old: one analogy, one simple definition, one example, and a check at the end. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I work in accounting. Think of a company's accounts as a family's notebook of money: everything that comes in, and everything that goes out. Put simply, my job is to make sure the notebook tells the truth. For example, if the company buys a new car, I write it down — and I also write that the car is worth a little less every year. Does that make sense? Let me put it another way: I'm the person who makes sure nobody lies with numbers.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it the expert's job to explain, or the listener's job to learn? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 1):** Put the spotlight on what the facility does: "The facility keeps solar energy for the evening."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: What the facility does is keep solar energy for the evening.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! An analogy, a simple definition, an example, a check — and jargon only when you explain it. Try the tutor's "Explain it to me". Next lesson: the words of energy and science — and two false friends.

## 7D — Energy and science

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 7        Aula: 7D — Energy and science        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário da transição energética e da ciência
  (storage, grid, capacity, emissions, breakthrough, peer-reviewed), reconhecer a fala ligada rápida
  (gonna, wanna, didja), e evitar os falsos amigos experience e physician.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: colocações (store / release energy, connect to the grid, reduce / cut emissions, carry out
  research, a peer-reviewed study, a major breakthrough, a pilot project, renewable energy, fossil fuels,
  the energy transition); verbos de ciência (test, prove, measure, find, confirm)
Palavras-alvo: storage, grid, capacity, emissions, renewable, fossil fuels, breakthrough, peer-reviewed,
  experiment, findings, measure, the energy transition
Reciclagem: 7A a 7C
Pronúncia: a fala ligada rápida — want to → wanna, going to → gonna, did you → didja, don't know → dunno
  (reconhecer; produzir só no registo informal)
Falsos amigos: experience (≠ experiência científica → experiment; experience = experiência de vida, prática);
  physician (≠ físico → physicist; physician = médico)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 7D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of energy and science, the fast, connected speech you'll hear from native speakers — and two false friends: one for the laboratory, and one for the doctor's surgery.

### 2. Energia e ciência (3 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Expressão | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| storage · store / release energy | The facility stores energy in the day and releases it in the evening. (armazenagem) |
| the grid · connect to the grid | Mokgalo is connected to the national grid. (a rede eléctrica) |
| capacity | The facility's capacity is enough for about 4,000 homes for four hours. |
| renewable energy · fossil fuels | Renewables are replacing fossil fuels. (renováveis / combustíveis fósseis) |
| emissions · cut emissions | The diesel generators produced a lot of emissions. |
| the energy transition | Batteries are a key part of the energy transition. (a transição energética) |
| carry out research · findings | The team carried out research for five years. Their findings are surprising. (resultados) |
| a peer-reviewed study | a study checked by other scientists before publication (revisto por pares) |
| a breakthrough | a major breakthrough in sodium batteries (um avanço decisivo) |

**PROF:** Repeat: storage. The grid. Capacity. Emissions. Breakthrough. Peer-reviewed.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ make research · ✔ carry out / do research · ✘ a research (contável) · ✔ some research / a study]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 3. Verbos de ciência (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: test (a hypothesis) · measure (the temperature) · find (that…) · prove (that…) · confirm (the results)]

[PAUSA 5 s]

[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Listen again and say the full form.
[PAUSA 10 s]
**PROF:** Use the reduced forms only with friends. In a meeting, at a community meeting, in an exam — the full forms.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (2 min)

[ECRÃ: **experience** ≠ experiência (científica)]

**PROF:** "Experience" means what you have lived or learned: "ten years of experience", "a great experience". A scientific test is an "experiment": "The lab carried out an experiment."
(PT: "Fizemos uma experiência no laboratório" → "We did an experiment in the lab". "Tenho experiência em baterias" → "I have experience with batteries".)
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **physician** ≠ físico]

**PROF:** A "physician" is a doctor of medicine, especially in American English. A scientist who studies physics is a "physicist".
(PT: "Ela é física" (cientista) → "She's a physicist". "Physician" = médico. E "físico", no sentido do corpo, é "physical": "exercício físico" → "physical exercise".)
[PAUSA 5 s]

**PROF:** Complete.
[ECRÃ: The students did an … to test the new battery.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: experiment]
[ECRÃ: Ela é física nuclear. → She's a nuclear …] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: physicist]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Describe one change in energy, technology or science that you've seen in your country in the last ten years. Use at least five words from today — and one verb of science used carefully (finds, suggests, shows). About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

### 2. Primeira escuta: o programa (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 12 de Agosto, 7:30; um estúdio de rádio; "The Science Slot" (fictício); o apresentador com auscultadores; a investigadora ao telefone, a partir de um laboratório]

**PROF:** First: what is the discovery, in one sentence?

**LWAZI:** Good morning, and welcome to The Science Slot. Today: batteries made of… salt? Dr Nomvula Khoza, are you gonna tell me I can power my house with the stuff on my dinner table?

**KHOZA:** People did try. The problem was that sodium batteries wore out too quickly. Think of it as a sponge that tears a little every time you squeeze it. After a few hundred uses, it doesn't hold water any more.
**LWAZI:** And you've fixed the sponge.

**LWAZI:** How long, then?
**KHOZA:** I'd be surprised if sodium batteries for big facilities were common before the end of the decade. When I started this research, in 2024, I thought we'd be there by now. I was wrong.
**LWAZI:** A scientist who admits she was wrong. Are you sure you're a scientist?
**KHOZA:** [ri-se] That's the whole job.
**LWAZI:** Last question. Should people wait for sodium before they buy solar batteries?
**KHOZA:** Absolutely not. The batteries we have today work. Waiting for the perfect technology is a way of never starting.
**LWAZI:** Dr Nomvula Khoza, thank you. Salt — who knew?
