Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md). Esta unidade tem 7 aulas (10A a 10G). É a última unidade do curso.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao C1. Novo: preparar uma negociação (interesses e posições, *our walk-away point*, a melhor alternativa, concessões e a ordem); desbloquear impasses e responder a tácticas de pressão (*We seem to have reached an impasse. **A história:** Novembro de 2030. A Mopane ganhou o concurso do Niassa (anúncio a 31 de Outubro), com uma condição: a manutenção das subestações e da nova central solar que a Morro Azul vai construir no Niassa — a primeira da Morro Azul em Moçambique — fica num só **contrato regional de cinco anos**, negociado pelas três empresas. ## 10A — Interests, not positions

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10A — Interests, not positions        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue preparar uma negociação complexa: separar posições de interesses,
  definir o ponto de saída e a melhor alternativa, listar concessões possíveis e decidir a ordem.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: Their position is… but their interest is…; Our walk-away point is…; If there's no deal, our
  best alternative is…; What we can trade is…; We'll open with…, and keep… in reserve; I'd rather concede
  on… than on…; condicionais e cenários revistos (C1, 3)
Palavras-alvo: position, interest, walk-away point, alternative, concession, trade, open with, in reserve,
  budget, volume
Reciclagem: posições e interesses (C1, 4C); cenários e condicionais (C1, 3); clivadas (C1, 1)
Pronúncia: o acento nos contrastes (em "position" e em "interest" — marcado por indicação, sem maiúsculas)
Contexto: segunda-feira, 4 de Novembro de 2030, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; a carta da Mussiro com o
  resultado do concurso; o Rajesh com a folha de cálculo
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 10A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 10
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to unit 10 — the last unit of the course. Everything you've learned comes together in one negotiation, with three companies and two languages. Today: how to prepare — interests, not positions.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 4 de Novembro, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; uma carta da Mussiro em cima da mesa; o Rajesh com o portátil; o Nuno de pé, a sorrir e preocupado ao mesmo tempo]

**THANDI:** We won Niassa. Congratulations. Now the hard part.
**NUNO:** One maintenance contract, five years, for Mussiro's substations and Morro Azul's new solar plant. Three companies at one table.
**RAJESH:** And three different wish lists. They want nine hundred thousand a year. We proposed one point one million.
**THANDI:** That's their position. What's their interest?
**NUNO:** Mussiro needs things to work — and local jobs. The provincial government is watching. Part of their budget is in meticais, so they want to pay part in meticais.
**THANDI:** And Morro Azul?
**NUNO:** It's their first plant in Mozambique. They want to look good, and they want it cheap. Femi will fight on price; Ingrid will fight on response times.
**THANDI:** And us — what do we really need?
**RAJESH:** Volume for five years, protection from the currency, and no promises the roads in Niassa won't let us keep.
**THANDI:** Good. What's our walk-away point?
**RAJESH:** Nine hundred and fifty thousand a year. Below that, we lose money in Niassa. And never penalties without a cap.
**THANDI:** And if there's no deal at all?
**NUNO:** Our best alternative is what we already have: the Niassa installation. Two point four million. Not bad — just not five years.
**THANDI:** So we can walk away. That's what makes us calm. What can we trade?
**NUNO:** Response time — four hours in Nampula now, Niassa later. Local technicians — more, but over time. And something they don't expect: we train twenty of their technicians in Joburg in year one.
**RAJESH:** Twenty places is a third of next year's courses — if the board confirms the hub in December.
**NUNO:** After this contract, I think it will.
**THANDI:** Keep that in reserve. Don't open with your best card.
**NUNO:** I'd rather concede on time than on the cap. And I'd rather give them training than price.
**THANDI:** Then that's the order. Price last.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Para… | Língua | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| separar posição e interesse | Their position is… but their interest is… | Their position is nine hundred thousand; their interest is reliability and local jobs. |
| o limite | Our walk-away point is… · Below that, … | Our walk-away point is nine hundred and fifty thousand. |
| a alternativa | If there's no deal, our best alternative is… | …the Niassa installation. |
| o que se pode trocar | What we can trade is… · We could offer… in exchange for… | What we can trade is response time. |
| a ordem | We'll open with… · Keep… in reserve. · Price last. | Keep the training in reserve. |
| as prioridades | I'd rather concede on… than on… | I'd rather concede on time than on the cap. |

**PROF:** Three ideas carry this lesson. First, positions are what people ask for; interests are why. Second, the walk-away point and the best alternative tell you when to say no — and knowing that keeps you calm. Third, concessions have an order: open with the small ones, keep the valuable one in reserve.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ our walk-out point · ✘ I'd rather concede in time than in the cap · ✔ our walk-away point · ✔ I'd rather concede on time than on the cap]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** When you contrast, stress the two words that differ. Listen: Their position is nine hundred thousand — [pausa] their interest is reliability. [acento em "position" e "interest"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** I'd rather concede on time than on the cap.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Find the interest behind the position.
[ECRÃ: Position: "We want delivery in one week."] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Interest: maybe they have an event in ten days — so delivery of part of the order might be enough.]
[ECRÃ: Position: "We won't pay more than last year."] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Interest: maybe their budget is fixed — so payment in two parts, over two years, might work.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Prepare a negotiation from your life or work — a salary, a rent, a contract, a price with a supplier. Say the other side's position and interest, your own interests, your walk-away point, your best alternative, what you can trade, and the order. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I'm renewing the rent on my flat. The landlord's position is a twenty percent increase; his interest is probably a reliable tenant and no empty months. My interest is staying near my children's school. My walk-away point is ten percent. My best alternative is a smaller flat two streets away. What I can trade: a two-year contract instead of one, and paying three months in advance. I'll open with the two-year contract and keep the advance payment in reserve.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it a good idea to tell the other side your walk-away point? Why, or why not?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 3):** Make a mixed conditional: "We didn't win Niassa — imagine. We would be negotiating nothing now."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: If we hadn't won Niassa, we wouldn't be negotiating this contract now.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! Positions and interests, the walk-away point, the best alternative, and the order of concessions. Next lesson: what to do when the other side pushes — and the talks get stuck.

## 10B — We seem to have reached an impasse

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10B — We seem to have reached an impasse        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue desbloquear um impasse e responder a tácticas de pressão (prazo
  falso, ultimato, "polícia bom e polícia mau", exigência de última hora) sem perder a calma nem o limite.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: nomear o impasse (We seem to have reached an impasse. We're going round in circles.);
  desbloquear (Let's take a step back. Suppose, for the sake of argument, … What would it take for you to…?
  Let's park that and come back to it.); responder à pressão (I'm not comfortable with that. That's not
  something I can agree to under pressure. I'd need to take that back to my team. If that's a condition,
  I'm afraid we'll have to stop here.); nomear a táctica com calma (I understand the deadline, but…)
Palavras-alvo: impasse, board, ultimatum, pressure, tactic, deadline, park, step back, discount,
  condition
Reciclagem: atenuação e recusas (C1, 2); inversão negativa (C1, 4); elipse (C1, 6A)
Pronúncia: a voz calma sob pressão: mais lenta e mais baixa
Contexto: quarta-feira, 6 de Novembro, 15:00; a sala de reuniões; ensaio: a Thandi faz de Femi Adeyemi e o
  Rajesh de Dr Cumbe; o Nuno e a Inês (por vídeo) do lado da Mopane
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 10B; conversa guiada "Find the interest"; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Every hard negotiation has a moment when nobody moves — and sometimes someone tries to push you: a false deadline, an ultimatum, a new demand at the end. Today: how to unblock an impasse, and how to stay calm under pressure.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 6 de Novembro, 15:00; a sala de reuniões; a Thandi com uma placa a dizer "FEMI"; o Rajesh com uma a dizer "DR CUMBE"; o Nuno; a Inês no ecrã]

**THANDI (como Femi):** Nine hundred thousand. That's our number. We've said it three times.
**NUNO:** And we've said one point one three times. We seem to have reached an impasse. Let's take a step back. What would it take for you to move on price?
**THANDI (como Femi):** A lower price.
**INÊS:** [ri-se] Femi wouldn't say that. He'd say "a reason to explain it to my board".
**NUNO:** Then let's give him one. Suppose, for the sake of argument, we include training for twenty technicians. Would that change the number your board sees as fair?
**RAJESH (como Dr Cumbe):** Interesting. But I need an answer on response times first. Four hours everywhere — or no deal.
**NUNO:** I understand why reliability matters. That said, Niassa's roads won't give us four hours in the rainy season, and I'd rather not promise what we can't deliver. Let's park response times for ten minutes and come back to them.
**THANDI (como Femi):** We need your final answer tonight. Our board meets tomorrow at eight.
**NUNO:** I understand the deadline. But that's not something I can agree to under pressure. I'd need to take it back to my team.
**THANDI (como Femi):** And one more thing — our board wants a fifteen percent discount on top. Or we talk to your competitor.
**NUNO:** [pausa] A new condition, at the end, isn't how we've worked so far. If it's a condition, I'm afraid we'll have to stop here. If it's a question, let's look at what we could offer instead of a discount.
**THANDI:** [tira a placa] Good. You didn't panic, and you didn't give anything away.
**RAJESH:** And you named the tactic without accusing her. "A new condition, at the end, isn't how we've worked."
**NUNO:** Not once did I want to say yes. But it's very tempting when someone says "tonight".
**INÊS:** That's exactly why they say it.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Para… | Língua |
|---|---|
| nomear o impasse | We seem to have reached an impasse. · We're going round in circles. |
| desbloquear | Let's take a step back. · What would it take for you to…? · Suppose, for the sake of argument, … · Let's park that and come back to it. |
| responder a um prazo falso | I understand the deadline, but that's not something I can agree to under pressure. · I'd need to take that back to my team. |
| responder a um ultimato | I understand why… matters. That said, … · If that's a condition, I'm afraid we'll have to stop here. |
| responder a uma exigência de última hora | A new condition at the end isn't how we've worked so far. · Let's look at what we could offer instead. |

**PROF:** Notice the pattern: Nuno names what is happening — an impasse, a deadline, a new condition — calmly, without accusing anyone. Then he gives the other side a way forward: a question ("What would it take…?"), a hypothesis ("Suppose, for the sake of argument…"), or a pause ("Let's park that").
[PAUSA 7 s]

**PROF:** "Suppose, for the sake of argument" is very useful: it lets you explore an idea without committing to it.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ We arrived to an impasse. · ✘ for the argument's sake of · ✔ We seem to have reached an impasse. · ✔ for the sake of argument]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Under pressure, your voice is your best tool. Slower and lower — never faster and higher. Listen: I understand the deadline. [pausa] But that's not something I can agree to under pressure.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Respond to the pressure.
[ECRÃ: "Sign today, or the price goes up tomorrow."] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: I understand. But that's not something I can agree to under pressure. I'll come back to you on Monday.]
[ECRÃ: "Take it or leave it."] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: If that's a condition, I'm afraid we'll have to leave it. If it's not, let's look at the options.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think of a negotiation that got stuck — at work, buying a car, renting a house, in a family. Describe the impasse and one pressure tactic, and say what you said — or what you would say now — to unblock it, using at least four expressions from today. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — When I bought my car, the seller wouldn't move from his price, and I wouldn't move from mine — we were going round in circles. Then he said another buyer was coming at five. Now I'd say: "I understand, but I can't agree to that under pressure." Then: "Let's take a step back. What would it take for you to move?" In the end, he wanted to sell quickly — so I offered to pay in cash that day, for a lower price.]

**PROF (opinião):** Are pressure tactics ever acceptable in a negotiation? Where's the line?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 6A):** Answer without repeating: "Will you accept the fifteen percent?" (no) "Did Nuno panic?" (no)
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: I'm afraid not. — He didn't.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Name the impasse, take a step back, park it, and never agree under pressure. Try the tutor's "Find the interest". Next lesson: how to sum up a deal — precisely.

## 10C — Subject to…

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 10        Aula: 10C — Subject to…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue resumir e confirmar um acordo complexo com precisão, usando a
  linguagem das condições de um acordo (subject to, on condition that, with effect from, in the event of,
  provided that, it is understood that).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 4
Gramática-alvo: subject to + nome (subject to board approval — depende de); on condition that / provided
  that + frase (desde que); with effect from + data (com efeito a partir de); in the event of + nome (em
  caso de); it is understood that… (fica entendido que); heads of terms / a term sheet (o resumo dos pontos
  acordados, antes do contrato); confirmar (Just to confirm what we've agreed… Is that your understanding
  too?)
Palavras-alvo: subject to, approval, on condition that, with effect from, in the event of, term sheet,
  summary, confirm, cap, court
Reciclagem: linguagem de contratos (C1, 6B); condições (B2, 3); orações participiais (C1, 5A)
Pronúncia: o ritmo de um resumo: uma ideia por frase, pausas entre os pontos
Contexto: sexta-feira, 8 de Novembro, 11:00; o gabinete do Tumelo; o Nuno com um primeiro resumo dos pontos
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 10C; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! The most dangerous moment in a negotiation isn't the impasse — it's the end, when everyone is tired and happy, and nobody is sure exactly what they agreed. Today: how to sum up a deal precisely.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 8 de Novembro, 11:00; o gabinete do Tumelo; o Nuno com uma folha: "Term sheet — draft"]

**TUMELO:** Read me your summary — as if you were saying it at the end of the meeting.
**NUNO:** "So we agree on a million a year, mostly in dollars, and four hours response time, and more local technicians later, and some penalties."
**TUMELO:** Every word of that will start an argument in a year. "Mostly"? "Later"? "Some"?
**NUNO:** OK. How would you say it?
**TUMELO:** One point per sentence, with the conditions. "The annual fee is one million dollars, with effect from the first of January 2031. Seventy percent is paid in dollars and thirty percent in meticais, on condition that the exchange rate is reviewed every year."
**NUNO:** "With effect from" — from that date.
**TUMELO:** Exactly. Next: "Response time is four hours in Nampula and six hours in Niassa, reducing to four hours in Niassa from year three, provided that the new access road is complete."
**NUNO:** Provided that the road is complete — good. If they don't build the road, we're not bound by four hours.
**TUMELO:** Now penalties.
**NUNO:** "In the event of a missed response time, a penalty applies — capped at five percent of the annual fee."
**TUMELO:** "Capped at" — that's your walk-away point, in three words. And the whole thing?
**NUNO:** "All of this is subject to approval by the three boards."
**TUMELO:** Perfect. And the last sentence — always the same: "Just to confirm, is that your understanding too?"
**NUNO:** So if anyone disagrees, we find out today, not in a year.
**TUMELO:** Having seen a few contracts go to court, I can promise you: that question is worth more than any clause.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Expressão | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| subject to + nome | depende de; só vale se… | …subject to approval by the three boards. |
| on condition that / provided that + frase | desde que | …provided that the new access road is complete. |
| with effect from + data | com efeito a partir de | …with effect from 1 January 2031. |
| in the event of + nome | em caso de | In the event of a missed response time, … |
| capped at | com um máximo de | …capped at five percent of the annual fee. |
| it is understood that… | fica entendido que | It is understood that training is included in the fee. |
| a term sheet / heads of terms | o resumo dos pontos, antes do contrato | — |

**PROF:** Notice the grammar: "subject to" and "in the event of" are followed by a noun; "on condition that" and "provided that" by a full sentence. And Tumelo's rule: one point, one sentence — with its condition.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Confirmar**

[ECRÃ: Just to confirm what we've agreed: … · Is that your understanding too? · Have I missed anything?]

**PROF:** The last question is the most important. If someone understood something different, you want to know now — at the table — not in a year, in court.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ subject to the boards approve it · ✘ in the event that of a delay · ✔ subject to the boards' approval · ✔ in the event of a delay / in the event that there is a delay]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** A summary has a rhythm: one point, a pause, the next point. Listen: The annual fee is one million dollars, [pausa] with effect from the first of January. [pausa] Seventy percent in dollars, thirty in meticais. [pausa] All subject to approval by the three boards.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 12 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Say it precisely.
[ECRÃ: The rent goes up in March — if the repairs are finished.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: The new rent applies with effect from 1 March, provided that the repairs are finished.]
[ECRÃ: If there's a delay, there's a penalty, but not more than 2%.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: In the event of a delay, a penalty applies, capped at two percent.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Sum up an agreement you know or imagine — a rent, a job offer, a purchase, a deal with a supplier — in four or five precise points, each with its condition, and end with a confirmation question. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Just to confirm what we've agreed. The salary is three hundred thousand kwanzas a month, with effect from the first of February. The company pays for my English course, on condition that I stay at least two years. In the event of a transfer to Benguela, housing is paid for six months. Everything is subject to a medical check. Is that your understanding too?]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it better to agree on a short summary and trust each other, or to write everything down in detail? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 6B):** Explain in plain English: "The contractor shall be liable for any damage, subject to clause 9."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The contractor pays for any damage — but clause nine may limit that.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Subject to, provided that, with effect from, in the event of, capped at — and "Is that your understanding too?". Next lesson: the whole of C1 in one small negotiation.
