# C1.1 · Revisão 1 (unidades 1 a 5) · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que a revisão pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2 e das unidades 1 a 5 do C1, e nada de novo, com uma excepção: duas estratégias de exame em blocos fixos — escolher duas de três opções e especular sobre elas (*Of the three, the two I'd like to talk about are… I'd leave aside… because… It could well be that… I'd imagine that…*) e matizar na discussão (*That's true up to a point, but… I'd go along with that, with one reservation:… Where I come down is…*). As estratégias do B2 mantêm-se (*The first option… The second, on the other hand,… So, we've agreed on…*).

**Não usar:** as estruturas do C1.2 — a elipse e a substituição ensinadas, a linguagem jurídica (*hereby, notwithstanding*), o futuro no passado como estrutura (*was to have been*), o *fronting* (*Expensive it may be…*), os marcadores *mind you, that said*, as expressões idiomáticas e a linguagem vaga informal da unidade 9 (*give or take, -ish, or so*).

**Personagens:** **Nuno** e **Inês Macuácua** (R1A); **Lurdes Cassoma**, a aluna-modelo dos exames (contabilista num banco de Benguela desde 2014), agora no exame do C1.1 (R1B).

**Quando:** segunda-feira, 3 de Junho de 2030. A Mussiro convidou a Mopane a apresentar uma proposta para a segunda fase, no Niassa — o que em Maio ainda era um rumor (5B, 5F) é agora um convite oficial.

## R1A — Review: persuasion, diplomacy and data


### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! You've finished the five units of C1.1. Today: no new grammar. We put persuasion, diplomacy, strategy, conflict and data together — in one conversation.

### 2. Situação: a Inês pede conselhos (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 3 de Junho de 2030, 17:00; videochamada; a Inês no escritório de Maputo, com uma carta da Mussiro; o Nuno em Joanesburgo]

**INÊS:** Nuno, it's official. Mussiro has invited us to propose for phase two — twenty substations in Niassa. And Dr Cumbe wants me to present it. To their whole board.
**NUNO:** Inês, that's fantastic! It was your work in Nampula that got us this invitation. What's worrying you?
**INÊS:** Everything. First: how do I start?
**NUNO:** With one sentence. What Niassa needs is what Nampula got — on time, the second time. Then the proof: twelve substations, handed over five days before the deadline.
**INÊS:** But Nampula was late. Very late.
**NUNO:** It was. So say it before they do: "Phase one was late — I won't pretend otherwise. What I can show you is what we've changed." Concede first, then come back.
**INÊS:** OK. Second: the competitor. Everyone says a foreign company has offered them a lower price.
**NUNO:** "Everyone says" isn't a fact. If it comes up: "A lower offer is said to be on the table. I'm not in a position to comment on it. What I can tell you is what our price includes."
**INÊS:** Third: the board will ask about risks. The rainy season, the roads in Niassa…
**NUNO:** Give them scenarios. In the most likely case, eighteen months. In the worst case — if the rains are as bad as last year — twenty-one. And had we planned Nampula around the rainy season, we would have lost far fewer weeks. Say that. It shows you've learned.
**INÊS:** "Had we planned…" — very formal.
**NUNO:** It's a board. One inversion, at the right moment.
**INÊS:** And Bongani? He'll be on site again.
**NUNO:** How are things with him now?
**INÊS:** [sorri] Not once in six weeks has he forgotten our Monday call. I never thought I'd say that.
**NUNO:** Then say it to Mussiro — in other words. They saw the tension in April.
**INÊS:** Last thing: numbers. Their engineers will check everything.
**NUNO:** Then be honest with approximations. Having done Nampula, you know the real costs. "Just under two and a half million dollars", not "about two and a half million", if it's two point four. And if you don't know a number, say so, and give them a date.
**INÊS:** You sound like Thandi.
**NUNO:** [ri-se] Everyone in this company ends up sounding like Thandi. I'll take it as a compliment.

### 3. O C1.1 numa conversa (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno e a Inês usaram, unidade a unidade]

| Unidade | Exemplo da conversa |
|---|---|
| 1. Making the case | It was your work in Nampula that got us this invitation. · What Niassa needs is… · Concede first, then come back. |
| 2. Diplomacy | I'm not in a position to comment on it. What I can tell you is… |
| 3. Strategy | In the most likely case… In the worst case… · Had we planned Nampula around the rainy season, we would have lost far fewer weeks. |
| 4. Conflict | Not once in six weeks has he forgotten our Monday call. |
| 5. Data and reports | A lower offer is said to be on the table. · Having done Nampula, you know the real costs. · just under four million |

**PROF:** Notice: Nuno doesn't give Inês "tricks". He gives her the same idea in five forms — honesty with structure. Concede what's true, separate facts from rumours, show scenarios, and measure every number.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 4. Os erros mais comuns do C1.1 (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Unidade | ✘ Erro | ✔ Correcto |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What we need it's more time. | What we need is more time. |
| 1 | The thing what worries me is the cost. | The thing that worries me is the cost. |
| 2 | There seem to be a problem with the invoice. | There seems to be a problem with the invoice. |
| 2 | I'd be surprised if it will take a week. | I'd be surprised if it took a week. |
| 3 | If we would have known, we would have called. | If we had known / Had we known, we would have called. |
| 3 | Hadn't we signed, we would have lost the client. | Had we not signed, we would have lost the client. |
| 4 | Never I have seen such a mess. | Never have I seen such a mess. |
| 4 | Only when he called I understood. | Only when he called did I understand. |
| 5 | Having analysed the data, the conclusion is clear. | Having analysed the data, we reached a clear conclusion. |
| 5 | The company is said that it is planning a merger. | The company is said to be planning a merger. |

**PROF:** At C1, mistakes are rarely about meaning — they're about form and position: "is" after a cleft, the singular after "there seems", the past after "I'd be surprised if", the auxiliary in the right place, and the right subject after a participle clause.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. A sua vez (2 min)

**PROF:** Now you. Imagine a colleague asks you for advice before an important presentation — to a client, a board or a committee. Give five pieces of advice in about one and a half minutes, using one structure from each unit of C1.1.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — What you need is one strong opening sentence. If they mention last year's problem, concede it: "It was a difficult year — I won't deny it." If they ask about a rumour, say "It's said to be happening, but I'm not in a position to confirm it." Had I prepared my first big presentation better, I'd have practised the questions, not the slides. Never have I seen a board forgive a wrong number. And having checked every figure, you'll feel much calmer.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! That's C1.1 in one conversation. Next lesson: how the C1.1 speaking exam works — and two new strategies.

## R1B — Getting ready for the C1.1 speaking exam

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: Revisão 1        Aula: R1B — Getting ready for the C1.1 speaking exam        Tipo: Revisão
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno conhece as quatro partes do exame do C1.1 e o que se avalia, e consegue
  usar duas estratégias novas: escolher duas de três opções e especular sobre elas, e matizar uma resposta
  na discussão.
Objectivos de unidade que revê: U1–U5, todos
Estratégias novas (blocos fixos): Of the three, the two I'd like to talk about are… · I'd leave aside… because…
  · It could well be that… · I'd imagine that… · That's true up to a point, but… · I'd go along with that,
  with one reservation:… · Where I come down is…
Situação: a Lurdes faz o exame de demonstração do C1.1
Duração: 14 min
Verificação: laboratório C1R1-2; conversa de ensaio com o tutor
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! At the end of C1.1, there's a speaking exam of about twelve minutes. Today: how it works, how it's assessed, two new strategies — and Lurdes, our model student, in her first C1 exam.

### 2. Como é o exame (2 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Parte | Duração | O que acontece |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Interview | cerca de 2 min | Três perguntas sobre o seu trabalho, uma decisão do passado e o futuro. |
| 2. Long turn | cerca de 3 min | O examinador descreve **três** opções; escolhe **duas**, compara-as e especula durante cerca de 1 minuto e meio; depois, duas perguntas. |
| 3. Decision task | cerca de 4 min | Uma de quatro situações: defender uma proposta, escolher uma estratégia, mediar entre duas pessoas ou explicar números. O examinador cria uma dificuldade, e a tarefa só termina com uma decisão. |
| 4. Discussion | cerca de 3 min | Uma pergunta abstracta, com **dois** contra-argumentos, um de cada vez. |

**PROF:** What's new compared with B2? In Part 2, three options instead of two — you choose. In Part 3, the examiner may play two people at once, and will always say the name before speaking. And in Part 4, the examiner wants nuance: not just "I agree" or "I disagree", but "up to a point".
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 3. O que é avaliado (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: Tarefa · Gramática · Vocabulário · Fluência (e a pronúncia, pela gravação, se aceitar)]

**PROF:** For a good mark at C1: long, organised answers; persuasion with emphasis; hedging and certainty where they belong; a range of complex structures — clefts, inversions, participle clauses — used naturally, not in every sentence; precise vocabulary; and a decision at the end of Part 3.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** What doesn't lower your mark: your accent; asking the examiner to repeat; taking a moment to think; reformulating; correcting yourself; and disagreeing politely — or agreeing only in part.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Duas estratégias novas (2 min 30 s)

**Escolher duas de três e especular**

[ECRÃ: Of the three, the two I'd like to talk about are… and… · I'd leave aside… because… · The first… The second, on the other hand,… · It could well be that… · I'd imagine that… · If I had to choose, I'd go for…]

**PROF:** Start by saying which two you'll talk about — and in one sentence, why you're leaving the third aside. Then compare, and speculate: "It could well be that…", "I'd imagine that…". End with a choice.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Matizar na discussão**

[ECRÃ: That's true up to a point, but… · I'd go along with that, with one reservation:… · I see the logic, but… · Where I come down is…]

**PROF:** When the examiner gives a counter-argument, the best answers accept the true part and keep the position: "That's true up to a point, but…". And "Where I come down is…" closes your view.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Um exame de demonstração (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: a Lurdes, em casa, em Benguela, com auscultadores; "Demonstration — C1.1 speaking exam"]

**EXAMINER:** Hello, Lurdes. Welcome to your C1.1 speaking exam. Tell me about your work. What's the part of it that people outside never see?
**LURDES:** I'm an accountant at a bank in Benguela — I've been there since 2014. What people outside never see is how much of my day is checking other people's numbers. It isn't the big decisions that take my time; it's the small mistakes.
**EXAMINER:** Thank you. Now Part 2. A school has money for one change: longer days with homework done at school; a new science laboratory; or English lessons from the first year. Choose two, compare them and say which would make the bigger difference.
**LURDES:** Of the three, the two I'd like to talk about are the laboratory and the English lessons. I'd leave aside the longer days, because I imagine the teachers are tired enough already. The laboratory is expensive, but it could well be that it changes how children think — they'd experiment instead of memorising. English from the first year, on the other hand, is cheaper and lasts a whole life. Had I learned English at seven, I wouldn't be taking this exam now! If I had to choose, I'd go for English.
**EXAMINER:** What could go wrong in the first year?
**LURDES:** It could well be that there aren't enough good teachers, I'd imagine — and some parents might think children should master Portuguese first.
**EXAMINER:** Thank you. Now Part 3. I'm the director of the school, and I don't think we can afford English teachers. Convince me.
**LURDES:** I understand. What I'm asking for isn't a full programme — it's a one-year trial with two classes… *(a tarefa continua: o director pergunta pelo custo; a Lurdes admite que não sabe o número exacto e promete-o para sexta; chegam a acordo num teste de um ano, com avaliação em Dezembro)*
**EXAMINER:** Thank you. Part 4. Should children choose what they study?
**LURDES:** Up to a certain age, no. Children need a base — reading, maths, a language. After fourteen, they should can choose — sorry, they should be able to choose more.
**EXAMINER:** Some people say that children who choose study harder.
**LURDES:** That's true up to a point, but a fourteen-year-old might choose what's easy, not what's useful. I'd go along with more choice, with one reservation: a teacher should guide it.
**EXAMINER:** Others say parents should decide, because they pay.
**LURDES:** I see the logic, but paying doesn't mean knowing what a child is good at. Where I come down is: the child chooses, the teacher guides, and the parents are told — not the other way round.
**EXAMINER:** Thank you, the exam is finished. You can press the finish button now.

**PROF:** Did you notice? Lurdes chose two options in one sentence, gave a reason for leaving the third aside, speculated, and made a choice. She used one inversion — "Had I learned English at seven…" — with humour. And she corrected herself — "they should can choose — sorry, they should be able to choose" — which doesn't lower the mark. In Part 4, she never just agreed or disagreed: she said "up to a point" and closed with "Where I come down is…".
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 6. Pratique cada parte (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Your turn — Part 2 only. A town has money for one project: more street lights; a sports centre for young people; or free buses at night. Choose two, compare them, speculate, and choose. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Of the three, the two I'd like to talk about are the sports centre and the night buses. I'd leave aside the street lights, because they're the council's job anyway. The sports centre would give young people somewhere to go; it could well be that crime falls. The night buses, on the other hand, help workers who finish late. I'd imagine nurses and security guards would use them every day. If I had to choose, I'd go for the buses.]

### 7. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Now practise the whole exam with the tutor — the "Exam rehearsal". Then do the review quiz. Good luck in your C1.1 exam!
