# C1.2 · Revisão 2 (unidades 6 a 10) · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md). São as últimas aulas do curso.

**Língua que a revisão pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao C1, e nada de novo, com uma excepção: duas estratégias de exame em blocos fixos — responder com estrutura e nuance a uma pergunta abstracta difícil em poucos segundos (*There are two ways of looking at this. … Much depends on… Where I come down is…*) e acompanhar o registo de quem faz um papel na parte 3 (formal com um director, neutro com um colega, calmo e simples com um morador). As estratégias do C1.1 mantêm-se (*Of the three, the two I'd like to talk about are… That's true up to a point, but…*).

**Personagens:** **Nuno** e **Lindiwe** (R2A); **Lurdes Cassoma**, a aluna-modelo dos exames (contabilista num banco de Benguela desde 2014), agora no seu último exame (R2B).

**Quando:** segunda-feira, 2 de Dezembro de 2030. A instalação da Marula abriu a 29 de Outubro (9A); o contrato regional foi assinado pelas três administrações (10G); a direcção da Mopane reúne-se a 12 de Dezembro para confirmar o centro de formação (10A).

## R2A — Review: ethics, science, media and deals

```

  corrigir os 10 erros mais comuns do C1.2.

```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! You've finished all ten units of C1. Today: no new grammar. We put ethics, science, media, register and negotiation together — in one conversation.

### 2. Situação: a Lindiwe pede conselhos (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 2 de Dezembro de 2030, 17:30; a cantina da Mopane, quase vazia; a Lindiwe com um caderno cheio de notas; o Nuno com dois cafés]

**LINDIWE:** Nuno, ten minutes? Thursday is a big day. In the morning, Marula wants to renegotiate the maintenance contract. In the afternoon, a radio station in Polokwane wants to interview me about the batteries.
**NUNO:** Both on the same day? Brave. Start with Marula. What do they want?
**LINDIWE:** A twenty percent discount. Their position is "the batteries hardly ever fail". Their interest, I think, is that their own budget was cut.
**NUNO:** Then don't argue about failures. Ask what it would take — and keep something in reserve. Our walk-away point?
**LINDIWE:** Ten percent, if they sign for three more years.
**NUNO:** Good. And if Mr Sithole says "we need an answer today"?
**LINDIWE:** "I understand the deadline, but that's not something I can agree to under pressure."
**NUNO:** Perfect. And at the end — one point, one sentence, every condition. "Subject to our board" — and "Is that your understanding too?".
**LINDIWE:** Got it. Now the radio. What if they ask if the batteries are dangerous?
**NUNO:** "That's a very reasonable question." Then the bucket — think of it as a bucket that fills with sun. And one number: in five weeks, not one incident.
**LINDIWE:** And if they ask about bills?
**NUNO:** Don't promise what isn't yours. "I'm afraid I can't promise lower bills. What I can promise is fewer power cuts in the evening." Difficult as it is, that's the honest answer.
**LINDIWE:** One more thing. Mr Sithole's assistant sent me a gift. A very nice bottle of whisky. Before the negotiation.
**NUNO:** Over fifty dollars?
**LINDIWE:** I'm afraid so.
**NUNO:** Then you know the policy. Declare it to Tumelo, and send it back with a kind note. Strictly speaking, it's a gift. In practice, before a negotiation, it's a grey area — and we don't do grey areas.
**LINDIWE:** I was going to keep it for Christmas. [ri-se] Fine. I'll send it back.
**NUNO:** And Lindiwe — on the radio, speak like a person, not like a contract. Save "notwithstanding" for Tumelo.
**LINDIWE:** Mind you, you sound more like Thandi every day.
**NUNO:** That, I'll take as a compliment.

Science and media meet on the radio: an analogy, one number, and honesty about what you can't promise. And register runs through everything: "Got it" to Nuno, "That's a very reasonable question" to a listener.

### 4. Os erros mais comuns do C1.2 (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Unidade | ✘ Erro | ✔ Correcto |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | I don't hope so. | I hope not. |
| 6 | "I can come." — So I can. | So can I. |
| 7 | I knew it will be difficult. | I knew it would be difficult. |
| 7 | We're on the verge to finish. | We're on the verge of finishing. |
| 8 | This can I promise you. | This I can promise you. |
| 8 | As difficult it was, we finished. | Difficult as it was, we finished. |
| 9 | They offered a discount, but we turned down it. | …but we turned it down. |
| 9 | Thirty or so of people came. | Thirty or so people came. |
| 10 | It's subject to the board approves it. | It's subject to the board's approval. |
| 10 | Our pretensions are reasonable. | What we're asking for is reasonable. |

**PROF:** Two kinds of mistake here: word order — "So can I", "This I can", "turn it down" — and false friends — "pretensions". Both are easy to fix once you've seen them.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. A sua vez (2 min)

**PROF:** Now you. A colleague has two difficult things on the same day — a negotiation and an interview — and asks for advice. Give five pieces of advice in about one and a half minutes, using one structure from each unit of C1.2.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — First, the gift you received: I'm afraid it has to go back — strictly speaking, it's a grey area before a negotiation. In the interview, think of your project as a bridge: explain where it starts and where it ends. Expensive it may be, but say why it's worth it. Keep it simple — no jargon, no idioms with the journalist. And in the negotiation, find their interest, keep your best card in reserve, and sum up every point, subject to your manager's approval.]

## R2B — Getting ready for the C1.2 speaking exam

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: Revisão 2        Aula: R2B — Getting ready for the C1.2 speaking exam        Tipo: Revisão
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno conhece as quatro partes do exame do C1.2 e o que se avalia, e consegue
  usar duas estratégias novas: responder com estrutura a uma pergunta abstracta difícil em poucos segundos,
  e acompanhar o registo de quem faz um papel na parte 3.
Objectivos de unidade que revê: U6–U10, todos
Estratégias novas (blocos fixos): There are two ways of looking at this. · Much depends on… · On balance,
  … · Where I come down is… · acompanhar o registo do papel (formal com um director, neutro com um colega,
  calmo e simples com um morador)
Situação: a Lurdes faz o exame de demonstração do C1.2 — o seu último exame
Duração: 14 min
Verificação: laboratório C1R2-2; conversa de ensaio com o tutor
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This is the last lesson of the course. At the end of C1.2, there's a speaking exam of about twelve minutes. Today: how it works, how it's assessed, two new strategies — and Lurdes, in her final exam.

### 2. Como é o exame (2 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Parte | Duração | O que acontece |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Interview | cerca de 2 min | Três perguntas: como fala com pessoas diferentes, o que se esperava no seu campo e não aconteceu, e onde se informa. |
| 2. Long turn | cerca de 3 min | Três opções sobre um tema técnico, ético ou social; escolhe duas, compara, especula e escolhe; duas perguntas. |
| 3. Decision task | cerca de 4 min | Uma de quatro: um dilema ético num negócio, explicar uma tecnologia a quem tem receio, uma entrevista de rádio difícil, ou a ronda final de uma negociação. O examinador faz um papel — acompanhe o registo. A tarefa só termina com uma decisão. |
| 4. Discussion | cerca de 3 min | Uma pergunta abstracta, com dois contra-argumentos, um de cada vez. |

**PROF:** What's new compared with C1.1? In Part 3, the examiner plays a character with a clear register — a formal director, a worried resident, a pushy journalist — and part of your mark is matching it. And in Part 4, the questions are more abstract: ethics, science, media, society.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 3. O que é avaliado (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: Tarefa · Gramática · Vocabulário · Fluência (e a pronúncia, pela gravação, se aceitar)]

**PROF:** For a good mark at the end of C1: you handle the task completely — including the difficulty; you use the full range of C1 grammar naturally; your vocabulary is precise, and your register fits the person; you speak almost effortlessly; and in Part 4, your answers have structure and nuance.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** What doesn't lower your mark: your accent; asking the examiner to repeat; taking a moment to think; correcting yourself; disagreeing politely; and — new at this level — explaining a technical word in simple terms instead of using it.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Duas estratégias novas (2 min 30 s)

**Uma pergunta abstracta, em poucos segundos**

[ECRÃ: There are two ways of looking at this. On the one hand… on the other… · Much depends on… · On balance, … · Where I come down is…]

**PROF:** When a question is big and abstract — "Do the media make us more or less informed?" — you don't need a perfect answer. You need a structure. "There are two ways of looking at this" gives you time and organisation. "Much depends on…" adds nuance. "Where I come down is…" closes your view.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Acompanhar o registo**

[ECRÃ: o examinador é… um director → formal, sem expressões idiomáticas · um colega → neutro, directo · um morador preocupado → calmo, simples, sem jargão · um jornalista insistente → curto, calmo, mensagens-chave]

**PROF:** In Part 3, listen to how the examiner speaks in the first sentence, and match it. With a worried resident, "That's a very reasonable question" — not "Notwithstanding the technical parameters". With a director, "I'd like to propose…" — not "Let's get the ball rolling".
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Um exame de demonstração (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: a Lurdes, em casa, em Benguela, com auscultadores; "Demonstration — C1.2 speaking exam"]

**EXAMINER:** Hello, Lurdes. Welcome to your C1.2 speaking exam. Tell me about a change at your bank that was supposed to happen, but didn't.
**LURDES:** Two years ago, the bank was going to close our branch and move everything online. Everyone thought it would happen within a year. Then the older clients protested — and, to be fair, they were right. So the branch is still open, but half the space is now a digital help desk.
**EXAMINER:** Thank you. Now Part 2. A town wants to reduce plastic waste. Three options: a ban on plastic bags, a deposit on bottles, or recycling bins in every street. Choose two, compare them and say which would work better.
**LURDES:** Of the three, the two I'd like to talk about are the deposit and the bins. I'd leave aside the ban — it was tried before, and it's hard to enforce. A deposit gives people a reason: get your money back. Bins, on the other hand, only work if someone empties them — and in my town, that's a big "if". It could well be that the deposit works faster. If I had to choose, I'd go for the deposit.
**EXAMINER:** What might go wrong with the deposit?
**LURDES:** Shops might not want to handle the bottles. In which case, the town would need collection points — at the markets, for example.
**EXAMINER:** Thank you. Now Part 3. I'm one of your older clients, and I don't trust this new mobile banking app. My grandson says I should use it. Why should I?
**LURDES:** That's a very reasonable question. Think of the app as a small branch in your pocket — the same account, the same money, but open at night and on Sundays. You don't have to use it for everything… *(a tarefa continua: o cliente pergunta "And if I lose my phone?"; a Lurdes explica, sem jargão, que o telemóvel perdido se bloqueia com uma chamada; não promete que nada pode correr mal; e combinam uma sessão de meia hora no balcão, com o neto)*
**EXAMINER:** Thank you. Part 4. Should banks close branches when almost everything can be done online?
**LURDES:** There are two ways of looking at this. For a bank, branches are expensive, and most young clients never visit them. But for older people, and for people far from a city, a branch is the bank. Much depends on the place.
**EXAMINER:** Some people say keeping branches only delays the inevitable.
**LURDES:** That's true up to a point. Mind you, "inevitable" in Lisbon isn't inevitable in a village in Huíla. Where I come down is: close slowly, and keep one help desk in every town.
**EXAMINER:** Others say banks should be free to decide — they're businesses, not public services.
**LURDES:** I see the logic, but banks hold everyone's money. That gives them a responsibility, too. On balance, I'd say: free to decide, but not free to leave people behind.
**EXAMINER:** Thank you, the exam is finished. You can press the finish button now.

**PROF:** Did you notice? In Part 3, Lurdes spoke to the older client calmly and simply — an analogy, no jargon, no promise she couldn't keep. In Part 4, she used the structure: "There are two ways of looking at this… Much depends on… Where I come down is…" — and never just agreed or disagreed. And one fronting, one marker, a future in the past — all natural.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**PROF:** And notice something else. Lurdes started the course with "My name is Lurdes. I'm an accountant." Today, she argues about banking policy in English. That journey is yours, too.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 6. Pratique cada parte (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Your turn — Part 4 only. Answer this question with the structure: "Is technology making people more or less free?" About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — There are two ways of looking at this. On the one hand, technology gives people more choice: you can work from anywhere and learn anything. On the other hand, it watches everything we do. Much depends on who controls it. Where I come down is: more free, if we understand it — less free, if we don't.]

### 7. Fecho (1 min)

**PROF:** Excellent. Now practise the whole exam with the tutor — the "Exam rehearsal". Then do the review quiz. And then — your C1.2 exam, the last one of the course. Whatever the result, remember where you started. Good luck — and congratulations.
