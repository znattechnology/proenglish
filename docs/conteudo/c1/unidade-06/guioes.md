# C1.2 · Unidade 6 — Compliance and ethics · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md). É a primeira unidade do C1.2.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2 e o C1.1 inteiro. Novo: elipse e substituição (*I think so. I hope not. I'm afraid not. — Has he signed? — He has. Kalandula can bid, and so can we. Mopane didn't, and neither did they. If you wish to do so, … the one we signed, the new ones*); linguagem de contratos e políticas, para reconhecer e explicar (*shall, may, hereby, notwithstanding, liable, in breach of, without prejudice to*); discutir dilemas éticos (*It's a grey area. Strictly speaking… The question is whether… Where do we draw the line?*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** o futuro no passado como estrutura ensinada (*was to have been, was about to*, unidade 7); o *fronting* e os marcadores *mind you, that said* (unidade 8); as expressões idiomáticas e a linguagem vaga informal (unidade 9). A linguagem jurídica nunca se produz numa conversa: ensina-se a reconhecê-la e a explicá-la em inglês simples.

**Nomes, empresas e factos:** a Mopane Systems, a Mussiro Energia, a Kalandula Tech e o *podcast* da 6E ("The Grey Area") são fictícios, como as personagens e os acontecimentos. As políticas citadas são da Mopane (fictícias); não se citam leis reais como se fossem nossas, e não se dão conselhos jurídicos. Os princípios gerais (não pagar subornos, declarar ofertas, recusar "pagamentos de facilitação") são prática comum das políticas anticorrupção das empresas.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**, **Thandi**, **Rajesh**, **Lindiwe**, **Bongani**, **Inês Macuácua** (Maputo), **Tumelo** (jurista da Mopane, C1 2E e 3G), **Eng.ª Lúcia Tembe** (compras da Mussiro, C1 2F). Novos e fictícios: **Sr. Ernesto Matsinhe** (moçambicano, apresenta-se como "consultor"); na 6E, **Zinhle** (sul-africana, apresentadora), **Prof. Tunde Adebayo** (nigeriano, professor de ética empresarial) e **Ms Halima Said** (queniana, directora de conformidade de uma empresa de telecomunicações).

**A história:** Julho de 2030. A Mussiro abriu o concurso para a segunda fase, no Niassa (20 subestações); as propostas fecham a 30 de Agosto. A proposta da Mopane anda nos 2,4 milhões de dólares (R1A). Na segunda, 1, a equipa lê o caderno de encargos (6A); na quarta, 3, o Tumelo explica as cláusulas difíceis (6B); na sexta, 5, a equipa discute três situações cinzentas (6C); na quarta, 10, o Nuno ouve um *podcast* de ética (6E); na segunda, 15, em Maputo, um "consultor" pede-lhe uma "comissão" (6F).

**A política anticorrupção da Mopane (igual em todas as aulas):**
- Nenhum pagamento, oferta ou vantagem para influenciar uma decisão — directamente ou através de outra pessoa.
- Ofertas e convites até 50 dólares, ocasionais e declarados, são aceitáveis; acima disso, só com autorização prévia de conformidade (o Tumelo).
- Viagens e alojamento de pessoas de um cliente só quando fazem parte de um contrato e estão por escrito.
- Nenhum "pagamento de facilitação" (um pagamento pequeno a um funcionário para acelerar um procedimento), qualquer que seja o valor.
- Qualquer pedido suspeito comunica-se a conformidade em 48 horas.

## 6A — I'm afraid not

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6A — I'm afraid not        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue evitar repetições com elipse e substituição (I think so, I'm
  afraid not, He has, So can we, Neither did they, do so, the one(s)), como fala quem é fluente.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: so / not depois de think, hope, expect, suppose, be afraid (I think so · I don't think so /
  I think not (formal) · I hope not · I'm afraid so / not); respostas curtas com o auxiliar (Has he signed? —
  He has. / He hasn't, but he will.); so / neither / nor + auxiliar + sujeito (So can we. Neither did they.);
  elipse depois do auxiliar ou de to (We'd like to bid alone, but we can't. We didn't want to, but we had to.);
  do so (formal: If you wish to do so…); one / ones (the one we signed, the new ones)
Palavras-alvo: tender, tender documents, deadline, bid, local partner, requirement, safety form,
  experience, signed, submit
Reciclagem: respostas curtas (A2 e B1); clivadas (C1, 1)
Pronúncia: o acento no auxiliar das respostas curtas (He HAS. So CAN we. — marcado por indicação)
Contexto: segunda-feira, 1 de Julho de 2030, 9:00; a sala de reuniões; o Nuno, o Rajesh e a Lindiwe com o
  caderno de encargos impresso; a Inês por vídeo
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 6A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 6
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to C1.2 — and to unit 6: Compliance and ethics. We start with a feature of fluent English that learners often miss: not repeating. "Will the deadline move?" — "I hope not." Today: ellipsis and substitution.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 1 de Julho de 2030, 9:00; a sala de reuniões; o caderno de encargos da Mussiro, com 140 páginas; a Inês no ecrã]

**NUNO:** Right — the tender for Niassa. A hundred and forty pages. Inês, do we need a local partner?
**INÊS:** I'm afraid so. At least thirty percent of the work must go to a Mozambican company.
**RAJESH:** Kalandula isn't Mozambican.
**INÊS:** No, but Mussiro accepts a consortium. Kalandula can bid with us, and so can a local firm.
**NUNO:** Will the deadline move? Thirtieth of August is tight.
**INÊS:** I hope not. If it moves, everyone gets more time — including the competitors.
**LINDIWE:** Do they want references from similar projects?
**INÊS:** They do. Three, from the last five years.
**NUNO:** Nampula is one. Tômbwa is another. And the third?
**LINDIWE:** The Lobito project, I think — though it's older than five years.
**RAJESH:** I don't think so, actually. It finished in June 2028. That's well within five years.
**NUNO:** Good. Has Bongani signed the safety form for the site visit?
**LINDIWE:** He has. And so has Inês.
**NUNO:** I haven't.
**LINDIWE:** Neither have I. We'll do it today.
**INÊS:** One more thing. The tender says that bidders "may visit the sites, and shall inform Mussiro in writing if they wish to do so".
**NUNO:** So — we write to them before we go. Which sites?
**INÊS:** The ones near Lichinga, at least. The old substations there are very different from the ones in Nampula.
**NUNO:** Then let's visit them. We didn't visit Nampula before the first bid — and we should have.
**RAJESH:** Will the price be lower than last time, per substation?
**NUNO:** I'm afraid not. Niassa is further, and the roads are worse.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**So e not**

[ECRÃ: Do we need a local partner? — **I'm afraid so.** (= I'm afraid we need one.) · Will the deadline move? — **I hope not.** (= I hope it won't move.) · Is Lobito older than five years? — **I don't think so.** · Will the price be lower? — **I'm afraid not.**]

**PROF:** After think, hope, expect, suppose and "I'm afraid", "so" replaces a positive clause, "not" a negative one. Notice: "I hope not", never "I don't hope so". And "I'm afraid not" is the polite way to give a negative answer.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**O auxiliar no lugar do verbo**

[ECRÃ: Has Bongani signed? — **He has.** · Do they want references? — **They do.** · Kalandula can bid, and **so can** a local firm. · I haven't. — **Neither have I.** · We didn't visit Nampula — **and we should have.**]

**PROF:** Fluent speakers repeat only the auxiliary. "So + auxiliary + subject" for "também"; "neither" or "nor" for "também não". And after a modal, the rest disappears: "we should have".
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Do so*, *one* e *ones***

[ECRÃ: …shall inform Mussiro in writing if they wish **to do so** (= to visit). · the ones near Lichinga · different from **the ones** in Nampula]

**PROF:** "Do so" is formal — you'll see it in contracts and tenders. "One" and "ones" replace a noun you've just said.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ I don't hope so. · ✘ So I can. (para "também posso") · ✘ Neither I have. · ✔ I hope not. · ✔ So can I. · ✔ Neither have I.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In short answers, stress the auxiliary. Listen: Has he signed? — He has. [acento em "has"] So can we. [acento em "can"] Neither have I. [acento em "I"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat all three.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Answer without repeating.
[ECRÃ: Will it rain tomorrow? (you hope it won't)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I hope not.]
[ECRÃ: "I can't come on Friday." (you can't either)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Neither can I.]
[ECRÃ: Did you send the report? (yes)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I did.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. A colleague asks you ten quick questions about a project or an event you're organising. Imagine the questions, and answer them naturally, without repeating — with "I think so", "I'm afraid not", "So did…", "Neither has…", "the one…". About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Is the venue booked? — It is. The one near the airport. Will the minister come? — I hope so, but I don't think she will. Has the caterer confirmed? — He hasn't yet. Neither has the sound company. Are the invitations ready? — The printed ones are; the digital ones aren't. Can we change the date? — I'm afraid not. We'd like to, but we can't.]

**PROF (opinião):** Do you think big tenders are fair to small local companies? Why, or why not?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 1):** Put the spotlight on the problem: "The roads make Niassa more expensive."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: It's the roads that make Niassa more expensive.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! I'm afraid so, I hope not, so can we, neither have I, the ones near Lichinga — fluent English repeats very little. Next lesson: the language of contracts — and how to explain it in plain English.

## 6B — The supplier shall…

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6B — The supplier shall…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue reconhecer a linguagem de contratos e políticas (shall, may,
  hereby, notwithstanding, liable, in breach of, without prejudice to) e explicá-la em inglês simples.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: shall (= must, num contrato: The bidder shall submit…); shall not (= must not); may (=
  can / is allowed to: Mussiro may reject…); hereby (= by this document); notwithstanding (= despite / even
  if: Notwithstanding clause 9…); liable for (= legally responsible for); in breach of (= not following);
  without prejudice to (= without affecting); explicar: In plain English, this means… · What it says, in
  short, is… · Basically, …
Palavras-alvo: clause, contract, bidder, liable, breach, terminate, confidential, plain English,
  obligation, right
Reciclagem: elipse (6A); modais de obrigação (B1 e B2); passivas (B2, 4)
Pronúncia: o acento em palavras jurídicas longas (confidentiality, liability, jurisdiction, obligation)
Contexto: quarta-feira, 3 de Julho, 11:00; o gabinete do Tumelo; o Nuno com três cláusulas sublinhadas
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 6B; conversa guiada "In plain English"; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Contracts are written in a special English — "shall", "hereby", "notwithstanding". You don't need to speak like that. You need to understand it, and explain it in plain words. Today: legal language, in plain English.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 3 de Julho, 11:00; o gabinete do Tumelo, com estantes de pastas; o Nuno com o caderno de encargos e três cláusulas sublinhadas a amarelo]

**NUNO:** Tumelo, three clauses. I understand every word, and none of the sentences.
**TUMELO:** [ri-se] That's normal. Read me the first.
**NUNO:** "The bidder shall submit a performance guarantee of ten percent of the contract value within fifteen days of the award."
**TUMELO:** "Shall" means "must". In plain English: if we win, we must give them a bank guarantee — ten percent — within fifteen days. If we don't finish the work, they can take that money.
**NUNO:** Two hundred and forty thousand. Rajesh will love that. Second: "Notwithstanding clause nine, Mussiro may terminate the contract at any time if the contractor is in breach of clause twenty-one."
**TUMELO:** "Notwithstanding clause nine" — "even though clause nine says something different". Clause nine gives us thirty days to fix any problem. So: normally we get thirty days; but if we break clause twenty-one, they can cancel immediately. "May" means they're allowed to — not that they must.
**NUNO:** And clause twenty-one is…?
**TUMELO:** Prohibited practices. Bribes, gifts, anything to influence the award. It's the one clause with no second chance.
**NUNO:** Third: "The contractor shall be liable for any damage caused by its subcontractors, without prejudice to Mussiro's right to claim directly against them."
**TUMELO:** "Liable" — legally responsible. If our local partner damages something, we pay. And "without prejudice to" means Mussiro can still go after the partner too. Both of us, basically.
**NUNO:** So we need to choose that partner very carefully.
**TUMELO:** You do. And so do I — I'll check them before anyone signs anything.
**NUNO:** One last question. Can we ask Mussiro to change clause twenty-one?
**TUMELO:** We could. But I wouldn't. Any company that asks to soften the anti-corruption clause tells the client something about itself.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| No contrato | Em inglês simples | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| shall | must | The bidder **shall submit** a guarantee. → We must give a guarantee. |
| shall not | must not | The contractor **shall not** subcontract more than 50% of the work. |
| may | can, is allowed to | Mussiro **may terminate** the contract. → They can cancel it (they don't have to). |
| hereby | by this document | The parties **hereby** agree… → In this document, both sides agree… |
| notwithstanding | even though… says something different | **Notwithstanding** clause 9, … → Even though clause 9 gives us 30 days, … |
| liable for | legally responsible for | The contractor shall be **liable for** any damage. → We pay for any damage. |
| in breach of | not following, breaking | …if the contractor is **in breach of** clause 21 |
| without prejudice to | without affecting | …**without prejudice to** Mussiro's right to… → Mussiro can still… |

**PROF:** You'll read these words; you won't say them in a meeting. What you say is the plain version, introduced with "In plain English, this means…", "Basically…" or "What it says, in short, is…".
[PAUSA 7 s]

**PROF:** Be careful with "shall" and "may": in a contract, "shall" is an obligation and "may" is a permission. Translating "may" as "will" changes the contract.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ "Mussiro may terminate" = Mussiro will terminate. · ✔ "Mussiro may terminate" = Mussiro is allowed to terminate.]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Long legal words have one strong stress. Listen: confidentiality [acento em "al"]. Liability [acento em "bil"]. Jurisdiction [acento em "dic"]. Obligation [acento em "ga"].
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat each one.
[PAUSA 10 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Say it in plain English.
[ECRÃ: "The tenant shall pay the rent on or before the fifth day of each month."] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: You must pay the rent by the fifth of every month.]
[ECRÃ: "The company may share anonymous data with research partners."] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: The company is allowed to share data — without names — with research partners.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think of a contract or a set of rules you know — a phone contract, a rental agreement, a gym, a school's rules. Explain three of its obligations and one right, in plain English, to a friend who's about to sign it. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — In plain English, the contract says three things you must do: pay by the fifth of each month, give two months' notice if you want to leave, and not paint the walls without permission. And one thing the landlord may do: visit the flat, but only with twenty-four hours' notice. Basically, read clause eight twice — it says you're liable for any damage, even if a guest causes it.]

**PROF (opinião):** Why do you think contracts are written in such difficult language? Should they be simpler?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1 e B2):** Say clause twenty-one with a modal of obligation, in plain English: "The bidder shall not offer any payment to influence the award."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Bidders mustn't offer any payment to influence who wins.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Shall, may, notwithstanding, liable, in breach of — read them, then say them in plain English. Try the tutor's "In plain English". Next lesson: grey areas — where do we draw the line?

## 6C — Where do we draw the line?

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6C — Where do we draw the line?        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue discutir um dilema ético: distinguir o que é claro do que é
  cinzento, pesar obrigações e consequências, e tomar uma posição fundamentada.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: It's a grey area. · Strictly speaking, … · Technically, …, but… · The question is whether… ·
  Where do we draw the line? · On the face of it, … · It depends on whether… · There's a difference between…
  and… · I'd feel uncomfortable + -ing · It would set a bad precedent · in principle / in practice ·
  elipse nas respostas (I think not. Neither should we.)
Palavras-alvo: dilemma, grey area, gift, hospitality, declare, facilitation payment, precedent, policy,
  compliance, transparent
Reciclagem: atenuação (C1, 2A); condicionais (C1, 3); elipse (6A)
Pronúncia: o tom ponderado: pausas antes de "but" e de "whether"
Contexto: sexta-feira, 5 de Julho, 15:00; a sala de reuniões; a Thandi, o Nuno, o Bongani (de visita a
  Joanesburgo) e a Lindiwe discutem três situações
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 6C; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Some ethical questions are easy: you don't pay bribes. Others are grey: a small gift, a dinner, twenty dollars at a border. Today: the language for discussing grey areas — and taking a position.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 5 de Julho, 15:00; a sala de reuniões; a Thandi com a política anticorrupção da Mopane, de duas páginas; o Nuno, o Bongani e a Lindiwe]

**THANDI:** Three real situations. You decide. First: the two Mussiro technicians in our training course. We paid their flights and their hotel in Joburg. Is that a problem?
**LINDIWE:** On the face of it, it looks like a gift to a client.
**NUNO:** Technically, yes, but it's in the Nampula contract. Two places in the training programme, flights and hotel included — in writing.
**THANDI:** Exactly. In the contract, transparent, documented. Not a grey area. Second: Dr Cumbe's assistant asked Inês, informally, if Mopane could "help" with the procurement team's end-of-year party.
**BONGANI:** During a tender? I'd feel very uncomfortable saying yes to that.
**LINDIWE:** Strictly speaking, it's not a payment to anyone.
**NUNO:** The question is whether it could influence the award. And the answer is: it could. The procurement team decides the tender.
**THANDI:** And even if it didn't influence anything, it would look as if it did. Inês said no — politely — and declared it to Tumelo. Third. Bongani?
**BONGANI:** Last year, at a border, an official told me the spare parts would take three days — or twenty minutes, for twenty dollars. No receipt.
**LINDIWE:** Twenty dollars! That's nothing compared with three days of a team waiting.
**THANDI:** In practice, I understand. In principle — and in our policy — it's a facilitation payment. Not allowed, whatever the amount.
**BONGANI:** I didn't pay. I waited the three days. My team was furious.
**NUNO:** Where do we draw the line, though? If twenty dollars is wrong, is a coffee for the official wrong?
**THANDI:** There's a difference between a courtesy and a condition. A coffee nobody asked for is a courtesy. A payment you need to get your parts is a condition. And it would set a precedent: next time, it's fifty.
**BONGANI:** So I was right to wait.
**THANDI:** You were. And next time, call Tumelo from the border. He'll write to the customs office — officially.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Para… | Língua |
|---|---|
| separar o claro do cinzento | It's a grey area. · It's not a grey area. · There's a difference between… and… |
| olhar para a letra e para o espírito | Strictly speaking, … · Technically, …, but… · On the face of it, … |
| pôr a pergunta certa | The question is whether… · It depends on whether… · Where do we draw the line? |
| princípio e prática | In principle, … In practice, … |
| tomar posição | I'd feel uncomfortable + -ing · It would set a bad precedent. · It would look as if… |

**PROF:** Notice Thandi's two tests: does it influence a decision — and would it look as if it did? And her distinction: a courtesy is offered freely; a condition is something you need to pay to get what you're entitled to.
[PAUSA 7 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ It's a grey zone. · ✘ The question is if it is legal or not. (aceitável na fala, mas fraco) · ✔ It's a grey area. · ✔ The question is whether it could influence the award.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Ethical discussions need a thoughtful rhythm: a pause before "but" and before "whether". Listen: Technically, yes, [pausa] but it's in the contract. — The question is [pausa] whether it could influence the award.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat both.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Grey or not? Say it with the language of the lesson.
[ECRÃ: A supplier sends you a calendar and a pen at New Year.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: Strictly speaking, it's a gift, but it's small and occasional — I'd declare it and keep it.]
[ECRÃ: A supplier offers you a weekend at a beach resort during a tender.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: It's not a grey area. The question is whether it could influence the decision — and it could.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Describe an ethical dilemma at work or in daily life — real or imagined. Say why it's a grey area, what the arguments are on each side, where you would draw the line, and what you would do. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — A friend who works at a hospital offered to move my mother up the waiting list. On the face of it, it's kind — she's family. But there's a difference between a favour and taking someone else's place. Strictly speaking, nobody pays anything. The question is whether another patient waits longer because of us — and they would. I'd feel very uncomfortable accepting. So I'd say no, and ask her how the list works instead.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is a small payment to speed up a procedure always wrong, or does it depend on the country and the situation?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 3):** Make a mixed conditional: "Bongani paid twenty dollars last year. Officials ask him for money every time now."
[PAUSA 7 s]
[ECRÃ: If Bongani had paid last year, officials would ask him for money every time now.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Grey areas, the question of whether, courtesy versus condition, and where to draw the line. Next lesson: the words of compliance — and two false friends.

## 6D — Compliance

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6D — Compliance        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário de conformidade, protecção de dados e
  anticorrupção (conflict of interest, due diligence, whistle-blower, consent, kickback), o acento em
  vocabulário jurídico, e evitar os falsos amigos exonerate e devolve.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 3
Gramática-alvo: colocações (declare a conflict of interest, carry out due diligence, blow the whistle on,
  give / withdraw consent, comply with, breach a policy, raise a concern, a code of conduct, a zero-tolerance
  policy); verbos de acção ética (declare, disclose, report, refuse, comply)
Palavras-alvo: compliance, comply with, conflict of interest, due diligence, whistle-blower, kickback,
  bribe, consent, disclose, code of conduct, zero tolerance, raise a concern
Reciclagem: 6A a 6C
Pronúncia: acento em palavras longas de conformidade (compliance, diligence, confidential, disclosure)
Falsos amigos: exonerate (≠ exonerar → dismiss, remove from office; exonerate = ilibar, declarar
  inocente); devolve (≠ devolver → return, give back; devolve = delegar, transferir poderes para um nível
  inferior)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 6D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Every international company has a compliance department and a code of conduct. Today: the words you'll meet in them — and two false friends that could make a newspaper headline say the opposite of what you mean.

### 2. Conformidade (3 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Expressão | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| comply with | All bidders must comply with clause 21. (cumprir) |
| compliance | Tumelo is responsible for compliance. (conformidade) |
| a conflict of interest | Lindiwe's cousin works at the local partner: she must declare a conflict of interest. |

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

### 2. Primeira escuta: os três casos (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 10 de Julho; "The Grey Area" — um podcast (fictício); um estúdio; três microfones]

**PROF:** First: in which case do the two guests agree?

**ZINHLE:** Welcome to The Grey Area. Three cases, two guests, no easy answers. Case one. A supplier sends you an invoice — by mistake, it's ten thousand dollars too low. Do you tell them? Professor?

**SAID:** I agree, but for a more practical reason. They'll find the error in their audit, in three months. And then the question won't be "who made the mistake?" but "why didn't they tell us?". You lose ten thousand dollars of trust to save ten thousand dollars.

**ADEBAYO:** [ri-se] And that's why you're in compliance and I'm in a university.

**SAID:** No. And I'd want to know why she thinks we would.
**ZINHLE:** That was fast!
**ADEBAYO:** Some questions aren't grey. That data isn't hers to give — the clients never gave their consent. And if she'd do it to them, she'd do it to you.
**SAID:** Exactly. And I'd write it down — that she offered, and that we refused. If the competitor goes to court, that paper is your best friend.
**ZINHLE:** So — one case where you disagree, two where you agree. Final word?
**SAID:** Write things down.
**ADEBAYO:** Ask what you'd want, if you were on the other side.
