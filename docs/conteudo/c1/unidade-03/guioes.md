# C1.1 · Unidade 3 — Strategy · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md). Esta unidade tem 7 aulas (3A a 3G).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2 e as unidades 1 e 2 do C1. Novo: condicionais mistos em todos os tempos (revistos do B2 10D e alargados: *If we hadn't worked on Lobito, we wouldn't know the market now. If we were bigger, we'd have won the tender. If we hadn't fixed things with Mussiro, we wouldn't be thinking of Nampula next year.*); *If… were to…* para hipóteses formais sobre o futuro; condicionais com inversão (*Had we known…, Should you need…, Were we to…, Had it not been for…, Were it not for…*); conectores de causa e consequência (*otherwise, in which case, assuming (that), given that, as a result of which*; *provided that, supposing* revistos); a linguagem dos cenários (*the best-case / worst-case scenario, the most likely outcome, a calculated risk, the upside / downside*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** a inversão com advérbios negativos (*Never have I…, Not only…*, unidade 4); as orações participiais (*Having analysed…*) e as passivas de relato produzidas (unidade 5); o *fronting* (unidade 8); as expressões idiomáticas da unidade 9 (*put all our eggs in one basket, hedge our bets*).

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mopane Systems, a Kalandula Tech, a Mussiro Energia, a Morro Azul Energia, a consultora Mbinda Consultores, a empresa do caso da 3F (Seringa Fresh) e o concurso perdido na Namíbia são fictícios. Luanda, Talatona, Nampula e Windhoek existem. O dólar namibiano está ligado ao rand sul-africano, ao par (facto real, usado como argumento).

**Personagens:** **Nuno**, **Thandi**, **Rajesh**, **Bongani**, **Lindiwe**, **Inês Macuácua** (Maputo), **Dr Lindani Mthembu** (director-geral) e **David Nkosi** (director financeiro, B1 8F e C1 1). Na 3F, um caso de estudo fictício gravado: **Simon Ngcobo** (fundador da Seringa Fresh), **Zodwa Ngcobo** (a filha, directora-geral), **Ravi Pillay** (director financeiro) e **Karen Holt** (investidora).

**A história:** Março de 2030. A direcção da Mopane tem 500 000 dólares para crescer num só sítio em 2031 e pede à equipa da Thandi uma proposta para a reunião de 28 de Março. Na segunda, 4, o Nuno e a Thandi olham para trás: o que teria sido diferente (3A); na quarta, 6, a Thandi corrige o memorando formal do Nuno (3B); na sexta, 8, o Rajesh e o Nuno passam pelos números (3C); na quarta, 13, a equipa constrói os cenários (3D); na terça, 19, o Nuno vê um caso de estudo de estratégia num curso *online* (3F); na quinta, 28, apresenta a proposta à direcção (3G).

## 3A — If we hadn't…, we wouldn't be…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3A — If we hadn't…, we wouldn't be…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue formular hipóteses sobre causas e consequências com condicionais
  mistos em todos os tempos (passado → presente, presente → passado, passado → futuro) e com If… were to…
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: If + past perfect, would + infinitivo (agora / no futuro): If we hadn't worked on Lobito,
  we wouldn't know the market now / we wouldn't be opening there next year; If + past simple (estado
  actual), would have + particípio: If we were bigger, we'd have won the tender; If + were to (hipótese
  formal sobre o futuro): If we were to open in Windhoek, we'd need a partner
Palavras-alvo: grow, expand, tender, local presence, partnership, market, reference, hindsight, board,
  proposal, budget
Reciclagem: condicionais mistos (B2, 10D); I wish / if only (B2, 10A); clivadas (C1, 1)
Pronúncia: as formas reduzidas we'd, wouldn't have ("wouldn't-uv"), hadn't
Contexto: segunda-feira, 4 de Março de 2030, 8:30; o gabinete da Thandi; a direcção pediu uma proposta de
  crescimento para 2031
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 3A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 3
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to unit 3: Strategy. Strategy means thinking about causes and consequences — in the past, the present and the future. Today: mixed conditionals, in all their combinations.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 4 de Março, 8:30; o gabinete da Thandi; no quadro branco, três palavras: "Luanda? Nampula? Windhoek?"]

**THANDI:** The board has five hundred thousand dollars to grow in one place in 2031. One place. They want our proposal on the twenty-eighth.
**NUNO:** Only one? Then we need to know why we're strong where we are. Let's look back first.
**THANDI:** Go on.
**NUNO:** Angola. If Mopane hadn't worked with Kalandula on the Lobito project, we wouldn't know the Angolan market now. And if we didn't know it, Tômbwa would never have happened.
**THANDI:** True. And Mozambique — if you hadn't saved the Mussiro contract last month, we wouldn't be talking about Nampula at all.
**NUNO:** [sorri] I had help. And Namibia?
**THANDI:** Namibia is the painful one. We lost a big tender in Windhoek last year. Two million dollars.
**NUNO:** Why?
**THANDI:** If we had an office there, we'd have won it. The client chose a company with people on the ground. And if we were bigger, we'd have had a better price.
**NUNO:** So the lesson is local presence.
**THANDI:** The lesson is local presence. Now the future. If we were to open in Windhoek, we'd need the whole budget — and we'd have no partner.
**NUNO:** And if we were to choose Luanda, we could share the cost with Kalandula.
**THANDI:** Careful. You're from Luanda. The board will say that.
**NUNO:** Then I'll need very good numbers. If I didn't believe in it, I wouldn't propose it.
**THANDI:** Good. Bring me the numbers on Friday — for all three.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Três combinações**

[ECRÃ:]

| Condição | Resultado | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| no passado (*had* + particípio) | no presente ou no futuro (*would* + infinitivo) | If Mopane **hadn't worked** on Lobito, we **wouldn't know** the market now. · If you **hadn't saved** the contract, we **wouldn't be talking** about Nampula. |
| no presente, um estado (*past simple*) | no passado (*would have* + particípio) | If we **had** an office there, we**'d have won** it. · If we **were** bigger, we**'d have had** a better price. |
| no futuro, hipótese formal (*were to*) | no futuro (*would*) | If we **were to open** in Windhoek, we**'d need** the whole budget. |

**PROF:** You learned the first two in B2. At C1, you choose freely: the "if" part and the result can be in any time. Ask yourself two questions: when is the condition? When is the result?
[PAUSA 7 s]

**PROF:** Notice the second type: "If we had an office there, we'd have won it." The condition is a permanent situation — we don't have an office, not then and not now. The result is in the past.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**PROF:** And "were to" makes a future hypothesis sound more distant and more formal: perfect for strategy meetings.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ If we would have had an office, we would win. · ✘ If we were to opened… · ✔ If we had had an office then, we would have won. · ✔ If we had an office, we would have won. · ✔ If we were to open…]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In speech, these forms are reduced. Listen: We'd have won it. ["we'd-uv won it"] We wouldn't know the market. [wouldn't, com o t quase mudo]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: If we had an office there, we'd have won it.
[PAUSA 7 s]
**PROF:** If you hadn't saved the contract, we wouldn't be talking about it.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make a mixed conditional.
[ECRÃ: I didn't learn English as a child. → I need this course now.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: If I had learned English as a child, I wouldn't need this course now.]
[ECRÃ: I'm not very organised. → I missed the deadline last week.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: If I were more organised, I wouldn't have missed the deadline last week.]
[ECRÃ: open a second shop next year → need a loan] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: If we were to open a second shop next year, we'd need a loan.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think of a decision your company, school, town or family made five years ago. How would things be different now if it had decided otherwise? And is there a current situation that has changed something in the past? About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Five years ago, my town decided to build a market near the bus station. If they hadn't done that, the street sellers would still be working on the main road, and the traffic would be terrible today. On the other hand, the market has no parking. If the town had more money, they'd have built a car park too. And if they were to build one now, they'd have to close the market for months.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it useful to think about "what would have happened if…", or is it a waste of time? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 10A):** Express a regret with "I wish": "We lost the Windhoek tender."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: I wish we hadn't lost the Windhoek tender. / I wish we had had an office in Windhoek.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! Past to present, present to past, and "were to" for the future. Next lesson: the formal version — "Had we known…".

## 3B — Had we known…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3B — Had we known…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar condicionais com inversão (Had we known…, Should you
  need…, Were we to…, Had it not been for…, Were it not for…) no registo formal, e saber quando soam
  naturais e quando soam pomposos.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: Had + sujeito + particípio (= If… had…); Should + sujeito + infinitivo (= If… happens to /
  If…, menos provável); Were + sujeito + to + infinitivo (= If… were to…); Had it not been for… (= se não
  fosse…, no passado); Were it not for… (= se não fosse…, no presente); a negativa sem contracção (Had we
  not…, nunca Hadn't we…)
Palavras-alvo: memo, formal, board paper, recommendation, approve, approval, partner, assumption, register,
  pompous
Reciclagem: 3A; atenuação (C1, 2A); escrita formal (B2, 4)
Pronúncia: o acento no auxiliar no início (Had we known…, Should the board…)
Contexto: quarta-feira, 6 de Março, 15:00; o gabinete da Thandi; o primeiro rascunho do memorando do Nuno
  para a direcção
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 3B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In formal writing and in formal speech — a board, a contract, an official letter — "if" sometimes disappears, and the auxiliary comes first: "Had we known…", "Should you need…". Today: conditional inversion — and when not to use it.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 6 de Março, 15:00; a Thandi com o memorando impresso e uma caneta vermelha; o Nuno sentado à frente]

**THANDI:** Your memo is clear. But it sounds like an e-mail to a friend. It's for the board.
**NUNO:** What's wrong with it?
**THANDI:** Nothing is wrong. It's the register. Look: "If we had had an office in Windhoek, we would have won the tender." For the board, I'd write: "Had we had an office in Windhoek, we would have won the tender."
**NUNO:** "Had we had" — two "hads"?
**THANDI:** It looks strange, but it's correct. The first one replaces "if". Next: "If the board approves the Luanda option…" — in a board paper: "Should the board approve the Luanda option, the office could open in January."
**NUNO:** "Should" — so it's less certain?
**THANDI:** A little more distant, and more polite. And here: "If we were to open in Windhoek, we would need the full budget." Formal version?
**NUNO:** "Were we to open in Windhoek, we would need the full budget."
**THANDI:** Perfect. And this one is lovely: "If it hadn't been for Kalandula, Tômbwa would never have happened."
**NUNO:** "Had it not been for Kalandula, Tômbwa would never have happened."
**THANDI:** Exactly — and notice: "Had it not been", never "Hadn't it been".
**NUNO:** Should I use this in the meeting too?
**THANDI:** One or two, at the right moment, yes. "Were it not for the currency risk, Luanda would be the obvious choice" — that sounds very good. But if you say "Should you want a coffee…" to David at the coffee machine, he'll laugh at you.
**NUNO:** [ri-se] Noted.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Quatro inversões**

[ECRÃ:]

| Com *if* | Com inversão (formal) |
|---|---|
| If we had known, … | **Had we known**, … |
| If the board approves / If the board should approve, … | **Should the board approve**, … |
| If we were to open in Windhoek, … | **Were we to open** in Windhoek, … |
| If it hadn't been for Kalandula, … | **Had it not been for** Kalandula, … |
| If it weren't for the currency risk, … | **Were it not for** the currency risk, … |

**PROF:** The auxiliary — "had", "should" or "were" — goes before the subject, and "if" disappears. The meaning doesn't change; the register does.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**A negativa**

[ECRÃ: ✘ Hadn't we signed… · ✔ Had we not signed… · ✘ Shouldn't you need… · ✔ Should you not need…]

**PROF:** In the negative, there's no contraction: "Had we not signed", not "Hadn't we signed" — that would be a question.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Quando usar**

[ECRÃ: ✔ cartas formais, contratos, relatórios, apresentações a uma direcção: Should you have any questions, please contact me. · ✘ conversa informal: "Should you want a coffee…" (soa pomposo)]

**PROF:** "Should you have any questions, please contact me" is very common at the end of formal letters and e-mails. In conversation, one inversion at an important moment sounds elegant. Five sound pompous.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Stress the auxiliary at the start, then continue smoothly. Listen: Had we known… Should the board approve… Were it not for the currency risk…
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: Had it not been for Kalandula, Tômbwa would never have happened.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make it formal.
[ECRÃ: If you need more information, please call me.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Should you need more information, please call me.]
[ECRÃ: If we had known about the delay, we would have warned you.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Had we known about the delay, we would have warned you.]
[ECRÃ: If it weren't for the cost, we would start tomorrow.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Were it not for the cost, we would start tomorrow.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. You're presenting to a board or a formal committee. Explain one past decision and one possible future decision, using at least three inversions — "Had we…", "Should…", "Were we to…", "Had it not been for…". Keep the rest natural. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Two years ago, we moved our servers to the cloud. Had we not done so, last year's flood in the office would have stopped our work for weeks. Had it not been for our IT manager, we would never have considered it. Looking ahead, we're thinking of opening on Saturdays. Were we to do this, we would need two more staff. Should the committee approve the idea, we could start in June.]

**PROF (opinião):** Does formal language make a proposal more convincing, or does it put people off? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 2A):** Hedge this sentence for the board: "The Luanda option is the best."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Arguably, the Luanda option is the strongest. / I'm inclined to think the Luanda option is the best.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Had we known", "Should you need", "Were we to", "Had it not been for" — formal, elegant, and best used in small doses. Next lesson: otherwise, in which case, given that.

## 3C — Otherwise…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3C — Otherwise…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue ligar causas, condições e consequências num raciocínio
  estratégico (otherwise, in which case, assuming that, given that, as a result of which; provided that,
  supposing revistos).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 3
Gramática-alvo: given that + facto (= uma vez que); assuming (that) + hipótese aceite; otherwise (= se não);
  in which case (= e nesse caso); as a result of which (formal, depois de vírgula); provided (that) / as long
  as (condição); supposing (hipótese a examinar)
Palavras-alvo: currency, exchange rate, depreciation, payment terms, cash flow, revenue, in dollars, break
  even, share, depend on
Reciclagem: condicionais (3A, 3B); números aproximados (B2, 2E)
Pronúncia: a pausa antes de "otherwise" e "in which case"
Contexto: sexta-feira, 8 de Março, 10:00; o gabinete do Rajesh; uma folha de cálculo com os três cenários
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 3C; conversa guiada "What if…?"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! A strategy is a chain: this is true, so that follows — unless something else happens. Today: the words that link the chain.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 8 de Março, 10:00; o gabinete do Rajesh; dois ecrãs com uma folha de cálculo; um café para cada um]

**RAJESH:** Right. Luanda first. Given that Kalandula pays half, it's the cheapest option for us: two hundred and fifty thousand.
**NUNO:** And the most likely revenue in year three is two point four million.
**RAJESH:** Assuming the kwanza stays where it is. If it falls thirty percent, you're down to one point six.
**NUNO:** Which is why the contracts should be in dollars, wherever possible. Otherwise the currency eats the profit.
**RAJESH:** Clients won't always agree to dollars.
**NUNO:** In which case we price in kwanzas, but review the price every six months.
**RAJESH:** Fine. Payments?
**NUNO:** Slow — ninety days on average. As long as we keep three months of cash, it's manageable.
**RAJESH:** Nampula. Three hundred thousand, one point five million likely. But Mussiro would be seventy percent of the revenue.
**NUNO:** Supposing Mussiro left — what happens?
**RAJESH:** You drop to six hundred thousand, as a result of which the office loses money from day one.
**NUNO:** And Windhoek?
**RAJESH:** Five hundred thousand — the whole budget, so nothing left for anything else. One point two million, provided we win one tender in five. Two million if we win two. Four hundred thousand if we win none.
**NUNO:** So Windhoek has the best currency and the worst uncertainty.
**RAJESH:** And Luanda has the best numbers and the worst currency. Nampula is cheap but fragile.
**NUNO:** Then it's not about which is perfect. It's about which risk we can manage.
**RAJESH:** [aponta para o ecrã] Exactly. And given that we can do something about currency, but nothing about tenders…
**NUNO:** …Luanda.
**RAJESH:** I didn't say that. You did.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Conector | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| given that | uma vez que (facto) | Given that Kalandula pays half, it's the cheapest. |
| assuming (that) | partindo do princípio de que | Assuming the kwanza stays where it is… |
| otherwise | se não, caso contrário | Contracts should be in dollars. Otherwise the currency eats the profit. |
| in which case | e nesse caso | Clients may refuse dollars, in which case we review prices every six months. |
| as a result of which | e, por isso (formal) | You drop to six hundred thousand, as a result of which the office loses money. |
| provided (that) / as long as | desde que | …provided we win one tender in five. |
| supposing | e se… (hipótese) | Supposing Mussiro left — what happens? |

**PROF:** "Given that" introduces a fact; "assuming" introduces something you accept for now, but could change. "Otherwise" starts a new sentence — or comes after a semicolon. "In which case" and "as a result of which" connect to the sentence before, after a comma.
[PAUSA 7 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ Given that we win the tender… (não é facto) · ✔ Assuming we win the tender… · ✘ We must hurry, otherwise of that we lose. · ✔ We must hurry; otherwise we'll lose it.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Pause briefly before "otherwise" and "in which case" — they open a new branch of the argument. Listen: The contracts should be in dollars. [pausa] Otherwise, the currency eats the profit.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** Clients may refuse, [pausa] in which case we review the price every six months.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Link the ideas.
[ECRÃ: We must book the hall this week. / We'll lose it. (otherwise)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: We must book the hall this week; otherwise we'll lose it.]
[ECRÃ: The supplier may be late. / We'll use the old system for a week. (in which case)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: The supplier may be late, in which case we'll use the old system for a week.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. You're planning something — an event, a move, a small business, a project. Explain your plan as a chain: one fact ("given that"), one assumption ("assuming"), one condition ("provided that"), and what you'll do if things go wrong ("in which case", "otherwise"). About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I'm planning a small catering business. Given that I already cook for three offices on Fridays, I have my first clients. Assuming they stay, I can start with two days a week. I'll rent a kitchen, provided the rent stays under a hundred thousand kwanzas. Prices may go up, in which case I'll cook at home for the first six months. And I need a licence before June; otherwise I can't sell to companies.]

**PROF (opinião):** When you make an important decision, do you think more about the best case or about the worst case? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 2E):** Say it with approximate numbers: "two point four million and six hundred thousand".
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: about two and a half million / just over half a million]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Given that, assuming, otherwise, in which case — the links of a strategy. Try the tutor's "What if…?". Next lesson: three scenarios.

## 3D — Three scenarios

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3D — Three scenarios        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue comparar cenários com graus de certeza e de risco (the best-case
  / worst-case scenario, the most likely outcome, a calculated risk, the upside / downside) e defender uma
  escolha.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: In the best-case / worst-case scenario, …; The most likely outcome is…; The upside / downside
  is (that)…; It's a calculated risk; It's not without risk; on balance; comparar: by far the…, nowhere near
  as… as…, slightly more… than…; graus de certeza (C1, 2B)
Palavras-alvo: scenario, outcome, upside, downside, calculated risk, fragile, on balance, workshop, compare,
  depend
Reciclagem: graus de certeza (C1, 2B); comparativos fortes (B2, 1); 3A a 3C
Pronúncia: o acento contrastivo ao comparar (Luanda is cheaper, but Windhoek is safer)
Contexto: quarta-feira, 13 de Março, 14:00; a sala de reuniões; um workshop com o Bongani, a Lindiwe, o
  Rajesh, a Inês (por vídeo) e o Nuno ao quadro
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 3D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Nobody knows the future, but a good strategist describes it in three versions: the best case, the worst case, and the most likely. Today: the language of scenarios.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 13 de Março, 14:00; a sala de reuniões; o Nuno ao quadro, com uma tabela de três colunas; o Bongani, a Lindiwe e o Rajesh à mesa; a Inês no ecrã]
