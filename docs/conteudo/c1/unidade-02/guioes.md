# C1.1 · Unidade 2 — Diplomacy · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2 e a unidade 1 do C1. Novo: atenuação com verbos e estruturas de distância (*It would appear that…, There seems to be…, seem / appear to (have)…, tend to*), *arguably*, *to some / a certain extent*, *I'm inclined to think…*, *would* de distância (*I would suggest…*); graus de certeza (*I'm fairly / reasonably confident that…, It's highly / somewhat unlikely…, In all likelihood…, There's a good / slim chance…, I'd be surprised if…, I wouldn't rule it out*); recusas diplomáticas (*I'm afraid we're not in a position to…, I'm afraid that falls outside…, What we can do is…, I'd rather not… What I can do is…*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** os condicionais com inversão (*Should you need…*, unidade 3) — nem nas fórmulas de cartas; a inversão com advérbios negativos (unidade 4); as orações participiais (*Having said that…*) e as passivas de relato produzidas (*is thought to…*, unidade 5); o *fronting* e os marcadores *that said, mind you* (unidade 8).

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mussiro Energia (empresa de electricidade de Moçambique), a Halvorsen Radio (fornecedor britânico de rádios) e os interlocutores da 2E são fictícios, como os acontecimentos. Nampula e o porto de Nacala existem; a época das chuvas em Nampula vai de Novembro a Abril.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**, **Thandi**, **Inês Macuácua** (Maputo), **Bongani** (Mopane, instalação). Novos e fictícios: **Dr Américo Cumbe** (moçambicano, director técnico da Mussiro Energia); **Eng.ª Lúcia Tembe** (moçambicana, compras da Mussiro); **Clare Whitlock** (britânica, gestora de conta da Halvorsen Radio); na 2E, **Graham Ashworth** (britânico, investidor), **Achieng Otieno** (queniana, compras de uma operadora na Zâmbia) e **Tumelo** (sul-africano, jurista da Mopane).

**A história:** Fevereiro de 2030. A Mopane assinou com a Mussiro Energia, em 2029, antes de o Nuno chegar, um contrato de 1,2 milhões de dólares para um sistema de monitorização das subestações da província de Nampula. A entrega era a 31 de Janeiro de 2030 e está atrasada: os contentores ficaram retidos no porto de Nacala por um problema de documentos, e a Halvorsen Radio atrasou os últimos rádios. O anterior gestor do projecto avisou a Mussiro por telefone, mas nunca por escrito. O Nuno herda o problema. Na segunda, 4, a Thandi ajuda-o a reescrever um *e-mail* demasiado directo (2A); na quarta, 6, o Nuno pergunta à Inês e ao Bongani quando é que tudo fica pronto (2B); na sexta, 8, recusa as exigências da Halvorsen (2C); na quarta, 13, recebe três chamadas difíceis de interpretar (2E); na segunda, 18, chega a carta da Mussiro, em português, e na terça, 19, há uma videochamada com a Mussiro (2F).

**Os números do contrato (iguais em todas as aulas):**
- Valor do contrato: 1 200 000 dólares. Entrega prevista: 31 de Janeiro de 2030. Nova data estimada: fim de Abril de 2030 (cerca de 13 semanas de atraso).
- Cláusula de penalização: 0,5% do valor por semana de atraso, no máximo 5% — ou seja, 60 000 dólares (13 semanas dariam 6,5%, mas o limite é 5%).
- A Mussiro pede: 20% de desconto (240 000 dólares), um ano adicional de manutenção gratuita e uma nova data garantida por escrito.
- A Halvorsen (2C): os últimos seis rádios chegam com mais duas semanas de atraso; pede à Mopane que pague o transporte aéreo urgente (9 000 dólares).

## 2A — It would appear that…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2A — It would appear that…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue atenuar uma afirmação ou uma crítica com verbos e estruturas de
  distância (It would appear that…, There seems to be…, seem to have…), arguably, to a certain extent e
  I'm inclined to think…, por cortesia e por rigor.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: It would appear / seem that…; There seems / appears to be…; sujeito + seem / appear + to
  (have) + particípio (The forms seem to have been incomplete.); tend to; arguably; to some / a certain
  extent; I'm inclined to think…; would de distância (I would suggest…, I would say…)
Palavras-alvo: delay, paperwork, customs, container, supplier, fault, apologise, draft, realistic,
  in writing
Reciclagem: modais de dedução no passado (B2, 6); clivadas (C1, 1A)
Pronúncia: o ritmo mais lento e o tom calmo de uma frase atenuada
Contexto: segunda-feira, 4 de Fevereiro de 2030, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; o Nuno mostra-lhe o rascunho
  de um e-mail para a Mussiro Energia
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 2A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 2
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to unit 2: Diplomacy. At this level, what you say matters — but how strongly you say it matters just as much. Today: hedging, the art of softening a statement — for politeness, and also for accuracy.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 4 de Fevereiro, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; o Nuno de pé, com o portátil aberto]

**NUNO:** Can you look at this before I send it? It's for Mussiro, in Mozambique. The Nampula project is late, and I've just inherited it.
**THANDI:** [lê em voz alta] "The equipment is stuck at Nacala because customs made a mistake. Halvorsen Radio was late too. It's not our fault. The system will be ready in April." [pausa] Nuno, is all of this true?
**NUNO:** Mostly.
**THANDI:** "Mostly" is the problem. Do we know customs made a mistake? Or did our agent fill in the forms wrong?
**NUNO:** Actually… we don't know yet.
**THANDI:** Then let's say what we know. "It would appear that there was a problem with the paperwork at Nacala."
**NUNO:** "It would appear" — so we're not blaming anyone.
**THANDI:** Exactly. And it's more accurate. Next: "There also seems to have been a delay on the supplier's side."
**NUNO:** And "It's not our fault"?
**THANDI:** Delete it. Arguably, it is partly our fault — nobody told them in writing. I'd write: "To a certain extent, the delay was outside our control. But we should have kept you informed, and I apologise for that."
**NUNO:** That's very honest.
**THANDI:** Clients tend to forgive delays. They don't forgive surprises. And "ready in April"?
**NUNO:** Bongani thinks the end of April.
**THANDI:** Then: "I'm inclined to think the end of April is realistic, and I'll confirm a date once the containers are released."
**NUNO:** It's twice as long — and much softer.
**THANDI:** Softer, yes. But notice — every sentence is now something we can defend.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Estruturas de distância**

[ECRÃ:
Directo: Customs made a mistake.
Atenuado: **It would appear that** there was a problem with the paperwork.
Directo: The supplier was late.
Atenuado: **There seems to have been** a delay on the supplier's side.
Directo: The forms were incomplete.
Atenuado: The forms **seem to have been** incomplete.]

**PROF:** "It would appear that…" and "There seems to be…" put a little distance between you and the statement. You're saying: this is what the facts show — not what I'm accusing someone of. For the past, use "seem to have" plus the past participle.
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Arguably*, *to a certain extent*, *tend to*, *I'm inclined to think***

[ECRÃ: **Arguably**, it is partly our fault. (pode defender-se que…) · **To a certain extent**, the delay was outside our control. (em certa medida) · Clients **tend to** forgive delays. (costumam) · **I'm inclined to think** the end of April is realistic. (inclino-me a pensar)]

**PROF:** "Arguably" introduces a position you can defend, without imposing it. "To a certain extent" limits a statement. "Tend to" turns a rule into a tendency. And "I'm inclined to think" is a soft "I think".
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Atenuar não é mentir**

[ECRÃ: ✘ It would appear that we are a little bit maybe late. (atenuar de mais: soa a fuga) · ✔ The project is late. It would appear that the paperwork was the main cause.]

**PROF:** Don't hedge facts you know — the project is late; say it. Hedge causes, opinions and predictions you're not sure about. Too much hedging sounds evasive.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ It would appear that there were a problem. · ✘ There seem to be a delay. · ✔ It would appear that there was a problem. · ✔ There seems to be a delay.]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Hedged sentences are usually said a little more slowly, with a calm tone and no strong stress. Listen: It would appear that there was a problem with the paperwork.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** To a certain extent, the delay was outside our control.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make it more diplomatic.
[ECRÃ: Your team made an error.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: There seems to have been an error somewhere in the process.]
[ECRÃ: Your price is too high.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Your price seems a little high compared with the market.]
[ECRÃ: Meetings here are always late.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Meetings here tend to start a little late.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. A project you know — at work, at school or in your community — went wrong. Explain to the people affected what happened and why, without blaming anyone and without hiding the facts. Use at least four hedging expressions. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — The new registration system opened two weeks late, and I'm sorry for the trouble that caused. It would appear that the main problem was the data from the old system: some of it seems to have been incomplete. To a certain extent, that was outside our control. But arguably, we should have tested it earlier. I'm inclined to think we'll be fully ready by Monday, and I'll confirm that on Friday.]

**PROF (opinião):** In your culture, is it more polite to be direct or to soften what you say? Does it depend on the situation?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 6):** Say it with a past modal: "I'm almost sure the agent filled in the forms wrong."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The agent must have filled in the forms wrong.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! "It would appear that…", "There seems to have been…", "arguably", "to a certain extent" — hedging for politeness and for accuracy. Next lesson: how sure are you, exactly?

## 2B — I'd be surprised if…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2B — I'd be surprised if…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue exprimir graus finos de certeza e de probabilidade (I'm fairly
  confident that…, It's highly unlikely…, In all likelihood…, I'd be surprised if…, I wouldn't rule it out).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: escala de certeza: I'm certain / (fairly / reasonably) confident that…; In all likelihood…;
  There's a good / fair / slim chance (that)…; It's (highly / somewhat) unlikely (that)…; I'd be surprised
  if + passado (I'd be surprised if it took more than ten days.); I wouldn't rule it out; I doubt (that)…
Palavras-alvo: release, installation, rainy season, estimate, realistic, worst case, chance, rule out,
  confirm, corrected forms
Reciclagem: 2A; be bound to / be likely to (B2, 5)
Pronúncia: o acento nos advérbios que graduam a certeza (fairly, highly, slim)
Contexto: quarta-feira, 6 de Fevereiro, 11:00; uma chamada de vídeo com a Inês, em Maputo; o Bongani na sala
  com o Nuno
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 2B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! "Maybe" is a very poor word for a professional. Is it ninety percent? Fifty? Ten? Today: a scale of certainty — so people know exactly how much to trust what you say.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 6 de Fevereiro, 11:00; o Nuno e o Bongani diante do ecrã; a Inês, em Maputo, com uma pasta de documentos]

**NUNO:** Inês, the big question: when will the containers be released?
**INÊS:** I'm fairly confident it'll be this week. Our agent sent the corrected forms on Monday.
**NUNO:** How confident is "fairly"?
**INÊS:** Let's say eighty percent. I'd be surprised if it took more than ten days.
**NUNO:** And it was the forms — not customs?
**INÊS:** It would appear so. One certificate was missing.
**NUNO:** OK. Bongani, once the equipment is in Nampula, how long for the installation?
**BONGANI:** In all likelihood, six weeks. Five, if everything goes well.
**NUNO:** Could we finish before mid-April?
**BONGANI:** It's highly unlikely. I'd say there's a slim chance — ten percent, maybe.
**NUNO:** And the end of April?
**BONGANI:** Reasonably likely. I'm reasonably confident about the end of April.
**INÊS:** Careful — it's the rainy season in Nampula until April. If the roads to the substations flood, we lose a week or two. I wouldn't rule it out.
**NUNO:** So May is possible.
**BONGANI:** It's somewhat unlikely — but possible. I doubt we'd go beyond mid-May.
**NUNO:** Good. So for the client: end of April as our estimate, mid-May as the worst case. And no promises about the weather.
**INÊS:** [ri-se] Nobody promises anything about the weather in Nampula.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**A escala**

[ECRÃ: uma escala vertical, de cima para baixo:
I'm certain / I'm sure that… (100%)
I'm fairly / reasonably confident that… · In all likelihood… (≈ 80%)
There's a good chance that… (≈ 65%)
There's a fair chance… · I wouldn't rule it out. (≈ 30–50%)
It's somewhat unlikely that… · I doubt that… (≈ 20–30%)
There's a slim chance… · It's highly unlikely that… (≈ 10%)
It's out of the question. (0%)]

**PROF:** Learn these as a ladder. "Fairly confident" is high, but not certain. "Highly unlikely" is low, but not zero. "I wouldn't rule it out" means: it's possible, and you should be ready for it.
[PAUSA 7 s]

***I'd be surprised if…***

[ECRÃ: **I'd be surprised if** it **took** more than ten days. (= é muito provável que demore menos) · **I wouldn't be surprised if** we **needed** May. (= é bastante possível)]

**PROF:** "I'd be surprised if" plus the past simple is an elegant way to say "very probably not". And "I wouldn't be surprised if…" means "quite possibly yes". Notice the past tense after "if" — it's the second conditional.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ It's high unlikely. · ✘ I'm fair confident. · ✘ I'd be surprised if it will take ten days. · ✔ It's highly unlikely. · ✔ I'm fairly confident. · ✔ I'd be surprised if it took ten days.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Stress the word that grades the certainty. Listen: I'm fairly confident. [acento em "fairly"] It's highly unlikely. [acento em "highly"] There's a slim chance. [acento em "slim"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat all three.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Say it with the right degree of certainty.
[ECRÃ: about 80% — the report will be ready on Friday] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I'm fairly confident the report will be ready on Friday.]
[ECRÃ: about 10% — the minister will come] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: It's highly unlikely that the minister will come.]
[ECRÃ: about 90% that it won't cost more than 5,000] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I'd be surprised if it cost more than five thousand.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Talk about three things in your life or work over the next year: one you're fairly confident about, one you wouldn't rule out, and one that's highly unlikely — with a reason for each. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I'm fairly confident I'll finish this English course by the end of the year — I study every evening. I wouldn't rule out a move to another department: my manager mentioned it twice. And it's highly unlikely that I'll buy a car this year — prices have gone up too much. I'd be surprised if they came down before next year.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it better for a manager to give an optimistic estimate or a careful one? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 5):** Say it with "be bound to": "It's absolutely certain that it'll rain in Nampula in March."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: It's bound to rain in Nampula in March.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! A ladder of certainty, from "I'm certain" to "it's highly unlikely" — and "I'd be surprised if…". Next lesson: how to say no.

## 2C — We're not in a position to…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2C — We're not in a position to…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue recusar um pedido e dar más notícias com diplomacia, propondo uma
  alternativa (I'm afraid we're not in a position to… What we can do is…).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: recusar: I'm afraid we're not in a position to…; I'm afraid that falls outside (what we
  agreed / my authority); I'd rather not…; That's not something we can agree to; dar a alternativa: What we
  can do is…; What I can offer is…; adiar: I'll need to check with…, Let me come back to you on that;
  amortecer: I appreciate that…, I understand your position, but…
Palavras-alvo: account manager, air freight, warranty, shipment, at your cost, appreciate, authority, cover,
  firm, goodwill
Reciclagem: clivadas (C1, 1A: What we can do is…); negociar com condições (B2, 3)
Pronúncia: "I'm afraid" com tom descendente e suave, sem pausa longa antes do "no"
Contexto: sexta-feira, 8 de Fevereiro, 15:00; chamada de vídeo entre o Nuno e a Clare Whitlock, da
  Halvorsen Radio, em Inglaterra
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 2C; conversa guiada "Put it diplomatically"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Saying "no" is easy. Saying "no" and keeping a good relationship is a skill. Today: diplomatic refusals — and always an alternative.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 8 de Fevereiro, 15:00; o Nuno ao computador; a Clare Whitlock no ecrã, num escritório em Inglaterra, com chuva na janela]

**CLARE:** Nuno, I'm afraid I have some bad news. The last six radios will be two more weeks.
**NUNO:** Two more weeks? That's… not ideal, Clare. We're already very late with the client.
**CLARE:** I know. So we'd suggest air freight instead of sea — it would save about ten days. It's nine thousand dollars, and we'd ask Mopane to cover it.
**NUNO:** I appreciate the suggestion. But I'm afraid we're not in a position to pay for that. The delay is on your side.
**CLARE:** I understand. But our factory had a supply problem — it wasn't really our choice.
**NUNO:** I don't doubt it. What we can do is accept the new date — if the radios go by air, at your cost.
**CLARE:** Hmm. I'll need to check with my manager. And one more thing: we'd like the warranty to start on the shipment date.
**NUNO:** I'm afraid that falls outside what we agreed. The contract says the warranty starts on installation.
**CLARE:** Even with a two-week delay on our side?
**NUNO:** Especially with a two-week delay on your side. [sorri] Clare, I'd rather not argue about the contract. What I can offer is this: if the radios arrive by the first of March, we'll write to your manager to say how helpful you've been. You'll be our preferred supplier for the next project.
**CLARE:** [ri-se] That's a very gentle way of saying "pay for the plane".
**NUNO:** It is, isn't it?
**CLARE:** Let me come back to you on Monday. But I think we can do it.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Recusar**

[ECRÃ:]

| Para… | Língua |
|---|---|
| recusar com suavidade | I'm afraid we're not in a position to… · That's not something we can agree to. |
| recusar com o contrato | I'm afraid that falls outside what we agreed. |
| recusar a discussão | I'd rather not… |
| amortecer | I appreciate the suggestion. · I understand your position, but… · I don't doubt it. |
| dar a alternativa | What we can do is… · What I can offer is… |
| ganhar tempo | Let me come back to you on that. · I'll need to check with… |

**PROF:** Notice the structure: soften, refuse, give the reason, offer an alternative. "We're not in a position to" sounds less personal than "we won't" — it's the situation, not your bad will. And the alternative, often with a cleft sentence — "What we can do is…" — keeps the conversation open.
[PAUSA 7 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ No. We don't pay. · ✘ We're not in position of paying. · ✔ I'm afraid we're not in a position to pay for that. What we can do is…]
[PAUSA 5 s]

**PROF:** And notice "not ideal". In diplomatic English, "That's not ideal" often means "That's a serious problem."
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "I'm afraid" is soft and falling, and the "no" comes straight after, without a long pause — a long pause sounds like you might say yes. Listen: I'm afraid we're not in a position to pay for that.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** What we can do is accept the new date.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Refuse with diplomacy and offer an alternative.
[ECRÃ: A client asks for a 30% discount.] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: I'm afraid we're not in a position to offer thirty percent. What we can do is ten percent on a two-year contract.]
[ECRÃ: A colleague asks you to present her report tomorrow.] [PAUSA 8 s] [ECRÃ: I'd rather not present it — it's your work. What I can do is help you prepare tonight.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Someone important — a client, a manager or a relative — asks you for something you can't give: a big discount, a weekend of extra work, or a loan. Refuse with diplomacy, give the reason, and offer an alternative. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I really appreciate that you thought of me, and I understand why you need it. But I'm afraid I'm not in a position to work this weekend: my daughter has an important exam on Monday. That's not something I can move. What I can do is come in early on Monday and Tuesday. And if it helps, I'd be happy to check the figures from home on Sunday evening.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it harder to say no to a client, to a manager or to a family member? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 3):** Make a conditional offer, in negotiation style: "accept the new date" / "the radios go by air at your cost".
[PAUSA 7 s]
[ECRÃ: We can accept the new date, provided that the radios go by air at your cost.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Soften, refuse, give the reason, offer an alternative. Try the tutor's "Put it diplomatically". Next lesson: the language of corporate communication — and what it really means.

## 2D — Corporate communication

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2D — Corporate communication        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue reconhecer e usar a linguagem institucional e os eufemismos mais
  comuns (challenging, an issue, not ideal, going forward), usar a entoação descendente-ascendente da
  reserva, e evitar os falsos amigos gentle e ingenuous.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 3
Gramática-alvo: eufemismos e o seu sentido real (challenging = difícil, mau; an issue = um problema; not
  ideal = mau; a slight delay = um atraso; we're reviewing it = ainda não decidimos; I'll bear it in mind =
  provavelmente não; going forward = a partir de agora; let someone go = despedir); adjectivos "suaves"
  (a little, slightly, somewhat, not entirely)
Palavras-alvo: challenging, issue, not ideal, slight, going forward, bear in mind, let go, review,
  stakeholder, transparent
Reciclagem: atenuação (2A); certeza (2B); recusas (2C)
Pronúncia: a entoação descendente-ascendente (It's possible… ; In theory… ; Usually…) que sugere "mas"
Falsos amigos: gentle (≠ gentil → kind, polite; gentle = suave, delicado, brando); ingenuous (≠ engenhoso →
  ingenious; ingenuous = ingénuo, cândido)
Contexto: ecrãs com frases de e-mails, comunicados e reuniões, e a sua "tradução"
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 2D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Companies rarely say "This is a disaster." They say "This is challenging." Today: corporate language — what it says, what it means — plus a melody that says "but" without saying it, and two false friends.

### 2. O que se diz e o que se quer dizer (3 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Diz-se | Quer-se dizer |
|---|---|
| It's been a challenging quarter. | Foi um trimestre mau. |
| We have an issue with the server. | Temos um problema (às vezes grave). |
| That's not ideal. | Isso é mau. |
| There's been a slight delay. | Há um atraso (talvez não tão pequeno). |
| We're reviewing the proposal. | Ainda não decidimos (e talvez não decidamos). |
| I'll bear it in mind. | Provavelmente não. |
| Going forward, we'll… | A partir de agora, vamos… (e antes não o fizemos) |
| We had to let ten people go. | Despedimos dez pessoas. |
| I'm not entirely convinced. | Não concordo. |

**PROF:** Repeat: challenging. An issue. Not ideal. Going forward. Bear it in mind.
[PAUSA 8 s]

**PROF:** You need to understand this language — and use it with care. Used well, it protects relationships. Used too much, it sounds like you're hiding something.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 3. Suavizar com advérbios (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: The price is high. → The price is **a little / slightly / somewhat** high. · I'm not convinced. → I'm **not entirely** convinced. · It's a problem. → It's **a bit of** a problem.]

**PROF:** Small words, big effect. "Not entirely convinced" is much easier to hear than "not convinced". And "somewhat" is more formal than "a bit".
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: a entoação descendente-ascendente (2 min)

**PROF:** In English, the voice can go down and then up at the end of a phrase, to suggest there's something you're not saying — usually "but". Listen: It's possible… [a voz desce e sobe: "mas não acredito"] — In theory, yes… [desce e sobe: "na prática, não"] — Usually… [desce e sobe: "mas hoje não"]
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Compare: It's possible. [só a descer: "sim, é possível"] — It's possible… [a descer e a subir: "mas duvido"]
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: In theory, yes…
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** This melody is very diplomatic: you express doubt without saying "no".
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **gentle** ≠ gentil]

**PROF:** "Gentle" means soft, calm, not rough: "a gentle voice", "a gentle reminder", "be gentle with him — he's had a bad week".
(PT: "Gentil", no sentido de amável ou educado, é "kind" ou "polite": "foi muito gentil da sua parte" → "that was very kind of you". Um "gentle reminder" é um lembrete delicado.)
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **ingenuous** ≠ engenhoso]

**PROF:** "Ingenuous" means innocent, a little naive: "an ingenuous question".
(PT: "Engenhoso" é "ingenious": "uma solução engenhosa" → "an ingenious solution". Chamar "ingenuous" a um engenheiro não é um elogio!)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (2 min 30 s)

**PROF:** What does it really mean?
[ECRÃ: "We'll bear your idea in mind."] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: Provavelmente não vamos usá-la.]
[ECRÃ: "Going forward, all reports will be checked twice."] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: A partir de agora — porque houve um erro.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. You must announce bad news to a team in a short message: the budget has been cut, and a project will stop. Use corporate language with care: honest, but gentle. Then say what it really means, in plain words. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — It's been a challenging year, and I'm afraid the budget for next year has been reduced. That means we won't be able to continue the mobile app project. I know this isn't ideal. Going forward, we'll focus on the website. — In plain words: there's less money, the app is cancelled, and we're doing only the website.]

**PROF (opinião):** Does corporate language help people, or does it hide the truth? Give an example.
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF:** Well done! Next lesson: three phone calls — and what people say between the lines.

## 2E — Real English: Between the lines

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2E — Between the lines        Tipo: Real English
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue perceber o que três pessoas querem realmente dizer em três
  chamadas diplomáticas: o que dizem, o que significam e o que não dizem.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2
Língua: A1 ao B2 e a unidade 2 do C1 até à 2D, sem estruturas novas
Vozes: Graham Ashworth (britânico, investidor, muito formal), Achieng Otieno (queniana, compras de uma
  operadora na Zâmbia, cordial e firme), Tumelo (sul-africano, jurista da Mopane, irónico); o Nuno; ruído de
  aeroporto na segunda chamada
Velocidade: natural, com pausas significativas, entoação descendente-ascendente e ironia
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 2E
```

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

**PROF:** On Wednesday, Nuno gets three phone calls. None of the callers says "no", "too expensive" or "you're wrong". But each of them means one of those things. Listen for what they say — and what they mean.

### 2. Primeira escuta: as três chamadas (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 13 de Fevereiro; o Nuno no gabinete, ao telefone três vezes]

**PROF:** First: which caller is the most negative?

[ECRÃ: chamada 1, 9:30 — Graham Ashworth, investidor, Londres]
**ASHWORTH:** Mr Kapinga, thank you for sending the proposal for the training hub. I read it with great interest. It's a very brave proposal.
**NUNO:** Thank you. Do you think your fund might invest?
**ASHWORTH:** Well. It's possible… I'm not sure the committee would be entirely comfortable with the risk at this stage. Perhaps we could revisit it in a year or two, when the pilot has some results.
**NUNO:** I see. Thank you for being honest.
**ASHWORTH:** Not at all. Do keep me informed.

[ECRÃ: chamada 2, 11:00 — Achieng Otieno, compras, operadora de telecomunicações na Zâmbia; ruído de aeroporto]
**ACHIENG:** Nuno, hi — I'm at the airport, so I'll be quick. We've looked at your offer for the network upgrade. The technical side is excellent — really. Your price, though, is quite ambitious.
**NUNO:** How ambitious?
**ACHIENG:** [ri-se] Let's say the other two offers are somewhat more… realistic. We'll certainly bear yours in mind. But if you'd like to look at the numbers again before the twenty-eighth, I'd be very happy to see a new version.
**NUNO:** Understood. You'll have it by Monday.
**ACHIENG:** Perfect. Got to run — they're calling my flight!

[ECRÃ: chamada 3, 16:00 — Tumelo, jurista da Mopane, do outro lado do corredor]
**TUMELO:** Nuno, it's Tumelo from legal. I've read your draft letter to Mussiro. Interesting.
**NUNO:** Interesting good, or interesting bad?
**TUMELO:** With the greatest respect, I'd question whether paragraph three is a good idea. You say you "guarantee" the end of April.
**NUNO:** I wanted to reassure them.
**TUMELO:** I appreciate that. But "guarantee" is a legal word. If it rains in Nampula, it's also an expensive word. You might want to have another look.
**NUNO:** I'll change it today.
**TUMELO:** Wonderful. That's all. [pausa] Oh, and the rest is fine. Mostly.

[ECRÃ: Ashworth — "a very brave proposal", "not entirely comfortable", "revisit it in a year or two" = não vai investir agora · Achieng — "quite ambitious", "bear it in mind" = o preço é demasiado alto; mas convida a uma nova proposta: ainda há hipótese · Tumelo — "with the greatest respect, I'd question…", "you might want to have another look" = está errado; mude isso]

**PROF:** The most negative? Ashworth: his "no" is polite — but it's a no, at least for now. Achieng says "too expensive" — but she opens a door. Tumelo says "you're wrong" — about one word.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 3. Segunda escuta: os pormenores (3 min)

1. What does Ashworth suggest? (to look at the proposal again in a year or two ✓ / to invest after the meeting / to visit Johannesburg)
2. What does Ashworth think of the risk? (His committee wouldn't be comfortable with it. ✓ / It's very low. / He didn't read the proposal.)
3. What does Achieng like about the offer? (the technical side ✓ / the price / the delivery date)
4. When does Achieng want the new version? (before the twenty-eighth ✓ / today / in a year)
5. Which word in Nuno's letter worries Tumelo? ("guarantee" ✓ / "apologise" / "realistic")
6. Why is that word a problem, according to Tumelo? (It's a legal word, and it could be expensive if it rains. ✓ / It's rude. / It's spelled wrong.)

*(Cada resposta repete-se; depois aparecem as perguntas, com 8 s para responder.)*

### 4. Terceira escuta: como disseram (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Listen and repeat — with the same melody.
**PROF:** It's possible… [descendente-ascendente] I'm not sure the committee would be entirely comfortable. [PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** Your price, though, is quite ambitious. [PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** With the greatest respect, I'd question whether that's a good idea. [PAUSA 6 s]

**PROF:** Notice: "With the greatest respect" sounds very polite — but in British English it usually comes before a strong disagreement. And "brave", for a proposal, often means "risky — perhaps foolish".
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. A sua vez (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Now you. Choose one of the three calls, and tell a colleague what the caller really meant, what they didn't say, and what Nuno should do next. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Achieng didn't say "your price is too high" — she said it was "quite ambitious", and that the other offers were "more realistic". So the price is the problem, not the quality. What she didn't say is how much lower we need to go. But she invited a new version before the twenty-eighth, so the door is open. I'd lower the price a little and add something the others don't offer.]

**PROF (opinião):** Would you prefer people to say "no" directly, or to say it diplomatically? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent listening! "Brave", "quite ambitious", "with the greatest respect" — now you can hear the "no" inside the politeness. Next lesson: Nuno and a very unhappy client.

## 2F — Work & Life: The unhappy utility

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2F — The unhappy utility        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue conduzir uma conversa delicada com um cliente insatisfeito:
  reconhecer, atenuar as causas sem negar os factos, dar graus de certeza honestos, recusar o que não pode
  dar e propor um compromisso; e resumir em inglês uma carta formal escrita em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2, 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: atenuação, graus de certeza, recusas diplomáticas e alternativas
Palavras-alvo: terminate, penalty clause, compensation, handover, on site, worst case, goodwill, weekly
  report, in writing, reassure
Reciclagem: clivadas (C1, 1); desculpas formais (B2, 4); concessão (B2, 7)
Pronúncia: tom calmo diante de um cliente zangado; "I'm afraid" descendente; a descendente-ascendente
Mediação: o Nuno resume à Thandi, em inglês, a carta formal da Mussiro Energia, escrita em português
Contexto: segunda-feira, 18 de Fevereiro, 9:00 (a carta) e terça-feira, 19 de Fevereiro, 10:00
  (videochamada com o Dr Américo Cumbe e a Eng.ª Lúcia Tembe, da Mussiro, em Nampula)
Duração: 14 min
Verificação: Lab 2F; conversa livre "The unhappy client"
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! The big conversation of this unit. A client in Mozambique is ready to cancel a 1.2-million-dollar contract. Nuno can't give them what they ask for — and he can't lose them. Watch how he does it.

### 2. A carta (2 min)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 18 de Fevereiro, 9:00; o gabinete da Thandi; uma carta em papel timbrado da Mussiro Energia]

[ECRÃ: a carta, em português: "Exmos. Senhores, Nos termos do contrato assinado em 2029, o sistema de monitorização das subestações da província de Nampula deveria ter sido entregue a 31 de Janeiro de 2030. Até à data, não recebemos qualquer explicação por escrito nem uma nova data de entrega. Nos termos da cláusula 14.ª, reservamo-nos o direito de rescindir o contrato. Antes de tomarmos essa decisão, solicitamos: 1. um desconto de 20% sobre o valor do contrato; 2. um ano adicional de manutenção gratuita; 3. uma nova data de entrega garantida por escrito. Aguardamos a vossa resposta até ao dia 22 de Fevereiro. Com os melhores cumprimentos, Américo Cumbe, Director Técnico."]

**THANDI:** My Portuguese isn't good enough for this. How bad is it?
**NUNO:** Bad, but not hopeless. In short: the system was due on the thirty-first of January, and they say they've had no explanation in writing and no new date. Under clause fourteen, they reserve the right to terminate. Before they decide, they're asking for three things: twenty percent off, an extra year of free maintenance, and a guaranteed new date in writing. They want an answer by Friday.
**THANDI:** "Reserve the right" — so they haven't decided.
**NUNO:** Exactly. It would appear that they want to be taken seriously more than they want to leave.
**THANDI:** Then call them tomorrow. Don't write first. And Nuno — no guarantees.

### 3. A videochamada (5 min)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 19 de Fevereiro, 10:00; o Nuno ao computador; no ecrã, em Nampula, o Dr Américo Cumbe, sério, e a Eng.ª Lúcia Tembe, com uma pasta]

**CUMBE:** Mr Kapinga. I hope you've read our letter.
**NUNO:** I have, Dr Cumbe, and thank you for agreeing to talk. First, you're right that you should have heard from us in writing weeks ago. That was our failure, and I apologise.
**CUMBE:** [pausa] Well. That's a start.
**NUNO:** Let me explain what we know. It would appear that one certificate was missing from the customs papers at Nacala — the containers were released last Friday. There also seems to have been a problem at one of our suppliers; the last radios arrive by air on the first of March.
**TEMBE:** And the new date?
**NUNO:** I'm reasonably confident about the end of April. I'd be surprised if it went beyond the fifteenth of May — but it's the rainy season, so I wouldn't rule out a week or two more.
**CUMBE:** We asked for a guaranteed date.
**NUNO:** I understand. I'd rather not guarantee a date that depends on the rain — that would be a promise I can't keep. What I can guarantee is a written report every Friday, with the exact status of each substation.
**CUMBE:** And our twenty percent?
**NUNO:** I'm afraid we're not in a position to offer twenty percent. The contract has a penalty clause — half a percent a week, up to five percent — and we'll apply it in full: sixty thousand dollars, without discussion.
**CUMBE:** Sixty thousand is not two hundred and forty thousand.
**NUNO:** It isn't. So let me add two things. One: a Mopane engineer on site in Nampula, at our cost, until the handover. Two: two places for your technicians in our new regional training programme in Johannesburg, this year.
**TEMBE:** And the extra year of maintenance?
**NUNO:** A full year falls outside what I can approve, I'm afraid. What I can offer is three extra months.
**TEMBE:** [para o Dr Cumbe, em voz baixa] An engineer on site is arguably worth more than the discount.
**CUMBE:** [longa pausa] Mr Kapinga, you've been honest with us — more than your predecessor. We'll accept, on condition that we receive the reports every Friday. But if we don't have the system by the fifteenth of May, we'll talk again. And not so politely.
**NUNO:** That seems entirely fair. You'll have the first report this Friday — and a letter confirming everything today.
[ECRÃ: depois da chamada, a Thandi, à porta: "That was very gentle — in the English sense."]

### 4. Como se fala com um cliente zangado (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno fez]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Reconhecer o que é justo, sem atenuar | You're right that… I apologise. | You're right that you should have heard from us in writing. |
| 2. Explicar as causas, atenuando | It would appear that… There seems to have been… | It would appear that one certificate was missing. |
| 3. Dar a certeza exacta | I'm reasonably confident… I'd be surprised if… I wouldn't rule out… | I'd be surprised if it went beyond the fifteenth of May. |
| 4. Recusar com uma razão | I'm afraid we're not in a position to… · I'd rather not… | I'd rather not guarantee a date that depends on the rain. |

| 6. Aceitar as condições | That seems entirely fair. | — |

**PROF:** Notice: Nuno doesn't hedge the facts about his own company — "That was our failure, and I apologise" is direct. He hedges the causes he isn't fully sure about, and his predictions. And he never says only "no": each refusal comes with something else.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**A mediação**

**PROF:** The letter is long and formal. Nuno gives Thandi the essentials in plain English: the situation, the threat, the three requests, the deadline. And he reads between the lines: "reserve the right" means they haven't decided yet.
(PT: As cartas formais em português têm fórmulas que não se traduzem à letra: "Exmos. Senhores" não precisa de tradução num resumo oral; "nos termos da cláusula 14.ª" → "under clause fourteen"; "reservamo-nos o direito de rescindir" → "they reserve the right to terminate".)
[PAUSA 6 s]

**PROF:** Now you. A client is angry: you delivered an order late, and they ask for a big discount and a guaranteed date. Speak to them: acknowledge, explain with hedging, give an honest degree of certainty, refuse the discount with a reason, and offer something else. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — You're right that we should have called you sooner, and I apologise. It would appear that the problem was with our transport company. I'm fairly confident the order will arrive on Thursday, and I'd be surprised if it took longer than Monday. I'm afraid we're not in a position to offer fifty percent. What we can do is free delivery on your next three orders, and I'll call you personally on Thursday morning.]

**PROF:** Now the mediation. Summarise this letter in English for a manager who doesn't read Portuguese, in three or four sentences.
[ECRÃ: "Exma. Senhora, Venho por este meio manifestar o meu descontentamento com o serviço de limpeza do nosso escritório durante o mês de Janeiro. Em várias ocasiões, as salas não foram limpas.
