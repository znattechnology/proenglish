# C1.2 · Unidade 9 — Register · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do C1](../unidade-01/guioes.md). Esta unidade tem 7 aulas (9A a 9G).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2, o C1.1 e as unidades 6 a 8. Novo: mudar de registo (formal, neutro, coloquial) na mesma situação; expressões idiomáticas comuns no trabalho (*the ball is in their court, a win-win, call it a day, bite the bullet, back to square one, on the same page, get the ball rolling, a ballpark figure, the elephant in the room, cut corners*), com o sentido exacto e o registo certo; linguagem vaga e aproximativa (*or so, -ish, give or take, sort of, kind of, and so on, and stuff, a bunch of, loads of*); *phrasal verbs* separáveis (*turn down the offer / turn the offer down / turn it down*) e de três partes (*put up with, come up with, get away with, look forward to, run out of, catch up on*). Na 9E, as reduções do registo informal (*gonna, kinda, y'know, gotta*) para reconhecer.

**Não usar nesta unidade:** nada de novo da unidade 10 como estrutura ensinada; a linguagem jurídica fica para ler (unidade 6).

**Nomes, empresas e factos:** a Mopane Systems, a Kalandula Tech, a Mussiro Energia, a Marula Power e a ShopPay (C1 1E) são fictícias, como a recepção da 9F ("Angola–South Africa Business Evening", da câmara de comércio — fictícia). Joanesburgo, Sandton e Luanda existem.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**, **Thandi**, **Rajesh**, **Lindiwe**, **Bongani**, **Inês Macuácua**, **Dr Lindani Mthembu** (director-geral); **Lerato** (sul-africana, da Mopane, em Luanda desde 2027, na equipa conjunta com a Kalandula); **Paulo Mendes** (Kalandula, amigo do Nuno desde o A1); **Kwabena** (ganês, fundador da ShopPay, C1 1E). Novos e fictícios, na 9F: **Mr Oliver Thornton** (britânico, banqueiro) e **Ms Chanda Mulenga** (zambiana, dona de uma empresa de transportes).

**A história:** Outubro de 2030. O escritório conjunto da Mopane e da Kalandula em Luanda abre a 6 de Janeiro de 2031 (decidido na 3G), e o Nuno reorganiza a equipa. A Mussiro decide o concurso do Niassa até ao fim do mês. A instalação da Marula (unidade 7) atrasa duas semanas. Na terça, 1, o Nuno escreve a mesma notícia em três registos (9A); na quinta, 3, espera com o Rajesh pela Mussiro (9B); na sexta, 4, há copos depois do trabalho (9C); na segunda, 7, a Thandi e o Nuno desenham a reorganização (9D); na quinta, 10, o Nuno vai a uma recepção de *networking* (9F); na segunda, 14, dá a notícia da reorganização a três pessoas diferentes (9G).

**A reorganização (igual em todas as aulas):**
- A partir de 6 de Janeiro de 2031, o escritório conjunto em Luanda tem uma responsável da Mopane: a **Lerato**, que deixa a antiga equipa conjunta e passa para a equipa do Nuno.
- A **Inês** passa a responsável por Moçambique (uma promoção).
- A **Lindiwe** fica com os projectos da África do Sul e da Namíbia.
- O **Bongani**, se a Mopane ganhar o Niassa, é o chefe de obra no local; recusou um lugar em Durban para isso.
- O **Rajesh** passa a dividir o tempo entre Joanesburgo e Luanda (finanças dos dois escritórios).
- O **Nuno** fica em Joanesburgo, com uma semana por mês em Luanda. A equipa continua com oito pessoas.

## 9A — Three ways to say it

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 9        Aula: 9A — Three ways to say it        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dizer a mesma coisa em três registos (formal, neutro, coloquial),
  mudando o vocabulário, a gramática e o tom, e escolher o registo certo para cada pessoa.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: formal (passivas, nomes em vez de verbos, inversão e linguagem distante: We regret to
  inform you that the opening has been postponed. Should you require…); neutro (frases activas e
  directas: The opening is two weeks late.); coloquial (contracções, phrasal verbs, linguagem vaga,
  expressões: Bad news — the opening's slipped a couple of weeks.); marcas de registo (inform / tell /
  let know; require / need / want; postpone / put off / push back; however / but / though)
Palavras-alvo: register, formal, neutral, informal, postpone, push back, inform, require, rude,
  cold
Reciclagem: inversão condicional (C1, 3B); passivas (B2, 4)
Pronúncia: o tom de cada registo: formal (ritmo regular, palavras completas), coloquial (contracções, ritmo
  rápido)
Contexto: terça-feira, 1 de Outubro de 2030, 9:00; o Nuno ao computador; a abertura da instalação da Marula
  passa de 15 para 29 de Outubro
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 9A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 9
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to unit 9: Register. At C1, being correct isn't enough — you need to sound right for the person in front of you. Today: the same news, in three registers.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 1 de Outubro, 9:00; o Nuno ao computador, com três janelas abertas: um e-mail para o director-geral da Marula Power, o grupo da equipa e uma mensagem para a Lindiwe; a Thandi passa com um café]

**THANDI:** Three windows. What are you writing?
**NUNO:** The same news, three times. The Marula opening has moved from the fifteenth to the twenty-ninth. The control software failed its last test.
**THANDI:** Read me the first.
**NUNO:** To Marula's managing director: "Dear Mr Sithole, I regret to inform you that the opening of the storage facility has been postponed until 29 October, following an unsuccessful test of the control software. Should you require further details, I would be happy to arrange a call."
**THANDI:** Formal, distant, no blame. Good. Second?
**NUNO:** The team group: "Hi all — the Marula opening is now on 29 October, two weeks later than planned. The control software failed its last test. Lindiwe's team is fixing it. Questions to me."
**THANDI:** Clear, neutral, everything people need. And Lindiwe?
**NUNO:** [ri-se] "Ugh. Software's flopped again, so we've pushed the opening back a couple of weeks — 29th-ish. Coffee later? I'll fill you in."
**THANDI:** "Twenty-ninth-ish" — she'll love that. Imagine sending that one to Mr Sithole.
**NUNO:** I'd be looking for a new job.
**THANDI:** And the first one to Lindiwe?
**NUNO:** She'd think I was angry with her.
**THANDI:** Exactly. Register isn't decoration. Get it wrong one way, you sound rude. Get it wrong the other way, you sound cold.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**A mesma notícia, três registos**

[ECRÃ:]

| | Formal | Neutro | Coloquial |
|---|---|---|---|
| a notícia | the opening has been postponed | the opening is now on 29 October | we've pushed the opening back |
| a razão | following an unsuccessful test | the software failed its last test | software's flopped again |
| o tempo | until 29 October | two weeks later than planned | a couple of weeks — 29th-ish |
| a oferta | Should you require further details, I would be happy to arrange a call. | Questions to me. | I'll fill you in. |

**PROF:** Look at what changes. Formal English uses passives, nouns instead of verbs ("an unsuccessful test" instead of "it failed"), longer Latin-based words, and inversion ("Should you require…"). Neutral English is active and direct. Colloquial English uses contractions, phrasal verbs ("push back", "fill in") and vague language ("a couple of", "-ish").
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Pares de registo**

[ECRÃ: inform → tell → let know · require → need → want · postpone → delay → push back / put off · however → but → though (no fim) · purchase → buy → get · assist → help → give a hand]

**PROF:** Many English words come in pairs or trios like these. The formal one often comes from Latin; the informal one is often a phrasal verb.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ (ao director) We've pushed it back a bit. · ✘ (a uma colega amiga) We regret to inform you… · ✔ escolher pela pessoa e pela relação, não só pelo assunto]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Register also lives in the voice. Formal: full forms, regular rhythm. Listen: The opening has been postponed until the twenty-ninth of October. — Colloquial: contractions, faster. We've pushed it back a couple of weeks.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat both.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make it more formal.
[ECRÃ: We need more info before we can say yes.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: We require further information before we can approve the request.]
**PROF:** Make it more colloquial.
[ECRÃ: The meeting has been postponed until Friday.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: We've put the meeting off till Friday.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Your company's office will be closed next Monday because of a water problem. Tell the news three times: to an important client (formal), to your team (neutral) and to a close colleague (colloquial). About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — To the client: "Please be advised that our office will be closed on Monday due to essential repairs to the water supply. Should you require urgent assistance, please call our emergency number." — To the team: "Hi everyone, the office is closed on Monday because of a water problem. Please work from home." — To my colleague: "No water on Monday, so no office! Working from home, then. Fancy a coffee somewhere?"]

**PROF (opinião):** Is English more formal or less formal at work than your first language? Give an example.
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 3B):** Make it formal with an inversion: "If you need anything, call me."
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: Should you need anything, please do not hesitate to call me.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! Formal, neutral, colloquial — and the right one for each person. Next lesson: idioms — what they mean, and when not to use them.

## 9B — The ball is in their court

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 9        Aula: 9B — The ball is in their court        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar expressões idiomáticas comuns no trabalho com o sentido exacto
  e no registo certo, e evitá-las quando o ouvinte pode não as perceber.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: the ball is in their court (agora é com eles); a win-win (bom para os dois lados); call it a
  day (dar o dia por terminado); bite the bullet (aceitar uma coisa desagradável); back to square one (de
  volta ao início); on the same page (de acordo, com a mesma informação); get the ball rolling (pôr as coisas
  a andar); a ballpark figure (um número aproximado); the elephant in the room (o problema de que ninguém
  fala); cut corners (fazer as coisas mal para poupar); evitar clichés (think outside the box)
Palavras-alvo: idiom, decision, wait, tender, price, figure, references, cliché, word for word, plain
Reciclagem: graus de certeza (C1, 2B); 9A
Pronúncia: o acento na palavra-chave da expressão (the ball's in their COURT — marcado por indicação)
Contexto: quinta-feira, 3 de Outubro, 18:00; o escritório quase vazio; o Nuno e o Rajesh à espera de notícias
  da Mussiro sobre o concurso do Niassa
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 9B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Native speakers use idioms all the time at work: "the ball's in their court", "a win-win", "let's call it a day". Today: twelve common ones — exactly what they mean, and when they cause problems.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 3 de Outubro, 18:00; o escritório quase vazio; o Nuno a olhar para o e-mail; o Rajesh com o casaco vestido]

**RAJESH:** Still checking your e-mail? We've done everything. The ball's in their court now.
**NUNO:** I know. Mussiro said "by the end of October". It's the third.
**RAJESH:** Then stop refreshing. Let's call it a day.
**NUNO:** What if we've lost it? Back to square one, after three months of work.
**RAJESH:** Look — the price was fair, the references are strong, and we reported the "consultant". If they're honest, it's a win-win: they get a team that knows the region; we get the contract.
**NUNO:** And if they're not?
**RAJESH:** Then we don't want it. You didn't cut corners, and you're not going to start now.
**NUNO:** There's an elephant in the room, though. If we win, Bongani has to be in Niassa for eighteen months. Nobody's asked him.
**RAJESH:** Then bite the bullet and ask him tomorrow. Better now than in November.
**NUNO:** You're right. And if we win, we need to get the ball rolling fast. Can you give me a ballpark figure for the first three months?
**RAJESH:** Six hundred thousand or so. I'll get you the real number on Monday. Are we on the same page?
**NUNO:** We are. [pausa] Rajesh — one thing. When I write to Inês, I won't say "the ball's in their court".
**RAJESH:** Why not?
**NUNO:** Last week I told her a delay was "not my cup of tea". She translated it word for word. She thought I was offering her tea.
**RAJESH:** [ri-se] Fair point. Idioms are for people who share them.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Expressão | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| the ball is in their court | agora é com eles (a decisão é deles) | We've done everything. The ball's in their court. |
| call it a day | dar o dia por terminado | Let's call it a day. |
| back to square one | de volta ao início | If we lose, it's back to square one. |
| a win-win | bom para os dois lados | It's a win-win. |
| cut corners | fazer mal para poupar tempo ou dinheiro | You didn't cut corners. |
| the elephant in the room | o problema de que ninguém fala | There's an elephant in the room. |
| bite the bullet | aceitar fazer algo desagradável | Bite the bullet and ask him. |
| get the ball rolling | pôr as coisas a andar | We need to get the ball rolling fast. |
| a ballpark figure | um número aproximado | Give me a ballpark figure. |
| on the same page | de acordo, com a mesma informação | Are we on the same page? |

**PROF:** These ten are common in business English, and you'll hear them in meetings, calls and films. Use them with colleagues and in neutral or informal situations — not in a formal letter.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Quando não usar**

[ECRÃ: ✘ com quem não fala inglês como primeira língua e pode não conhecer a expressão · ✘ em cartas formais e contratos · ✘ clichés gastos: think outside the box, at the end of the day (repetido), going forward (repetido) · ✔ em inglês simples: "The decision is theirs now."]

**PROF:** Nuno's lesson: idioms are for people who share them. With an international team, plain English is often more respectful — and clearer.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ The ball is in their field. · ✘ Let's call it the day. · ✘ back to the square one · ✔ the ball's in their court · ✔ call it a day · ✔ back to square one]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Idioms are said quickly, as one unit, with the stress on the key word. Listen: The ball's in their court. [acento em "court"] Let's call it a day. [acento em "day"] It's a win-win. [acento no segundo "win"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat all three.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Which idiom fits?
[ECRÃ: We've sent our offer. Now we wait for them to decide.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: The ball's in their court.]
[ECRÃ: The test failed. We have to start again from the beginning.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: We're back to square one.]
[ECRÃ: Nobody wants to talk about the budget problem.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: The budget is the elephant in the room.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Tell a colleague about a decision you're waiting for — at work, at school or in your life — using at least four idioms naturally. Then say the most important sentence again, in plain English, for someone who doesn't know the idioms. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I've applied for a transfer to the Benguela office. I've done everything I can — the ball's in their court now. If they say no, it's back to square one. But it could be a win-win: they need someone there, and I want to be near my parents. The elephant in the room is my manager — she doesn't know yet. So tomorrow I'll bite the bullet and tell her. In plain English: I'm waiting for their decision, and tomorrow I'll tell my manager.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should people in international teams avoid idioms? Why, or why not?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 2B):** How likely is it that Mopane wins the Niassa tender? Use the certainty scale.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Exemplo: I'm reasonably confident — but I wouldn't rule out a cheaper bid winning.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! The ball's in their court, a win-win, back to square one, the elephant in the room — and plain English when your listener may not share them. Next lesson: the vague language fluent speakers use all the time.

## 9C — Fifty or so

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 9        Aula: 9C — Fifty or so        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar linguagem vaga e aproximativa de forma natural (or so, -ish,
  give or take, sort of, kind of, and so on, and stuff, a bunch of, loads of) e saber onde é informal demais.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: números e horas (fifty or so, sevenish, thirty-ish, give or take a few); quantidades (a bunch
  of, loads of, a couple of, a few); suavizar (sort of, kind of, a bit); listas (and so on, and stuff like
  that, or something); registo: em conversa informal sim; em relatórios e com clientes, não (aí: about,
  approximately, roughly — C1 5C)
Palavras-alvo: vague, approximate, journalists, opening, satellite, weekend, shade, roughly, loads, stuff
Reciclagem: números aproximados formais (C1, 5C); 9A e 9B
Pronúncia: o ritmo descontraído da conversa: reduções e acento leve em "-ish", "or so"
Contexto: sexta-feira, 4 de Outubro, 18:30; uma esplanada em Braamfontein; copos depois do trabalho com a
  Lindiwe e o Bongani
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 9C; conversa guiada "Formal or friendly?"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In a report, you say "approximately fifty". With friends, you say "fifty or so". Fluent speakers are often vague on purpose — it sounds relaxed and natural. Today: vague language, and where it belongs.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 4 de Outubro, 18:30; uma esplanada em Braamfontein; a Lindiwe, o Bongani e o Nuno com bebidas e um prato de petiscos]

**LINDIWE:** So, the Marula software. We ran the test about fifty times today, give or take.
**NUNO:** And?
**LINDIWE:** It sort of works. It crashed twice — at sevenish, and again after lunch. We'll get there.
**BONGANI:** How many people are coming to the opening on the twenty-ninth?
**NUNO:** A hundred or so. The community, Marula, a bunch of journalists, the minister's office — if they come — and so on.
**BONGANI:** Journalists. Great. I'll hide behind a container.
**LINDIWE:** [ri-se] You'd need loads of containers.
**NUNO:** Bongani — can I ask you something kind of serious?
**BONGANI:** Uh-oh.
**NUNO:** If we win Niassa, it's eighteen months on site. Eighteen months or so. Would you do it?
**BONGANI:** [pausa] As it happens, Durban offered me a job last week. A nice office, near the sea, and stuff.
**NUNO:** Oh.
**BONGANI:** I turned it down. Niassa is a real project. Durban is… a nice office.
**NUNO:** Seriously? Bongani, thank you.
**BONGANI:** Don't thank me. Just get me a decent satellite connection and a coffee machine. And a fan. Or two.
**LINDIWE:** Thirty-ish degrees in the shade, Niassa. You'll need three.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Para… | Linguagem vaga | Formal (C1 5C) |
|---|---|---|
| números | fifty or so · fifty, give or take · a hundred-ish | approximately fifty · roughly a hundred |
| horas e idades | sevenish · thirty-ish · in his forties | at about seven · approximately thirty |
| quantidades | a bunch of journalists · loads of containers · a couple of weeks | several journalists · many containers · two weeks |
| suavizar | It sort of works. · kind of serious | It partly works. · fairly serious |
| listas | …and so on · …and stuff · …or something | …among others · …etc. (escrito) |

**PROF:** Vague language does two jobs: it gives a number without pretending to be exact, and it makes the conversation relaxed. "It sort of works" is honest — it works, but not completely.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Onde não usar**

[ECRÃ: ✘ num relatório: "Revenue was four million-ish." · ✘ a um cliente formal: "We'll finish in a couple of weeks or something." · ✔ entre colegas, amigos, numa conversa descontraída]

**PROF:** In unit 5, Nuno presented "just over four point six million" to the board. Here, with friends, he says "a hundred or so". Same skill — approximating — in two registers.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ fifty or so of people · ✘ seven-ishes · ✔ fifty people or so / fifty or so people · ✔ sevenish]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In relaxed speech, vague expressions are light and quick. Listen: A hundred or so. [leve] At sevenish. [acento leve em "ish"] It sort of works. ["sorta works"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make it vague and relaxed.
[ECRÃ: About 30 people came.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Thirty or so people came.]
[ECRÃ: Let's meet at approximately 8 p.m.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Let's meet at eightish.]
[ECRÃ: There were many cars, buses, trucks…] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: There were loads of cars, buses and so on.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. A friend asks about your last weekend or a recent trip. Tell them, relaxed and natural, with at least five vague expressions: or so, -ish, give or take, sort of, a bunch of, loads of, and so on. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — It was great, thanks! We drove to Cabo Ledo on Saturday — left at sixish, got there three hours later, give or take. There were loads of people on the beach, a bunch of surfers and so on. We had grilled fish — kind of expensive, but amazing. We stayed till fiveish and got home around nine or so. I'm sort of tired today, to be honest.]

**PROF (opinião):** Does vague language sound lazy, or does it sound natural? When?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 5C):** Say it formally, for a report: "We ran the test fifty times, give or take."
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: The test was run approximately fifty times.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Or so, -ish, give or take, sort of, loads of — relaxed with friends, never in a report. Try the tutor's "Formal or friendly?". Next lesson: phrasal verbs — and where the object goes.

## 9D — Turn it down

```
Curso: C1.2        Unidade: 9        Aula: 9D — Turn it down        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar phrasal verbs separáveis com o objecto na posição certa
  (turn down the offer / turn the offer down / turn it down) e phrasal verbs de três partes (put up with,
  come up with, get away with).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 4
Gramática-alvo: separáveis: nome antes ou depois da partícula (set up the office / set the office up);
  pronome sempre no meio (set it up — nunca set up it); não separáveis (look after, deal with: look after
  them); de três partes, nunca separáveis (put up with, come up with, get away with, look forward to, run
  out of, catch up on, keep up with); registo: phrasal verbs são neutros ou informais; o equivalente formal
  (establish, decline, tolerate)
Palavras-alvo: reorganisation, rumours, office, set up, take over, turn down, hand over, come up with,
  put off, look forward to
Reciclagem: 9A (registo); elipse (C1, 6A)
Pronúncia: o acento na partícula (turn it DOWN, set it UP — marcado por indicação)
Contexto: segunda-feira, 7 de Outubro, 10:00; o gabinete da Thandi; o organigrama da equipa no quadro branco
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 9D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Phrasal verbs are everywhere in neutral and informal English — and one small rule decides whether you sound fluent: where the object goes. "Turn it down", never "turn down it". Today: separable and three-part phrasal verbs.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 7 de Outubro, 10:00; o gabinete da Thandi; um organigrama no quadro branco, com nomes em post-its]

**THANDI:** The Luanda office opens on the sixth of January. Who's going to set it up?
**NUNO:** Lerato. She's been in Luanda since 2027 — she knows everyone. She'll take over the office from day one, and she'll join my team.
**THANDI:** Has she said yes?
**NUNO:** She has. She came up with half the plan herself.
**THANDI:** And Mozambique?
**NUNO:** Inês takes it over. She's earned it. I'm handing the Mussiro relationship over to her completely.
**THANDI:** Bongani?
**NUNO:** He turned down an offer from Durban. If we win Niassa, he's site manager there. If we don't, he stays with Lindiwe.
**THANDI:** He turned it down? For eighteen months in Niassa?
**NUNO:** He says he can put up with the heat if he gets a good coffee machine.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Separáveis: o objecto**

[ECRÃ:]

| Com um nome | Com um pronome |
|---|---|
| set up **the office** / set **the office** up | set **it** up ✔ · ~~set up it~~ ✘ |
| take over **the office** / take **the office** over | take **it** over |
| turn down **the offer** / turn **the offer** down | turn **it** down |
| hand over **the relationship** / hand **the relationship** over | hand **it** over |
| put off **the meeting** / put **the meeting** off | put **it** off |

**PROF:** With a noun, the object can go before or after the particle. With a pronoun — it, them, her — it always goes in the middle: "turn it down", "set it up". "Turn down it" is one of the most common C1 mistakes.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Não separáveis e de três partes**

[ECRÃ: Não separáveis: look after (the money / it) · deal with (the problem / it) · Três partes — nunca se separam: put up with (the heat / it) · come up with (a plan / it) · get away with (it) · look forward to (it / + -ing) · run out of (time) · catch up on (the news) · keep up with (the work)]

**PROF:** Some phrasal verbs never separate: "look after it", "deal with it". And three-part verbs always stay together: "put up with the heat", "put up with it". Notice "look forward to" + -ing: "I'm looking forward to working with you".
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Registo**

[ECRÃ: set up → establish · turn down → decline · put up with → tolerate · put off → postpone · come up with → devise / propose · take over → assume responsibility for]

**PROF:** Phrasal verbs are neutral or informal. In a formal letter, the Latin-based verb is often better: "We regret that we must decline your offer."
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

[PAUSA 3 s]

[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: She turned down the job.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: She turned it down.]
[ECRÃ: We set up the new system.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: We set it up.]
