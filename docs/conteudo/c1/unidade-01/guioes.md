# C1.1 · Unidade 1 — Making the case · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B2](../../b2/unidade-01/guioes.md): tudo em inglês, e o português só nos falsos amigos e na mediação. No C1, o professor fala à velocidade natural, com registo variado; as práticas pedem turnos de 1 minuto e meio a 2 minutos.

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2. Novo: orações clivadas com *what* (*What we need is…*), *all* (*All I'm asking for is…*), *the reason why / the thing that / the person who* (*The reason why it worked is…*) e com *it* (*It was the training that made the difference.*); *do / does / did* enfático (*We do have the budget.*); responder a contra-argumentos (conceder, reformular, devolver a pergunta, voltar ao argumento central).

**Não usar nesta unidade (fica para unidades seguintes do C1):** a atenuação sistemática (*It would appear that…, arguably*, unidade 2); os condicionais com inversão (*Had we known…*, unidade 3); a inversão com advérbios negativos (*Never have I…*, unidade 4); as orações participiais (*Having analysed…*) e as passivas de relato produzidas (*is thought to have…*, unidade 5); o futuro no passado como estrutura ensinada (unidade 7); o *fronting* (*Expensive it may be…*, unidade 8). As expressões fixas que já vêm do B2 (*What worries me is…, The good news is…*) continuam.

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Mopane Systems, a Kalandula Tech e a Morro Azul Energia são fictícias, como o centro de formação proposto, a competição de *start-ups* da 1E e as três empresas que nela se apresentam. Joanesburgo, Sandton e Braamfontein existem.

**Personagens:** **Nuno** (Project Lead para a África Austral na Mopane, em Joanesburgo, desde 7 de Janeiro de 2030 — B2 10G); **Thandi** (agora chefe do Nuno, Head of Projects); **Rajesh** (Mopane, custos, de Durban — B2 1E); **Bongani** e **Lindiwe** (Mopane — B2 1E); **David Nkosi** (sul-africano, director financeiro da Mopane desde o B1 8F, onde aprovou o orçamento do projecto do Lobito; céptico). Novos e fictícios: **Dr Lindani Mthembu** (sul-africano, director-geral da Mopane); **Inês Macuácua** (moçambicana, engenheira no escritório de Maputo, ligada por vídeo); na 1E, três fundadores de *start-ups* — **Thato** (sul-africana), **Kwabena** (ganês) e **Elena** (portuguesa, a viver em Joanesburgo) — e uma jurada, **Ms Dube**.

**A história:** Janeiro de 2030. O Nuno chegou a Joanesburgo; a central do Tômbwa abriu no fim de 2029 e os seis técnicos formados pela Kalandula fazem a manutenção (B2 3F, R1A). O Nuno quer convencer a Mopane a criar um **centro de formação regional** em Joanesburgo. Na segunda, 14, conta a ideia à Thandi (1A); na quarta, 16, explica à equipa porque o Tômbwa correu bem (1B); na sexta, 18, o Rajesh faz de advogado do diabo (1C); na quinta, 24, o Nuno vê uma competição de *start-ups* (1E); na quinta, 31, apresenta a proposta à direcção (1F).

**Os números da proposta (iguais em todas as aulas em que aparecem):**
- Custo: 240 000 dólares para montar o centro (salas, um laboratório com equipamento real, dois formadores) e cerca de 90 000 dólares por ano para o manter.
- Capacidade: 60 técnicos por ano, da África do Sul, de Angola, de Moçambique, da Zâmbia, da Namíbia e do Botsuana, em cursos de duas semanas, com uma parte teórica *online* antes.
- Despesas de deslocação de engenheiros da Mopane a clientes fora da África do Sul em 2029: cerca de 500 000 dólares. Se o centro as reduzir a metade (250 000 dólares por ano), descontados os 90 000 de funcionamento, poupa cerca de 160 000 dólares por ano e paga-se em cerca de ano e meio; se as reduzir em um terço, poupa cerca de 77 000 e paga-se em cerca de três anos.
- O exemplo do Tômbwa: desde a abertura, o tempo de resposta a uma avaria passou de dois dias (à espera de um engenheiro de Luanda ou de Joanesburgo) para cerca de três horas, porque os técnicos locais resolvem quase tudo.
- O inquérito às equipas de Angola e Moçambique (1F, em português): 48 respostas; 85% querem formação certificada; 70% esperam por um engenheiro de fora pelo menos uma vez por mês; a maior dificuldade: "não há formação prática perto de nós" (61%).

## 1A — What we need is…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1A — What we need is…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dar ênfase à ideia principal com orações clivadas com what,
  all e the reason why / the thing that (What we need is a training hub. All I'm asking for is an hour.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: What + sujeito + verbo + is / was + ideia em destaque (What we need is…, What surprised me
  was…); All + sujeito + verbo + is (All I'm asking for is…, all = a única coisa); The reason why… is…;
  The thing that… is…; The person who… is…; is / was conforme o tempo; com verbo: What we should do is
  (to) train them.
Palavras-alvo: hub, training, technician, regional, travel costs, set up, run, countries, proposal,
  budget, first impression
Reciclagem: What worries me is… (B2, 9D)
Pronúncia: uma pausa curta antes da ideia em destaque e o acento forte nela (marcado por indicação)
Contexto: segunda-feira, 14 de Janeiro de 2030, 8:30; o escritório da Mopane em Sandton, Joanesburgo; o
  Nuno, na segunda semana, conta à Thandi a ideia que trouxe de Angola
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 1A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 1
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to C1 — the last level of the course. This unit is about making the case: putting your strongest idea where people will notice it, and answering objections with skill. Today: sentences that put one idea in the spotlight — "What we need is…".

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 14 de Janeiro de 2030, 8:30; um escritório envidraçado em Sandton; a Thandi com uma chávena de rooibos; o Nuno com um caderno]

**THANDI:** So, second week. How are you finding Joburg?
**NUNO:** Cold in the mornings, which nobody warned me about! But I love it. What surprised me most was how many countries we work in — I counted six on the project list.
**THANDI:** Ha! Welcome to the region. And you said on Friday you had an idea from Angola.
**NUNO:** I do. Here it is. What we need is a regional training hub — here in Joburg — for technicians from all six countries.
**THANDI:** A training centre? Go on.
**NUNO:** Think about Tômbwa. The reason why it's working so well is that six local technicians can fix almost everything themselves. Before, they waited two days for an engineer. Now it takes about three hours.
**THANDI:** I've seen the reports. It's impressive.
**NUNO:** And the thing that worries me is the opposite: last year, we spent about five hundred thousand dollars flying our engineers to clients outside South Africa.
**THANDI:** That much?
**NUNO:** Rajesh checked it. What I'm proposing is simple: two-week courses, sixty technicians a year, theory online first, then practice here, in a real lab.
**THANDI:** And the cost?
**NUNO:** About two hundred and forty thousand to set it up, and ninety thousand a year to run.
**THANDI:** David will hate that number.
**NUNO:** I know. All I'm asking for is half an hour with the board to make the case.
**THANDI:** [sorri] Then make it a very good half hour. The board meets on the thirty-first.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Clivadas com *what***

[ECRÃ:
Normal: We need a regional training hub.
Ênfase: **What we need is** a regional training hub.
Normal: How many countries we work in surprised me.
Ênfase: **What surprised me was** how many countries we work in.]

**PROF:** A cleft sentence splits one idea into two parts. The first part — "What we need" — prepares the listener; the second — "a regional training hub" — is the spotlight. It's the most common way to give emphasis in professional English.
[PAUSA 6 s]

***All*, *the reason why*, *the thing that***

[ECRÃ: **All I'm asking for is** half an hour. (= a única coisa) · **The reason why** it's working **is that** six technicians fix almost everything. · **The thing that** worries me **is** the cost of travel. · **The person who** checked it **was** Rajesh.]

**PROF:** "All I'm asking for is…" sounds modest — it makes a request look small. "The reason why… is that…" puts the cause in the spotlight. Use "is" or "was" to match the time.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Com um verbo**

[ECRÃ: **What we should do is** train them here. · **What I did was** call Rajesh.]

**PROF:** When the spotlight is an action, use the verb after "is": "What we should do is train them". After "What I did was", use the bare verb too.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ What we need it's a hub. · ✘ The thing what worries me… · ✔ What we need is a hub. · ✔ The thing that worries me is…]

**PROF:** Two typical mistakes: "it's" after the first part — just "is" — and "the thing what". It's "the thing that".
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Pause briefly before the spotlight, then stress it. Listen: What we need is… [pausa] a regional training hub. [acento forte em "training hub"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: All I'm asking for is… half an hour.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** The reason why it's working is that six technicians fix almost everything.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Put the spotlight on the end.
[ECRÃ: We need more time.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: What we need is more time.]
[ECRÃ: The price surprised me.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: What surprised me was the price.]
[ECRÃ: I only want an answer by Friday.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: All I want is an answer by Friday.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. You've just joined a new team, or you're imagining it. Tell your manager about your first impressions and propose one change — with at least four cleft sentences: what surprised you, what works, the reason why something doesn't work, and what you're asking for. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — What surprised me most in my first month was how friendly everyone is. What works really well is the Monday meeting: short and practical. What doesn't work is the way we share files — the reason why we lose so much time is that everyone uses a different system. What I'd suggest is one shared folder for the whole department. All I'm asking for is one afternoon to set it up.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it better to propose big changes in your first months in a new job, or to wait until you know the place? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 9D):** You learned "What worries me is…" as a fixed expression in B2. Make two more sentences like it, with "What concerns me" and "What I like".
[PAUSA 8 s]
[ECRÃ: Exemplo: What concerns me is the budget. What I like about the idea is that it's simple.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! "What we need is…", "All I'm asking for is…", "The reason why… is that…" — the first part prepares, the second shines. Next lesson: "It was Tômbwa that…" — and the emphatic "do".

## 1B — It was Tômbwa that…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1B — It was Tômbwa that…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dar ênfase com clivadas com it (It was the training that made
  the difference.) e com do / does / did enfático (We do have the budget.), também para corrigir alguém.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: It is / was + elemento em destaque + that / who + resto (It was the technicians who solved
  it. It's not the equipment that matters, it's the people.); corrigir com it-clefts (It wasn't Luanda
  that sent the engineer, it was Joanesburgo.); do / does / did enfático no presente e no passado (We did
  test it. She does care.)
Palavras-alvo: response time, fault, local, maintenance, difference, credit, on the ground, fly in,
  midnight, correct
Reciclagem: passiva (B2, 4A); 1A
Pronúncia: acento forte no elemento em destaque depois de It was…, e no do / does / did enfático
Contexto: quarta-feira, 16 de Janeiro, 10:00; a sala de reuniões; o Nuno conta à equipa (o Bongani, a
  Lindiwe, o Rajesh, a Inês por vídeo) a história do Tômbwa
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 1B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Sometimes the spotlight needs to fall on a person, a place or a moment: "It was the technicians who solved it." Today: "it" cleft sentences — and the emphatic "do", which is perfect for correcting misunderstandings politely.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 16 de Janeiro, 10:00; a sala de reuniões; o Bongani, a Lindiwe e o Rajesh à mesa; a Inês no ecrã, em Maputo]

**NUNO:** I'd like to tell you why I think Tômbwa is a model. Lindiwe, you said yesterday it was the new antennas that made the difference.
**LINDIWE:** Well, they're very good antennas.
**NUNO:** They are. But it wasn't the equipment that changed everything — it was the people. It was six technicians from Tômbwa who learned to maintain the network.
**BONGANI:** Were they trained by Mopane?
**NUNO:** No, it was Kalandula that trained them — Elsa ran the course. Mopane did help with the satellite part, though.
**INÊS:** [pelo vídeo] Can I say something? In Maputo, we don't have that. When something breaks, it's always someone from Joburg who has to fly in.
**RAJESH:** And it's our travel budget that pays for it. I checked: five hundred thousand dollars last year.
**LINDIWE:** But we do send people quickly. Clients don't complain.
**INÊS:** They do complain, Lindiwe — just not to you! It's me they call at midnight.
**LINDIWE:** [ri-se] Fair enough. I didn't know that.
**NUNO:** That's exactly my point. It's not that our engineers are slow — they're excellent. It's that the region needs people on the ground.
**BONGANI:** So what you're saying is: train them here, and let them fix things there.
**NUNO:** Exactly. And I do think the board will listen — if we show them the numbers.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Clivadas com *it***

[ECRÃ:
Normal: The technicians solved it.
Ênfase: **It was** the technicians **who** solved it.
Normal: Kalandula trained them.
Ênfase: **It was** Kalandula **that** trained them.
Normal: Inês gets the midnight calls.
Ênfase: **It's** Inês **they call** at midnight. (that omitido no registo informal)]

**PROF:** "It is" or "It was", then the thing in the spotlight, then "that" — or "who" for people. Everything else stays the same. Use it when you want to say: "this one, not another".
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Para corrigir**

[ECRÃ: **It wasn't** the equipment **that** changed everything — **it was** the people. · **It's not that** our engineers are slow. **It's that** the region needs people on the ground.]

**PROF:** This is very useful for correcting someone without saying "you're wrong": "It wasn't X, it was Y." And "It's not that…, it's that…" corrects the reason.
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Do / does / did* enfático**

[ECRÃ: We **do** send people quickly. · Clients **do** complain. · Mopane **did** help with the satellite. · I **do** think the board will listen.]

**PROF:** Adding "do", "does" or "did" before the verb gives emphasis — often to insist that something is true, or to contrast with what someone else said. In speech, "do" is stressed.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ It was Kalandula who trained them that. · ✘ Mopane did helped. · ✔ It was Kalandula that trained them. · ✔ Mopane did help.]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Stress what's in the spotlight, and stress the emphatic "do". Listen: It wasn't the equipment — it was the people. [acento em "equipment" e em "people"] They do complain. [acento em "do"]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat both.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Correct the statement with "It wasn't… it was…".
[ECRÃ: "Paulo installed the satellite." (Sipho's team)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: It wasn't Paulo who installed the satellite — it was Sipho's team.]
[ECRÃ: "You don't like the plan." (I do like it.)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: I do like the plan — I just have one question.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Tell the story of a success — at work, at school or in your community — and correct one common misunderstanding about why it worked. Use "It was… that / who…", "It wasn't… it was…", and one emphatic "do" or "did". About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Last year, our school's results improved a lot. Everyone thinks it was the new computers that made the difference. But it wasn't the computers — it was the extra reading hour every morning. It was our director who insisted on it, even though some parents complained. And the computers did help, of course — just much less than people think.]

**PROF (opinião):** When a project succeeds, who usually gets the credit — and who should? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 4A):** Say it in the passive: "Kalandula trained the technicians."
[PAUSA 5 s]
[ECRÃ: The technicians were trained by Kalandula.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "It was the people that…", "It wasn't X, it was Y", "They do complain" — emphasis that corrects without attacking. Next lesson: how to answer objections like a professional.

## 1C — I take your point, but consider this…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1C — I take your point, but consider this…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue responder a contra-argumentos com subtileza: conceder o que é
  justo, reformular a objecção, devolver a pergunta e voltar ao argumento central.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: conceder (That's a fair point. I'll grant you that… You're right that…); reformular (If I
  understand you correctly, your concern is… So the real question is…); devolver (What would happen if we
  didn't…? Let me turn that around…); voltar (But consider this… The point I'd come back to is… Which is
  exactly why…); clivadas (1A, 1B) dentro da resposta
Palavras-alvo: objection, devil's advocate, concern, competitor, retention, common practice, online,
  hands-on, spare parts
Reciclagem: concessão (B2, 7A); 1A e 1B
Pronúncia: uma pausa depois de conceder e o tom a subir no "but consider this…"
Contexto: sexta-feira, 18 de Janeiro, 16:00; o gabinete do Nuno; o Rajesh faz de advogado do diabo para
  preparar a reunião da direcção
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 1C; conversa guiada "Convince me"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! A strong case isn't one without objections — it's one that has already answered them. Today: four moves for answering a counter-argument.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 18 de Janeiro, 16:00; o gabinete do Nuno; o Rajesh de pé, com um café, a sorrir]

**RAJESH:** OK, I'm David now. Ready to be destroyed?
**NUNO:** Go ahead.
**RAJESH:** Two hundred and forty thousand dollars, Mr Kapinga. Why should we spend that in a difficult year?
**NUNO:** That's a fair point — it's a lot of money. But consider this: last year, we spent five hundred thousand on travel. If the hub halves that, it pays for itself in about a year and a half — after the running costs.
**RAJESH:** And when we've trained them, they'll leave for a competitor. You'll have trained our rivals' staff.
**NUNO:** I'll grant you that some will leave. But let me turn that around: what happens if we don't train them? We keep flying engineers in, and our clients depend on us forever. And we can ask clients to keep their trained staff for two years — it's common in the industry.
**RAJESH:** Hmm. Why Joburg? Wouldn't it be cheaper to train them in Luanda or Maputo?
**NUNO:** If I understand you correctly, your concern is cost again. The reason why it should be here is the lab: our equipment, our engineers, our spare parts are all in Joburg. Moving all that to six countries would cost much more.
**RAJESH:** Last one. Why not do it all online? It's the twenty-first century.
**NUNO:** You're right that the theory works online — that's exactly why the first part is online. But you can't learn to repair a radio link on a screen. What these technicians need is two weeks of hands-on practice.
**RAJESH:** [pausa] OK, David is impressed. Annoyed, but impressed.
**NUNO:** Ha! Any advice?
**RAJESH:** Don't argue with every point. Agree with the true part first — then hit him with the numbers.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Quatro movimentos**

[ECRÃ:]

| Movimento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Conceder | That's a fair point. · I'll grant you that… · You're right that… | I'll grant you that some will leave. |
| 2. Reformular | If I understand you correctly, your concern is… · So the real question is… | If I understand you correctly, your concern is cost again. |
| 3. Devolver | Let me turn that around: … · What would happen if we didn't…? | What happens if we don't train them? |
| 4. Voltar ao argumento | But consider this… · Which is exactly why… · The point I'd come back to is… | That's exactly why the first part is online. |

**PROF:** You don't need all four every time. But notice the order: concede first. When you agree with the true part, the other person listens to the rest. Then reformulate or turn the question around — and come back to your main argument, often with a cleft sentence.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Cuidado com o tom**

[ECRÃ: ✘ No, you're wrong, because… · ✘ That's not true. · ✔ That's a fair point. But consider this… · ✔ I see why you'd say that. The thing is…]

**PROF:** At C1, a direct "You're wrong" sounds aggressive — even when you are right.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Concede with a falling tone, pause, then go up on "but consider this", to show something important is coming. Listen: That's a fair point. [descida, pausa] But consider this… [subida]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** I'll grant you that some will leave. But let me turn that around.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Answer the objection: concede, then come back.
[ECRÃ: "Your plan is too expensive."] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: That's a fair point — it isn't cheap. But consider what it saves: …]
[ECRÃ: "Nobody will use it."] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: I'll grant you that not everyone will. But let me turn that around: what if only half do?]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Make the case for one change in your workplace, school or town. Then answer this objection: "We tried something like that before, and it didn't work." Concede, reformulate, turn it around, and come back to your main point. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — What I'm proposing is a weekly English conversation club at work, during lunch. — I'll grant you that we tried a course three years ago, and people stopped coming. If I understand you correctly, your concern is that people won't come. But consider this: that course was in the evening, after work. What people need is something short, in the day, with colleagues. Which is exactly why I'd start with just thirty minutes on Thursdays.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it a good idea to ask a colleague to play devil's advocate before an important meeting? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 7A):** Concede and come back, with "admittedly" and "even so": "The hub is expensive."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Admittedly, the hub is expensive. Even so, it pays for itself in about a year.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Concede, reformulate, turn it around, come back — and never start with "You're wrong". Try the tutor's conversation "Convince me". Next lesson: the words of persuasion — and two tricky false friends.

## 1D — Persuasion

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1D — Persuasion        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário da persuasão e do investimento (compelling,
  a strong case, return on investment, pay for itself, win over, the bottom line), o acento contrastivo
  para dar ênfase, e evitar os falsos amigos consistent e terrific.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: colocações (make / build / present a case, a compelling / convincing argument, win someone
  over, raise / address an objection, a return on investment, pay for itself, the bottom line, a selling
  point); adjectivos de força (compelling, persuasive, convincing, sound, flawed, weak)
Palavras-alvo: compelling, persuasive, convincing, sound, flawed, a strong case, return on investment (ROI),
  pay for itself, payback period, win over, selling point, the bottom line, buy-in
Reciclagem: clivadas (1A, 1B); responder a objecções (1C)
Pronúncia: acento contrastivo (I said the training hub, com o acento em "training" ou em "hub" — marcado por
  indicação, sem maiúsculas)
Falsos amigos: consistent (≠ consistente → solid, well-founded; consistent = coerente, constante);
  terrific (≠ terrífico → terrifying; terrific = excelente, fantástico)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 1D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words people use to persuade — and to judge whether an argument is any good. And two false friends that can make a compliment sound like an insult.

### 2. Persuadir (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| a strong case | Nuno has a strong case for the hub. (bons argumentos) |
| compelling / convincing | The numbers are compelling. (muito convincente) |
| sound / flawed | The plan is sound. · The argument is flawed. (sólido / com uma falha) |
| return on investment (ROI) | The return on investment is high. |
| pay for itself | The hub would pay for itself in a year. (pagar-se a si próprio) |
| win someone over | He won the board over with one example. (conquistar) |
| a selling point | The lab is the main selling point. (argumento de venda) |
| the bottom line | The bottom line is: we save money. (o essencial, o resultado final) |
| buy-in | We need the managers' buy-in. (adesão, apoio) |

**PROF:** Repeat: compelling. Sound. Flawed. Return on investment. Pay for itself. Win over.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: "compelling" > "convincing" > "persuasive" — todos positivos; "compelling" é o mais forte.]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 3. Colocações (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **make / build / present** a case · **raise / address** an objection · **win** someone **over** · **get** buy-in **from** · a **payback period** of one year]

**PROF:** We "make" a case, or "build" one step by step. Someone "raises" an objection; you "address" it. And when it works, you "win them over".
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ do a case · ✔ make a case · ✘ answer to an objection · ✔ address an objection · ✔ respond to an objection]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: acento contrastivo (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** In English, you can change the meaning of a sentence just by moving the stress. Listen: I said the training hub. [acento em "training" — não outro hub] — I said the training hub. [acento em "hub" — não um curso] — I said the training hub. [acento em "said" — eu disse, sim]
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: It wasn't the equipment — it was the people. [acento em "equipment" e "people"]
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **consistent** ≠ consistente]

**PROF:** "Consistent" means the same every time, or logical — without contradictions: "Her results are consistent." "Your argument isn't consistent with the numbers."
(PT: "Consistente" no sentido de sólido, bem fundamentado diz-se "solid", "well-founded" ou "sound": "um argumento consistente" → "a solid argument"; e uma sopa consistente é "thick".)
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **terrific** ≠ terrífico]

**PROF:** "Terrific" is positive: "a terrific presentation" — excellent, fantastic.
(PT: "Terrífico" — que causa terror — é "terrifying": "um acidente terrífico" → "a terrifying accident". Dizer a alguém que a apresentação foi "terrific" é um elogio!)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (2 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Complete.
[ECRÃ: The new system would … for itself in two years.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: pay]
[ECRÃ: She … the sceptics over with a simple story.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: won]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think of a product, a project or an idea you believe in. Make the case for it in about a minute: its main selling point, the return on investment (or the benefit), one objection you'd address, and the bottom line. Use one cleft sentence and contrastive stress.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I'd like to make the case for solar water heaters in every school kitchen. The main selling point is the cost: they pay for themselves in about two years. The usual objection is the price at the start — and that's fair. But what schools forget is the gas they buy every month. The bottom line is simple: it's the cheapest hot water they'll ever have.]

**PROF (opinião):** What persuades you more: numbers or stories? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF:** Well done! Next lesson: three start-up pitches in Johannesburg — and a tough jury.

### 2. Primeira escuta: os três *pitches* (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 24 de Janeiro, 19:00; uma sala cheia em Braamfontein; um palco; três fundadores; uma jurada com um caderno; o Nuno numa das filas do meio]

**HOST:** Good evening, Joburg! Three founders, two minutes each. First up: Thato.
**THATO:** Hi everyone. So, um, my company is called FarmLink. We've been working on it for two years. **HOST:** And finally, Elena.
**ELENA:** Imagine you're seventy-five, and you live alone. [pausa] The reason why so many older people end up in hospital is simple: nobody notices small changes. ## 1F — Work & Life: The training hub

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 1        Aula: 1F — The training hub        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue defender uma proposta diante de uma direcção céptica: pôr a ideia
  mais forte em destaque, responder às objecções com os quatro movimentos, e levar a uma decisão; e
  apresentar em inglês os resultados de um inquérito escrito em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2, 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: clivadas com what / all / the reason why / it; do enfático; conceder,
  reformular, devolver, voltar
Palavras-alvo: board, proposal, running costs, retention clause, pilot, approve, review, survey,
  certified, hands-on
Reciclagem: números aproximados (B2, 2E); 1.º condicional (B1); I can live with that (B2, R1B)
Pronúncia: a ideia em destaque com pausa e acento; a concessão com tom descendente

  equipas de Angola e Moçambique

  Nkosi e a Thandi; o Nuno de pé
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! The big moment of this unit. Nuno has half an hour with the board of Mopane — and a finance director who doesn't like spending money. Watch how he puts his strongest idea in the spotlight and answers every objection.

### 2. Situação (6 min)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 31 de Janeiro, 10:00; a sala da direcção, no último andar; o Dr Mthembu à cabeceira; o David Nkosi com os braços cruzados; a Thandi; o Nuno de pé, ao lado de um ecrã: "One hub. Six countries. Three hours instead of two days."]

**MTHEMBU:** Mr Kapinga, welcome. Thandi says you have something for us.
**NUNO:** Thank you, Dr Mthembu. In one sentence: what this region needs is technicians who can fix things themselves — and a place to train them. [pausa] At Tômbwa, in Angola, six local technicians were trained before the plant opened. The average response time to a fault went from two days to about three hours. It wasn't new equipment that did that — it was training.
**NUNO:** What I'm proposing is a regional training hub here in Joburg: sixty technicians a year from six countries, two-week courses, theory online first, then practice in our lab.
**MTHEMBU:** And the people themselves — do they want it?
**NUNO:** They do. We asked them. The survey is in Portuguese, because we sent it to Angola and Mozambique — let me give you the results.

[ECRÃ: o inquérito, em português: "Inquérito às equipas técnicas de Angola e Moçambique — 48 respostas. Querem formação certificada: 85%. Esperam por um engenheiro de fora pelo menos uma vez por mês: 70%. Maior dificuldade: 'não há formação prática perto de nós' (61%); 'o equipamento muda depressa' (27%). Comentário frequente: 'Aprendemos a resolver os problemas sozinhos, mas sem certificado ninguém confia em nós.'"]

**NUNO:** Forty-eight people answered. Eighty-five percent want certified training. Seventy percent wait for an engineer from outside at least once a month. And their biggest difficulty — for almost two thirds — is that there's no practical training near them. One comment sums it up: "We learn to solve problems on our own, but without a certificate, nobody trusts us."
**NKOSI:** Very moving. Now the numbers. Two hundred and forty thousand dollars, Mr Kapinga. Plus ninety thousand a year. In a difficult year.
**NUNO:** That's a fair point — it is a lot of money. But consider this: last year, we spent about five hundred thousand dollars on flights and hotels for engineers outside South Africa. If the hub cuts that by half, it pays for itself in about a year and a half, running costs included. Even if it only cuts it by a third, it pays for itself in about three years.
**NKOSI:** "If." And when we've trained them, they'll go and work for our competitors.
**NUNO:** I'll grant you that some will. But let me turn that around: what happens if we don't train anyone? We keep flying engineers in, and our clients keep waiting two days. And there's a simple answer to the risk: a two-year retention clause in the client contracts. It's common practice.
**NKOSI:** Hmm. And why here? Luanda or Maputo would be cheaper.
**NUNO:** If I understand you correctly, your concern is cost. The reason why it should be here is that the lab, the spare parts and our best engineers are all in Joburg. Building that in six places would cost six times more.
**NKOSI:** [pausa] I don't like spending money. You know that.
**NUNO:** I do know that. That's why I'm not asking for the full amount. All I'm asking for is a one-year pilot: thirty technicians, half the running cost, and a review in December. If the travel costs don't fall, we stop.
**MTHEMBU:** David?
**NKOSI:** [longa pausa] I can live with a pilot. With a review in December — and real numbers, not "ifs".
**MTHEMBU:** Then let's approve the pilot. Thandi, can you and Mr Kapinga bring a detailed plan by the end of February?
**THANDI:** We will.
**NUNO:** Thank you. You won't regret it.

### 3. Como se defende uma proposta diante de uma direcção céptica (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno fez]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
