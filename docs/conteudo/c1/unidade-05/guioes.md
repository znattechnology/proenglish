## 5A — Having analysed the data…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5A — Having analysed the data…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue condensar informação com orações participiais (Looking at the
  figures…, Having analysed the data…, Compared with last year…, Based on the survey…), com o mesmo sujeito
  da oração principal.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: particípio presente (Looking at the figures, we can see…; Travelling less, our engineers…);
  particípio passado (Compared with last year, …; Based on the survey, …; Faced with the delay, …); particípio
  perfeito (Having analysed the data, we…; Having completed the handover, …); negativa (Not having received
  the payment, …); o erro do sujeito trocado (Having analysed the data, the conclusion is… ✘)
Palavras-alvo: summary, figures, quarter, target, revenue, margin, travel costs, analyse, condense,
  executive summary
Reciclagem: conectores (C1, 3C); passiva (B2, 4)
Pronúncia: a pausa depois da oração participial
Contexto: segunda-feira, 6 de Maio de 2030, 10:00; o gabinete do Rajesh; o primeiro rascunho do resumo do
  relatório trimestral
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 5A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 5
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Welcome to unit 5: Data and reports — the last unit of C1.1. Busy people read the first paragraph of a report and stop. Today: participle clauses, the tool for putting more information into fewer words.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 6 de Maio, 10:00; o gabinete do Rajesh; no ecrã, o resumo do Nuno, com sete frases curtas]

**RAJESH:** Your summary is correct. But it's seven sentences for three ideas. The board reads the first paragraph — that's all.
**NUNO:** So what do I cut?
**RAJESH:** Nothing. You combine. Look: "We analysed the data. We found that revenue was eight percent below target." One sentence: "Having analysed the data, we found that revenue was eight percent below target."
**NUNO:** "Having analysed" — because the analysis came first.
**RAJESH:** Exactly. Next: "Revenue was four point six million. This is twelve percent more than last year." —
**NUNO:** "Compared with the first quarter of 2029, revenue grew by twelve percent."
**RAJESH:** Good. And this one: "We were faced with a delay in Nampula. We kept an engineer on site."
**NUNO:** "Faced with a delay in Nampula, we kept an engineer on site."
**RAJESH:** Perfect. Now the travel costs.
**NUNO:** "Looking at travel costs, we can see a fall of eighteen percent."
**RAJESH:** Careful with one thing. You wrote here: "Having fallen by eighteen percent, the board will be pleased with travel costs."
**NUNO:** [ri-se] So the board fell by eighteen percent.
**RAJESH:** That's what the sentence says. The subject after the comma must be the one doing the action. "Having fallen by eighteen percent, travel costs are now at their lowest level in three years."
**NUNO:** Three sentences instead of seven.
**RAJESH:** And the board might actually read the second paragraph.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Três formas**

[ECRÃ:]

| Forma | Quando | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| *-ing* (presente) | ao mesmo tempo, ou como forma de olhar | **Looking at** travel costs, we can see a fall of eighteen percent. |
| particípio passado | sentido passivo | **Compared with** last year, revenue grew by twelve percent. · **Based on** the survey, … · **Faced with** a delay, we… |
| *having* + particípio | uma acção antes da outra | **Having analysed** the data, we found that… · **Having completed** the handover, the team… |

**PROF:** A participle clause replaces a full clause — usually at the start of the sentence, with a comma. It's typical of reports, presentations and formal speech.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**O sujeito**

[ECRÃ: ✘ Having analysed the data, the conclusion is clear. (a conclusão não analisou nada) · ✔ Having analysed the data, we reached a clear conclusion. · ✘ Looking at the figures, revenue is down. · ✔ Looking at the figures, we can see that revenue is down.]

**PROF:** The most important rule: the subject of the main clause must be the one who does the action in the participle clause. Otherwise, the sentence says something absurd — like the board falling by eighteen percent.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**A negativa**

[ECRÃ: **Not having received** the payment, Mussiro's milestone moved to the second quarter. ✘ → **Not having received** the payment, **we** moved Mussiro's milestone to the second quarter. ✔]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Pause briefly after the participle clause — it's the comma you can hear. Listen: Having analysed the data, [pausa] we found that revenue was eight percent below target.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 7 s]
**PROF:** Compared with last year, [pausa] revenue grew by twelve percent.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Combine into one sentence.
[ECRÃ: We read the survey. We changed the plan.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Having read the survey, we changed the plan.]
[ECRÃ: We looked at the results. We saw two problems.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Looking at the results, we saw two problems.]
[ECRÃ: Sales were compared with last year. They rose by ten percent.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Compared with last year, sales rose by ten percent.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Summarise the results of something you know — a project, a shop, a class, a sports team — for busy readers, in four or five sentences, with at least three participle clauses. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Looking at our school's results this year, we can see clear progress. Compared with last year, the pass rate rose from sixty to seventy-two percent. Having introduced a reading hour every morning, we saw the biggest change in the first years. Faced with fewer teachers in maths, however, we couldn't improve the maths results. Based on these figures, we recommend two more maths teachers next year.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should a report give the bad news in the first paragraph, or later? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 3C):** Link with a connector of cause: "Kalandula pays half. Luanda is the cheapest option."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Given that Kalandula pays half, Luanda is the cheapest option.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Very good! Looking at, compared with, having analysed — and always the right subject after the comma. Next lesson: what is said, thought and expected.

## 5B — It is thought to have…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5B — It is thought to have…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue relatar o que se pensa, se diz ou se espera com passivas de relato
  e narrativas (The kwanza is expected to fall… The delay is thought to have cost… Mussiro is said to be
  planning… It was found that…), distinguindo factos de informações não confirmadas.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: sujeito + is / are + said / thought / believed / expected / reported + to + infinitivo
  (presente ou futuro: is expected to fall), to be + -ing (is said to be planning), to have + particípio
  (passado: is thought to have cost); It is said / thought / believed / expected that… ; It was found /
  agreed / decided that…; a diferença entre facto e relato
Palavras-alvo: rumour, confirmed, unconfirmed, source, expected, forecast, tender, phase, competitor,
  analyst
Reciclagem: atenuação (C1, 2A); passivas (B2, 4); passiva de relato reconhecida (B2, 9E)
Pronúncia: o ritmo de "is thought to have" ("is-THOUGHT-tuh-uv") e "is said to be"
Contexto: quarta-feira, 8 de Maio, 9:00; a chamada semanal da equipa: o Nuno e o Rajesh em Joanesburgo, a Inês
  em Maputo, o Bongani em Nampula
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 5B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! A report contains facts — and things people say, think or expect. Today: how to report information without claiming it's a fact: "The kwanza is expected to fall…", "Mussiro is said to be planning…".

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 8 de Maio, 9:00; quatro janelas: o Nuno e o Rajesh em Joanesburgo, a Inês em Maputo, o Bongani num contentor em Nampula]

**NUNO:** For the report, I need two lists: what we know, and what we've heard. Bongani, the handover?
**BONGANI:** Fact: eleven substations online. The last one is expected to be connected on Friday morning.
**NUNO:** Good. Inês, any news from Mussiro?
**INÊS:** One fact, one rumour. The fact: the penalty has been paid — they confirmed it yesterday. The rumour: Mussiro is said to be planning a second phase in Niassa province. Twenty substations.
**NUNO:** Who told you?
**INÊS:** Two people at Mussiro, unofficially. So: "is said to be planning". Not "is planning".
**RAJESH:** And there's something else for the report. The kwanza fell six percent in the first quarter. It's expected to fall another five percent by the end of the year — that's from two bank forecasts.
**NUNO:** And the cost of the Nampula delay?
**RAJESH:** That's harder. The delay is thought to have cost us about three hundred and fifty thousand in the first quarter — but that money isn't lost, it's moved to the second quarter.
**NUNO:** So "is thought to have cost" isn't right. We know the number.
**RAJESH:** True. Then: "The delay moved three hundred and fifty thousand dollars of revenue into the second quarter." Fact.
**INÊS:** And one more rumour. A Chinese competitor is believed to have offered Mussiro a lower price for phase two.
**NUNO:** Believed by whom?
**INÊS:** By Dr Cumbe's assistant. Over coffee.
**NUNO:** [sorri] Then it goes in the "heard" list. With a question mark.
**BONGANI:** It was agreed, by the way, that Inês presents the handover on Friday. I'll do the technical part.
**NUNO:** That one is a fact. I was there.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Passivas de relato**

[ECRÃ:]

| Tempo do que se relata | Estrutura | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| agora ou no futuro | is / are + said / expected… + to + infinitivo | The kwanza **is expected to fall** another five percent. · The last one **is expected to be** connected on Friday. |
| em curso | is said… + to be + -ing | Mussiro **is said to be planning** a second phase. |
| no passado | is thought… + to have + particípio | A competitor **is believed to have offered** a lower price. |
| com *it* | It is said / believed / expected that… | **It is believed that** a competitor offered a lower price. |
| decisões e conclusões | It was found / agreed / decided that… | **It was agreed that** Inês presents the handover. |

**PROF:** These structures say: "this is what people say or think — not necessarily what I know". They protect you, and they're honest with the reader. You recognised them in B2; now you'll use them.
[PAUSA 7 s]

**Facto ou relato?**

[ECRÃ: Facto (sabemos): The penalty has been paid. · The delay moved 350 000 dollars into Q2. · Relato (não confirmado): Mussiro is said to be planning a second phase. · A competitor is believed to have offered a lower price.]

**PROF:** Notice Nuno's correction: when you know a number for certain, don't use "is thought to". Reporting passives are for information you can't confirm — not for hiding facts.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ Mussiro is said that it is planning… · ✘ The competitor is believed to offer… (no passado) · ✔ Mussiro is said to be planning… · ✔ The competitor is believed to have offered…]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** These phrases are said quickly, with the stress on the reporting verb and on the main verb. Listen: It's thought to have cost… ["is THOUGHT tuh-uv COST"] Mussiro is said to be planning…
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Report it with a passive.
[ECRÃ: People say the minister is planning a visit.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: The minister is said to be planning a visit.]
[ECRÃ: Experts think the fire started in the kitchen.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: The fire is thought to have started in the kitchen.]
[ECRÃ: Analysts expect prices to rise.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Prices are expected to rise.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Give a short news update about your sector, your town or your company, like a radio presenter: two facts, and three things that are said, thought or expected — using reporting passives. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Here's the news from Benguela this week. The new bridge opened on Monday, and it was found that traffic in the centre fell by a third. The mayor is said to be planning a second bridge. The project is expected to cost over twenty million dollars. And a large supermarket chain is believed to have bought land near the port. More on that next week.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should rumours ever go into a report for a board? Why, or why not?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — C1, 2A):** Hedge it differently, without a passive: "A competitor is believed to have offered a lower price."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: It would appear that a competitor has offered a lower price.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Is expected to", "is said to be", "is thought to have" — and the line between facts and what we've heard. Next lesson: how to say numbers that aren't round.

## 5C — A fraction over…

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5C — A fraction over…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue aproximar e qualificar números para uma apresentação (a fraction
  over, just shy of, in the region of, the best part of, roughly a third, well over / under, getting on for)
  sem perder o rigor.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: acima: a fraction over, just over, well over, getting on for (quase, a subir); abaixo: just
  shy of, just under, well under, the best part of (quase todo); à volta: in the region of, roughly, around,
  approximately; fracções e proporções (roughly a third, nearly half, two in five, nine out of ten); quando
  arredondar e quando dar o número exacto
Palavras-alvo: approximately, roughly, fraction, shy of, region, proportion, round, precise, audience,
  rehearse
Reciclagem: números aproximados (B2, 2E); orações participiais (5A)
Pronúncia: o ritmo de números longos; o acento no número que importa
Contexto: sexta-feira, 10 de Maio, 18:00; o gabinete da Thandi; a entrega de Nampula foi feita de manhã; o
  Nuno ensaia os números da apresentação
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 5C; conversa guiada "Talk me through the numbers"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! "Four million, six hundred and twelve thousand, three hundred dollars" — nobody remembers that. "Just over four and a half million" — everybody does. Today: how to approximate numbers without being vague.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 10 de Maio, 18:00; o gabinete da Thandi; no telemóvel do Nuno, uma fotografia da Inês e do Bongani com o Dr Cumbe numa subestação]

**THANDI:** Before we start — Nampula?
**NUNO:** Handed over this morning. Inês presented, Bongani did the technical part, and Dr Cumbe shook both their hands. He sent this photo.
**THANDI:** [sorri] Five days before Dr Cumbe's deadline. Well done. Now — your numbers. Rehearse them.
**NUNO:** "Revenue in the first quarter was four million, six hundred and twelve thousand dollars, which is…"
**THANDI:** Stop. Nobody will remember that. Round it.
**NUNO:** "Revenue was just over four and a half million dollars — a fraction over four point six."
**THANDI:** Choose one. For the board: "just over four point six million". And the target?
**NUNO:** "Just shy of the five-million target — eight percent below."
**THANDI:** Good. The Mussiro payment?
**NUNO:** "In the region of three hundred and fifty thousand dollars moved into the second quarter."
**THANDI:** No — that one you know exactly. Say "three hundred and fifty thousand". Approximate when the exact number doesn't help — not when it does.
**NUNO:** Right. Travel costs: "down by nearly a fifth compared with last year".
**THANDI:** Eighteen percent — "nearly a fifth" is perfect. People see a picture.
**NUNO:** Customer satisfaction: "four point one out of five — well over our target of four".
**THANDI:** "Well over"? Point one isn't "well over". "Just over" — or "slightly above".
**NUNO:** [ri-se] Honest numbers.
**THANDI:** Honest numbers. David will check every one of them. And Naledi Moyo will be there — she used to be an auditor.
**NUNO:** Then "the best part of a day" for the response time is a bad idea?
**THANDI:** For Tômbwa? It's about three hours. Say "about three hours".

### 3. Língua (4 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Sentido | Expressão | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| um pouco acima | a fraction over · just over · slightly above | just over four point six million |
| muito acima | well over · well above | well over half of our clients |
| um pouco abaixo | just shy of · just under · slightly below | just shy of the five-million target |
| muito abaixo | well under · well below | well under budget |
| à volta de | in the region of · roughly · approximately · around | in the region of two hundred clients |
| quase (a subir) | getting on for · nearly · almost | getting on for a thousand technicians |
| quase todo | the best part of | the best part of a year (quase um ano inteiro) |
| proporções | roughly a third · nearly half · two in five · nine out of ten | nearly a fifth (18%) |

**PROF:** Two rules from Thandi. First: approximate when the exact number doesn't help the listener. Second: the approximation must be honest — "well over" for point one is not honest.
[PAUSA 7 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ just shy from · ✘ in the region from · ✘ a fraction more of · ✔ just shy of · ✔ in the region of · ✔ a fraction over]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In a long number, group the words and stress the number that matters. Listen: Revenue was just over four point six million — [acento em "four point six"] just shy of the five-million target.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Say it in an approximate — but honest — way.
[ECRÃ: 49.2% of customers] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: nearly half of our customers / just under half]
[ECRÃ: 3 hours 50 minutes] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: getting on for four hours / just under four hours]
[ECRÃ: 34% of the budget] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: roughly a third of the budget]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Present four or five numbers from your life or work — your monthly costs, a team's results, a town's population — for a listener who'll hear them only once. Approximate where it helps, and give one number exactly. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Our small shop had a good year. Sales were just over twelve million kwanzas — roughly a fifth more than last year. Nearly half came from just three customers, which is a risk. Rent is the biggest cost: well over a third of everything we spend. And we had exactly one week closed, for the water problem in March.]

**PROF (opinião):** Do numbers in presentations convince you, or do you need to see them written? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 2E):** Say it as a trend: revenue went from 4.1 to 4.6 million in a year.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Revenue rose by twelve percent / grew steadily, from four point one to four point six million.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! A fraction over, just shy of, in the region of, the best part of — and honest approximations. Try the tutor's "Talk me through the numbers". Next lesson: the words of reports — and two false friends.

## 5D — KPIs and annual reports

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5D — KPIs and annual reports        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário de indicadores e relatórios (KPI, benchmark,
  variance, margin, year on year, outlier, on track), o ritmo de uma apresentação de números, e evitar os
  falsos amigos instance e actual.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: colocações (meet / miss / exceed a target, a positive / negative variance, year on year,
  quarter on quarter, on track / off track, a healthy / thin margin, an outlier, a benchmark, a breakdown
  of, a forecast, the bottom line — revisto); verbos de tendência com precisão (edge up / down, plateau,
  recover, dip)
Palavras-alvo: KPI, benchmark, variance, margin, year on year, outlier, on track, breakdown, forecast,
  dip, recover, plateau
Reciclagem: tendências (B2, 2); aproximar (5C)
Pronúncia: o ritmo de uma apresentação de números: pausa antes do número importante, acento nele
Falsos amigos: instance (≠ instância → authority, court; instance = exemplo, caso: for instance); actual (≠
  actual → current; actual = real, efectivo: actual vs. planned)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 5D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Reports have their own vocabulary: KPIs, variances, outliers. Today: the words of numbers — and two false friends that appear in almost every report.

### 2. Indicadores e relatórios (3 min)

[ECRÃ:]

| Expressão | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| a KPI (key performance indicator) | Response time is one of our KPIs. (indicador-chave) |
| meet / miss / exceed a target | We missed the revenue target, but exceeded the satisfaction target. |
| a variance | a negative variance of four hundred thousand dollars (desvio) |
| year on year | Revenue grew twelve percent year on year. (em relação ao mesmo período do ano anterior) |
| a healthy / thin margin | Twenty-one percent is still a healthy margin. |
| an outlier | Angola was the outlier: well above target. (um caso fora da média) |
| on track / off track | The Luanda office is on track to open in January. (no bom caminho) |
| a breakdown (of) | Here's a breakdown of the variance. (discriminação) |
| a benchmark | Three hours is now our benchmark for response time. (referência) |
| a forecast | the forecast for the second quarter (previsão) |

**PROF:** Repeat: KPI. Variance. Year on year. Outlier. On track. Breakdown. Benchmark.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 3. Tendências com precisão (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: edge up / down (subir / descer um pouco) · dip (uma descida curta) · recover (recuperar) · plateau (estabilizar) · The margin dipped to twenty-one percent, but it's expected to recover in the second quarter.]

**PROF:** In B2, you learned "rise sharply" and "fall gradually". At C1, you choose more precise verbs: a margin "dips" and "recovers"; satisfaction "edges up".
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: apresentar números (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** When you present a number, pause just before it, then stress it. Listen: The margin dipped to [pausa] twenty-one percent. — Travel costs fell by [pausa] nearly a fifth.
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Repeat both.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (2 min)

[ECRÃ: **instance** ≠ instância]

**PROF:** "Instance" means an example, a case: "for instance", "in this instance", "several instances of late payment".
(PT: "Instância", no sentido de tribunal ou autoridade, é "court" ou "authority": "tribunal de primeira instância" → "court of first instance" só em linguagem jurídica muito específica; normalmente "lower court". "Em última instância" → "as a last resort" ou "ultimately".)
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **actual** ≠ actual]

**PROF:** "Actual" means real — what really happened, often compared with the plan: "actual versus planned", "the actual cost was higher".
(PT: "Actual", no sentido de presente, é "current": "a situação actual" → "the current situation". "The actual figures" são os números reais, não os de agora. Atenção: "actually" (B1) quer dizer "na verdade".)
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (2 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Complete.
[ECRÃ: We … the revenue target by eight percent.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: missed]
[ECRÃ: Here's the … versus the planned cost.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: actual]
[ECRÃ: A situação actual → The … situation] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: current]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Present three KPIs of something you know — a team, a shop, a school — with this vocabulary: which targets were met or missed, one variance and its reason, one outlier, and whether things are on track. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — We track three KPIs. Attendance exceeded the target: ninety-two percent. Exam results missed it by four points — a negative variance caused by the teachers' strike in March. The outlier is the evening class, well above the average. Overall, we're on track for the year, and results are expected to recover in the next term.]

**PROF (opinião):** Can a company measure too much? What happens when people only care about KPIs?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF:** Well done! Next lesson: an earnings call — and how good and bad news are wrapped.

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

**PROF:** On Wednesday, Nuno listens to an earnings call — a phone conference where a listed company presents its results to investors and analysts. The company is Nyala Telecom, a client of Mopane. Listen for the good news, the bad news — and how the bad news is wrapped.

### 2. Primeira escuta: a conferência (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 15 de Maio; "Nyala Telecom — First-quarter results call (fictional)"; um gráfico de barras]

**PROF:** First: what is the best news, and what is the worst?

**OPERATOR:** Good afternoon, and welcome to the Nyala Telecom first-quarter results call. I'll now hand over to the chief financial officer, Ms Thuli Zwane.
**ZWANE:** Thank you. Looking at the quarter as a whole, we're pleased with the progress. Revenue rose seven percent year on year, to just over two point three billion rand. **ZWANE:** Turning to costs, the picture is more challenging. Energy costs rose by the best part of a third, driven by power cuts and diesel prices. **OPERATOR:** Our first question is from Owen Price.
**PRICE:** Thanks. Thuli, "challenging" is a nice word for a three-point fall in margin. How confident are you about the recovery? And what if the power cuts continue?
**ZWANE:** Fair question. I'm reasonably confident. The solar sites are already working — energy costs there fell by roughly forty percent. Should the power cuts continue, the recovery would be slower, but I'd be surprised if the margin went below twenty-five.
**OPERATOR:** Next question: Farida Ismail.
**ISMAIL:** Good afternoon. You mentioned four hundred solar sites. How many sites do you have in total?
**ZWANE:** Just shy of three thousand.
**ISMAIL:** So roughly one in seven. When will the rest be done?
**ZWANE:** The programme is expected to take three years.
**ISMAIL:** So for three years, most of your network depends on diesel.
**ZWANE:** [pausa] Most of it, yes. That's why the programme is our top priority.
**ISMAIL:** Thank you. That's very clear.

[ECRÃ: Boas notícias: receita +7% ano a ano; dados móveis +20%; clientes +10% · Más notícias: custos de energia quase +33%; margem de 29% para 26% · Como se embrulhou: "the picture is more challenging"; começar pelas boas notícias; "Having invested in solar…" logo a seguir à má notícia · O que a analista descobriu: só cerca de um em sete locais tem energia solar, e o programa demora três anos]

### 3. Segunda escuta: os pormenores (3 min)

1. How much did revenue rise year on year? (seven percent ✓ / twenty percent / three percent)
2. Which part of the business was the outlier? (mobile data ✓ / energy costs / new customers)
3. Why did energy costs rise? (power cuts and diesel prices ✓ / new offices / higher salaries)
4. What happened to the margin? (It dipped from twenty-nine to twenty-six percent. ✓ / It rose to twenty-nine percent. / It stayed the same.)
5. How many sites does Nyala have in total? (just under three thousand ✓ / four hundred / three hundred)
6. What did Farida Ismail show with her questions? (Most of the network will depend on diesel for three years. ✓ / The results are excellent. / The CFO is leaving.)

*(Cada resposta repete-se; depois aparecem as perguntas, com 8 s para responder.)*

### 4. Terceira escuta: como disseram (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Listen and repeat.
**PROF:** Looking at the quarter as a whole, we're pleased with the progress. [PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** Turning to costs, the picture is more challenging. [PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** Having invested in solar at four hundred sites, we expect the margin to recover. [PAUSA 6 s]

**PROF:** Did you notice the two analysts? Owen attacks the word — "challenging" — and gets a confident answer. Farida asks three simple questions and gets the real story: one in seven sites. Simple questions are often the most dangerous.

**PROF (opinião):** Should companies use words like "challenging" for bad results, or should they speak more plainly? Why?

## 5F — Work & Life: The quarterly report

```
Curso: C1.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5F — The quarterly report        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue apresentar um relatório trimestral a uma administração: sintetizar,
  explicar um desvio, distinguir factos de relatos, responder a perguntas sobre os números e recomendar; e
  explicar em inglês uma folha de cálculo em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2, 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: orações participiais, passivas de relato, números aproximados
Palavras-alvo: quarterly report, variance, breakdown, margin, on track, outlier, receivables, forecast,
  recommendation, spreadsheet
Reciclagem: clivadas (C1, 1); atenuação e certeza (C1, 2); cenários (C1, 3)
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! The last big moment of C1.1. Nuno presents his first quarterly report — with a negative variance, a former auditor in the room, and a spreadsheet in Portuguese. Watch how he handles the numbers, the doubts and the recommendation.

### 2. Situação (6 min)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 23 de Maio, 10:00; a sala da administração; o Dr Mthembu, o David Nkosi, a Naledi Moyo com um lápis e o relatório impresso, e a Thandi; no ecrã: "Southern Africa — Q1 2030"]

**NUNO:** Thank you. Three messages, then the detail. One: revenue was just over four point six million dollars — twelve percent up year on year, but eight percent below target. Two: almost all of that variance is timing, not loss. Three: the region is on track for the year.

**NKOSI:** And the margin?
**NUNO:** It dipped to twenty-one percent, against a target of twenty-four. That's the penalty — sixty thousand dollars — and the cost of keeping an engineer on site. Both were decisions, not accidents.
**MOYO:** Mr Kapinga, page four. Customer satisfaction, "four point one". How many clients answered the survey?

**MOYO:** Not all of them, then.
