# Commercial adult coursebook series (2020–2026) as scope-and-sequence reference for "English Communication Skills" (A1–C1)

Research date: 2026-11-16. Purpose: reference only. ProEnglish content is written from scratch, and nothing from these books is copied.
Method note: several publisher sites blocked automated fetching (oup.es 404, cambridge.org 403, oup.com.au 403, lexicallab 500). Where only search snippets or retailer pages were available, this is flagged. Two Pearson GSE mapping PDFs were downloaded and text-extracted: Speakout 3e A1 and Business Partner C1. They are the most detailed primary data in these notes.

## 1. Series profiles (publisher, edition, levels, speaking focus, structure, digital, scope-and-sequence availability)

### Takeaway
Every major adult general-English series released or updated in 2020–2026 covers A1 to at least C1 in 6–8 levels. Most build a functional or real-world speaking lesson into each unit, plus a pronunciation strand, video and an online practice platform. Speakout 3e (Pearson/BBC, 2022), Empower 2e (Cambridge, 2022), English File 4e (OUP, 2019–21), Life 3e (NGL, 2025) and Outcomes 3e (NGL) are current. Navigate, Personal Best, Keynote and Market Leader 3e Extra are older (pre-2020) or unverified.

### Cited Findings

## 2. Which 2–3 series are the best reference for a speaking-first adult course, and why?

### Takeaway
Best references are (1) **Speakout 3rd ed.**, (2) **Empower 2nd ed.** and (3) **English File 4th ed.**. Roadmap is a strong alternative because it is explicitly speaking-led and has public GSE booklets. **Business Partner** is the workplace-strand reference.

### Cited Findings
- Speakout 3e's stated emphasis is "developing learners' confidence in speaking". It has a "How to…" real-world lesson per unit, an out-of-class speech-recognition tool ("Speak Anywhere"), an enhanced pronunciation syllabus and is designed for online/hybrid use — [Speakout A1 booklet](https://pearsonssha.blob.core.windows.net/teacher/Speakout-3rd-Edition/SAMPLES/Level%20A1/Speakout%20e3%20A1%20-%20GSE%20mapping%20booklet.pdf); [Pearson NL](https://www.pearson.com/nl/en_NL/english-language-teaching/general-english-courses/speakout-3rd-edition.html)
- Empower 2e covers A1–C1 in 6 levels (matching ProEnglish's range), has a dedicated Everyday English lesson per unit and validated CEFR assessment — [Cambridge Empower](https://www.cambridge.org/empower); [eltbooks](https://www.eltbooks.com/p/empower-second-edition)
- English File 4e runs A1–C1.2 with Sound Bank pronunciation videos and Practical English video episodes — [OUP Spain](https://www.oup.es/english-file-4th-edition/); [OUP Australia](https://www.oup.com.au/elt/english-file)
- Roadmap 2e is described by Pearson as "speaking-led learning" — [Pearson Roadmap](https://www.pearson.com/english/catalogue/general-english/roadmap/levels.html)
- Business Partner offers an A1–C1 (8-level) business course with a video-based communication skills strand — [BP C1 booklet](https://www.pearson.com/content/dam/one-dot-com/one-dot-com/english/SampleMaterials/business-english/BusinesPartner/GSE-Mapping-Booklet-Business-Partner-C1.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Speakout 3e** matches the ProEnglish model best: adult, online/hybrid, video-led, speech-recognition practice and GSE-granular objectives. Its public GSE booklets give per-lesson can-do statements to benchmark against.
- **Empower 2e** is the cleanest A1–C1 six-level ladder and could mirror the platform's levels 1:1. Its assessment orientation helps with level tests.
- **English File 4e** is the market-dominant reference for grammar sequencing and has a strong pronunciation strand. It is useful for Portuguese-speaker pronunciation priorities, since OUP produces Iberian editions.
- For working professionals in Angola, Business Partner's communication-skills topics (networking, meetings, presentations, performance reviews) are a good model for an optional "workplace" track at B1–C1.

### Gaps
- No independent comparative review (e.g. ELT Journal / ELTJam) of these series' speaking effectiveness was retrieved.

## 4. Common per-level progression A1→C1 (convergence)

### Takeaway
The series converge strongly on grammar sequencing and functional situations. A1 covers be, present simple, can and there is, ending with past simple. A2 covers past, future and comparatives. B1 covers present perfect, conditionals and passives. B2 covers narrative tenses, modals of deduction and reported speech. C1 covers inversion, ellipsis, hedging and discourse. Functions move from survival transactions to opinion and discussion, then negotiation, persuasion and presentations.

### Cited Findings
- A1 (Speakout 3e): be; possessives; have; present simple and frequency adverbs; can; there is/are; was/were; past simple. Functions: greetings and questions, short conversations, shopping, ordering in a café, requests and offers, asking directions, saying you're unwell, buying a ticket — [Speakout A1 booklet](https://pearsonssha.blob.core.windows.net/teacher/Speakout-3rd-Edition/SAMPLES/Level%20A1/Speakout%20e3%20A1%20-%20GSE%20mapping%20booklet.pdf)
- Empower A2 Everyday English examples: "Well, if you ask me", "me too / me neither". Pronunciation focus: word groups — [Scribd A2 TB contents, via search snippet](https://www.scribd.com/document/663511260/Empower-a2-2ed-Tb)
- C1 (Business Partner): articles and substitution, advanced passives and ellipsis, future attitudes, hypothesising, inversion and fronting, participle clauses, hedging, verb patterns. Speaking can-dos at GSE 70–79: evaluate solutions, persuade, argue formally, summarise presentations — [BP C1 booklet](https://www.pearson.com/content/dam/one-dot-com/one-dot-com/english/SampleMaterials/business-english/BusinesPartner/GSE-Mapping-Booklet-Business-Partner-C1.pdf)
- Roadmap B1+ speaking objective example: "talk about matters of personal information and interest in some detail" — [Roadmap B1+ booklet (snippet)](https://www.pearson.com/content/dam/one-dot-com/one-dot-com/english/SampleMaterials/GeneralEnglish/Roadmap/RoadmapB1PGSEMappingBooklet.pdf)

## 5. Copyright and adaptation policies

### Takeaway
Coursebook content (texts, dialogues, exercises, audio/video scripts, artwork) is copyrighted and cannot be reproduced or adapted for a commercial platform without permission. "Photocopiable" permissions are narrow: classroom use with the purchased course only. Syllabus-level facts (grammar point names, topic labels, CEFR descriptors) are not protected expression. CEFR descriptors themselves are © Council of Europe and need permission to reproduce in published materials.

### Cited Findings
- OUP: photocopiable permission "is always indicated clearly in the book… at the front and on every photocopiable page". It is limited to classroom use (handouts, worksheets, tests) and excludes use for other materials, schools or courses — [OUP Japan copyright](https://www.oupjapan.co.jp/en/copyright/index.shtml)
- Macmillan Education (comparison): permission is needed "to reword, adapt or translate text" from its products — [Springer Nature / Macmillan Education rights](https://www.springernature.com/it/macmillaneducation/work-with-us/rights-permissions)
- OUP regional rights and permissions request channels: [OUP Canada](https://oupcanada.com/corporate/rights_and_permissions.html), [OUP Southern Africa](https://oxford.co.za/talk-to-us/rights-permissions)
- In southern Africa, DALRO administers reprographic rights for most publishers — [search summary citing DALRO](https://oxford.co.za/talk-to-us/rights-permissions)
- Pearson booklets carry the notice: "Copyright © 2022 by Pearson Education… Photocopying for classroom use is permitted. © The copyright of the Can Do statements taken from the Common European Framework… belongs to the Council of Europe. All users and publishers must ask formal and written permission prior to using these" (language.policy@coe.int) — [Speakout A1 booklet](https://pearsonssha.blob.core.windows.net/teacher/Speakout-3rd-Edition/SAMPLES/Level%20A1/Speakout%20e3%20A1%20-%20GSE%20mapping%20booklet.pdf)
- Author perspective on why ELT copyright matters — [Peter Viney, "Why copyright"](https://peterviney.com/about/elt-articles/why-copyright/)

### Inferences
- ProEnglish should take only abstract syllabus decisions from these books (which grammar or function at which level). It should write all dialogues, scripts, exercises and examples originally. It should not mirror any single book's unit order or titles too closely.
- Pearson's GSE Learning Objectives are separately published by Pearson as a free framework. Check its terms of use before quoting GSE descriptors verbatim. Write own can-do statements instead of copying CEFR or GSE wording verbatim in the product.

### Gaps
- No global OUP, Pearson, Cambridge or NGL statement on adapting non-photocopiable content for commercial digital products was retrieved. Contact the rights teams if any adaptation is ever considered.
- Angolan copyright law specifics were not researched.
