# Open-licensed and free-to-reuse English learning materials (A1–C1) for a paid platform

Scope: sources ProEnglish (a paid platform, so commercial use) could legally adapt into lesson scripts, exercises, dialogues and audio. Checked November 2026. "Verified" means the licence text was read on the owner's page. Anything else is flagged.

## Which sources allow commercial adaptation (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0/public domain) versus non-commercial only?

### Takeaway
Few sources are both commercially reusable and full ESL courseware. The clearest ones are:
- **VOA Learning English**: public domain, commercial reprint allowed with credit, but agency material is excluded.
- **Mozilla Common Voice English sentences**: CC0.
- **Tatoeba**: sentences under CC BY 2.0 FR. Audio licences vary by contributor.
- **NGSL word list**: CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **CMU Pronouncing Dictionary**: unrestricted use with acknowledgement.
- **Lingua Libre / Wikimedia Commons audio**: CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA, file by file.

Most ESL open textbooks with speaking content are CC BY-NC or CC BY-NC-SA, so they are off limits for a paid platform. The commercial ESL sites (BBC, British Council, ELLLO, Breaking News English) are all-rights-reserved and cannot be adapted without a licence.

### Cited Findings

**A. Commercially reusable (public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, or permissive custom terms)**

- **VOA Learning English** (learningenglish.voanews.com, owned by Voice of America / USAGM, US government)
  - Licence: public domain. The terms say "Learning English texts, MP3s, photos and videos are in the public domain."
  - Commercial use: reprinting for educational *and commercial* purposes is allowed with the credit "learningenglish.voanews.com".
  - Exception: "stories, photos and video images from news agencies such as AP, Reuters and AFP are copyrighted" and may not be republished.
  - Extra access: high-resolution audio and video are free through USAGM Direct, which requires registration.
  - Source: [VOA Learning English terms](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/p/6861.html)
- **Tatoeba** (tatoeba.org, community sentence corpus)
  - Sentences: default licence is CC BY 2.0 FR. Each sentence can carry its own licence, chosen when it is submitted. "using, reusing, modifying and distributing the sentence is only allowed if the name of the author is cited."
  - Audio: must be at least BY, but each contributor picks a licence for all their audio. Some audio is NC, ND or SA, and some uses a non-CC licence when the contributor has not approved use outside Tatoeba. Audio therefore has to be filtered by licence.
  - Source: [Tatoeba terms of use](https://tatoeba.org/en/terms_of_use)
- **Mozilla Common Voice, English** (Mozilla Foundation, via Mozilla Data Collective)
  - Scripted Speech 25.0 (released 30 March 2026):
    - Licence: CC0-1.0.
    - Text: about 1.72M sentences, 1.68M of them validated.
    - Audio: about 3,766 hours from about 99.7k speakers.
  - Dataset terms: users must not try to identify speakers.
  - Spontaneous Speech 1.0 is CC0 but adds a "do not re-host or re-share" term. Check each version's datasheet.
  - Sources: [Common Voice Scripted 25.0 EN](https://prod.datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmndapwry02jnmh07dyo46mot); [Scripted 23.0 EN](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmflnuzw52mzok78yz6woemc1); [Spontaneous 1.0 EN](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmihqzerk023co20749miafhq); [Wikipedia: Common Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Voice)
- **New General Service List (NGSL)** (Browne, Culligan, Phillips; 2,809 words covering about 92% of everyday English)
  - Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0 International (verified on the NGSL page).
  - The site says the lists are "Free under Creative Commons, including commercial use".
  - Related lists on the same site: NGSL-Spoken (conversation), BSL (business), TSL (TOEIC), the academic list, and NGSL-Graded Reader. The site says the BSL was commissioned by NHK and then released publicly.
  - Sources: [NGSL licence page](https://www.newgeneralservicelist.com/new-general-service-list); [NGSL home](https://www.newgeneralservicelist.com/)
- **CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (CMUdict)** (Carnegie Mellon Speech Group)
  - Accent: American English.
  - Terms: the readme says "Use of this dictionary for any research or commercial purpose is completely unrestricted" and asks users to acknowledge its origin.
  - Maintenance: active, with occasional releases.
  - Source: [cmusphinx/cmudict on GitHub](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict)
- **Lingua Libre → Wikimedia Commons pronunciation audio**
  - Licence: each recorder chooses CC0 1.0, CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0. CC BY-SA 4.0 is the default.
  - Recordings upload automatically to Wikimedia Commons.
  - Check the licence template on each file.
  - Sources: [Lingua Libre RecordWizard manual](https://www.lingualibre.org/wiki/Help:RecordWizard_manual); [Wikipedia: Lingua Libre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Libre)
- **American English** (americanenglish.state.gov, US Department of State)
  - Secondhand account: information on State Department websites is public domain and may be copied without permission unless a copyright is indicated, and the Department should be credited.
  - Materials created by others and hosted on the site are not public domain. They are marked "with permission" or "copyright XYZ".
  - Not verified: the site's own "Copyright Info" page returned HTTP 403.
  - Source: [Georgetown Library guide, open access ESL/EFL](https://library.georgetown.edu/scholarly-communication/open-access-esl-efl)
- **CC BY / CC BY-SA ESL open textbooks** (licences come from search-result summaries of the SaskOER directory and were not verified on each book's page):
  - *Advanced Academic Grammar for ESL Students* (Rebecca Al Haider): CC BY.
  - *Intermediate Reading and Writing* (Al Haider): CC BY.
  - *A Digital Workbook for Beginning ESOL*: CC BY.
  - *Daily Departures* (reading): CC BY.
  - *Writing with Grammar* (Charity Davenport): CC BY-SA.
  - These are mostly academic, reading or writing books, not speaking-focused.
  - Sources: [SaskOER OER-by-discipline, ESL chapter](https://www.saskoer.ca/oerdiscipline/chapter/ell/); [pressbooks.saskpolytech.ca listing](https://pressbooks.saskpolytech.ca/oerdiscipline/?p=356)

**B. Non-commercial only (not usable by a paid platform without permission)**

- ***Communication Beginnings: An Introductory Listening and Speaking Text for English Language Learners*** (Della Jean Abrahams, Portland State University, 2017)
  - Licence: CC BY-NC.
  - Level and content: beginning to intermediate; 7 everyday-topic chapters built on NGSL vocabulary.
  - Reviews: 15 reviews on the Open Textbook Library, with an average of about 4.1–4.9 out of 5 per criterion.
  - Source: [Open Textbook Library](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/communication-beginnings-an-introductory-listening-and-speaking-text-for-english-language-learners)
- ***Foundations of Listening, Speaking, and Pronunciation*** (Larissa Conley, Conestoga Open Learning, 2025)
  - Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, according to a search summary. The book page returned 403.
  - Source: [eCampusOntario Pressbooks](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/foundationslsp)
- ***ESL Grammar The Way You Like It*** (Don Bissonnette, Levels 1–5): CC BY-NC.
  - Sources: [LibreTexts](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Languages/English_as_a_Second_Language/ESL_Grammar_The_Way_You_Like_It_(Bissonnette)); [SaskOER](https://www.saskoer.ca/oerdiscipline/chapter/ell/)
- ***College ESL Writers*** (Hall and Wallace): CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
  - Source: [SaskOER](https://www.saskoer.ca/oerdiscipline/chapter/ell/)
- **LibreTexts ESL bookshelf**: lists 21 books. The bookshelf as a whole shows "not declared license", so each book must be checked separately. Titles include:
  - *People and Places* and *Getting to Know You* (Ferguson), intermediate integrated skills
  - *In the Community* (NorQuest College)
  - *Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs: Intermediate* (Zemlick)
  - *Grammar Slides for English Learners* (Ferguson)
  - *ESL Worksheets* (Borhan)
  - *Explorations 1* (grammar)
  - I did not verify licences for these. A search for Zemlick's licence found nothing.
  - Source: [LibreTexts ESL bookshelf](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Languages/English_as_a_Second_Language)

**C. All rights reserved or custom terms (no commercial adaptation)**

- **ELLLO** (elllo.org, Todd Beuckens)
  - Footer: "elllo productions © copyright 2021".
  - The FAQ allows teachers to download audio for classroom or LMS use only. Requests for more than 10 MP3s cost about USD 2 per file, with a 20-file minimum. Videos are not available for classroom use.
  - The FAQ says nothing about commercial use, and the podcast feeds say "All rights reserved".
  - Sources: [ELLLO FAQ](https://elllo.org/about/faq.htm); [ELLLO Beginner podcast](https://elllobeginner.podbean.com/)
- **Breaking News English** (Sean Banville)
  - Every lesson PDF carries "Copyright Sean Banville 2024". I found no open licence.
  - Sources: [example lesson PDF](https://breakingnewsenglish.com/2406/240627-copyright-infringement.pdf); [About](https://breakingnewsenglish.com/about.html)
- **BBC Learning English**
  - I could not retrieve the official terms. BBC-related licence copies say commercial use needs separate BBC permission and possibly a fee.
  - Sources: [Ravensbourne copy of BBC content licence](https://www.ravensbourne.ac.uk/bbc-motion-graphics-archive/bbc-content-usage-licence); [BBC Written Archives licensing (mirror)](https://southbankcentre.pages.dev/archiveservices/written-archives-centre/licensing-and-copyright)
- **British Council LearnEnglish**
  - The terms pages returned 403 or timed out, so I could not verify them. LearnEnglish has its own house rules page.
  - Source: [LearnEnglish house rules](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/house-rules)
- **Open Textbook Library**: the cached Languages subject page lists no ESL titles.
  - Source: [OTL Languages](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/subjects/languages)

### Inferences
- **VOA Learning English is the best commercially usable source of graded spoken-style content.**
  - It covers roughly A2–B2: slow-paced news, "Let's Learn English" dialogue courses, and "Everyday Grammar" / "English in a Minute" style series. These series names are from background knowledge and were not verified in this session.
  - Every item must be checked for AP, Reuters or AFP photos and text before reuse.
  - Adapting VOA scripts into new dialogues and scenarios for an AI teacher fits within public-domain terms.
- **Tatoeba plus Common Voice give large example-sentence banks** for gap-fill, ordering, dictation and pronunciation drills.
  - Common Voice is CC0, so it is the lowest-friction choice.
  - Tatoeba needs a per-sentence author credit, and its audio must be filtered to BY or BY-SA only.
- **NGSL and NGSL-Spoken can drive the vocabulary syllabus by level.**
  - Being CC BY-SA 4.0, a *published adapted version of the list itself* (for example, a graded word list with Portuguese glosses) must also be CC BY-SA.
  - Lesson content that merely uses words on the list is not an adaptation of the list. This is my legal inference and should be confirmed by counsel.
- **CMUdict and Lingua Libre or Wiktionary audio support pronunciation exercises.**
  - CMUdict is American English only.
  - Audio files need per-file licence checks. CC BY-SA audio that is edited (trimmed, normalised) arguably becomes an adaptation, so the edited file must stay CC BY-SA.
- **The most useful speaking-focused open textbooks are NC.**
  - Examples: *Communication Beginnings*, *Foundations of LSP*, Bissonnette.
  - They can serve only as inspiration for topic sequence (ideas and structure are not copyrightable). Their text and exercises cannot be copied. Another option is to request a commercial permission from the author.
- **Most content will have to be original.**
  - No commercially licensed, CEFR-graded, speaking-focused A1–C1 adult course turned up in this research.
  - The open sources serve as raw material: sentences, vocabulary lists, pronunciation data, VOA topics and scripts. The structured lessons and dialogues will mostly need to be written in-house or AI-generated.

### Gaps
- I did not read the official americanenglish.state.gov "Copyright Info" page (403). Many of its books, such as *Let's Teach English* and *Shaping the Way We Teach*, may carry third-party copyright.
- I did not verify the official BBC and British Council terms pages (403 or timeout). My knowledge suggests both are all-rights-reserved for commercial use, but this is unconfirmed.
- I did not verify per-book licences on the LibreTexts ESL bookshelf (Ferguson, NorQuest, Zemlick, Borhan).
- I did not verify licences for NAWL, BSL, TSL and NGSL-Spoken individually. Only the NGSL page states CC BY-SA 4.0 explicitly.
- I did not check the Wiktionary text licence (likely CC BY-SA 4.0 plus GFDL) or MERLOT and OER Commons listings in this session.
- I did not find a CC-licensed CEFR-graded adult reader series. Known CC BY graded readers (StoryWeaver, Global Digital Library) are children's books, which is background knowledge and was not verified here.

## Which are good enough in quality to adapt for A1–C1 speaking-focused adult lessons?

### Takeaway
VOA Learning English is the highest-quality commercially reusable source with dialogue and audio: professionally produced, current and adult-oriented. Corpora (Tatoeba, Common Voice) and lists (NGSL, CMUdict) are good raw material but not lessons. The best-reviewed speaking textbook (*Communication Beginnings*) is NC.

### Cited Findings
- **VOA Learning English** is professionally produced by a US government broadcaster, with text, MP3 and video all public domain, and its content is current. — [VOA terms](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/p/6861.html)
- ***Communication Beginnings***: reviewers rated structure and modularity about 4.8–4.9 out of 5. They noted level-fit problems (recordings harder than the target level), Portland-specific examples, and no glossary. — [OTL](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/communication-beginnings-an-introductory-listening-and-speaking-text-for-english-language-learners)
- **Common Voice sentences** are crowd-sourced and validated (1.68M of 1.72M). They are not graded by CEFR. — [Common Voice 25.0](https://prod.datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmndapwry02jnmh07dyo46mot)
- **NGSL** gives frequency-based coverage of about 92% of everyday English, and NGSL-Spoken targets conversation. — [NGSL](https://www.newgeneralservicelist.com/)
- **CMUdict** is maintained, comes without accuracy guarantees, and covers American English only. — [GitHub](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict)

### Inferences
- **Practical mix:**
  - Syllabus and vocabulary: NGSL / NGSL-Spoken.
  - Topics, models and listening for B1–B2: VOA.
  - Example sentences, dictation and minimal drills for A1–B1: Tatoeba (BY only) and Common Voice.
  - Pronunciation: CMUdict for phoneme data, Lingua Libre audio for word models. Prefer the platform's own TTS for consistency.
- **C1 coverage from open sources is thin.**
  - VOA is mostly A2–B2.
  - Advanced material would come from original writing or public-domain literature (out-of-copyright texts, for example via Project Gutenberg), which was not checked this session.
- **Common Voice audio is for listening practice, not models.** It varies in accent and quality, so it suits accent-exposure listening tasks better than pronunciation models.

### Gaps
- I have no CEFR mapping of VOA series to levels from a primary source.
- I made no independent quality review of Tatoeba English sentences. They are known to contain errors, but I did not verify this here.

## What attribution or share-alike obligations would apply in an app or website?

### Takeaway
- **Public domain / CC0 (VOA, Common Voice):** no legal obligation, but VOA requests credit "learningenglish.voanews.com".
- **CC BY (Tatoeba sentences, CC BY Commons audio, CC BY textbooks):** credit the author, link the licence, and indicate changes.
- **CC BY-SA (NGSL, many Lingua Libre or Wiktionary files):** the same credit, and adapted versions of that material must be released under CC BY-SA. This does not extend to the whole app.
- **CMUdict:** asks for acknowledgement.

### Cited Findings
- **VOA:** reprint "with credit to learningenglish.voanews.com". Agency (AP, Reuters, AFP) content is excluded. — [VOA terms](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/p/6861.html)
- **Tatoeba:** the author's name must be cited when using, modifying or distributing a sentence, and reusers must keep the licence. Licences can only become more restrictive. — [Tatoeba terms](https://tatoeba.org/en/terms_of_use)
- **NGSL:** CC BY-SA 4.0, credited to Browne, C., Culligan, B. and Phillips, J. The suggested citation is "Browne, C., Culligan, B. (2013). The New General Service List." Note that this citation omits Phillips. — [NGSL](https://www.newgeneralservicelist.com/new-general-service-list)
- **CMUdict:** "acknowledge its origin". — [GitHub](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict)
- **Lingua Libre:** per-file CC0, CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0. — [RecordWizard manual](https://www.lingualibre.org/wiki/Help:RecordWizard_manual)
- **Common Voice:** CC0, plus a dataset term against re-identifying speakers. Some versions forbid re-hosting the dataset. — [Common Voice 25.0](https://prod.datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmndapwry02jnmh07dyo46mot); [Spontaneous 1.0](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmihqzerk023co20749miafhq)
- **State Department sites:** credit the State Department, and third-party items keep their own copyright (secondhand). — [Georgetown guide](https://library.georgetown.edu/scholarly-communication/open-access-esl-efl)

### Inferences
- **Implementation:**
  - Add a "Créditos e licenças" page in the app and website, listing each source, its author, its licence with a link, and the note "adaptado" where content was changed. This is the usual "TASL" practice (Title, Author, Source, Licence).
  - Keep a per-item `source`/`licence`/`author` field in the content database, so that attribution can be shown or exported per exercise. This matters especially for Tatoeba sentence IDs and Commons file names.
- **Share-alike scope (inference, confirm with counsel):**
  - CC BY-SA applies to the adapted work, such as an edited audio file or a modified word list.
  - It does not apply to the whole course or the app code, provided the BY-SA item is a separable component.
  - Keeping BY-SA items separate (for example, word-list data and pronunciation clips) limits the share-alike exposure.
- **Avoid mixing NC material in.** A CC BY-NC item placed in paid lessons breaches the licence.
- **Tatoeba workflow:** filter the export by licence before ingestion. Tatoeba publishes per-sentence licence and audio-licence data in its downloads (background knowledge, not verified).

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the CC licence deed texts in this session. The standard deeds are at https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/fr/ and https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/.
- Whether AI-generated derivative scripts "based on" VOA or Tatoeba content count as adaptations is a legal question that this research did not resolve.
