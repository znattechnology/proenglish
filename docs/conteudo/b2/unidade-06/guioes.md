# B2.2 · Unidade 6 — Health, safety and environment · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B2](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2, do B1 e do B2.1 (unidades 1 a 5). Novo: *needn't have* + particípio (fez-se, mas não era preciso) e *didn't need to* + infinitivo (não se fez, porque não era preciso); *was / were supposed to* e *be meant to* (o que estava previsto e não aconteceu); especular sobre o passado com *may have, might have, could have, must have, can't have* (revistos do B1 e alargados: na passiva, *it may have been left*; e no contínuo, *he can't have been watching*); a passiva com modais no passado (revista, B2 4B).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** *whereas, while* (de contraste), *admittedly, even if, even though, nevertheless* (unidade 7); verbos de relato como *suggest, insist, deny, warn, advise* e a passiva de relato (unidade 9); *wish / if only*, *would* para hábitos do passado, o *past perfect continuous* e os condicionais mistos (unidade 10).

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Kalandula Tech, a Mopane Systems, a Morro Azul Energia, a Kunene Freight e a central do Tômbwa são fictícias, como a construtora "Kuvango Construções". O Tômbwa, o deserto do Namibe e as welwitschias (plantas do deserto que só existem no Namibe e na Namíbia) são reais; a "zona protegida" junto à obra é descrita de forma genérica, sem dizer que pertence a um parque concreto.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**; **Paulo Mendes**; **Elsa Chilombo** (forma os seis técnicos do Tômbwa desde Fevereiro, B2 R1A); **Hannah** (Morro Azul, agora no Tômbwa); **Ingrid Solberg** (directora de operações da Morro Azul). Novos e fictícios: **Ruth Mwangi** (queniana, responsável de segurança e ambiente da Morro Azul); **Telma Cahinga** (angolana, inspectora de segurança da Morro Azul; escreve o relatório da 6F, em português); **Sr. Faustino** (operador da grua da Kuvango; só fala português); dois dos seis técnicos, **Celso** e **Matilde**; na 6E, **Kgosi** (sul-africano, chefe da equipa de montagem da Mopane) e **Lebo** (sul-africana, montadora da Mopane).

**A história:** Março de 2029. As obras do Tômbwa começaram em Fevereiro; o Nuno passa duas semanas por mês na obra. Na segunda, 5, a equipa descobre que se preocupou de mais com a fronteira (6A). Na quarta, 7, nada está pronto como estava previsto (6B). Na quinta, 15, há um quase-acidente: um contentor cai de uma grua; na sexta, 16, a equipa especula sobre as causas (6C). Na segunda, 19, às 7:00, o Kgosi faz a conversa de segurança no início do turno (6E); às 15:00, o Nuno relata o incidente à Ingrid e à Ruth (6F).

**O quase-acidente (igual em todas as aulas em que aparece):**
- Quinta-feira, 15 de Março de 2029, cerca das 10:40. A grua da Kuvango, operada pelo Sr. Faustino, descarrega o contentor dos mastros, que chegou no segundo camião da Kunene Freight.
- A regra da obra: não se levanta carga com vento acima de 40 km/h. O anemómetro da cabina da grua estava avariado desde segunda, 12; a avaria foi comunicada, mas não foi reparada. O anemómetro da Hannah, na sala provisória, marcou rajadas de 52 km/h às 10:30.
- Uma das quatro cintas de elevação estava gasta e partiu-se; o contentor balançou e caiu de cerca de três metros para a areia, a cinco metros do caminho por onde o Celso e a Matilde tinham passado um minuto antes. Ninguém ficou ferido. Os mastros não se estragaram.
- O contentor caiu sobre dois bidões de gasóleo que estavam junto à zona de descarga (deviam estar no depósito de combustível com bacia de retenção, a 50 metros). Um bidão rachou: cerca de 60 litros de gasóleo derramaram-se na areia, a 200 metros da zona protegida onde crescem welwitschias.
- O derrame foi contido com areia e material absorvente até às 11:30; cerca de um metro cúbico de areia contaminada foi retirado na sexta, 16.
- As medidas: vento medido com um anemómetro portátil antes de cada elevação; cintas inspeccionadas todos os dias e etiquetadas; zona de exclusão de 15 metros à volta de cada elevação; combustível só no depósito com bacia de retenção; kits de derrame na zona de descarga; conversa de segurança todas as manhãs.

## 6A — We needn't have worried

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6A — We needn't have worried        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue distinguir o que se fez sem ser preciso (needn't have done) do
  que não se fez porque não era preciso (didn't need to do).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: needn't have + particípio (We needn't have booked the hotel. = reservámos, mas não era
  preciso); didn't need to + infinitivo (We didn't need to pay the agent. = não pagámos, porque não era
  preciso; ou não se sabe); don't need to / needn't no presente (revisão)
Palavras-alvo: border, customs, paperwork, agent, hotel, booking, crane, extra weeks, on time, worry, lesson
Reciclagem: be bound to (B2, 5A)
Pronúncia: needn't have dito curto (needn't've), com o acento em "needn't"
Contexto: segunda-feira, 5 de Março de 2029, 18:00; a obra do Tômbwa; o primeiro camião da Kunene Freight
  (os cabos) chegou; o Nuno e o Paulo junto ao contentor
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 6A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 6
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello, and welcome to B2.2! This unit is about health, safety and the environment on a building site. Today: two ways to talk about things that weren't necessary — "We needn't have worried" and "We didn't need to".

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 5 de Março, 18:00; a obra do Tômbwa, ao fim da tarde; um camião da Kunene Freight com um contentor; o Paulo e o Nuno, cansados mas a sorrir]

**PAULO:** I can't believe it. The truck crossed the border in one day.
**NUNO:** You said trucks were bound to be late at Oshikango. Every truck, every time!
**PAULO:** I know, I know. This time the papers were perfect — Elsa sent them in December, like you promised Femi.
**NUNO:** So we needn't have booked the extra hotel nights in Ondjiva for the driver. That's three nights we paid for nothing.
**PAULO:** And I needn't have flown to Ondjiva on Saturday to check. I lost a whole weekend.
**NUNO:** At least we didn't need to pay the customs agent. Moses's driver did everything himself.
**PAULO:** True. And we didn't need to use the two extra weeks either — not yet.
**NUNO:** Hold on — did you tell the builders the truck would arrive late? They were going to rent a second crane for Wednesday.
**PAULO:** No, I didn't need to. I called them this morning, when the truck left the border, so they didn't rent it.
**NUNO:** Good. Then that's money we didn't spend. And Hannah? She was worried about the road.
**PAULO:** She needn't have been. The road was dry all the way.
**NUNO:** OK. Lesson for the next two trucks: keep the extra weeks, but don't book hotels until we know.
**PAULO:** Agreed. And next time I'll stay home on Saturday!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

***Needn't have* ou *didn't need to*?**

[ECRÃ:]

| | Exemplo | Fez-se? |
|---|---|---|
| needn't have + particípio | We **needn't have booked** the hotel nights. | Sim — mas não era preciso (dinheiro perdido). |
| | I **needn't have driven** to the border. | Sim — e perdi o fim-de-semana. |
| didn't need to + infinitivo | We **didn't need to pay** the customs agent. | Não — porque não era preciso. |
| | I **didn't need to** tell them. | Não — já tinham a informação. |

**PROF:** "Needn't have" plus the participle: you did it, and it was a waste. "Didn't need to" plus the infinitive: it wasn't necessary, so usually you didn't do it.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: Em português: "Não precisávamos de ter reservado o hotel." → We needn't have booked the hotel. · "Não foi preciso pagar ao despachante." → We didn't need to pay the agent.]

**PROF:** Portuguese helps here: "não precisávamos de ter feito" is "needn't have done"; "não foi preciso fazer" is "didn't need to do".
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ We needn't to have booked. · ✘ We didn't need booking. · ✔ We needn't have booked. · ✔ We didn't need to book.]

**PROF:** No "to" after "needn't". And "didn't need" always takes "to".
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Com *be***

[ECRÃ: Hannah was worried about the road. She **needn't have been**. (estava preocupada — sem razão)]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "Needn't have" is short in speech: needn't've, with the stress on "needn't". Listen: We needn't have booked the hotel.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: I needn't have driven to the border.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** We didn't need to pay the agent.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** "Needn't have" or "didn't need to"?
[ECRÃ: Levei o guarda-chuva, mas não choveu.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I needn't have taken my umbrella.]
[ECRÃ: Não levei o guarda-chuva: a previsão era de sol.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I didn't need to take my umbrella.]
[ECRÃ: Cozinhámos para trinta, mas vieram doze.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: We needn't have cooked so much food.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Tell me about a time you worried or prepared too much — for an exam, a trip, a meeting or a visit. What did you do that you needn't have done, and what didn't you need to do in the end? About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Last year, my boss from Johannesburg came to visit our office. I was so nervous that I stayed until midnight preparing forty slides. I needn't have bothered: she only wanted to talk over coffee. I needn't have bought a new suit either! In the end, I didn't need to present anything — she just asked us what we needed.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it better to prepare too much or too little? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 5A):** Paulo said, in December: "The trucks are ___ to be late at the border." Complete it — and say whether he was right.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The trucks are bound to be late. — He was wrong this time.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Needn't have done" — you did it, for nothing. "Didn't need to do" — it wasn't necessary. Next lesson: things that were supposed to happen, and didn't.

## 6B — It was supposed to be ready

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6B — It was supposed to be ready        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar do que estava previsto e não aconteceu com was / were
  supposed to e be meant to, e de regras com be supposed to no presente.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 3
Gramática-alvo: was / were supposed to + infinitivo (The bases were supposed to be ready on Monday. = e não
  estavam); be meant to (igual, um pouco mais formal); no presente, para regras (You're supposed to wear a
  helmet. = é a regra); wasn't supposed to (não devia, mas aconteceu); a passiva depois de supposed to
  (was supposed to be finished)
Palavras-alvo: concrete base, cement, supplier, schedule, behind schedule, builder, site manager, helmet,
  rule, guard, visitor, sign in, unloading area
Reciclagem: should have been + particípio (B2, 4B); previsões (B2, 5A)
Pronúncia: supposed to dito como "suppose-to", sem o "d"
Contexto: quarta-feira, 7 de Março, 8:00; a obra; o Nuno, o Paulo e a Hannah percorrem o terreno
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 6B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! On every building site, many things are "supposed to" happen. Today: how to talk about plans that didn't happen, and rules that people don't follow.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 7 de Março, 8:00; a obra; buracos na areia onde deviam estar as bases de betão; uma placa "Uso obrigatório de capacete"; o Nuno, o Paulo e a Hannah]

**HANNAH:** Good morning! Welcome to day three of "nothing is ready".
**NUNO:** The concrete bases for the masts were supposed to be finished on Monday.
**HANNAH:** I know. The cement was meant to arrive last week, but the supplier sent it to Namibe city by mistake.
**PAULO:** And the road to the control room? It was supposed to be done by the end of February.
**HANNAH:** Also late. The builders are about two weeks behind schedule.
**NUNO:** OK. So when are the bases meant to be ready now?
**HANNAH:** Friday — if the cement arrives today.
**PAULO:** That's a big "if". [aponta para um homem sem capacete] And look at that guy. You're supposed to wear a helmet everywhere on this site!
**HANNAH:** You're right. And he's not supposed to be in the unloading area at all — that's for the crane team only.
**NUNO:** Are visitors supposed to sign in at the gate? Nobody asked us yesterday.
**HANNAH:** They're supposed to. The guard was meant to check everyone, but he was helping with the cement. I'll talk to the site manager.
**PAULO:** So, our plan for this week was supposed to be: bases on Monday, masts on Thursday.
**NUNO:** Now it's bases on Friday, masts next week — if we're lucky.
**HANNAH:** Welcome to construction!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

***Was supposed to*: o plano que falhou**

[ECRÃ: The bases **were supposed to be finished** on Monday. (não estavam) · The cement **was meant to arrive** last week. (não chegou) · The road **was supposed to be done** by February. (não está)]

**PROF:** "Was supposed to" means: this was the plan, or the expectation — but it didn't happen. "Was meant to" is the same. After "supposed to", you can use the passive: "was supposed to be finished".
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Be supposed to* no presente: a regra**

[ECRÃ: You**'re supposed to** wear a helmet everywhere. (é a regra) · He**'s not supposed to** be in the unloading area. (é proibido — mas está lá)]

**PROF:** In the present, "supposed to" is the rule — and often, the rule isn't followed. "You're supposed to wear a helmet" sounds like: but some people don't.
[PAUSA 5 s]

***Supposed to* ou *should*?**

[ECRÃ: The bases **were supposed to** be ready. (era o plano) · The bases **should have been** ready. (alguém falhou — crítica, B2 4B)]

**PROF:** "Was supposed to" reports the plan. "Should have been" criticises. Use "supposed to" when you don't want to blame anyone yet.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ It was suppose to be ready. · ✘ It supposed to be ready. · ✔ It was supposed to be ready.]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In speech, the "d" of "supposed" disappears: suppose-to. Listen: The bases were supposed to be finished on Monday.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: You're supposed to wear a helmet.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** The cement was meant to arrive last week.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Say what was supposed to happen.
[ECRÃ: O voo devia sair às 9h. Saiu às 13h.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: The flight was supposed to leave at nine, but it left at one.]
[ECRÃ: A regra: telemóveis desligados nas reuniões. O Rui atende chamadas.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: We're supposed to switch our phones off in meetings, but Rui answers calls.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Think of a plan that went wrong — a trip, a party, a project or a delivery. What was supposed to happen, what actually happened, and what was meant to be done that wasn't? And mention one rule that people where you work or study don't always follow. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — My cousin's wedding was supposed to start at three. The band was meant to arrive at two, but they got stuck in traffic, and the food was supposed to be served at six — it came at nine! In the end, everyone danced until midnight. At my office, we're supposed to leave our desks clean every evening, but almost nobody does.]

**PROF (opinião):** Why do people break safety rules, even when they know them?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 4B):** Criticise, with the passive: the guard didn't check the visitors.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The visitors should have been checked at the gate.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Was supposed to" for the plan that failed; "you're supposed to" for the rule. Next lesson: something goes really wrong — and we try to work out why.

## 6C — It may have been the wind

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6C — It may have been the wind        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue especular sobre as causas de um acidente com may / might /
  could / must / can't have, também na passiva e no contínuo.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: may / might / could have + particípio (possível); must have (quase certo); can't / couldn't
  have (impossível) — revistos do B1 (10C); alargados: na passiva (The sling must have been damaged.) e no
  contínuo (He can't have been watching the wind.)
Palavras-alvo: crane, sling, lift, gust, anemometer, worn, drum, diesel, spill, near miss, evidence, rush,
  pressure
Reciclagem: modais no passado (B1, 10C); was supposed to (6B)
Pronúncia: must have been, may have been, can't have been ditos curtos, com o acento no modal
Contexto: sexta-feira, 16 de Março, 8:00; a sala provisória da obra, no dia seguinte ao quase-acidente; o
  Nuno, o Paulo, a Elsa e a Hannah
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 6C; conversa guiada "What went wrong?"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Yesterday, on the Tômbwa site, a container fell from a crane. Nobody was hurt — but it was close. Today: how to speculate about what caused it.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 16 de Março, 8:00; um contentor de lado na areia; à volta, fita de segurança; na sala provisória, o Nuno, o Paulo, a Elsa e a Hannah; em cima da mesa, uma cinta de elevação partida]

**NUNO:** OK. Before Ruth's inspector arrives, let's think. Why did the container fall?
**PAULO:** Look at this sling. It's worn on one side. It must have been damaged before yesterday — a sling doesn't wear like that in one lift.
**HANNAH:** And the wind. My anemometer showed gusts of fifty-two kilometres an hour at ten thirty. Our limit is forty.
**ELSA:** So why did they lift? Mr Faustino has twenty years of experience.
**PAULO:** He can't have been watching the wind. The anemometer in his cab was broken — since Monday.
**HANNAH:** Wait. It was reported on Monday. Someone was supposed to repair it.
**NUNO:** So the operator may not have known how strong the wind was.
**ELSA:** Or he might have felt pressure to finish. We were already a week behind.
**HANNAH:** That's possible. Nobody said it, but they could have been rushing.
**NUNO:** And the diesel drums? They're supposed to be in the fuel area, fifty metres away.
**PAULO:** Someone must have left them there after refuelling the generator. They can't have been there for long — I walked past at eight, and the area was empty.
**ELSA:** What scares me is Celso and Matilde. They'd walked past a minute before. It could have killed them.
**NUNO:** I know. That's why we report everything — even what we're not sure about.
**HANNAH:** Agreed. But let's keep "may have" and "must have" separate in the report. Facts first, then possible causes.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Do possível ao impossível**

[ECRÃ:]

| Certeza | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| quase certo | The sling **must have been damaged** before yesterday. · Someone **must have left** the drums there. |
| possível | The operator **may not have known** how strong the wind was. · He **might have felt** pressure. · They **could have been rushing**. |
| impossível | He **can't have been watching** the wind. · They **can't have been** there for long. |

**PROF:** You know these from B1. At B2, two extensions. With the passive: "must have been damaged". And with the continuous, for an action in progress: "can't have been watching", "could have been rushing".
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ He mustn't have seen the wind. · ✔ He can't have seen the wind. · ✘ It must have damaged. (= o contentor danificou alguma coisa) · ✔ It must have been damaged.]

**PROF:** Remember: the opposite of "must have" is "can't have", not "mustn't have". And if the thing received the action, use the passive.
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Could have*: dois sentidos**

[ECRÃ: They **could have been** rushing. (talvez estivessem — especulação) · It **could have killed** them. (podia ter acontecido, mas não aconteceu)]

**PROF:** "Could have" can mean "maybe it happened", or "it was possible, but it didn't happen". Context tells you which.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** These forms are short in speech, with the stress on the modal. Listen: It must've been damaged. He can't've been watching.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: The sling must have been damaged before yesterday.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** He can't have been watching the wind.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Speculate about the cause.
[ECRÃ: A porta do armazém estava aberta de manhã. (certeza alta)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Someone must have left the warehouse door open.]
[ECRÃ: O Rui diz que esteve no escritório às 10h, mas a câmara do parque mostra-o lá fora. (impossível)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: He can't have been working in the office at ten.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Tell me about an accident or a near miss you saw or heard about — at work, on the road or at home. What may have caused it, what must have happened, and what can't have been the cause? About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Last month, a taxi hit a motorbike near my house. Nobody was badly hurt. The taxi driver must have been looking at his phone — the passengers said he was writing a message. The motorbike might have been going too fast, too. It can't have been the road: it was dry and in good condition. Both of them could have died. The driver should have been stopped by the police long ago — everyone knows him.]

**PROF (opinião):** After an accident, is it more important to find out what happened or who is responsible? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — 6B):** What was supposed to happen to the anemometer on Monday?
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: It was supposed to be repaired.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Must have been", "may have been", "can't have been watching" — and facts first, then causes. Now try the tutor's conversation "What went wrong?". Next lesson: the words of risk and the environment.

## 6D — Risk and the environment

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6D — Risk and the environment        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário de segurança e ambiente (near miss, hazard,
  risk assessment, spill, waste, emissions, biodiversity), colocações (carry out an inspection, reduce the
  risk, comply with), dizer grupos consonânticos no fim das palavras, e evitar os falsos amigos prevent e
  precise.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: colocações (carry out an inspection / a risk assessment, reduce / minimise the risk, comply
  with regulations, report an incident, contain a spill, wear protective equipment, raise a concern);
  nomes compostos (risk assessment, exclusion zone, spill kit, safety officer)
Palavras-alvo: hazard, risk, near miss, injury, risk assessment, protective equipment (PPE), exclusion
  zone, spill, leak, contamination, waste, emissions, biodiversity, protected area, comply, regulations
Reciclagem: must have / can't have (6C); a passiva (B2, 4A)
Pronúncia: grupos consonânticos no fim (risks, tests, months, asked, sixths) — dito, sem símbolos no ecrã
Falsos amigos: prevent (≠ prevenir → warn, let someone know; prevent = impedir, evitar); precise (≠
  precisar → need; precise = exacto)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 6D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of safety and the environment — the words of every inspection report. And two false friends that can cause real confusion on a site.

### 2. Segurança (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| hazard | A worn sling is a hazard. (perigo, fonte de risco) |
| risk | The risk is that the load falls. (risco: a probabilidade e a gravidade) |
| near miss | Thursday was a near miss: nobody was hurt. (quase-acidente) |
| injury | There were no injuries. (ferimento, lesão) |
| risk assessment | We carried out a risk assessment before the lift. (avaliação de riscos) |
| PPE | Helmets, vests and gloves are PPE. (equipamento de protecção individual) |
| exclusion zone | Nobody enters the exclusion zone during a lift. (zona interdita) |

**PROF:** Repeat: hazard. Near miss. Injury. Risk assessment. Exclusion zone.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: **hazard** (a coisa perigosa: uma cinta gasta) ≠ **risk** (o que pode acontecer: o contentor cair)]

**PROF:** A hazard is the dangerous thing. A risk is what could happen because of it.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 3. Ambiente e colocações (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| spill / leak | Sixty litres of diesel spilled. The drum was leaking. (derrame / fuga) |
| contamination | The contaminated sand was removed. |
| waste | Building waste must be taken to the dump. (resíduos) |
| emissions | The generators produce emissions. |
| biodiversity | The protected area is rich in biodiversity. |

[ECRÃ: **carry out** an inspection / a risk assessment · **reduce** the risk · **comply with** the regulations · **report** an incident · **contain** a spill · **raise** a concern]

**PROF:** "Carry out" is the verb for inspections and assessments — not "make" or "do". And we "comply with" rules — with "with".
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: grupos de consoantes (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** English words often end with three or four consonants. Don't add a vowel at the end — say all the consonants. Listen: risks. Tests. Months. Asked.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: The risks were assessed. [PAUSA 4 s] We carried out tests for three months. [PAUSA 5 s] He asked about the spill. [PAUSA 4 s]
(PT: Evite dizer "riskes" ou "testes": o "s" vem logo a seguir ao "k" e ao "t".)

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **prevent** ≠ prevenir (avisar)]

**PROF:** "Prevent" means to stop something from happening: "An exclusion zone prevents accidents."
(PT: "Prevenir" no sentido de avisar alguém diz-se "warn" ou "let someone know": "Previne o Paulo de que a grua vem amanhã" → "Let Paulo know that the crane is coming tomorrow". Mas "prevenir acidentes" é mesmo "prevent accidents".)
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **precise** ≠ precisar]

**PROF:** "Precise" is an adjective: exact. "We need precise measurements."
(PT: "Precisar" é "need": "Precisamos de mais cintas" → "We need more slings".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (2 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Complete with the right verb.
[ECRÃ: Ruth will … an inspection on Monday.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: carry out]
[ECRÃ: All contractors must … with the safety regulations.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: comply]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Describe the main hazards in your workplace, your school or your home, the risks they create, and what is done — or should be done — to reduce them. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — In our office, the biggest hazard is the electrical cables under the desks: someone could trip and fall. The generator is another hazard: it produces emissions and it's very noisy. A risk assessment was carried out last year, but the cables still haven't been moved. They should be covered, and the generator should be checked every month.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should companies pay a fine for every environmental mistake, even small ones? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF:** Well done! Next lesson: a real safety talk at the start of a shift — with noise, questions and a few jokes.

## 6F — Work & Life: The near miss

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 6        Aula: 6F — The near miss        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue relatar um quase-acidente à direcção: os factos, as causas
  prováveis, o que devia e o que não precisava de ter sido feito, o risco ambiental e as medidas; e
  resumir em inglês um relatório de inspecção escrito em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2, 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: modais no passado (também na passiva e no contínuo); was supposed to;
  needn't have / didn't need to; a passiva com modais no passado
Palavras-alvo: near miss, incident report, direct cause, contributing factor, recommendation, spill,
  contained, protected area, inspection, exclusion zone
Reciclagem: a passiva (B2, 4A e 4B); future perfect (B2, 2C); responder a perguntas difíceis (B2, 2D)
Pronúncia: factos com a voz firme e a descer; causas possíveis com a voz mais aberta
Mediação: o Nuno resume à Ingrid, em inglês, o relatório de inspecção da Telma Cahinga, escrito em português
Contexto: segunda-feira, 19 de Março, 15:00; videochamada; o Nuno e a Hannah na sala provisória do Tômbwa;
  a Ingrid e a Ruth Mwangi na sede, em Luanda
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 6F; conversa livre "Safety inspection"
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today, Nuno reports the near miss to Morro Azul's director of operations and their safety manager. Watch how he separates facts from causes, admits what went wrong, and explains the measures.

### 2. Situação (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 19 de Março, 15:00; videochamada; o Nuno e a Hannah na sala provisória; a Ingrid e a Ruth no ecrã; no ecrã partilhado, "Near miss — 15 March — Tômbwa"]

**INGRID:** Thank you for joining, Nuno, Hannah. Ruth and I have read the first email, but I'd like to hear it from you.
**NUNO:** Of course. I'll start with the facts, then the causes, then the measures. On Thursday, at about ten forty, the builders' crane was unloading the container with our masts. One of the four slings broke, and the container fell about three metres onto the sand — five metres from a path where two of our technicians had walked a minute earlier. Nobody was injured, and the masts weren't damaged.
**RUTH:** And the diesel?
**NUNO:** The container fell on two diesel drums. One split, and about sixty litres spilled into the sand, two hundred metres from the protected area. The spill was contained by eleven thirty, and the contaminated sand was removed on Friday.
**INGRID:** Why were the drums there?
**NUNO:** They weren't supposed to be. They're meant to be in the fuel area, with the bund. Someone must have left them there after refuelling the generator that morning. We haven't found out who yet.
**RUTH:** Inspector Cahinga's report is in Portuguese, and my Portuguese is slow. Could you summarise her conclusions?
**NUNO:** Certainly.

[ECRÃ: o relatório (em português): "Relatório de inspecção — quase-acidente de 15/03/2029. Causa directa: rotura de uma cinta de elevação com desgaste visível, que não foi retirada de serviço. Factores contribuintes: (1) vento com rajadas de 52 km/h, acima do limite de 40 km/h; o anemómetro da grua estava avariado desde 12/03 e a reparação não foi feita; (2) ausência de zona de exclusão marcada; (3) armazenamento de combustível fora da área com bacia de retenção. Impacto ambiental: derrame de cerca de 60 litros de gasóleo, contido; areia contaminada removida (cerca de 1 m³); sem danos visíveis na zona protegida. Recomendações: medição do vento antes de cada elevação; inspecção diária das cintas; zona de exclusão de 15 m; combustível só na área com bacia; formação de toda a equipa. Telma Cahinga, Inspectora de Segurança"]

**NUNO:** The direct cause was the sling: it was visibly worn, and it should have been taken out of service. She lists three contributing factors. First, the wind: gusts of fifty-two kilometres an hour, over our limit of forty — and the crane's anemometer had been broken since Monday the twelfth. It was supposed to be repaired, and it wasn't. Second, there was no exclusion zone marked around the lift. And third, the fuel was stored outside the bunded area. On the environment, she says the spill was contained, the sand was removed — about one cubic metre — and there's no visible damage to the protected area. She makes five recommendations, and all five were in place by this morning.
**INGRID:** That's a clear summary. Thank you. Now — honestly — could this have been avoided?
**NUNO:** Yes. Honestly, it could. If the anemometer had been repaired, the operator would have known the wind was too strong. And the sling must have been worn for weeks — a daily check would have found it.
**RUTH:** Was the operator under pressure? You were behind schedule.
**NUNO:** He may have been. He says not, but we were a week late, and everyone wanted the masts up. I can't say for sure, so I'd rather not guess. What I can say is that from now on, no schedule is more important than the wind limit.
**INGRID:** Good. I noticed you stopped the whole site on Friday.
**NUNO:** We did. Looking back, we needn't have closed everything — the inspector only needed the unloading area. But on Friday morning, we didn't know that, and I preferred to be careful.
**RUTH:** I'd have done the same. And you didn't need to call the fire brigade?
**NUNO:** No, we didn't need to. The spill was small, and our spill kit was enough. But we've put a second kit in the unloading area now.
**INGRID:** Thank you, Nuno. I appreciate how open you've been. Ruth, will you visit the site?
**RUTH:** On Thursday. I'd like to see the exclusion zone during a real lift.
**NUNO:** You're welcome. The next container arrives on Wednesday — so by Thursday, we'll have done at least two lifts with the new rules.

### 3. Como se relata um quase-acidente (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno fez]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Anunciar a ordem | I'll start with the facts, then the causes, then the measures. | (a frase inteira) |
| 2. Os factos (passado, passiva) | broke, fell, was contained, was removed | The spill was contained by eleven thirty. |
| 3. O que estava previsto | weren't supposed to be · were meant to be | They weren't supposed to be there. |
| 4. As causas, com graus de certeza | must have · may have · I can't say for sure | Someone must have left them there. · He may have been under pressure. |
| 5. Assumir (passiva com modais) | should have been · could have been avoided | It should have been taken out of service. |
| 6. Olhar para trás | needn't have · didn't need to | We needn't have closed everything. |
| 7. As medidas e o futuro | from now on · by Thursday, we'll have… | By Thursday, we'll have done two lifts with the new rules. |

**PROF:** Notice the most important sentence: "I can't say for sure, so I'd rather not guess." In an incident report, a wrong cause is worse than no cause. Nuno uses "must have" only when the evidence is strong — the worn sling — and "may have" when it isn't — the pressure on the operator.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**A mediação**

**PROF:** The inspector's report is formal and dense: "causa directa", "factores contribuintes", "recomendações". Nuno keeps her structure — the cause, three factors, the environment, the recommendations — and adds one thing Ruth needs to know: they're all in place.
(PT: Num relatório técnico, os termos têm equivalentes fixos: "causa directa" → "direct cause"; "factores contribuintes" → "contributing factors"; "bacia de retenção" → "bund" ou "bunded area"; "retirar de serviço" → "take out of service".)
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Facts sound firm, with the voice going down. Possible causes sound more open. Listen: The sling broke. [voz firme] … The operator may have been under pressure. [voz mais aberta]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat both.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 5. A sua vez (3 min)

**PROF:** Now you. Report a near miss to your manager — your own, or this one: in your office, a heavy shelf fell over at night; nobody was there; the shelf was supposed to be fixed to the wall. Give the facts, the possible causes with degrees of certainty, what should have been done, one thing you needn't have done afterwards, and the measures. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I'll start with the facts. On Tuesday night, the big shelf in the archive fell over. Nobody was there, and nobody was hurt, but three boxes of files were damaged. The shelf was supposed to be fixed to the wall, but it wasn't. The screws must have come out — the wall is old. It may also have been too heavy at the top. It should have been checked when we moved in. We needn't have called the police — nothing was stolen. From now on, all shelves will be fixed and checked every year.]

**PROF:** Now the mediation. Summarise this short report in English.
[ECRÃ: "Inspecção ao armazém, 12/04. Extintores: 2 de 6 fora de validade. Saída de emergência bloqueada por caixas. Iluminação de emergência a funcionar. Recomendações: substituir os extintores esta semana; desimpedir a saída hoje."]
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — The warehouse was inspected on the twelfth of April. Two of the six fire extinguishers were out of date, and the emergency exit was blocked by boxes. The emergency lights were working. The inspector recommends replacing the extinguishers this week and clearing the exit today.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should a near miss — when nobody is hurt — always be reported? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Fantastic! You can now talk about what wasn't necessary, what was supposed to happen, and what may have caused an accident — and report it clearly. Go to the tutor for "Safety inspection". Next unit: City life.
