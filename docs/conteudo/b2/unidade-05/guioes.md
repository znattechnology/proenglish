# B2.1 · Unidade 5 — Innovation · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B2](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2, do B1 e das unidades 1 a 4 do B2. Novo: previsões graduadas (*will definitely, is bound to, is likely to, may well, there's a good chance that, is unlikely to, definitely won't*); o *future perfect continuous* (*By June, we'll have been testing it for three months.*), em contraste com o *future continuous* (B1) e o *future perfect* (B2, 2C); especular com *suppose / imagine / what if* e os condicionais, e pesar prós e contras; formação de palavras com mudança de acento.

**Não usar nesta unidade:** *needn't have*, *was supposed to* (unidade 6); *whereas, admittedly, even if, nevertheless* (unidade 7); a passiva de relato (*It is said that…*) e verbos de relato como *suggest, insist, deny, warn* (unidade 9); *wish / if only*, *would* para hábitos do passado e os condicionais mistos (unidade 10).

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Kalandula Tech, a Mopane Systems, a Morro Azul Energia e a central do Tômbwa são fictícias, como o assistente "HelpMate" da 5F e o programa de rádio da 5E. A notícia da 5F foi escrita pela ProEnglish e não fala de nenhuma empresa real.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**; **Paulo Mendes**; **Elsa Chilombo**; **Lerato**; **Sílvia**; **Thandi** (Mopane, por vídeo); **Hannah** (Morro Azul); **Rosa** (do apoio ao cliente, B2 4A; aparece pela primeira vez na 5F). Novos e fictícios, na 5E: **Wanjiku Kamau** (queniana, apresentadora de rádio), **Sizwe Ndlovu** (sul-africano, sindicalista), **Tunde Bakare** (nigeriano, fundador de uma *start-up*) e **Professora Marta Kiluange** (angolana, professora de engenharia informática em Luanda).

**A história:** Dezembro de 2028. O contrato do Tômbwa foi assinado na terça, 28 de Novembro (B2 4C). Na quarta, 6 de Dezembro, o Nuno, o Paulo e a Elsa visitam pela primeira vez o terreno da central, com a Hannah, e fazem previsões para 2029 (5A). Na terça, 12, a Lerato faz ao Nuno a avaliação do ano (5B). Na quarta, 13, ao almoço, a equipa imagina o trabalho daqui a dez anos (5C). Na sexta, 15, o Nuno ouve um debate de rádio sobre inteligência artificial (5E). Na segunda, 18, a Thandi propõe que a Kalandula e a Mopane usem um assistente de inteligência artificial no apoio ao cliente, e o Nuno tem de dar uma recomendação (5F).

**Datas da vida do Nuno (para a 5B):** entrou na Kalandula Tech em Outubro de 2026 (A2); começou a preparar o exame B2 em Junho de 2028 (B1 10F); os testes da rede do Tômbwa vão de Junho a Agosto de 2029 (B2 2C); a central abre no fim de 2029.

## 5A — It's bound to happen

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5A — It's bound to happen        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue fazer previsões com graus de certeza (will definitely, is
  bound to, is likely to, may well, there's a good chance that, is unlikely to, definitely won't).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: be bound to + infinitivo (quase certo); be likely / unlikely to + infinitivo; may well (bem
  possível); there's a good chance (that)…; will definitely / probably, probably won't, definitely won't
  (B1, 4C); a posição de definitely e probably (antes de won't, depois de will)
Palavras-alvo: site, sand, dune, wind, forecast, delay, border, rain, flood, generator, prediction
Reciclagem: will probably, might, I doubt (B1, 4C); future perfect (B2, 2C)
Pronúncia: bound to dito como um bloco (bound-to), e o acento em "likely" e "unlikely"
Contexto: quarta-feira, 6 de Dezembro de 2028, 11:00; o terreno da futura central do Tômbwa, perto do
  deserto; o Nuno, o Paulo, a Elsa e a Hannah, com capacetes e coletes
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 5A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 5
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This unit is about the future: how sure we are, what will be happening, and how technology will change our work. Today: predictions — from "definitely" to "definitely not".

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 6 de Dezembro, 11:00; um terreno plano e amarelo; ao fundo, dunas e o mar; estacas no chão; o vento levanta areia; o Nuno, o Paulo, a Elsa e a Hannah de capacete]

**HANNAH:** Welcome to Tômbwa! Well, to the sand where Tômbwa will be.
**ELSA:** It's even windier than I expected.
**HANNAH:** And this is a quiet day. In the afternoon, the wind is bound to get stronger. It always does.
**PAULO:** Then the masts are definitely going on concrete bases, not just in the sand.
**NUNO:** Let's think about next year. What's likely to go wrong?
**PAULO:** The trucks are bound to be late at the border. Every truck I've ever sent through Oshikango has been late.
**NUNO:** That's why we have the two extra weeks. What else?
**HANNAH:** The builders may well finish the control room late. They're already behind with the roads.
**ELSA:** And the sand. There's a good chance that it'll get into the equipment cabinets. We'll need better filters than in Namibe.
**NUNO:** Rain?
**HANNAH:** Rain is very unlikely. It rains here maybe twice a year. But when it does, the road floods.
**PAULO:** So, it probably won't rain, but if it does, we definitely won't get here.
**NUNO:** Ha! OK. Anything that's definitely going to go well?
**HANNAH:** The satellite. After Namibe, I'm sure of that. And your team is bound to be faster this time — you've done it once.
**ELSA:** And by the end of August, we'll have tested everything.
**NUNO:** Good. I'll put these predictions in the risk plan — with the most likely at the top.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**A escala da certeza**

[ECRÃ:]

| Certeza | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| 100% | The wind **will definitely** get stronger. · The wind **is bound to** get stronger. |
| 80% | The trucks **are likely to** be late. · There's **a good chance that** the sand will get in. |
| 60% | The builders **may well** finish late. · They**'ll probably** finish late. |
| 20% | Rain **is unlikely**. · It **is unlikely to** rain. · It **probably won't** rain. |
| 0% | We **definitely won't** get here if it rains. |

**PROF:** "Be bound to" means "almost certain" — from experience. "Likely" and "unlikely" are adjectives, so we use "be": "is likely to", "are unlikely to". "May well" is a strong "may": quite possible.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**A forma**

[ECRÃ: ✘ The trucks will bound to be late. · ✘ They likely to be late. · ✔ The trucks **are bound to** be late. · ✔ They**'re likely to** be late. · ✔ It's **likely that** they'll be late.]

**PROF:** Don't put "will" before "bound to" or "likely to" — the "be" already looks to the future.
[PAUSA 5 s]

***Definitely* e *probably***

[ECRÃ: It **will definitely** rain. · It **definitely won't** rain. · It**'ll probably** rain. · It **probably won't** rain.]

**PROF:** With "will", the word goes after. With "won't", it goes before. You learned this in B1 — at B2, use it every time.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "Bound to" is one block, and the "t" almost disappears: bound-to. Listen: The wind is bound to get stronger.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: The trucks are bound to be late at the border.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** And "likely" — the stress is at the start: likely, unlikely. Rain is very unlikely.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Say it with the word in brackets.
[ECRÃ: I'm almost sure the meeting will start late. (bound to)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: The meeting is bound to start late.]
[ECRÃ: I don't think prices will fall this year. (unlikely)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Prices are unlikely to fall this year.]
[ECRÃ: It's quite possible that she'll get the job. (may well)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: She may well get the job.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Make five predictions about next year — for your work, your city, your family or your country — from "definitely" to "definitely won't", and explain one of them. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Next year, my company will definitely open the Lubango office — the building is ready. Prices are bound to go up again; they always do in January. There's a good chance that I'll get a promotion, and my sister may well move to Huambo. But the traffic in Luanda definitely won't get better: there are more cars every month.]

**PROF (opinião):** Are you an optimist or a pessimist when you make predictions? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 2C):** Elsa says: "By the end of August, we'll have tested everything." What tense is this, and why?
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The future perfect: the testing will be finished before the end of August.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Bound to" for almost certain, "likely" and "unlikely" with "be", "may well" for quite possible. Next lesson: how long something will have been happening — "By June, I'll have been working here for…".

## 5B — We'll have been testing it

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5B — We'll have been testing it        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue dizer há quanto tempo uma acção estará a decorrer num momento
  do futuro com o future perfect continuous (By March, I'll have been working here for two and a half
  years.), e escolher entre os três futuros (will be doing, will have done, will have been doing).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: will have been + -ing + for…, by…; contraste: future continuous (this time next year, I'll
  be travelling — a meio da acção), future perfect (by June, I'll have finished — acção terminada),
  future perfect continuous (by June, I'll have been studying for a year — duração até esse momento)
Palavras-alvo: appraisal, review, goal, progress, promotion, responsibility, career, achievement,
  feedback, development
Reciclagem: present perfect continuous (B1, 2B); future perfect (B2, 2C)
Pronúncia: will have been dito curto (I'll've been)
Contexto: terça-feira, 12 de Dezembro, 15:00; o gabinete da Lerato; a avaliação anual do Nuno
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 5B; conversa guiada "Crystal ball"; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In B1, you learned "I've been working here since…". Today, we take that idea into the future: "By March, I'll have been working here for two and a half years."

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 12 de Dezembro, 15:00; o gabinete da Lerato; uma folha "Annual review 2028 — Nuno Kapinga"; uma caneca de chá]

**LERATO:** So, Nuno, your annual review. First, congratulations. You led the Tômbwa proposal, and we won it.
**NUNO:** Thank you. The team did a lot of the work.
**LERATO:** Good answer — but you led them. Now, next year. In March, how long will you have been working here?
**NUNO:** Let me think. I started in October 2026, so by March, I'll have been working here for two and a half years.
**LERATO:** And in June?
**NUNO:** In June, I'll have been preparing for the B2 exam for a whole year. I'm taking it in July.
**LERATO:** Good. And Tômbwa: by the end of August, what will be happening?
**NUNO:** By the end of August, Elsa will have been testing the network for three months, and we'll have finished the installation. If everything goes well, this time next year I'll be travelling to Tômbwa only once a month.
**LERATO:** Let's talk about your development. You're bound to get more responsibility in 2029. Would you like a bigger team?
**NUNO:** I'd like that. But first, I'd like to learn more about budgets. Sílvia did most of the numbers this year.
**LERATO:** That can be arranged. Here's my feedback, then. Strengths: you listen, you're honest with clients — Kwame told me about the Lumo call — and you never invent numbers.
**NUNO:** I learned that the hard way.
**LERATO:** To improve: you say yes to too many things. By next December, you'll have been working sixty-hour weeks for a year if you don't stop.
**NUNO:** Ha — fair point. I'll learn to say "That might be difficult for me."
**LERATO:** Perfect. Tentative, but firm.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**O *future perfect continuous***

[ECRÃ:
By March, I**'ll have been working** here **for** two and a half years.
In June, I**'ll have been preparing** for the exam **for** a whole year.
By the end of August, Elsa **will have been testing** the network **for** three months.]

**PROF:** "Will have been" plus "-ing": we stand at a moment in the future and look back at an action that is still going on — and we say how long. That's why it almost always comes with "for".
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: linha do tempo: Out. 2026 ——— (working) ——→ Março de 2029: "two and a half years"]

**Os três futuros**

[ECRÃ:]

| Forma | Exemplo | Ideia |
|---|---|---|
| will be + -ing (B1) | This time next year, I'll be travelling once a month. | a meio da acção, num momento futuro |
| will have + particípio (B2, 2C) | By August, we'll have finished the installation. | acção terminada antes desse momento |
| will have been + -ing | By August, Elsa will have been testing for three months. | duração até esse momento |

**PROF:** Ask yourself: am I in the middle? Is it finished? Or how long has it been going on?
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ By March, I'll be working here for two years. · ✘ By March, I'll have working… · ✔ By March, I'll have been working here for two and a half years.]

**PROF:** In Portuguese, we often say "em Março, faz dois anos e meio que trabalho cá". In English, with "by" and "for", you need the future perfect continuous.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "I will have been" is very short in speech: I'll've been. Listen: By March, I'll've been working here for two and a half years.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: By March, I'll have been working here for two and a half years.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make a sentence with "will have been" + "-ing".
[ECRÃ: A Ana começou a estudar medicina em 2025. Em 2031 — quantos anos?] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: By 2031, Ana will have been studying medicine for six years.]
[ECRÃ: O Paulo conduz desde as 6h. Às 14h — quantas horas?] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: By two o'clock, Paulo will have been driving for eight hours.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Talk about your next year with the three futures: how long you'll have been doing something by a certain date, one thing you'll have finished, and what you'll be doing this time next year. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — By June, I'll have been working at the bank for five years, and I'll have been studying English with ProEnglish for almost a year. By then, I'll have finished my accounting course, too. And this time next year, I'll probably be working in a new department — if everything goes well.]

**PROF (opinião):** Are annual reviews useful, or just a formality? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 2B):** How long has Nuno been working at Kalandula now, in December 2028?
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: He's been working there for two years and two months — since October 2026.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Will have been" plus "-ing" plus "for" — how long, at a moment in the future. Now try the tutor's conversation "Crystal ball". Next lesson: imagining the future of work — "What if robots…?".

## 5C — What if robots…?

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5C — What if robots…?        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue especular sobre o futuro com suppose / imagine / what if e os
  condicionais, e pesar prós e contras numa discussão.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: suppose / imagine / what if + presente (possível) ou passado (imaginado): What if drones
  cleaned the panels? Suppose a robot does the inspections — what then?; pesar (On the one hand… on the
  other hand… The main advantage / drawback is… The risk is that… It depends on…); previsões (5A)
Palavras-alvo: drone, robot, automate, inspection, advantage, drawback, risk, benefit, replace, skill,
  in the long run
Reciclagem: 2.º condicional (B1, 4A); previsões graduadas (5A)
Pronúncia: what if dito como um bloco, com a voz a subir no fim da pergunta
Contexto: quarta-feira, 13 de Dezembro, 13:00; a cantina; o Nuno, o Paulo e a Elsa almoçam e falam do
  trabalho daqui a dez anos
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 5C; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! "What if…?" is the question behind every new idea. Today: how to imagine the future, and how to weigh the good and the bad.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 13 de Dezembro, 13:00; a cantina; o Nuno, o Paulo e a Elsa; no telemóvel do Paulo, um vídeo de um *drone* a limpar painéis solares]

**PAULO:** Look at this. A drone that cleans solar panels. No people, no ladders.
**ELSA:** That's amazing. What if we used them in Tômbwa? The panels there are bound to be covered in sand every week.
**NUNO:** On the one hand, it would save a lot of time. On the other hand, who would repair the drones? Not us!
**PAULO:** Suppose they break in a sandstorm. Then you need people again — and fast.
**ELSA:** True. But imagine our job in ten years. Robots do the inspections, drones clean, and we just read the data from Luanda.
**NUNO:** That sounds a bit lonely. I like going to the plants.
**PAULO:** And what if the robot makes a mistake? Who's responsible — the robot, or the person who bought it?
**ELSA:** That's the real question. The main advantage is safety: nobody climbs a mast in the midday sun. The main drawback is jobs.
**NUNO:** It depends on what happens to the people. If the technicians learned to programme the drones, they'd have better jobs, not no jobs.
**PAULO:** In the long run, maybe. In the short run, someone loses a job.
**ELSA:** So, are drones a good idea for Tômbwa or not?
**NUNO:** Maybe for cleaning, yes. For repairs, not yet. And the six technicians we're training could learn to use them.
**PAULO:** Now that's a good answer. I'll send the video to Hannah.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

***What if, suppose, imagine***

[ECRÃ:
**What if** we **used** them in Tômbwa? (imaginado — 2.º condicional)
**Suppose** they **break** in a sandstorm. Then you need people. (possível)
**Imagine** our job in ten years. (imagine…)]

**PROF:** "What if", "suppose" and "imagine" open a hypothesis. With the past — "What if we used them?" — the idea is more imaginary. With the present — "Suppose they break" — it's a real possibility.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: What if the robot **makes** a mistake? (possível) · What if the robot **made** a mistake? (mais imaginado)]
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Pesar prós e contras**

[ECRÃ:]

| Para | Língua |
|---|---|
| dois lados | On the one hand, … On the other hand, … |
| o melhor | The main advantage / benefit is… |
| o pior | The main drawback / disadvantage is… · The risk is that… |
| depende | It depends on… |
| prazo | In the short run… In the long run… |

**PROF:** "On the one hand" and "on the other hand" go together. "Drawback" is a very common word for a disadvantage.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ In one hand… in the other hand… · ✔ On the one hand… on the other hand… · ✘ It depends of… · ✔ It depends on…]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** "What if" is one block, and the question goes up at the end. Listen: What if we used them in Tômbwa?
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: What if the robot makes a mistake?
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** On the one hand, it would save time. On the other hand, who would repair them?
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Ask a "what if" question.
[ECRÃ: Ideia: semana de trabalho de quatro dias.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: What if we worked four days a week?]
[ECRÃ: Ideia: aulas só online.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: What if all classes were online?]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Choose one idea — robots in hospitals, self-driving taxis in Luanda, or a four-day working week. Imagine it with "what if" or "suppose", give one advantage and one drawback, and give your conclusion with "it depends on". About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — What if Luanda had self-driving taxis? On the one hand, they might be safer, because they don't get tired or drink. On the other hand, the main drawback is jobs: thousands of people drive taxis. And suppose the system fails in the middle of the Marginal? In the long run, it could work, but it depends on the roads and the rules.]

**PROF (opinião):** Would you trust a robot to do your job, or part of it? Which part?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — 5A):** How sure are you? "Drones will clean most solar panels by 2035."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Exemplo: That's quite likely. / That may well happen. / I think that's unlikely.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "What if", "suppose", "imagine" — and "on the one hand… on the other hand". Next lesson: the words of technology.

## 5D — Tech talk

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5D — Tech talk        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário de tecnologia e inovação (artificial
  intelligence, automation, data privacy, start-up, scale up, roll out), formar palavras com mudança de
  acento (technology / technological, innovate / innovation), e evitar os falsos amigos application e
  informatics.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: formação de palavras (nome, verbo, adjectivo: innovate – innovation – innovative;
  automate – automation – automated; technology – technological; analyse – analysis – analytical); phrasal
  verbs de tecnologia (roll out, scale up, set up, phase out, back up)
Palavras-alvo: artificial intelligence (AI), automation, algorithm, data privacy, cybersecurity, cloud,
  app, start-up, fintech, pilot, roll out, scale up, phase out, user-friendly, cutting-edge
Reciclagem: back up (B2, 1D); cloud backup (B2, 4F)
Pronúncia: mudança de acento (technology / technological, photograph / photography, analyse / analysis) —
  dito, sem símbolos nem maiúsculas no ecrã
Falsos amigos: application (≠ aplicação financeira → investment; application = candidatura, programa,
  pedido); informatics (≠ informática → IT, computing, computer science; informatics = ciência da
  informação, pouco usado)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 5D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of technology — and how the stress moves when a word changes its form.

### 2. Tecnologia e inovação (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| artificial intelligence (AI) | The AI assistant answers simple questions. |
| automation | Automation could save us ten hours a week. |
| algorithm | The algorithm decides which alerts are urgent. (algoritmo) |
| data privacy | Clients worry about data privacy. (protecção de dados) |
| cybersecurity | Cybersecurity is our biggest risk. |
| start-up | Tunde founded a fintech start-up. |
| pilot | Let's run a three-month pilot. (projecto-piloto) |
| user-friendly | The app is very user-friendly. (fácil de usar) |
| cutting-edge | It's cutting-edge technology. (de ponta) |

**PROF:** Repeat: artificial intelligence. Automation. Algorithm. Data privacy. Pilot. User-friendly.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: **roll out** (lançar, estender a todos) · **scale up** (aumentar a escala) · **phase out** (retirar aos poucos) · **set up** (montar, configurar)]

**PROF:** "We'll run a pilot in one office, then roll it out to all offices, and phase out the old system." Four phrasal verbs, one plan.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 3. Formação de palavras (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Verbo | Nome | Adjectivo |
|---|---|---|
| innovate | innovation | innovative |
| automate | automation | automated |
| analyse | analysis | analytical |
| — | technology | technological |
| rely | reliability | reliable |

**PROF:** At B2, you need families of words. If you know "innovate", you can say "an innovation" and "an innovative idea". Learn the family, not one word.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ an innovate idea · ✔ an innovative idea · ✘ the technologic sector · ✔ the technological sector]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: o acento muda (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** When a word grows, the stress often moves. Listen: technology [acento em "nol"] — technological [acento em "log"]. Photograph [acento em "pho"] — photography [acento em "tog"]. Analyse [acento em "an"] — analysis [acento em "nal"].
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: technology, technological. Innovate, innovation. Analyse, analysis.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **application** ≠ aplicação (financeira)]

**PROF:** "Application" is a job application, a request, or a computer program: "Fill in the application." "Download the application."

### 2. Primeira escuta: o debate (5 min)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 15 de Dezembro, 19:00; o Nuno no carro, no trânsito, com o rádio ligado; um estúdio com quatro microfones]

**WANJIKU:** So, what should our listeners do?
**MARTA:** Learn. Not programming necessarily — learn to check what the machine says. AI makes mistakes, very confident mistakes.
**SIZWE:** And companies should pay for that training. Not the workers.

**WANJIKU:** A rare moment of agreement! Last question, quickly: in ten years, will there be more jobs or fewer? Sizwe?

## 5F — Work & Life: Should we use AI?

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 5        Aula: 5F — Should we use AI?        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue discutir a adopção de uma tecnologia nova, pesar benefícios e
  riscos, fazer previsões graduadas e recomendar uma decisão com condições; e explicar em inglês uma
  notícia sobre tecnologia escrita em português.

  condições (B2, 3A)
Palavras-alvo: assistant, pilot, data privacy, encrypted, server, human check, roll out, recommendation,
  condition, supervise

Mediação: o Nuno explica à Thandi, em inglês, uma notícia em português sobre o uso de assistentes de IA
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! The last lesson of B2.1. Thandi wants Kalandula and Mopane to use an AI assistant for client support. Nuno has to weigh the benefits and the risks — and make a recommendation.

### 2. Situação (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 18 de Dezembro, 10:00; a sala de reuniões; a Lerato, a Sílvia, a Rosa e o Nuno à mesa; a Thandi no ecrã, entusiasmada, com um diapositivo: "HelpMate — AI support assistant"]

**THANDI:** So, that's HelpMate. It answers clients twenty-four hours a day, in English and Portuguese, it reads the alerts from every system, and it never gets tired. Three companies in Johannesburg already use it. I think we should start in January.
**LERATO:** Thank you, Thandi. Nuno, you've looked at it. What's your view?
**NUNO:** I think it's very promising — but I'd like to look at both sides. On the one hand, the benefits are clear. Remember Lumo: the alert went to an old address, and nobody saw it for two days. HelpMate would have seen it in minutes. And clients are bound to like twenty-four-hour support.
**THANDI:** Exactly!
**NUNO:** On the other hand, there are three risks. First, data privacy. Our clients' camera footage and network data would go to HelpMate's servers. Where are those servers? Who can see the data?
**THANDI:** In South Africa, I think. I'll check.
**NUNO:** Second, mistakes. Suppose it tells a client to restart a router in the middle of a big transfer. Who's responsible? And third, people. Rosa, what do you think?

**SÍLVIA:** And cost? Thandi?

**NUNO:** Before I give my recommendation, I found this article on a news site this morning — in Portuguese. Let me give you the main points, Thandi.
