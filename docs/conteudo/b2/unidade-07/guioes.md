# B2.2 · Unidade 7 — City life · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B2](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2, do B1, do B2.1 e da unidade 6 do B2. Novo: conceder e contrastar com *admittedly, while, whereas, nevertheless, even so* (alargando o *although / despite / however* do B1); *even if* (mesmo que — condição) e *even though* (embora — facto), em contraste com *if*; construir um argumento em quatro partes (ideia, razões, exemplo, conclusão) com marcadores (*The main point is… What's more… On top of that… All in all…*); falar de ideias abstractas (qualidade de vida, desigualdade, sustentabilidade, crescimento).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** adjectivos não graduáveis com intensificadores (*absolutely fantastic*) e advérbios de comentário como *frankly, surprisingly* (unidade 8; *admittedly* entra já aqui); verbos de relato como *suggest, insist, deny, warn, advise* e a passiva de relato (unidade 9); *wish / if only*, *would* para hábitos do passado, o *past perfect continuous* e os condicionais mistos (unidade 10).

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** Luanda, Talatona, Viana, Camama e a Via Expressa existem; o condomínio "Jardim do Sol", a transportadora da 7F e o comunicado da administração municipal são fictícios e foram escritos pela ProEnglish. O programa de rádio da 7E é fictício.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**; **Paulo Mendes** (vive com os pais em Viana, A1); **Lerato**. Novos e fictícios, na 7F, vizinhos do Nuno no condomínio: **Sr. Abílio Neto** (angolano, administrador do condomínio, preside), **Ravi Menon** (indiano, engenheiro numa petrolífera; contra a proposta) e **Claire Dubois** (francesa, professora; a favor, com dúvidas); na 7E: **Edna Sambo** (angolana, apresentadora), **Fernanda Lopes** (angolana, urbanista), **Dário Kanga** (angolano, de uma associação de taxistas) e **Yuri Pascoal** (angolano, 24 anos, activista da bicicleta).

**A história:** Abril de 2029. O Nuno voltou do Tômbwa para três semanas em Luanda. Vive no condomínio "Jardim do Sol", no Camama, com 120 famílias, muitas delas estrangeiras; demora uma hora e meia a chegar ao escritório de manhã. Na segunda, 9, no trânsito, o Paulo e o Nuno discutem as soluções para a cidade (7A). Na quarta, 11, o Paulo pensa em sair de casa dos pais, em Viana (7B). Na sexta, 13, a Lerato ajuda o Nuno a construir o argumento para a reunião de condóminos (7C). Na quarta, 18, o Nuno ouve um debate de rádio sobre Luanda em 2040 (7E). **A proposta do Nuno (igual em todas as aulas em que aparece):** duas carrinhas de 25 lugares, contratadas a uma transportadora; partidas às 7:00 e às 7:30 para Talatona e para o centro; regresso às 17:30 e às 18:00; 15 000 kwanzas por mês por pessoa; um período experimental de três meses; só avança se pelo menos 40 pessoas se inscreverem. ## 7A — Admittedly, it's expensive

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 7        Aula: 7A — Admittedly, it's expensive        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue conceder e contrastar com admittedly, while, whereas,
  nevertheless e even so, ao lado do although e do however do B1.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: whereas / while + frase (contraste entre duas coisas: The bus lane is fast, whereas the
  normal lanes are stuck.); admittedly (concedo que…: Admittedly, it's expensive.); nevertheless / even so
  (mesmo assim, depois de uma concessão); revisão: although, despite, however (B1, 9A)
Palavras-alvo: traffic, bus lane, rush hour, commute, public transport, bus line, minibus, condominium,
  price, crazy
Reciclagem: although, despite, however (B1, 9A); was supposed to (B2, 6B)
Pronúncia: admittedly e nevertheless com uma pausa curta a seguir, e a voz a subir na concessão
Contexto: segunda-feira, 9 de Abril de 2029, 7:40; o carro do Paulo, parado no trânsito; o Paulo e o Nuno
  vão para o escritório
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 7A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 7
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This unit is about life in the city — and about how to build a strong argument. Today: how to admit the other side and still make your point — "Admittedly…", "whereas…", "nevertheless…".

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: segunda-feira, 9 de Abril, 7:40; uma estrada cheia de carros parados; o carro do Paulo; ao lado, uma faixa de autocarros quase vazia, com um autocarro a passar]

**PAULO:** Look at that bus. It's flying, whereas we haven't moved for ten minutes.
**NUNO:** That's the new bus lane. It's been open for a month.
**PAULO:** So why don't more people use the bus?
**NUNO:** Admittedly, there aren't many lines yet. And the buses were supposed to come every ten minutes, but sometimes you wait half an hour.
**PAULO:** Exactly. While the bus is fast when it comes, nobody knows when it's coming.
**NUNO:** True. Nevertheless, it's a start. Ten years ago, there was nothing.
**PAULO:** Fair point. And the price is good, although the buses are full at rush hour.
**NUNO:** You know what I'd like? A minibus for my condominium. A hundred and twenty families, almost all of us driving to Talatona or to the centre at the same time.
**PAULO:** That's a nice idea. Admittedly, your neighbours like their cars.
**NUNO:** I know. Even so, an hour and a half every morning is crazy. I've calculated it: that's about fifteen hours a week in the car.
**PAULO:** Ouch. Whereas I spend about eight — and I live in Viana!
**NUNO:** Then you should come to the meeting too!
**PAULO:** Ha! Despite the traffic, I'm not moving to your condominium. My mother cooks too well.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

***Whereas* e *while*: duas coisas lado a lado**

[ECRÃ: The bus is flying, **whereas** we haven't moved. · **While** the bus is fast, nobody knows when it's coming. · I spend eight hours, **whereas** you spend twelve.]

**PROF:** "Whereas" and "while" compare two different things — two sides of a contrast. "Whereas" is a bit more formal. Both join two parts of one sentence.
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Admittedly* e *nevertheless / even so*: conceder e depois insistir**

[ECRÃ: **Admittedly**, there aren't many lines yet. **Nevertheless**, it's a start. · **Admittedly**, your neighbours like their cars. **Even so**, an hour and a half is crazy.]

**PROF:** This is the classic move of a good argument: first, admit a point from the other side — "admittedly" — then come back to your view — "nevertheless", "even so". It makes you sound fair, and stronger.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Com o que já sabe**

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Onde | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| although (B1) | junta duas frases | The price is good, **although** the buses are full. |
| despite (B1) | + nome ou -ing | **Despite** the traffic, I'm staying in Viana. |
| however (B1) | começa uma frase nova | The bus is cheap. **However**, it's slow. |
| whereas / while | junta duas frases que se comparam | I spend eight hours, **whereas** you spend twelve. |
| nevertheless / even so | começa uma frase nova, depois de uma concessão | **Nevertheless**, it's a start. |

[ECRÃ: ✘ Despite there are few lines… · ✔ Despite the small number of lines… / Although there are few lines… · ✘ Nevertheless the traffic, … · ✔ Despite the traffic, …]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** After "admittedly" and "nevertheless", make a short pause — they're signposts. Listen: Admittedly, [pausa] there aren't many lines. Nevertheless, [pausa] it's a start.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 7 s]
**PROF:** Admittedly, your neighbours like their cars. Even so, an hour and a half is crazy.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Join or continue with the word in brackets.
[ECRÃ: Lisbon has a metro. Luanda doesn't. (whereas)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Lisbon has a metro, whereas Luanda doesn't.]
[ECRÃ: The rent is high. I love the neighbourhood. (admittedly… nevertheless)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: Admittedly, the rent is high. Nevertheless, I love the neighbourhood.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Compare two places you know — two neighbourhoods, two cities, or your city now and ten years ago. Use "whereas" or "while" to compare, "admittedly" to admit a weak point of the place you prefer, and "nevertheless" or "even so" to defend it. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I grew up in Huambo, but I live in Luanda now. Huambo is calm and green, whereas Luanda is noisy and full of cars. While Luanda has more jobs, Huambo has a better quality of life. Admittedly, there are fewer opportunities in Huambo. Even so, if I could work from there, I'd go back tomorrow.]

**PROF (opinião):** What would make you leave your car at home and use public transport?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 6B):** What was supposed to happen with the buses in the new lane?
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: They were supposed to come every ten minutes.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Whereas" to compare; "admittedly" to admit; "nevertheless" and "even so" to come back to your point. Next lesson: "even if" and "even though".

## 7B — Even if it costs more…

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 7        Aula: 7B — Even if it costs more…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue distinguir even if (mesmo que — uma condição, real ou
  imaginada), even though (embora — um facto) e if, e usá-los para argumentar.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: even though + facto (Even though Viana is far, I stay.); even if + condição (Even if the rent
  were lower, I'd stay. / Even if it rains, we'll go.); contraste com if; even if com o 1.º e o 2.º
  condicional; as long as / unless (B2, 3A) revistos
Palavras-alvo: rent, flat, deposit, move out, share, stranger, independent, afford, salary, fuel, retired
Reciclagem: condicionais 1.º e 2.º (B1, 4A); as long as, unless (B2, 3A)
Pronúncia: even if e even though com o acento em "even"
Contexto: quarta-feira, 11 de Abril, 13:00; a cantina; o Paulo conta ao Nuno que está a pensar sair de
  casa dos pais
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 7B; conversa guiada "Two sides"; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! "Mesmo que" or "embora"? In English, "even if" and "even though" look similar, but they mean different things. Today we'll make them clear.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 11 de Abril, 13:00; a cantina; o Paulo mostra no telemóvel um anúncio: "T1 em Talatona — 450 000 Kz/mês"]

**PAULO:** Look at this. A one-bedroom flat in Talatona. Ten minutes from the office.
**NUNO:** Are you finally leaving your parents' house?
**PAULO:** I'm thinking about it. I'm thirty-two. Even though I love living with them, I'd like to be independent.
**NUNO:** Four hundred and fifty thousand a month, though. Can you afford it?
**PAULO:** Just. Even if I spend half my salary on rent, I'll save the fuel and the time.
**NUNO:** True. And your mother's cooking?
**PAULO:** Ha! That's the problem. Even if the flat were free, I'd miss her food.
**NUNO:** And your parents? What do they think?
**PAULO:** My father says it's a waste of money. Even though he's retired, he still gives me lectures about money!
**NUNO:** What about sharing? You could rent a two-bedroom flat with someone.
**PAULO:** I thought about that. If I find the right person, I'll do it. But even if it's cheaper, I don't want to share with a stranger.
**NUNO:** OK. So what's your decision?
**PAULO:** I'll visit the flat on Saturday. Even if I don't take this one, I'm going to move this year — as long as I can pay the deposit.
**NUNO:** Good. And I'll come for dinner — even if you can't cook!

### 3. Língua (4 min)

***Even though*: um facto**

[ECRÃ: **Even though** I love living with them, I'd like to be independent. (adoro — é verdade) · **Even though** he's retired, he still gives me lectures. (está reformado — é verdade)]

**PROF:** "Even though" is a stronger "although": the first part is a fact, and the second is surprising.
[PAUSA 5 s]

***Even if*: uma condição, real ou imaginada**

[ECRÃ: **Even if** I spend half my salary on rent, I'll save time. (talvez gaste — e mesmo assim) · **Even if** the flat **were** free, I'd miss her food. (imaginado — 2.º condicional) · **Even if** I don't take this one, I'm going to move. (condição possível)]

**PROF:** "Even if" means "it doesn't matter whether this happens — the result is the same". It works with the first and the second conditional.
[PAUSA 6 s]

***If*, *even if* e *even though***

[ECRÃ:]

| | Exemplo | Quer dizer |
|---|---|---|
| if | **If** it rains, we'll stay home. | a chuva muda o plano |
| even if | **Even if** it rains, we'll go. | com chuva ou sem chuva, vamos |
| even though | **Even though** it's raining, we're going. | está a chover, e vamos na mesma |

[ECRÃ: Sabemos que está reformado → ✔ **Even though** he's retired, he gives me lectures. · "Even if he's retired…" daria a ideia de que não sabemos se está.]

**PROF:** Ask yourself: is it a fact? Use "even though". Is it a condition — maybe, or imagined? Use "even if".
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Stress "even" — that's where the surprise is. Listen: Even though I love living with them, I'd like to be independent.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: Even if the flat were free, I'd miss her food.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** Even though he's retired, he still gives me lectures.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** "Even if" or "even though"?
[ECRÃ: … it's expensive, I'm buying it. (é caro — facto)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Even though it's expensive, I'm buying it.]
[ECRÃ: … they offered me more money, I wouldn't leave. (imaginado)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Even if they offered me more money, I wouldn't leave.]
[ECRÃ: … it rains tomorrow, the match will go on. (talvez chova)] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Even if it rains tomorrow, the match will go on.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Talk about where you live: one thing you'd never change, even if you had more money; one problem you accept, even though it's annoying; and one condition that would make you move. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I live in Benguela, near the beach. Even if I earned twice as much, I wouldn't move to Luanda: I love the calm here. Even though the water is cut almost every week, I don't really mind — we have a tank. But if my company closed the Benguela office, I'd have to move, even though it would break my heart.]

**PROF (opinião):** At what age should young people leave their parents' house? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 3A):** Complete Paulo's sentence: "I'm going to move this year — as long as…"
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: …as long as I can pay the deposit.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Even though" for a fact; "even if" for a condition — real or imagined. Try the tutor's conversation "Two sides". Next lesson: how to build an argument that people remember.

## 7C — The main argument is…

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 7        Aula: 7C — The main argument is…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue construir um argumento em quatro partes (ideia, razões,
  exemplo, conclusão) e falar de ideias abstractas (qualidade de vida, sustentabilidade, desigualdade).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 3
Gramática-alvo: marcadores de argumento (The main point is… First of all… What's more… On top of that…
  For instance… All in all… That's why…); nomes abstractos (quality of life, sustainability, inequality,
  growth, safety, wellbeing, community); concessão dentro do argumento (7A)
Palavras-alvo: argument, point, reason, list, example, conclusion, object (verbo), quality of life,
  sustainability, inequality, community, wellbeing
Reciclagem: admittedly / nevertheless (7A); números aproximados (B2, 2E)
Pronúncia: subida em cada razão de uma lista e descida na conclusão
Contexto: sexta-feira, 13 de Abril, 17:00; o gabinete da Lerato; o Nuno ensaia o argumento para a reunião
  de condóminos
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 7C; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! People don't remember long arguments — they remember clear ones. Today: a four-part argument, and the abstract words that make it sound serious.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 13 de Abril, 17:00; o gabinete da Lerato; o Nuno com uma folha de notas; a Lerato a ouvir]

**NUNO:** Can I try my argument on you? The meeting's next Saturday.
**LERATO:** Go ahead. I'm a difficult neighbour.
**NUNO:** OK. Er… the traffic is terrible, and the minibus is good, and it's cheaper, and also it's better for the environment, and people are tired, and…
**LERATO:** Stop. That's a list, not an argument. Give me one main point first.
**NUNO:** Right. The main point is this: we're losing time we'll never get back.
**LERATO:** Good. Now your reasons — two or three, not ten.
**NUNO:** First of all, time: most of us spend about three hours a day in the car. What's more, it's money: the fuel alone costs more than the minibus would. On top of that, it's about our quality of life — less stress, more time with our families.
**LERATO:** Better. Now an example. People remember stories, not numbers.
**NUNO:** For instance, my neighbour Claire leaves at six fifteen to get to her school by eight. Her children are asleep when she leaves and tired when she comes back.
**LERATO:** Perfect. And the conclusion?
**NUNO:** All in all, a shared minibus would give us back our time, our money and our evenings. That's why I'm proposing a three-month trial.
**LERATO:** Now add one concession — Ravi will object anyway.
**NUNO:** Admittedly, a minibus isn't as flexible as a car. Nevertheless, for the morning and the evening rush, it makes sense.
**LERATO:** That's a real argument. One more thing: say "sustainability" once, but don't make a speech about it. Your neighbours care about time.
**NUNO:** Time first, planet second. Got it.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**O argumento em quatro partes**

[ECRÃ:]

| Parte | Marcadores | O Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Ideia principal | The main point is… · What I'm arguing is… | The main point is this: we're losing time we'll never get back. |
| 2. Razões (duas ou três) | First of all… What's more… On top of that… | First of all, time… What's more, money… On top of that, quality of life. |
| 3. Exemplo | For instance… · Take… | For instance, my neighbour Claire leaves at six fifteen… |
| 4. Conclusão | All in all… · That's why… | All in all, a shared minibus would give us back our time. |
| + concessão | Admittedly… Nevertheless… | Admittedly, a minibus isn't as flexible as a car. Nevertheless… |

**PROF:** One idea, two or three reasons, one real example, a conclusion — and a concession before the other side makes it. "What's more" and "on top of that" add a stronger reason.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Ideias abstractas**

[ECRÃ: **quality of life** (qualidade de vida) · **wellbeing** (bem-estar) · **sustainability** (sustentabilidade) · **inequality** (desigualdade) · **community** (comunidade) · **growth** (crescimento)]

**PROF:** Abstract nouns make an argument bigger than your street: not just "I'm tired", but "our quality of life". Use them — but always with a concrete example.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ The quality of the life · ✔ quality of life · ✘ the sustainability is important · ✔ sustainability is important]

**PROF:** Abstract nouns usually go without "the": "Inequality is growing", "Quality of life matters".
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In a list of reasons, the voice goes up after each one — there's more coming — and down at the conclusion. Listen: First of all, time. [a subir] What's more, money. [a subir] On top of that, our quality of life. [a descer]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Put these in the right order: conclusion, main point, example, reason.
[ECRÃ: a) For instance, my street floods every rainy season. b) All in all, the city must invest in drains. c) The main point is that the drains are too old. d) What's more, the floods damage houses.] [PAUSA 10 s] [ECRÃ: c, d, a, b]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. What's the biggest problem in your city, and what's the best solution? Build a four-part argument — main point, reasons, an example, a conclusion — and give one argument against your own solution. About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — The main point is that Luanda needs more public transport, not more roads. First of all, new roads fill up with cars in a few months. What's more, buses move many more people in the same space. On top of that, cleaner air improves everyone's quality of life. For instance, the new bus lane is always moving, whereas the car lanes are stuck. Admittedly, buses need a lot of investment, and people love their cars. Nevertheless, all in all, it's the only solution that works in the long run.]

**PROF (opinião):** Which matters more for a city: economic growth or quality of life? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 2E):** Say it with an approximate number: "We spend two hours and fifty-five minutes a day in the car."
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: We spend almost three hours a day in the car. / just under three hours]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! One idea, two or three reasons, an example, a conclusion — plus a concession. Next lesson: the words of the city.

## 7D — The city

```

Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário da cidade e da sociedade (urban sprawl,

Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: colocações (tackle / address a problem, raise awareness, put forward a proposal, carry out a
  survey, take action, have an impact on); nomes compostos (traffic congestion, housing shortage, public

Palavras-alvo: urban sprawl, housing shortage, commute, commuter, infrastructure, congestion, public
  space, green space, street lighting, drainage, slum / informal settlement, residents, local council
Reciclagem: argumento em quatro partes (7C); concessão (7A)

Falsos amigos: mayor (≠ maior → bigger, older, the biggest; mayor = presidente da câmara); pavement (≠

```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words people use to talk about cities — in the news, in meetings, and in debates. And two false friends that sound exactly like Portuguese words.

### 2. A cidade (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| urban sprawl | Urban sprawl means longer commutes. (crescimento desordenado da cidade) |
| housing shortage | There's a housing shortage in the centre. (falta de habitação) |

| residents | The residents' meeting is on Saturday. (moradores) |

**PROF:** Repeat: urban sprawl. Housing shortage. Commute. Infrastructure. Congestion.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: "musseque" em inglês: **informal settlement** (neutro) — evite "slum" quando fala de um bairro concreto: soa a desprezo.]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 3. Colocações (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **tackle** / **address** a problem · **raise** awareness · **put forward** a proposal · **carry out** a survey · **take** action · **have an impact on**]

**PROF:** "Tackle a problem" means deal with it seriously. "Raise awareness" means make people know about it. "Put forward a proposal" is what Nuno will do on Saturday.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: The council should **tackle** congestion. · The campaign **raised awareness** of road safety. · Nuno will **put forward a proposal** at the meeting. · Traffic **has an impact on** our health.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: a fala rápida (1 min 30 s)

**PROF:** In fast English, sounds join and disappear. "Next day" sounds like "nex day". "Last time" sounds like "las time". Listen: I'll see you next day. It was the last time.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** In very informal speech, "want to" becomes "wanna", and "going to" becomes "gonna". Learn to understand them — but in a meeting, say "want to" and "going to". Listen: I wanna go. [informal] — I want to go. [neutro]
[PAUSA 4 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: Next time, let's take the bus.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **mayor** ≠ maior]

**PROF:** A "mayor" is the head of a city government: "The mayor opened the new park."
(PT: "Maior" é "bigger", "older" ou "the biggest": "o maior bairro" → "the biggest neighbourhood". Em Luanda, o cargo mais próximo de *mayor* é o administrador municipal; "the municipal administrator".)
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **pavement** ≠ pavimento]

**PROF:** In British English, the "pavement" is the path for people at the side of the road: "Cars park on the pavement, so people walk in the road."
(PT: "Pavimento" — o chão ou o piso de uma estrada — é "floor" ou "road surface": "o pavimento está cheio de buracos" → "the road surface is full of holes".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (2 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Complete with the right verb.
[ECRÃ: The campaign … awareness of the floods.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: raised]
[ECRÃ: Nuno will … forward a proposal on Saturday.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: put]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Describe your neighbourhood or your town to a foreign visitor: the infrastructure, one problem the local council should tackle, and one thing that makes the quality of life good. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I live in Cazenga, one of the busiest parts of Luanda. The infrastructure is improving: there's a new road and better street lighting. But drainage is still a big problem — when it rains, the streets flood, and the council should tackle it first. What makes life good here is the community: everyone knows everyone, and on Sundays the street is full of music.]

### 1. Apresentação (30 s)

### 2. Primeira escuta: o debate (5 min)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 18 de Abril, 21:00; o Nuno na varanda, com auscultadores; as luzes da cidade ao fundo]

**PROF:** First: who wants fewer cars, and who doesn't?

**EDNA:** Welcome to "Cities of Africa". I'm Edna Sambo, and tonight we're in Luanda, a city of almost ten million people. The question: what should Luanda look like in 2040? Fernanda Lopes, you're an urban planner. Start us off.

**DÁRIO:** Can I say something? I drive a taxi — a candongueiro — and I've done it for nineteen years. People talk about the future, but they forget us. Thousands of families live from taxis. Even if the city builds a metro, we still need a place in it.

**YURI:** No, no — I agree with Dário about the families. But the future can't be more cars. Whereas a car takes the space of ten people, a bicycle takes the space of one. I'd like bike lanes on every main road by 2040.
**DÁRIO:** Bicycles? In this heat? With these hills? [ri-se]
**YURI:** [ri-se] Even though it's hot, people cycle in hot cities all over the world. With trees and shade, it works.

**EDNA:** Let's talk about inequality. Dário?
**DÁRIO:** For me, that's the main point. The rich areas get the new roads, while the informal settlements still don't have drainage. On top of that, the poor live the furthest from the jobs. So, all in all, the first thing to tackle is water and drains, not bike lanes.
**YURI:** I can live with that. Drains first, bikes second.
**EDNA:** Last question, quickly: one word for Luanda in 2040.
**FERNANDA:** Closer.
**DÁRIO:** Fairer.
**YURI:** Greener.
**EDNA:** Closer, fairer, greener. Thank you all.

4. What does Yuri say about cycling in the heat? (People cycle in hot cities all over the world. ✓ / It's impossible. / Only at night.)
5. Why does Fernanda want green spaces? (They make the city cooler and improve quality of life. ✓ / For cars to park. / For tourists.)
6. What does Dário say the city should tackle first? (water and drains ✓ / bike lanes / new roads in rich areas)

*(Cada resposta repete-se; depois aparecem as perguntas, com 8 s para responder.)*

**PROF:** Listen and repeat.
**PROF:** Admittedly, that takes decades. Nevertheless, every new building should follow that idea. [PAUSA 7 s]

## 7F — Work & Life: The neighbourhood meeting

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: 7        Aula: 7F — The neighbourhood meeting        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue defender uma proposta numa reunião de moradores, com um
  argumento em quatro partes, prós e contras, e responder a objecções com concessão e contraste; e
  explicar em inglês um comunicado da administração municipal escrito em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2, 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: concessão e contraste; even if / even though; marcadores de argumento
Palavras-alvo: residents' meeting, proposal, minibus, trial, sign up, monthly fee, objection, vote, majority,
  bus lane, road works
Reciclagem: condições (B2, 3A); a passiva (B2, 4A); linguagem tentativa (B2, 3B)
Pronúncia: responder a uma objecção com calma: "I take your point" com a voz a descer e uma pausa
Mediação: o Nuno explica aos vizinhos, em inglês, um comunicado da administração municipal escrito em
  português (texto fictício, escrito pela ProEnglish)
Contexto: sábado, 21 de Abril, 10:00; a sala comum do condomínio Jardim do Sol; cerca de quarenta
  moradores; o Sr. Abílio Neto preside
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 7F; conversa livre "The neighbourhood meeting"
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today, Nuno puts forward his proposal at the residents' meeting. Many of his neighbours are foreign, so the meeting is in English. Watch how he argues, admits weak points, and answers a tough neighbour.

### 2. Situação (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sábado, 21 de Abril, 10:00; uma sala com cadeiras de plástico; cerca de quarenta pessoas; o Sr. Abílio à frente; o Nuno de pé, com uma folha; o Ravi de braços cruzados; a Claire na primeira fila]

**ABÍLIO:** Good morning, everyone. Item three: Mr Kapinga's proposal for a shared minibus. Nuno, five minutes, please.
**NUNO:** Thank you, Mr Neto. Good morning, neighbours. The main point is this: we're losing time we'll never get back. Most of us spend about three hours a day in the car. What's more, it costs us money: fuel alone is more than this minibus would cost. On top of that, it's our quality of life — less stress, and more time with our families. For instance, Claire leaves at six fifteen to get to her school by eight.
**CLAIRE:** That's true. My children are asleep when I leave.
**NUNO:** So here's the proposal: two twenty-five-seat minibuses from a transport company, leaving at seven and seven thirty for Talatona and the centre, and coming back at five thirty and six. Fifteen thousand kwanzas a month per person. A three-month trial — and only if at least forty people sign up. Admittedly, a minibus isn't as flexible as a car. Nevertheless, for the rush hours, it makes sense.
**RAVI:** May I? With respect, Nuno, I don't see it. I leave at different times every day. Even if the minibus were free, I wouldn't use it.
**NUNO:** I take your point, Ravi. It isn't for everyone — and nobody has to use it. Even though you wouldn't use it, you'd still benefit: forty fewer cars at the gate at seven o'clock.
**RAVI:** Hmm. And security? A minibus full of people stopping in the city?
**NUNO:** That's a fair question. The company has its own drivers, and the minibus only stops at the two offices — no stops on the road.
**CLAIRE:** I like it. But what if only twenty people sign up?
**NUNO:** Then we don't start. The trial only goes ahead as long as there are forty people — otherwise the price per person is too high.
**ABÍLIO:** Before we vote, Nuno, you mentioned a letter from the municipal administration.
**NUNO:** Yes — it arrived yesterday, in Portuguese. Let me explain it in English.

[ECRÃ: o comunicado (texto fictício, escrito pela ProEnglish): "A Administração Municipal informa que, entre 2 de Maio e 30 de Junho, decorrerão obras de alargamento na estrada principal de acesso ao Camama. Durante as obras, estará encerrada uma faixa em cada sentido, das 6h00 às 20h00. Prevê-se um aumento do tempo de viagem de até 40 minutos nas horas de ponta. Será criada uma faixa temporária reservada a autocarros e a viaturas com mais de 15 lugares. Recomenda-se o uso de transporte colectivo e a partilha de viaturas."]

**NUNO:** The municipal administration says there will be works to widen the main road to Camama from the second of May to the thirtieth of June. During the works, one lane in each direction will be closed from six in the morning to eight at night. They expect journeys to take up to forty minutes longer at rush hour. But — and this is the important part — there'll be a temporary lane only for buses and vehicles with more than fifteen seats. They recommend public transport and car sharing.
**RAVI:** So a twenty-five-seat minibus could use that lane.
**NUNO:** Exactly. Whereas our cars will be stuck for an extra forty minutes, the minibus will use the bus lane.
**RAVI:** [pausa] OK. I still won't use it. But I won't vote against it.
**ABÍLIO:** Thank you, Ravi. Let's vote. Those in favour of a three-month trial? … That's a clear majority. Nuno, please open the list. If forty people sign up by Friday, the trial starts on the second of May — the same day as the road works.
**NUNO:** Thank you, everyone. The list is on the table — and Ravi, you can still change your mind!

### 3. Como se defende uma proposta numa reunião (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno fez]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo do Nuno |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Ideia principal | The main point is… | The main point is this: we're losing time we'll never get back. |
| 2. Razões e exemplo | What's more… On top of that… For instance… | For instance, Claire leaves at six fifteen. |
| 3. A proposta, com condições | only if · as long as | Only if at least forty people sign up. |
| 4. Conceder antes da objecção | Admittedly… Nevertheless… | Admittedly, a minibus isn't as flexible as a car. |
| 5. Responder a uma objecção | I take your point. · Even though… · That's a fair question. | Even though you wouldn't use it, you'd still benefit. |
| 6. Trazer um facto novo (mediação) | The administration says… · this is the important part | there'll be a temporary lane only for buses |
| 7. Contrastar | Whereas… | Whereas our cars will be stuck, the minibus will use the bus lane. |

**PROF:** Notice how Nuno answers Ravi. He doesn't fight. He agrees with the true part — "It isn't for everyone" — and then shows a benefit even for Ravi: forty fewer cars at the gate. In the end, Ravi doesn't change his mind, but he doesn't vote against. That's often the best you can get.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**A mediação**

**PROF:** The letter is long and formal. Nuno gives the dates, the closed lanes and the forty minutes — and then he stops on the one sentence that changes the discussion: the bus lane. He even says it: "this is the important part".
(PT: "Alargamento" → "widening"; "faixa" → "lane"; "horas de ponta" → "rush hour"; "partilha de viaturas" → "car sharing". "Prevê-se" fica natural como "they expect".)
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** When someone objects, don't answer fast. Say "I take your point", with the voice going down, and pause before your answer. Listen: I take your point, Ravi. [pausa] It isn't for everyone.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. A sua vez (3 min)

**PROF:** Now you. At a meeting of your street, your building or your workplace, put forward a proposal — your own, or this one: a community garden on an empty piece of land. [PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — The main point is that the empty land at the end of our street is wasted. First of all, it's full of rubbish, which brings mosquitoes. What's more, our children have nowhere to play. For instance, last week my son played football in the road. I propose a community garden. Admittedly, it needs work every week. — I take your point. That's why we'll start with a rota: two families a week. Even if only ten families join, it will still be better than rubbish.]

**PROF:** Now the mediation. Explain this notice in English to a foreign neighbour.
[ECRÃ: "Aviso: o abastecimento de água será interrompido na quinta-feira, das 8h00 às 16h00, para reparação de uma conduta. Recomenda-se que os moradores façam reservas de água na véspera."]
[PAUSA 40 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — There's a notice: the water will be cut on Thursday from eight in the morning to four in the afternoon, because they're repairing a pipe. They say we should store some water the day before.]

**PROF (opinião):** Should decisions in a building or a neighbourhood be taken by majority vote, even when some people lose? Why?
