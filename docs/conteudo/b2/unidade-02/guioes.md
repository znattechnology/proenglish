# B2.1 · Unidade 2 — Presentations · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B2](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2, do B1 e da unidade 1 do B2. Novo: marcadores de discurso formal (*I'd like to start by… To begin with… Moving on to… This brings me to… Subsequently… To sum up… In conclusion…*); descrever tendências com verbos, nomes, adjectivos e advérbios (*rose sharply, a sharp rise, fell slightly, a slight fall, steady / steadily, gradual / gradually, peak, level off, fluctuate, remain stable*; *rise by / to / from… to…*); números aproximados (*roughly, approximately, just over, just under, nearly*); o *future perfect* simples (*By June, we will have installed… We won't have finished…*); responder a perguntas do público.

**Não usar nesta unidade:** *provided that, as long as, supposing* (unidade 3); a passiva contínua, no *present perfect* e com modais, e *have something done* (unidade 4); *be bound to*, *be likely to* e o *future perfect continuous* (*will have been doing*, unidade 5); *needn't have*, *was supposed to* (unidade 6); *whereas, admittedly, even if, nevertheless* (unidade 7); verbos de relato como *suggest, insist, deny, warn* (unidade 9); *wish / if only*, *would* para hábitos do passado e os condicionais mistos (unidade 10).

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Kalandula Tech, a Mopane Systems e a Morro Azul Energia são fictícias, como a central do Tômbwa e o "Windhoek Clean Power Forum" da 2F.

**Personagens:** **Nuno**; **Lerato** (coordenadora; treina o Nuno); **Paulo Mendes** (apoio técnico; plano de instalação); **Elsa Chilombo** (testes); **Sílvia** (directora financeira da Kalandula Tech); **Rajesh** (Mopane; custos); **Sr. Baptista** (director da Kalandula Tech; só por escrito). Da Morro Azul: **Ingrid Solberg** (directora de operações, B1 9F e 10F); **Femi Adeyemi** (director financeiro, B1 3F); **Hannah Becker** (engenheira de projecto, por vídeo). Novos e fictícios: **Celeste Kiala** (presidente do conselho de administração da Morro Azul, de Luanda); na 2F, **Tobias Shikongo** (namibiano), **Aïcha Diallo** (senegalesa) e **Martin Hale** (britânico), oradores num fórum de energia.

**A história:** fim de Setembro e início de Outubro de 2028. O Nuno entregou o primeiro rascunho da proposta ao Sr. Baptista na sexta, 22 (B2 1F); a Elsa e o Paulo estão a testar as duas opções no Namibe até 6 de Outubro. Na terça, 26, a Lerato ajuda o Nuno a abrir a apresentação (2A); na quarta, 27, o Nuno e a Elsa preparam o gráfico das horas sem rede no Namibe (2B); na quinta, 28, o Paulo explica o calendário da instalação (2C); na segunda, 2 de Outubro, a Sílvia e a Lerato fazem de administração num ensaio com perguntas difíceis (2D); na quinta, 5, o Nuno vê três aberturas gravadas num fórum de energia (2F); na quinta, 12, apresenta a proposta à administração da Morro Azul (2G).

**Os números da proposta (iguais em todas as aulas):**

## 2A — I'd like to start by…

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2A — I'd like to start by…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue abrir, sinalizar e fechar uma apresentação com marcadores de
  discurso formal (I'd like to start by… Moving on to… This brings me to… To sum up…).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: marcadores para abrir (I'd like to start by + -ing; To begin with; The purpose of this
  presentation is to…), para mudar de parte (Moving on to… Let's now turn to… This brings me to…
  Subsequently…), para fechar (To sum up… In conclusion… Thank you for your attention. I'm happy to take
  questions.); start by / finish by + -ing
Palavras-alvo: presentation, purpose, outline, section, point, board, audience, draft, attention,
  conclusion, take questions
Reciclagem: which sobre uma frase (B2 1B); sequenciadores do B1 (first, then, after that, finally)
Pronúncia: pausa curta depois de cada marcador (Moving on to… [pausa] the costs.)
Contexto: terça-feira, 26 de Setembro de 2028, 15:00; uma sala pequena; o Nuno ensaia a abertura; a
  Lerato ouve e ajuda
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 2A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 2
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In two weeks, Nuno presents the Tômbwa proposal to the Morro Azul board. This unit is about presentations: structure, numbers, plans, and difficult questions. Today: how to open, move between parts, and close.

### 2. Situação (3 min)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 26 de Setembro, 15:00; uma sala pequena; um portátil e um projector; a Lerato sentada, com um caderno; o Nuno de pé]

**LERATO:** OK, Nuno. Imagine I'm the board. Just the first minute, please.
**NUNO:** Right. Um… OK, so, this is our proposal. Slide one. Er… there are a lot of slides, sorry. So, the network…
**LERATO:** Stop there. Can I be honest?
**NUNO:** Please.
**LERATO:** The board don't know where you're going. Tell them three things first: who you are, why you're there, and the parts of your talk. And never apologise for your slides.
**NUNO:** Fair enough. Let me try again.
**NUNO:** Good morning, everyone. My name is Nuno Kapinga, and I lead the Kalandula and Mopane team for Tômbwa. The purpose of this presentation is to show you how we'll keep your new plant online, every hour of every day. I'd like to start by looking at what happened in Namibe. Then I'll move on to our two options for Tômbwa. After that, I'll talk about the timeline, and finally, the costs. The presentation takes about fifteen minutes, and I'm happy to take questions at the end.
**LERATO:** Much better! I know exactly where we're going. Now, how do you move from Namibe to the options?
**NUNO:** So, that's Namibe. Moving on to Tômbwa: we have two options.
**LERATO:** Good. Or: "This brings me to the most important part: our two options." It tells them something big is coming.
**NUNO:** I like that. And the end?
**LERATO:** Sum up in three sentences, and finish with what you want from them. Not "That's all, I think."
**NUNO:** To sum up: Namibe shows the problem, option A solves it, and option B is cheaper but riskier. We'd like you to choose the option that suits your plant. Thank you for your attention.
**LERATO:** Perfect. Do that on the twelfth, and they'll listen to every word.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Abrir**

[ECRÃ: The purpose of this presentation is **to show** you… · I'd like to start **by looking** at… · To begin with, … · The presentation **takes** about fifteen minutes. · I'm happy **to take** questions at the end.]

**PROF:** First, the purpose, then the outline — the parts of your talk, in order. Notice: "start by" and "finish by" are followed by "-ing": "I'd like to start by looking at Namibe."
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ I'd like to start by look at… · ✘ I'd like to start for looking at… · ✔ I'd like to start by looking at…]
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Mudar de parte**

[ECRÃ: **Moving on to** the costs… · **Let's now turn to** the timeline. · **This brings me to** the most important part. · **Subsequently,** we'll test the network. (formal = depois)]

**PROF:** These phrases are signposts: they tell the audience that one part has finished and another is starting. "This brings me to…" is excellent before your key point. "Subsequently" means "after that" — it's formal; use it in a presentation, not in a café.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Fechar**

[ECRÃ: **To sum up,** … · **In conclusion,** … · We'd like you **to** choose… · Thank you for your attention. · Are there any questions?]

**PROF:** Close with a short summary and a clear request — what you want the audience to do. Don't finish with "That's all" or "I think that's it".
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ In conclusion, I finished. · ✘ Resuming, … (PT "resumindo") · ✔ To sum up, …]

**PROF:** Careful: "resuming" means "starting again", not "summing up".
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** After a signpost, make a short pause, so the audience is ready for the next part. Listen: Moving on to… [pausa] the costs.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: This brings me to… [pausa] the most important part.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** To sum up: [pausa] Namibe shows the problem, and option A solves it.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Which signpost?
[ECRÃ: Acabou de falar da história da empresa; agora vai falar dos resultados.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: Moving on to our results… / Let's now turn to our results.]
[ECRÃ: Vai começar a última parte, a mais importante.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: This brings me to the most important part.]
[ECRÃ: Vai terminar.] [PAUSA 5 s] [ECRÃ: To sum up, … Thank you for your attention.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Open a presentation about your company, your department, your course or your town. Say who you are, the purpose, and three parts in order. Then move to the first part with a signpost. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Good morning, everyone. My name is Teresa Gomes, and I work in the training department of a bank in Luanda. The purpose of this presentation is to show you our new training plan. I'd like to start by looking at last year's results. Then I'll move on to the new courses, and finally, the budget. I'm happy to take questions at the end. So, to begin with, last year's results…]

**PROF (opinião):** In your opinion, what makes a presentation boring? And what makes it interesting?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2 1B):** Comment on the whole idea with "which": "The presentation takes fifteen minutes." (leaves time for questions)
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: The presentation takes fifteen minutes, which leaves time for questions.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! Purpose and outline, signposts with a pause, and a summary with a clear request. Next lesson: how to describe a chart — "a sharp rise", "levelled off".

## 2B — A sharp rise

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2B — A sharp rise        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue descrever um gráfico e uma tendência com verbos, nomes,
  adjectivos e advérbios (rose sharply / a sharp rise; fell slightly / a slight fall; levelled off;
  peaked; fluctuated).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: verbo + advérbio (rose sharply, fell gradually, increased steadily) e adjectivo + nome
  (a sharp rise, a gradual fall, a steady increase); peak, level off, fluctuate, remain stable;
  preposições: rise by 10% (a diferença), rise to 40 (o ponto final), from 12 to 1, a fall of 11 hours
Palavras-alvo: rise, increase, go up, fall, drop, decrease, go down, sharp, steady, slight, gradual,
  dramatic, peak, level off, fluctuate, remain stable, trend
Reciclagem: passado simples irregular (rise – rose – risen; fall – fell – fallen); comparativos (B1)
Pronúncia: increase e decrease: o nome com o acento na primeira sílaba, o verbo com o acento na segunda —
  dito, sem símbolos nem maiúsculas no ecrã
Contexto: quarta-feira, 27 de Setembro, 11:00; o escritório; o Nuno e a Elsa (por videochamada, do Namibe)
  olham para o gráfico das horas sem rede
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 2B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! A good chart is useless if you can't describe it. Today: the language of trends — things that rise, fall, peak and level off.

### 2. Situação (3 min)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 27 de Setembro, 11:00; o Nuno no escritório; a Elsa no ecrã, na sala de controlo do Namibe; no ecrã partilhado, um gráfico de barras: "Network downtime, Namibe plant (hours per month), Aug 2027 – Aug 2028": 31, 38, 34, 12, 1, e depois quase zero até Agosto]

**ELSA:** Can you see the chart? I made it from the logs.
**NUNO:** Yes, it's great. Can you help me describe it? I want it to sound natural.
**ELSA:** Sure. Start with what it shows: network downtime at the Namibe plant, in hours per month, from August 2027 to August 2028.
**NUNO:** OK. In August 2027, the plant was offline for thirty-one hours. In September, the figure rose to thirty-eight — that was the month of the big sandstorm.
**ELSA:** So September was the peak. You can say "it peaked in September, at thirty-eight hours".
**NUNO:** It peaked in September, at thirty-eight hours. Then it fell slightly in October, to thirty-four.
**ELSA:** Good. And November?
**NUNO:** The satellite started working on the seventeenth, so the downtime fell sharply, to twelve hours. And in December, it dropped to just one hour.
**ELSA:** That's a dramatic fall. From thirty-four to one in two months.
**NUNO:** And since then, it's levelled off at almost zero — under one hour a month.
**ELSA:** Exactly. Except June: the forty minutes when someone kicked the cable. But that's still under one hour.
**NUNO:** Ha — I won't mention the cable to the board!
**ELSA:** Maybe just one sentence: "Even in our worst month this year, we lost less than an hour."
**NUNO:** I love that. Thanks, Elsa. And how are the tests going?
**ELSA:** Good so far. I'll send you the first results on Friday.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**Verbo + advérbio, ou adjectivo + nome**

[ECRÃ:]

| Verbo + advérbio | Adjectivo + nome |
|---|---|
| It **fell sharply**. | There was **a sharp fall**. |
| It **rose slightly**. | There was **a slight rise**. |
| It **increased steadily**. | There was **a steady increase**. |
| It **dropped dramatically**. | There was **a dramatic drop**. |
| It **decreased gradually**. | There was **a gradual decrease**. |

**PROF:** Two ways to say the same thing. With a verb, use an adverb: "fell sharply". With a noun, use an adjective: "a sharp fall". Sharp and dramatic mean big and fast; slight means small; steady and gradual mean slow and regular.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Outras tendências**

[ECRÃ: **peak** (atingir o máximo): It peaked in September, at 38 hours. · **level off** (estabilizar): It levelled off at almost zero. · **remain stable** (manter-se estável) · **fluctuate** (oscilar): It fluctuated between 31 and 38 hours.]
[PAUSA 5 s]

**As preposições**

[ECRÃ:
It rose **to** 38 hours. (o número final)
It rose **by** 7 hours. (a diferença: de 31 para 38)
It fell **from** 34 **to** 12.
a fall **of** 22 hours · **at** its peak]

**PROF:** "To" is where it finished. "By" is the difference. In Portuguese, both can be "para" or "em" — so think: the final number, or the difference?
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: rise – **rose** – **risen** · fall – **fell** – **fallen** · ✘ It raised to 38. (raise = levantar alguma coisa: raise prices)]

**PROF:** "Rise" has no object: prices rise. "Raise" has an object: the company raised its prices.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Some words change stress: the noun "increase" has the stress at the start — an increase [acento em "in"]. The verb has it at the end — to increase [acento em "crease"]. Same with "decrease". Listen: There was an increase. Prices increased.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: There was a sharp decrease. The downtime decreased sharply.
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Change the verb into a noun.
[ECRÃ: Sales rose sharply in March.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: There was a sharp rise in sales in March.]
[ECRÃ: The number of students fell slightly.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: There was a slight fall in the number of students.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Look at this chart and describe it: the trend, the peak, the biggest change and the end.
[ECRÃ: gráfico de linhas: "Customers per month, a café in Benguela, 2028": Jan 900; Feb 950; Mar 1 000; Apr 1 600 (abertura da esplanada); May 1 650; Jun 1 600; Jul 1 200 (obras na rua); Aug 1 250]
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — The chart shows the number of customers per month at a café in Benguela in 2028. From January to March, the number rose steadily, from nine hundred to a thousand. In April, there was a sharp rise, to sixteen hundred, when the café opened its terrace. It peaked in May, at sixteen hundred and fifty, and then fell sharply in July, to twelve hundred, because of the roadworks. In August, it rose slightly again.]

**PROF (opinião):** Do you prefer to read numbers in a table or in a chart? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1):** Compare two months with a comparative: September and December at the Namibe plant.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: December was much better than September: the plant was offline for thirty-seven hours less.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Fell sharply" or "a sharp fall"; "to" for the final number, "by" for the difference. Next lesson: how to talk about deadlines in the future — "by June, we will have installed…".

## 2C — By June, we will have…

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2C — By June, we will have…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue falar do que estará feito num momento futuro com o future
  perfect (By June, we will have installed the antennas. We won't have finished the control room by May.).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3
Gramática-alvo: will have + particípio; won't have + particípio; Will you have…? Yes, we will. / No, we
  won't.; by + momento (by June, by the end of 2029, by then, by the time the plant opens); contraste
  com o future continuous (B1, 8C: this time next year, we'll be testing)
Palavras-alvo: timeline, phase, lay cables, put up, mast, install, control room, extra weeks, by then,
  by the time
Reciclagem: future continuous (B1, 8C); going to e will (A2)
Pronúncia: will have → we'll've, dito curto, e won't have
Contexto: quinta-feira, 28 de Setembro, 9:30; o Paulo, de volta do Namibe por um dia, mostra ao Nuno o
  calendário da instalação, num quadro branco
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 2C; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Boards love deadlines. Today: how to say what will be finished by a certain time — "By June, we will have installed the antennas."

### 2. Situação (3 min)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 28 de Setembro, 9:30; o escritório; o Paulo ao quadro branco; um calendário de Novembro de 2028 a Dezembro de 2029, com quatro fases a cores]

**PAULO:** So, here's the installation plan, if we win. We'd sign the contract in November.
**NUNO:** And the work starts in February?
**PAULO:** Yes. Phase one is February and March: cables and masts. By the end of March, we'll have laid all the cables, and we'll have put up the six masts.
**NUNO:** OK. Phase two?
**PAULO:** April and May: the antennas and the satellite. By the end of May, we'll have installed everything — if the antennas arrive on time.
**NUNO:** After Namibe, I'll believe that when I see it!
**PAULO:** Ha! That's why I've planned two extra weeks. Then phase three, June to August: testing. By the end of August, Elsa will have tested the network for three months, in real conditions.
**NUNO:** And the control room? Will we have finished it by May too?
**PAULO:** No, we won't have finished it by May. The building won't be ready until July. So, this time next year, we'll be finishing the control room — but the network will already be working.
**NUNO:** And when does the plant open?
**PAULO:** At the end of 2029. By then, we'll have tested every cable twice.
**NUNO:** That's a great line for the board: "By the time the plant opens, we'll have tested the network for three months."
**PAULO:** Use it. But don't promise anything before February — rule number two!
**NUNO:** Don't worry. I remember Mr Baptista's note.

### 3. Língua (4 min 30 s)

**O *future perfect***

[ECRÃ:
By the end of March, we**'ll have laid** all the cables.
By the end of May, we **will have installed** everything.
We **won't have finished** the control room by May.
**Will** we **have finished** it by June? — No, we **won't**.]

**PROF:** "Will have" plus the past participle: the action is finished before a moment in the future. Imagine you're standing at the end of March, looking back: the cables are done.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: linha do tempo: hoje → [cabos ✔] → fim de Março (by the end of March)]

***By* + momento**

[ECRÃ: **by** June (até Junho, o mais tardar) · **by** the end of 2029 · **by** then · **by the time** the plant opens (= quando a central abrir)]

**PROF:** The future perfect almost always comes with "by". "By the time" is followed by the present simple, not "will": "by the time the plant opens".
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ By the time the plant will open, … · ✔ By the time the plant opens, … · ✘ Until June, we will have installed… · ✔ By June, …]

**PROF:** And "until" is not "by": "until" means the whole time up to that moment. "We'll be in Namibe until Friday" — but "we'll have finished by Friday".
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Future perfect* ou *future continuous*?**

[ECRÃ: This time next year, we**'ll be finishing** the control room. (a meio da acção — B1) · By August, we**'ll have tested** the network. (acção terminada)]
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** In speech, "will have" becomes very short: we'll've. Listen: By May, we'll've installed everything.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: By the end of March, we'll have laid all the cables.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** And the negative: We won't have finished it by May.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make a sentence with the future perfect.
[ECRÃ: Março: o Rui acaba o curso. Hoje é Janeiro. (by April)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: By April, Rui will have finished his course.]
[ECRÃ: A obra só termina em Outubro. (by September — negativa)] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: They won't have finished the work by September.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Present one goal for your team, your studies or your family, and say what will have happened by the end of this year — and one thing that won't have happened yet. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Our goal this year is to open a second shop in Lubango. By the end of October, we'll have found the place, and by November, we'll have hired three people. By the end of the year, we'll have sold our first products there. But we won't have paid back the bank loan — that'll take two more years.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it better to promise an early deadline and work hard, or a later deadline you're sure about? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 8C):** What will Paulo be doing this time next year?
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: He'll be finishing the control room at Tômbwa.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Will have" plus the participle, with "by" — and "by the time" plus the present. Next lesson: the scariest part of any presentation — the questions.

## 2D — That's a good question

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 2        Aula: 2D — That's a good question        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue responder a perguntas do público: ganhar tempo, verificar e
  reformular a pergunta, responder com clareza, admitir que não sabe e prometer voltar ao assunto.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 4
Gramática-alvo: ganhar tempo (That's a good question. Let me think about that for a moment.);
  reformular (So, if I understand correctly, you're asking… Do you mean…?); admitir (I'm afraid I don't
  have that figure with me.); prometer (I'll get back to you on that by Friday.); verificar (Does that
  answer your question?); adiar (Can I come back to that at the end?)
Palavras-alvo: question, figure, insurance, paperwork, customs, nasty, get back to, as I mentioned,
  put it another way, in short
Reciclagem: which sobre uma frase (B2 1B); should have (B1, 10B)
Pronúncia: entoação calma e a descer em "That's a good question." (não a subir, para não parecer
  surpreendido)
Contexto: segunda-feira, 2 de Outubro, 16:00; ensaio na sala de reuniões; a Sílvia e a Lerato fazem de
  administração da Morro Azul
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 2D; conversa guiada "Walk me through it"; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! You can prepare every slide, but you can't prepare every question. Today: how to answer questions calmly — and what to say when you don't know the answer.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)
