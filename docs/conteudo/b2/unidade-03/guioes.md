# B2.1 · Unidade 3 — Negotiating · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B2](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que esta unidade pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2, do B1 e das unidades 1 e 2 do B2. Novo: condições com *provided / providing that, as long as, unless, supposing, in case*; os quatro condicionais revistos (0, 1.º, 2.º e 3.º); linguagem tentativa (*We'd be prepared to… Would you consider…? That might be difficult for us. We were hoping for… It would help if… Could you possibly…?*); regatear (*If you could…, we could… in exchange for… meet halfway… That's as far as we can go.*); resumir um acordo (*So, just to confirm: … Let me sum up what we've agreed.*).

**Não usar nesta unidade:** a passiva contínua, no *present perfect* e com modais, e *have something done* (unidade 4); *be bound to*, *be likely to* e o *future perfect continuous* (unidade 5); *needn't have*, *was supposed to* (unidade 6); *whereas, admittedly, even if, nevertheless* (unidade 7); verbos de relato como *suggest, insist, deny, warn* (unidade 9); *wish / if only*, *would* para hábitos do passado e os condicionais mistos (unidade 10).

**Nomes de empresas e lugares:** a Kalandula Tech, a Mopane Systems, a Morro Azul Energia e a central do Tômbwa são fictícias, como a Kunene Freight (transportes, de Windhoek) e a mina de Limpopo da 3E (B2 1E).

**Personagens:** **Nuno**; **Sílvia** (directora financeira da Kalandula Tech); **Paulo Mendes**; **Lerato**; **Sr. Baptista** (só por escrito); **Thandi** (Mopane); **Femi Adeyemi** (director financeiro da Morro Azul); **Hannah** (Morro Azul). Novos e fictícios: **Moses Haufiku** (namibiano, dono da Kunene Freight); na 3E, **Chanda Mwale** (B1 R1) e **Mrs Banda** (vendedora num mercado de Lusaca), e **Grace Molefe** (sul-africana, directora de operações da mina de Limpopo, cliente da Mopane).

**A história:** Outubro e início de Novembro de 2028. A administração da Morro Azul decide até ao fim de Outubro (B2 2G). Na segunda, 16, o Nuno envia à Celeste o número prometido: 14 pessoas do Tômbwa na obra. Na quarta, 18, a Hannah diz-lhe, em privado, que a administração gosta da opção A mas que o Femi vai querer baixar o preço; nessa tarde, a Sílvia, o Paulo e o Nuno decidem os limites da Kalandula (3A). Na sexta, 27, o Femi telefona: a Morro Azul escolhe a opção A, "subject to the contract", e marca a negociação (3B). Na terça, 31, o Nuno negoceia o transporte das antenas com a Kunene Freight (3C). Na quarta, 1 de Novembro, ouve um *podcast* com duas negociações (3E). Na quinta, 2, negoceia o contrato com o Femi (3F).

**Os números do contrato (iguais em todas as aulas):**
- Preço da opção A na proposta: 412 000 dólares (B2 2G).
- Limites da Kalandula (internos, nunca ditos ao cliente): o preço pode descer até 385 000, mas só com um sinal de 30% na assinatura; com um sinal de 20%, o mínimo é 395 000; penalizações até 0,5% do valor por semana de atraso, com um máximo de 5%, e nunca por atrasos da alfândega se os papéis forem entregues a tempo.
- A carta do Sr. Baptista ao cliente (3F): 395 000 dólares; sinal de 30%; penalizações de 0,5% por semana, até 5%, excepto atrasos da alfândega ou do cliente.
- O pedido do Femi: 370 000 dólares; sinal de 10%; 1% por semana, sem máximo.
- O acordo (3F): 385 000 dólares; sinal de 30% (depois 40% em Maio e 30% na entrega); 0,5% por semana, até 5%; os atrasos causados pela Morro Azul não contam, e os da alfândega também não, desde que a Kalandula entregue os papéis até 15 de Dezembro; e a Kalandula forma, sem custos, seis técnicos do Tômbwa.

## 3A — Provided that…

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3A — Provided that…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue pôr condições com provided that, as long as, unless,
  supposing e in case, e escolher o condicional certo para cada situação.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1
Gramática-alvo: provided / providing that e as long as (= só se); unless (= if … not); supposing
  (= imagine que…, e se…?); in case (por precaução); os quatro condicionais revistos (0: if it rains, the
  link fails; 1.º: if they sign, we'll start; 2.º: if they asked, we'd say; 3.º: if we had known, we'd have
  …); depois de provided that / as long as / unless / in case: presente para o futuro
Palavras-alvo: price, discount, deposit, penalty, limit, condition, terms, upfront, delay, bottom line
Reciclagem: 2.º e 3.º condicional (B1, 4A e 10A); future perfect (B2, 2C)
Pronúncia: provided that e as long as ditos como um bloco, sem pausa no meio
Contexto: quarta-feira, 18 de Outubro de 2028, 15:00; o gabinete da Sílvia; a Sílvia, o Paulo e o Nuno
  decidem até onde a Kalandula pode ir
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 3A; perguntas 1 e 2 do questionário da unidade 3
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! This unit is about negotiating: conditions, polite proposals, give and take, and closing a deal. Today: how to set conditions — "provided that", "as long as", "unless".

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 18 de Outubro, 15:00; o gabinete da Sílvia; uma folha de cálculo no ecrã; a Sílvia, o Paulo e o Nuno à volta da mesa]

**NUNO:** Hannah called me this morning — off the record. The board likes option A, but Femi wants a lower price. And penalties if we're late.
**SÍLVIA:** I expected that. So, let's decide our limits now, before anyone calls. Our price is four hundred and twelve thousand. How low can we go?
**PAULO:** Not too low. If we cut the price too much, we'll have no money for the extra weeks I planned.
**SÍLVIA:** Here's my view. We can go down to three hundred and eighty-five thousand, provided that they pay thirty percent when they sign. With that money, we can order the antennas early.
**NUNO:** And if they only pay twenty percent?
**SÍLVIA:** Then we don't go below three hundred and ninety-five. As long as the deposit is low, the price stays high.
**NUNO:** OK. Penalties?
**PAULO:** Half a percent a week is fair, as long as there's a maximum — say, five percent. And no penalties for customs delays, unless the delay is our fault.
**NUNO:** What do you mean, "our fault"?
**PAULO:** If we send the papers late, it's our fault. If we send them on time and customs is slow, it isn't.
**SÍLVIA:** Good. Supposing Femi asks for one percent a week, with no maximum. What do we say?
**NUNO:** We say no — politely. If we accepted that, one bad month at customs would cost us a fortune.
**SÍLVIA:** Exactly. And, Nuno, take these numbers with you in case they call you before the meeting. But never show them to the client.
**NUNO:** Of course. Our bottom line stays in this room.
**PAULO:** And remember Mr Baptista's rule: no promises about dates unless you check with me!
**NUNO:** Ha! How could I forget?

### 3. Língua (4 min)

***Provided that, as long as, unless***

[ECRÃ:
We can go down to 385,000, **provided (that)** they pay 30% when they sign. (só se)
Half a percent is fair, **as long as** there's a maximum. (só se)
No penalties for customs delays, **unless** the delay is our fault. (a não ser que = if … not)]

**PROF:** "Provided that" and "as long as" mean "only if" — a strong condition. "Providing that" is the same. "Unless" means "if not": "unless the delay is our fault" means "if the delay is not our fault".
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ unless the delay isn't our fault (dupla negação) · ✘ provided that they will pay · ✔ provided that they pay (presente, para o futuro)]

**PROF:** Two mistakes to avoid. After "unless", don't add "not" — it's already inside. And after these words, use the present for the future: "provided that they pay", not "will pay".
[PAUSA 6 s]

***Supposing* e *in case***

[ECRÃ:
**Supposing** Femi asks for 1% a week. What do we say? (e se…?, imagine que…)
Take these numbers **in case** they call you. (por precaução, para o caso de)]

[ECRÃ: in case ≠ if: Take an umbrella **in case** it rains. (leve-o já) · Take an umbrella **if** it rains. (só se chover)]

**PROF:** "In case" means you do something now, just in case something happens later. "If" means you do it only when it happens.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Os quatro condicionais**

[ECRÃ:]

| Condicional | Exemplo | Quando |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | If the wind is strong, the radio link fails. | sempre verdade |
| 1.º | If they pay 30%, we'll order early. | possível, real |
| 2.º | If they asked for 1% a week, we'd say no. | imaginado, pouco provável |
| 3.º | If we hadn't hired the customs agent in Namibe, the antennas would have missed the crane. | passado imaginado |

**PROF:** You know all four from A2 and B1. In a negotiation, you need them all — sometimes in the same minute.
[PAUSA 5 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** Say "provided that" and "as long as" as one block, with no pause in the middle. Listen: We can go down to three hundred and eighty-five thousand, provided that they pay thirty percent.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: As long as there's a maximum, half a percent is fair.
[PAUSA 6 s]
**PROF:** No penalties for customs delays, unless the delay is our fault.
[PAUSA 6 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Join the two ideas with the word in brackets.
[ECRÃ: We'll deliver on Friday. You confirm the order today. (provided that)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: We'll deliver on Friday, provided that you confirm the order today.]
[ECRÃ: You can't enter the site. You have a helmet. (unless)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: You can't enter the site unless you have a helmet.]
[ECRÃ: Bring a copy of the contract. They might ask for it. (in case)] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: Bring a copy of the contract in case they ask for it.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. You're selling something — your car, your services, or your company's product. Set your conditions: what you'll accept, provided that…; what you won't do unless…; and one "supposing" question you need to prepare for. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — I'm selling my car for six million kwanzas. I can go down to five and a half, provided that the buyer pays in cash. As long as it's this month, I'll include the new tyres. I won't accept less than five million unless the buyer finds a problem with the engine. And supposing someone offers me a car in exchange? I'll say no, politely.]

**PROF (opinião):** Is it a good idea to decide your limits before a negotiation? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B2, 2C):** Paulo's plan: if they sign in November, what will they have done by the end of March?
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: By the end of March, they'll have laid all the cables and put up the masts.]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! "Provided that" and "as long as" for "only if"; "unless" for "if not" — with no extra "not"; "in case" for "just in case". Next lesson: how to say difficult things politely — "Would you consider…?".

## 3B — Would you consider…?

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3B — Would you consider…?        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue propor, pedir e recusar com linguagem tentativa e cortês
  (We'd be prepared to… Would you consider…? That might be difficult for us. We were hoping for…).
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2
Gramática-alvo: would, could, might para suavizar (We'd be prepared to… That could be a problem.
  That might be difficult for us.); perguntas indirectas e tentativas (Would you consider…? Could you
  possibly…? Would it be possible to…?); o passado contínuo de cortesia (We were hoping for… I was
  wondering if…); amortecedores (I'm afraid… To be honest… With respect…)
Palavras-alvo: subject to, terms, position, flexible, consider, possibly, wonder, prepared to, with
  respect, reasonable
Reciclagem: perguntas indirectas (B1, 1B); 2.º condicional (B1, 4A)
Pronúncia: subida no fim de uma proposta (Would you consider twenty percent?) e voz suave em
  "I'm afraid…"
Contexto: sexta-feira, 27 de Outubro, 17:30; o Femi telefona ao Nuno; depois, a Lerato comenta a chamada
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 3B; perguntas 3 e 4 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! In a negotiation, how you say something matters as much as what you say. Today: tentative language — soft words that keep the door open.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 27 de Outubro, 17:30; o Nuno no escritório, ao telefone; a Lerato na secretária ao lado, a ouvir]

**FEMI:** Mr Kapinga, good news. The board has chosen your option A — subject to the contract.
**NUNO:** That's wonderful news, Mr Adeyemi. Thank you.
**FEMI:** Now, to be honest, four hundred and twelve thousand is more than we were hoping to pay. Would you consider a lower figure?
**NUNO:** We'd certainly be prepared to look at the price. Could you give me an idea of what you have in mind?
**FEMI:** We were thinking of something closer to three hundred and seventy.
**NUNO:** I see. I'm afraid that might be difficult for us. But I'm sure we can find something reasonable.
**FEMI:** There's also the deposit. We'd prefer to pay ten percent when we sign.
**NUNO:** That could be a problem, to be honest. We need a larger deposit to order the antennas early — which, as you know, is how we avoid the customs delays.
**FEMI:** Hmm. And the penalties. The board would like one percent a week if you're late.
**NUNO:** With respect, that's quite high. Would it be possible to discuss all three points together, in person?
**FEMI:** Of course. Would Thursday at ten suit you?
**NUNO:** Thursday at ten is perfect. I was wondering if Sílvia, our finance director, could join us.
**FEMI:** Please bring her. See you on Thursday.
**NUNO:** Thank you, Mr Adeyemi. Have a good weekend.
[ECRÃ: o Nuno desliga; a Lerato sorri]
**LERATO:** Very good. You didn't say "no" once — and you didn't say "yes" to anything either.
**NUNO:** That was the plan. Our first position is in Mr Baptista's letter. I'll give it to him on Thursday.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Directo e tentativo**

[ECRÃ:]

| Directo (pode soar rude) | Tentativo |
|---|---|
| Lower the price. | **Would you consider** a lower figure? |
| We want 30%. | **We'd be looking for** a deposit of around 30%. |
| No. That's impossible. | **I'm afraid that might be difficult** for us. |
| That's too high. | **With respect,** that's quite high. |
| We want 370. | **We were hoping for** something closer to 370. |
| Can Sílvia come? | **I was wondering if** Sílvia could join us. |

**PROF:** "Would", "could" and "might" make a sentence softer. So does the past continuous: "We were hoping…", "I was wondering…" — it's about now, but the past tense makes it polite.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**Amortecedores**

[ECRÃ: **I'm afraid** … (infelizmente) · **To be honest,** … · **With respect,** … (antes de discordar) · **I see.** (ganhar tempo, sem concordar)]

**PROF:** These little phrases prepare the listener for bad news. "With respect" comes before a disagreement — never after.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ Would you consider to lower the price? · ✔ Would you consider lowering the price? / Would you consider a lower price?]

**PROF:** After "consider", use "-ing" or a noun — never "to".
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** A proposal goes up at the end — it sounds open. Listen: Would you consider twenty percent? [voz a subir]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** "I'm afraid" is soft and slow. Listen: I'm afraid that might be difficult for us.
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat both.
[PAUSA 9 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Make it tentative.
[ECRÃ: Give us a discount.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: Would you consider giving us a discount?]
[ECRÃ: That's too late for us.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: I'm afraid that might be a bit late for us.]
[ECRÃ: We want delivery in March.] [PAUSA 6 s] [ECRÃ: We were hoping for delivery in March.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. A client phones you and asks for a big discount, a longer time to pay, and free delivery. Answer each point tentatively: don't say "yes", don't say "no", and propose to meet. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Thank you for calling. We'd certainly be prepared to look at the price, but I'm afraid twenty percent might be difficult for us. On payment, to be honest, sixty days could be a problem; we were hoping for thirty. Free delivery is something we could consider. Would it be possible to meet next week and discuss all three together?]

**PROF (opinião):** Is tentative language a sign of weakness, or of strength? Why?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — B1, 1B):** Make it an indirect question: "When can you sign?"
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: Could you tell me when you can sign?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Excellent! "Would you consider…?", "We'd be prepared to…", "I'm afraid that might be difficult" — firm ideas, soft words. Next lesson: the give and take of bargaining.

## 3C — If you could…, we could…

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3C — If you could…, we could…        Tipo: Língua
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue regatear: pedir, oferecer algo em troca, encontrar um meio-termo,
  recusar com cortesia e fechar um acordo.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: concessões condicionais (If you could…, we could… · We could… if you… · in exchange for…
  · on condition that…); meio-termo (Let's meet halfway. Shall we split the difference?); recusar (I'm
  afraid that's as far as we can go. That's our final offer.); fechar (So, do we have a deal? It's a deal.
  Let me sum up what we've agreed.)
Palavras-alvo: quote, rate, per truck, load, shipment, discount, final offer, meet halfway, split the
  difference, deal, in writing
Reciclagem: provided that, unless (3A); linguagem tentativa (3B)
Pronúncia: descida firme em "That's as far as we can go."
Contexto: terça-feira, 31 de Outubro, 11:00; videochamada; o Nuno, em Luanda, e o Moses Haufiku, da
  Kunene Freight, em Windhoek, negoceiam o transporte das antenas da África do Sul até ao Tômbwa
Duração: 12 min
Verificação: Lab 3C; conversa guiada "Let's make a deal"; perguntas 5 e 6 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Negotiating is give and take: nobody gets everything. Today: how to ask for something and offer something in exchange.

### 2. Situação (3 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: terça-feira, 31 de Outubro, 11:00; videochamada; o Nuno no escritório; o Moses num armazém em Windhoek, com camiões atrás; no ecrã do Nuno, a cotação: "Johannesburg – Tômbwa: 6,800 USD per truck"]

**MOSES:** So, Mr Kapinga, you've seen our quote. Six thousand eight hundred dollars per truck, from Johannesburg to Tômbwa.
**NUNO:** Thank you, Mr Haufiku. To be honest, that's more than we expected. We'll need three trucks — the cables, the masts and the antennas.
**MOSES:** Three trucks is good. But our price is fair. It's a long road, and the border at Oshikango can take two days.
**NUNO:** I understand. Here's an idea: if you could bring the price down to six thousand, we could give you all three shipments, not just the first one.
**MOSES:** Six thousand is too low, I'm afraid. But I like the idea of all three. I could do six thousand five hundred, in exchange for a signed agreement for all three trucks.
**NUNO:** That's closer. Shall we meet halfway? Six thousand two hundred and fifty.
**MOSES:** Ha! You're a good negotiator. Six thousand four hundred — and that's as far as I can go.
**NUNO:** Six thousand four hundred, provided that you include the insurance.
**MOSES:** Insurance… OK. As long as you pay the first truck in advance.
**NUNO:** Done — on condition that we sign our contract with the client first. We expect to sign this month.
**MOSES:** That's fair. So, do we have a deal?
**NUNO:** It's a deal. Let me sum up what we've agreed: three trucks at six thousand four hundred dollars each, with insurance included; the first truck paid in advance; and all of this only after we sign with our client.
**MOSES:** Correct. Send it to me in writing, and I'll sign.
**NUNO:** I'll send it this afternoon. A pleasure doing business with you, Mr Haufiku.

### 3. Língua (4 min)

**Pedir e oferecer em troca**

[ECRÃ: **If you could** bring the price down to 6,000, **we could** give you all three shipments. · I could do 6,500, **in exchange for** a signed agreement. · Done — **on condition that** we sign with our client first.]

**PROF:** The key rule: never give something for nothing. Every time you give, ask for something back. "If you could…, we could…" is the classic structure: it's a second conditional, so it sounds like an idea, not a demand.
[PAUSA 6 s]

**O meio-termo**

[ECRÃ: Shall we **meet halfway**? · Let's **split the difference**. · That's closer.]

**PROF:** "Meet halfway" and "split the difference" mean the same: each side moves the same distance. Nuno's halfway point between six thousand and six thousand five hundred is six thousand two hundred and fifty.
[PAUSA 5 s]

**Recusar e fechar**

[ECRÃ: I'm afraid **that's as far as I can go**. · That's our **final offer**. · So, **do we have a deal**? · **It's a deal.** · **Let me sum up what we've agreed:** … · Send it to me **in writing**.]

**PROF:** When you close, always sum up: every number, every condition. A deal that isn't summed up becomes an argument later.
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: ✘ It's a deal? (como pergunta) · ✔ Do we have a deal? · ✔ It's a deal. (como resposta)]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia (1 min)

**PROF:** A final offer goes down — firm, but calm. Listen: Six thousand four hundred — and that's as far as I can go. [voz a descer]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat: That's as far as I can go.
[PAUSA 5 s]
**PROF:** And the proposal goes up: If you could bring the price down, we could give you all three shipments. [voz a subir em "shipments"]
[PAUSA 7 s]

### 5. Prática guiada (3 min)

**PROF:** Offer something in exchange.
[ECRÃ: Quer um desconto; pode pagar adiantado.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: If you could give us a discount, we could pay in advance.]
[ECRÃ: Quer entrega mais cedo; pode encomendar mais unidades.] [PAUSA 7 s] [ECRÃ: If you could deliver earlier, we could order more units.]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Describe a negotiation you had — at work, at a market, buying a car or renting a house. What did you want? What did the other side want? What did each side give, and what was the final deal? About one and a half minutes.
[PAUSA 90 s]
[ECRÃ: modelo — Last year I rented a flat in Talatona. The owner wanted six months in advance, and I wanted a lower rent. I said that if she could bring the rent down a little, I could pay three months in advance. She agreed, provided that I signed for two years. In the end, we met halfway: a ten percent discount, three months in advance, and a two-year contract.]

**PROF (opinião):** In your culture, is it normal to bargain in shops or markets? Where is it normal, and where isn't it?
[PAUSA 25 s]

**PROF (reciclagem — 3B):** Moses says six thousand is too low. Say "no" to his six thousand five hundred — tentatively.
[PAUSA 6 s]
[ECRÃ: I'm afraid that might still be a bit high for us. Would you consider six thousand two hundred and fifty?]

### 6. Fecho (30 s)

**PROF:** Well done! Never give without getting; meet halfway; close with a summary. Now try the tutor's conversation "Let's make a deal". Next lesson: the words of deals and contracts.

## 3D — Deals and contracts

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3D — Deals and contracts        Tipo: Vocabulário e pronúncia
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue usar o vocabulário de contratos e negociação (terms, clause,
  deposit, penalty, warranty, bulk discount), colocações (reach a compromise, make a concession, strike a
  deal), a entoação tentativa e firme, e evitar os falsos amigos billion e anticipate.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 2, 3
Gramática-alvo: colocações (reach an agreement / a compromise, make a concession / an offer, strike a
  deal, sign a contract, break a contract, meet a deadline, negotiate terms); nomes compostos (bulk
  discount, payment terms, penalty clause, down payment)
Palavras-alvo: contract, terms, clause, deposit, down payment, instalment, penalty, warranty, bulk
  discount, invoice, quote, compromise, concession, deal, breach
Reciclagem: condições (3A); meio-termo (3C)
Pronúncia: entoação tentativa (a subir) e firme (a descer) na mesma frase
Falsos amigos: billion (≠ bilião → trillion; billion = mil milhões); anticipate (≠ antecipar → bring
  forward; anticipate = prever, esperar)
Contexto: ecrãs com expressões e exemplos da história
Duração: 11 min
Verificação: Lab 3D; perguntas 7 e 8 do questionário
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today: the words of contracts — and two false friends that can cost a lot of money.

### 2. Contratos (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ:]

| Palavra | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| terms | We agreed on the terms of the contract. (condições) |
| clause | The penalty clause is on page six. (cláusula) |
| deposit / down payment | They pay a 30% deposit when they sign. (sinal) |
| instalment | The rest is paid in two instalments. (prestação) |
| penalty | The penalty is 0.5% a week. (penalização, multa) |
| warranty | The antennas have a three-year warranty. (garantia) |
| bulk discount | Three trucks get a bulk discount. (desconto por quantidade) |
| invoice | We'll send the invoice in May. (factura) |
| quote | Moses sent a quote for the trucks. (orçamento, cotação) |

**PROF:** Repeat: terms. Clause. Deposit. Instalment. Penalty. Warranty. Invoice. Quote.
[PAUSA 8 s]

[ECRÃ: **warranty** (de um produto) ≈ **guarantee** (promessa em geral, ou garantia do produto) · **quote** (nome, informal) = **quotation**]
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 3. Colocações (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **reach** an agreement / a compromise · **make** a concession / an offer · **strike** a deal · **sign** / **break** a contract · **negotiate** terms · **accept** / **reject** an offer]

**PROF:** Learn the verb with the noun. We "reach" an agreement or a compromise. We "make" a concession or an offer. And we "strike" a deal — it sounds strong and final.
[PAUSA 6 s]

[ECRÃ: make a deal (conversa) · strike a deal / reach a deal (mais profissional)]

**PROF:** "Make a deal" is fine in conversation; "strike" or "reach" a deal sounds more professional.
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 4. Pronúncia: tentativa e firme (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: We could include the insurance ↗ — but that's our final offer ↘.]

**PROF:** In one sentence, you can be open and firm. Listen: We could include the insurance [voz a subir] — but that's our final offer. [voz a descer]
[PAUSA 3 s]
**PROF:** Repeat.
[PAUSA 8 s]

### 5. Falsos amigos (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **billion** ≠ bilião]

**PROF:** In English, a "billion" is a thousand million: one, followed by nine zeros.
(PT: Em Portugal e em Angola, "um bilião" é um milhão de milhões — doze zeros — que em inglês é "a trillion". "Mil milhões" é "a billion". Num contrato, este erro multiplica o valor por mil!)
[PAUSA 5 s]

[ECRÃ: **anticipate** ≠ antecipar]

**PROF:** "Anticipate" means to expect or to predict: "We anticipate some delays at the border."
(PT: "Antecipar" no sentido de fazer mais cedo diz-se "bring forward": "antecipar a reunião" → "bring the meeting forward".)
[PAUSA 4 s]

### 6. Prática e fecho (2 min 30 s)

**PROF:** Complete with the right verb.
[ECRÃ: After three hours, we … a compromise.] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: reached]
[ECRÃ: Can we … the meeting forward to Tuesday?] [PAUSA 4 s] [ECRÃ: bring]

**PROF:** Now a longer turn. Explain the main terms of a contract you know — a phone contract, a rental, a loan, or a work contract: the price, how you pay, the penalty if something goes wrong, and the warranty or guarantee. About one minute.
[PAUSA 60 s]

## 3F — Work & Life: The contract

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: 3        Aula: 3F — The contract        Tipo: Work & Life
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue negociar um contrato com condições, linguagem tentativa e
  concessões em troca, chegar a um acordo e resumi-lo; e passar ao cliente, em inglês, as condições
  da direcção escritas em português.
Objectivo da unidade a que serve: 1, 2, 3, 4
Gramática-alvo: toda a da unidade: condições, linguagem tentativa, concessões, resumo do acordo
Palavras-alvo: deposit, penalty, cap, maximum, customs, paperwork, local technicians, training,
  agreement, sign
Reciclagem: future perfect (B2, 2C); relativas explicativas (B2, 1A); responder a perguntas (B2, 2D)
Pronúncia: resumo do acordo com pausas entre os pontos e a voz a descer no fim
Mediação: o Nuno passa ao Femi, em inglês, a carta do Sr. Baptista com as condições da Kalandula
  Tech, escrita em português

  e a Sílvia
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: Lab 3F; conversa livre "The contract"
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Today, everything comes together. Watch Nuno and Sílvia negotiate the Tômbwa contract with Femi: three points, conditions, give and take — and a summary at the end.

### 2. Situação (5 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: quinta-feira, 2 de Novembro, 10:00; uma sala de reuniões; café e água; o Femi de um lado; o Nuno e a Sílvia do outro; um envelope com o logótipo da Kalandula Tech]

**FEMI:** Good morning. Shall we start with your position?
**NUNO:** Of course. Mr Baptista, our director, has written to you. The letter is in Portuguese, so let me give you the main points.

[ECRÃ: a carta, em português: "Exmo. Senhor Femi Adeyemi, Director Financeiro da Morro Azul Energia. Agradecemos a escolha da opção A para a central do Tômbwa. Propomos as seguintes condições: 1) Preço: 395 000 USD (menos 17 000 USD do que a proposta inicial), desde que o contrato seja assinado até 30 de Novembro. 2) Pagamento: 30% na assinatura, 40% em Maio de 2029 e 30% na entrega. 3) Penalizações por atraso: 0,5% do valor por semana, até um máximo de 5%, excepto atrasos causados pela alfândega ou pela Morro Azul. **NUNO:** First, he thanks you for choosing option A. He proposes three things. Two: thirty percent when we sign, forty percent in May, and thirty percent on delivery. Three: penalties of half a percent a week if we're late, up to a maximum of five percent — unless the delay is caused by customs or by Morro Azul.
**FEMI:** Thank you. That's clear. I'll be honest: the board was hoping for three hundred and seventy, ten percent at signing, and one percent a week, with no maximum.
**SÍLVIA:** With respect, Femi, one percent a week with no maximum is a risk no company can take. After twenty weeks, we'd be paying you to work.
**FEMI:** [ri-se] Fair point. Would you consider one percent, with a maximum?

**FEMI:** Very worried. "Customs" could become an excuse for every delay.
**NUNO:** I understand. Here's an idea: customs delays don't count, as long as we deliver all the papers by the fifteenth of December. If we're late with the papers, the delay is ours, and you apply the penalty.
**FEMI:** Hmm. That's reasonable. Half a percent, a five percent cap, and customs excluded only with the papers on time. Agreed. Now, the price.
**SÍLVIA:** We've already come down seventeen thousand.
**FEMI:** Three hundred and seventy-five?
**NUNO:** That's below what we can do, I'm afraid. But if you could keep the thirty percent deposit, we could go to three hundred and eighty-five. That's as far as we can go.
**FEMI:** Three eighty-five with thirty percent… The board will ask what else they get.
**NUNO:** Here's something we'd be prepared to add. Dr Kiala asked about local jobs. We could train six technicians from Tômbwa, at no cost, so they can look after the network after we leave.
**FEMI:** Now that the board will like. [pausa] So, do we have a deal?

Delays caused by Morro Azul don't count, and customs delays don't count either, provided that we deliver all the papers by the fifteenth of December. **FEMI:** Correct. Send me the final text by Monday, and I'll take it to the board.
**SÍLVIA:** You'll have it on Monday morning.
**FEMI:** Thank you both. It's been a tough but fair morning.

### 3. Como se negocia um contrato (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: o que o Nuno e a Sílvia fizeram]

| Momento | Língua | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Apresentar a posição (mediação) | … proposes three things. One… Two… Three… | Mr Baptista proposes three things. |
| 2. Recusar com respeito | With respect, … · I'm afraid … might be difficult. | With respect, one percent with no maximum is a risk no company can take. |
| 3. Perceber o medo do outro | I know the board is worried about… | I know the board is worried about Namibe. |
| 4. Pôr uma condição | as long as · provided that | Customs delays don't count, provided that we deliver all the papers by the fifteenth. |

| 6. Acrescentar valor | We'd be prepared to add… | We could train six technicians from Tômbwa. |
| 7. Fechar e resumir | Do we have a deal? Let me sum up what we've agreed. | The price is three hundred and eighty-five thousand dollars… |

**PROF:** Notice: Nuno never goes below the limit Sílvia set in October — three hundred and eighty-five, and only with thirty percent. And the last concession costs little but means a lot to the client: training local technicians — the answer to Dr Kiala's question about jobs.
