# B2.2 · Revisão 2 (unidades 6 a 10) · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B2](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que a revisão pode usar:** tudo do A1 ao B2 (unidades 1 a 10), e nada de novo, com uma excepção, que é uma estratégia de exame em blocos fixos: atenuar e conceder num debate (*I take your point, but… That may be true, but… To some extent, yes. I see where you're coming from. Even so…*). As estratégias anteriores mantêm-se (*That's an interesting question. It depends on… On the one hand… On the other hand… The first option… The second, on the other hand,… Shall we go for…? I could live with that.*).

**Não usar:** estruturas do C1 — inversão (*Had I known…, Never have I…*), orações clivadas como estrutura de ênfase variada (*It was Paulo who…*), atenuação sistemática em bloco (*It would seem that… arguably…*).

**Personagens:** **Nuno**, **Paulo Mendes** e **Elsa Chilombo**, num jantar (R2A); **Lurdes Cassoma**, a aluna-modelo dos exames (contabilista num banco de Benguela desde 2014), agora no exame do B2.2 (R2B).

**Quando:** sexta-feira, 29 de Junho de 2029, uma semana depois da decisão do Nuno (B2 10G).

### 2. Situação: o jantar (4 min)

[ECRÃ: sexta-feira, 29 de Junho de 2029, 20:00; um restaurante na Ilha de Luanda; o Paulo, a Elsa e o Nuno a uma mesa junto ao mar]

**ELSA:** To Nuno — and to Johannesburg!
**PAULO:** To Johannesburg! But not before January.
**NUNO:** Thank you both. Honestly, you needn't have organised all this.
**ELSA:** Of course we did. It's been an absolutely crazy six months.
**PAULO:** Tell me about it. Remember March? The container on the sand. I still think the sling must have been worn for weeks.
**NUNO:** It can't have been new, that's for sure. And the anemometer was supposed to be repaired. Lesson learned.
**ELSA:** And the city meeting! Nuno, the minibus is working, isn't it?
**NUNO:** It is — forty-six people use it now. Admittedly, Ravi still drives his car. Even so, the bus lane saves us forty minutes a day.
**PAULO:** And the festival. Surprisingly, people still talk about the solar zone.
**NUNO:** Even though the Wi-Fi died during the concert!
**ELSA:** And Mr Sapalo — he denied causing the delay, but he admitted starting the roof late. Classic.
**PAULO:** And Lerato persuaded you to ask for January. Brilliant idea.
**NUNO:** It was. If she hadn't suggested it, I'd still be lying awake at night.
**ELSA:** Do you have any regrets?
**NUNO:** Only one. I wish I'd learned to cook before moving. I'd been planning to take a course for months, and I never did.
**PAULO:** Ha! When we were kids, my mother would cook for half the street. I'll ask her to teach you.
**ELSA:** And you'll come back to visit, won't you?
**NUNO:** Every time I can. If I hadn't worked with you two, I wouldn't be the person I am now.

## R2B — Getting ready for the B2.2 speaking exam

```
Curso: B2.2        Unidade: Revisão 2        Aula: R2B — Getting ready for the B2.2 speaking exam        Tipo: Revisão
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue descrever as quatro partes do exame oral do B2.2, sabe o que é
  avaliado e usa uma estratégia nova: atenuar e conceder num debate (I take your point, but… That may be
  true, but… To some extent, yes. I see where you're coming from. Even so…).
Objectivos de unidade que revê: U6–U10, todos
Gramática revista: toda a do B2
Língua nova (blocos fixos): That may be true, but… · To some extent, yes. · I see where you're coming from. ·
  Even so, …
Situação: a Lurdes faz o exame de demonstração do B2.2
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: conversa de ensaio; exame oral do B2.2
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Soon you take your B2.2 speaking exam — the last exam of B2. Today you see how it works, you learn one more strategy for the debate, and you watch a practice exam.

### 2. Como é o exame (2 min)

[ECRÃ: quatro blocos]

| Parte | O quê | Tempo |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Interview | As suas escolhas e arrependimentos, o sítio onde vive, a infância | cerca de 2 minutos |
| 2. Long turn | O examinador descreve duas opções sobre um tema da actualidade; o aluno compara-as, durante cerca de 1 minuto e meio, e escolhe; depois, duas perguntas | cerca de 3 minutos |
| 3. Decision task | Uma tarefa sorteada de quatro: relatar um quase-acidente; defender uma proposta numa reunião; avaliar um evento para um patrocinador; sintetizar uma reunião para a direcção. No fim, uma decisão com o examinador | cerca de 4 minutos |
| 4. Discussion | Uma pergunta abstracta (sociedade, tecnologia, ambiente); o examinador discorda duas vezes | cerca de 3 minutos |

**PROF:** The structure is the same as in B2.1 — twelve minutes, four parts. What changes is the content: the tasks come from units six to ten, and the discussion questions are more abstract.
(PT: A estrutura é a do B2.1: doze minutos, quatro partes. Mudam as tarefas, que vêm das unidades 6 a 10, e as perguntas da discussão, que são mais abstractas.)

**PROF:** The rules are the same: the recording is your choice, the exam opens when you finish most of the B2.2 lessons, and when you don't pass, you can try again a week later.
(PT: A gravação é opcional (com ela, a pronúncia conta), o exame abre com 80% das aulas do B2.2 feitas, e pode repetir uma semana depois.)

### 3. O que é avaliado (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: cinco ícones]

| Competência | No B2.2, quer dizer… |
|---|---|
| Task — a tarefa | Argumentos desenvolvidos, com razões e exemplos; compara e escolhe; leva a tarefa até uma decisão; responde aos dois contra-argumentos concedendo o que é justo. |
| Grammar — a gramática | Variedade de estruturas do B2 (modais no passado, concessão, verbos de relato, *wish*, condicionais mistos), com erros raros e muitas vezes corrigidos. |
| Vocabulary — o vocabulário | As palavras das unidades 6 a 10 (segurança, cidade, cultura, relatórios, escolhas), colocações, paráfrase, mudança de registo. |
| Fluency — a fluência | Fala com espontaneidade e à vontade; conduz a tarefa e o debate. |
| Pronunciation — a pronúncia | Clara e natural; a entoação mostra atitude (concessão, firmeza, ironia ligeira). (Só com a gravação.) |

### 4. Uma estratégia nova: atenuar e conceder (2 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: **I take your point, but…** · **That may be true, but…** · **To some extent, yes.** · **I see where you're coming from.** · **Even so, …**]

**PROF:** In the discussion, the examiner disagrees twice. Don't fight, and don't give up. First, accept what is true in the other view — "That may be true", "To some extent, yes" — and then come back to your point: "but…", "Even so…". You learned "admittedly" and "even so" in unit seven; now use them in a debate.
[PAUSA 4 s]
[ECRÃ: "Should children have phones at school?" — Examiner: "Phones help children in emergencies." — "That may be true, but emergencies are rare, and phones distract them every day. To some extent, I agree: they could keep them switched off in their bags. Even so, I'd ban them in class."]
**PROF:** That may be true, but emergencies are rare, and phones distract them every day. To some extent, I agree: they could keep them switched off in their bags. Even so, I'd ban them in class.
[PAUSA 4 s]
(PT: Conceder não é perder o debate. No B2, quem reconhece o que é verdade no outro lado e mesmo assim defende a sua posição mostra mais nível do que quem repete a mesma ideia.)

[ECRÃ: já conhece — **That's an interesting question.** · **On the one hand… On the other hand…** · **The first option… The second, on the other hand,…** · **Shall we go for…? I could live with that.**]
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 5. Um exame de demonstração (5 min)

**PROF:** Now watch Lurdes — her last B2 exam.

[ECRÃ: "Practice exam B2.2 — Lurdes"; ícone do examinador]

**EXAMINADOR:** Hello, Lurdes. Welcome to your B2.2 speaking exam. Is there anything you wish you'd done differently in your career?
**LURDES:** Yes, one thing. I wish I'd studied finance earlier. I'd been working at the bank for ten years before I started my master's, and if I'd started sooner, I'd probably be a branch manager now.
**EXAMINADOR:** What would you change about the place where you live?
**LURDES:** Benguela is lovely, but the beach is getting dirtier. Admittedly, the city cleans it every week. Even so, I'd put more bins and fine people who leave rubbish.
**EXAMINADOR:** Thank you. Now Part 2. Your bank has money for staff training. Option one: a big conference abroad for the ten managers. Option two: online courses for all two hundred employees. Compare them, and choose.
**LURDES:** OK. The first option is exciting, and the managers would meet people from other banks. The second, on the other hand, reaches everyone — even the cashiers, who usually get no training at all. The main difference is who benefits. Admittedly, online courses can be boring, and some people never finish them. Even so, I'd go for the online courses, provided that each team gets one hour a week, during working hours, to do them.
**EXAMINADOR:** What might the managers say?
**LURDES:** They'd probably say they need the conference to learn about new technology. That may be true, but they could watch the main talks online too.
**EXAMINADOR:** And how would you know it had worked?
**LURDES:** If, after six months, the complaints at the counters had fallen, I'd say it had worked.
**EXAMINADOR:** Now Part 3. I'm the regional director, and I missed yesterday's meeting about the new branch in Lobito. The meeting was in Portuguese. Please summarise it — I have two minutes.
**LURDES:** Of course. Three points. First, the opening: the project manager confirmed that the branch opens on the first of October, but she admitted that the security doors haven't been delivered yet. Second, the staff: HR suggested moving four people from Benguela, and two of them have already agreed. Third, the cost: finance warned us that the budget is about ten percent over. My assessment: the date isn't at risk, as long as the doors arrive by September. What worries me is the budget.
**EXAMINADOR:** Ten percent over? That's not acceptable. What do you propose?
**LURDES:** I take your point. We could open with three counters instead of four, and add the fourth in January. That would bring us almost back to the budget.
**EXAMINADOR:** I could live with that. Agreed. Now a question for discussion. Should people retire later, because we live longer?
**LURDES:** That's an interesting question. On the one hand, we live longer and healthier lives, so many people can work until sixty-five or seventy. On the other hand, some jobs are physically very hard. So, overall, I think it should depend on the job.
**EXAMINADOR:** But some people say older workers take jobs from young people.
**LURDES:** I see where you're coming from. To some extent, yes — in a bank, if nobody retires, nobody gets promoted. Even so, young people learn a lot from experienced colleagues. I'd let older workers stay part-time and train the young ones.
**EXAMINADOR:** Others say people deserve to rest after forty years of work.
**LURDES:** That may be true, but it should be a choice, not an obligation. My father retired at sixty and was bored within a month!
**EXAMINADOR:** Thank you, Lurdes. The exam is finished. You can press the finish button now.

**PROF:** Look at what Lurdes does well.
[ECRÃ: lista com vistos]
**PROF:** In part one, she uses "wish", the past perfect continuous and a mixed conditional. In the long turn, she compares, concedes with "admittedly", and chooses with a condition. In the task, she summarises with reporting verbs, adds her assessment, and finds a compromise. And in the discussion, she uses the new strategy — "I see where you're coming from", "To some extent, yes", "That may be true, but" — and answers both counter-arguments.
(PT: Usa o *wish* e um condicional misto, compara e concede, sintetiza com verbos de relato, encontra um compromisso, e responde aos dois contra-argumentos concedendo o que é justo.)
[PAUSA 4 s]
