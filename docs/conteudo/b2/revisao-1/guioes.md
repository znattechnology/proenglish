# B2.1 · Revisão 1 (unidades 1 a 5) · Guiões das vídeo-aulas

Guiões para o professor de IA. Conteúdo original da ProEnglish. Convenções: as dos [guiões da unidade 1 do B2](../unidade-01/guioes.md).

**Língua que a revisão pode usar:** tudo do A1, do A2, do B1 e das unidades 1 a 5 do B2, e nada de novo, com uma excepção, que são duas estratégias de exame em blocos fixos: comparar duas opções num turno longo (*The first option… The second, on the other hand,… The main difference is… If I had to choose, I'd go for…*) e chegar a uma decisão com o examinador (*Shall we go for…? I could live with that. So, we've agreed on…*). As estratégias anteriores mantêm-se (*That's an interesting question. It depends on… On the one hand… On the other hand… So, overall… What I mean is… Do you mean…?*).

**Não usar:** as estruturas do B2.2 — *needn't have*, *was supposed to*, *whereas, admittedly, even if, nevertheless*, verbos de relato como *suggest, insist, deny, warn*, a passiva de relato, *wish / if only*, *would* para hábitos do passado, o *past perfect continuous* e os condicionais mistos. (O plano do B2 sugeria *Whereas the first…* na R1B; foi trocado por *The second, on the other hand,…*, porque *whereas* só se ensina na unidade 7.)

**Personagens:** **Nuno** e **Elsa Chilombo** (R1A); **Lurdes Cassoma**, a aluna-modelo dos exames (contabilista num banco, de Benguela), agora no exame do B2.1 (R1B).

**Quando:** quarta-feira, 10 de Janeiro de 2029. As obras do Tômbwa começam em Fevereiro; o piloto do HelpMate começou na segunda, 8 (B2 5F).

### 2. Situação: a Elsa pede conselhos (4 min)

[ECRÃ: quarta-feira, 10 de Janeiro de 2029, 17:30; a cantina, quase vazia; o Nuno com um café; a Elsa com um caderno]

**ELSA:** Nuno, have you got ten minutes? Lerato has asked me to lead the training of the six technicians from Tômbwa, which is great — but I'm terrified.
**NUNO:** Congratulations! You'll be great. What's worrying you?
**ELSA:** First, I have to present the training plan to Hannah and Femi on the twenty-fifth. I've never presented to a client.
**NUNO:** OK. Start with the purpose, give them the outline, and use one strong number. For example: by the time the plant opens, the technicians will have been training for ten months.
**ELSA:** Ten months — that's true. We start in February. And by June, they'll have done all the safety courses.
**NUNO:** Perfect. And if they ask something you don't know?
**ELSA:** "I'm afraid I don't have that figure with me. I'll get back to you by Friday."
**NUNO:** Ha! Spoken like a professional.
**ELSA:** Second problem: the training centre in Namibe wants four thousand dollars for the room. That's too much.
**NUNO:** Don't say no. Say: "We could sign for all six months, provided that you bring the price down." Never give without getting something back.
**ELSA:** And if they say no?
**NUNO:** Then you say, "I'm afraid that's as far as we can go," and wait. They're bound to call you back — nobody else will rent that room for six months.
**ELSA:** OK. And third: Joel told me that the laptops for the technicians haven't been ordered yet. They should have been ordered in December.
**NUNO:** That's a problem, but not a disaster. Have them ordered today, and tell Hannah the truth if they're late. Remember Lumo: clients forgive mistakes, not lies.
**ELSA:** True. And one more thing: Paulo, who's done this training before, says I should use HelpMate for the exercises. What do you think?
**NUNO:** It may well help — but the pilot only started on Monday. Let's see what we've learned by April before we use it with the technicians.
**ELSA:** That makes sense. Thanks, Nuno. You sound like Lerato!
**NUNO:** Ha! I'll take that as a compliment.

## R1B — Getting ready for the B2.1 speaking exam

```
Curso: B2.1        Unidade: Revisão 1        Aula: R1B — Getting ready for the B2.1 speaking exam        Tipo: Revisão
Objectivo da aula: No fim, o aluno consegue descrever as quatro partes do exame oral do B2.1, sabe o que é
  avaliado e usa duas estratégias novas: comparar duas opções num turno longo e chegar a uma decisão com
  o examinador.
Objectivos de unidade que revê: U1–U5, todos
Gramática revista: toda a do B2.1
Língua nova (blocos fixos): The first option… The second, on the other hand,… · The main difference is… ·
  If I had to choose, I'd go for… · Shall we go for…? · I could live with that. · So, we've agreed on…
Situação: a Lurdes faz o exame de demonstração do B2.1
Duração: 13 min
Verificação: conversa de ensaio; exame oral do B2.1
```

### 1. Abertura (30 s)

**PROF:** Hello! Soon you take your B2.1 speaking exam — your first B2 exam. Today you see how it works, you learn two new strategies, and you watch a practice exam.

### 2. Como é o exame (2 min)

[ECRÃ: quatro blocos]

| Parte | O quê | Tempo |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Interview | A sua equipa, o que terá conseguido até ao fim do ano e como o seu trabalho vai mudar | cerca de 2 minutos |
| 2. Long turn | O examinador descreve duas opções; o aluno compara-as sozinho, durante cerca de 1 minuto e meio, e diz qual escolhe; depois, duas perguntas | cerca de 3 minutos |
| 3. Decision task | Uma tarefa sorteada de quatro: apresentar uma proposta; negociar um acordo; acalmar um cliente zangado; decidir sobre uma tecnologia nova. No fim, o aluno e o examinador chegam a uma decisão | cerca de 4 minutos |
| 4. Discussion | Uma pergunta abstracta ligada à tarefa; o examinador discorda duas vezes, com argumentos diferentes | cerca de 3 minutos |

**PROF:** The B2 exam is longer — about twelve minutes — and there are two big changes. In the long turn, you compare two options. And in the task, you don't just answer: you and the examiner have to reach a decision together.
(PT: O exame do B2 é mais longo — cerca de doze minutos — e muda em duas coisas: no turno longo, compara duas opções; na tarefa, o aluno e o examinador têm de chegar juntos a uma decisão.)

**PROF:** The rules are the same as in B1: the recording is your choice, the exam opens when you finish most of the B2.1 lessons, and when you don't pass, you can try again a week later.
(PT: A gravação é opcional (com ela, a pronúncia conta), o exame abre com 80% das aulas do B2.1 feitas, e pode repetir uma semana depois.)

### 3. O que é avaliado (1 min 30 s)

[ECRÃ: cinco ícones]

| Competência | No B2.1, quer dizer… |
|---|---|
| Task — a tarefa | Argumentos claros e desenvolvidos; compara as opções e escolhe com razões; leva a tarefa até uma decisão; responde aos dois contra-argumentos. |
| Grammar — a gramática | Variedade de estruturas complexas (relativas, passivas, futuros, condições, linguagem tentativa); erros que não impedem a compreensão, muitas vezes corrigidos. |
| Vocabulary — o vocabulário | As palavras das unidades 1 a 5 (equipas, apresentações, contratos, clientes, tecnologia), colocações, e paráfrase quando falta uma palavra. |
| Fluency — a fluência | Fala com espontaneidade, conduz a conversa, poucas pausas longas. |
| Pronunciation — a pronúncia | Clara e natural, com a entoação que mostra atitude (tentativa, firme, empática). (Só com a gravação.) |

### 4. Duas estratégias novas (2 min 30 s)

**Comparar duas opções**

[ECRÃ: **The first option…** · **The second, on the other hand,…** · **The main difference is…** · **If I had to choose, I'd go for…, because…**]

**PROF:** In the long turn, don't describe the options one after the other and stop. Compare them: what's good and bad about each, the main difference — and then choose, with a reason.
[PAUSA 4 s]
[ECRÃ: "Two ways to learn a language: a class with a teacher, or an app." — "The first option, a class, gives you a real person to talk to, and someone who corrects you. The second, on the other hand, is cheaper and you can use it anywhere. The main difference is speaking practice. If I had to choose, I'd go for the class, because speaking is what I need most."]
**PROF:** The first option, a class, gives you a real person to talk to, and someone who corrects you. The second, on the other hand, is cheaper, and you can use it anywhere. The main difference is speaking practice. If I had to choose, I'd go for the class, because speaking is what I need most.
[PAUSA 4 s]

**Chegar a uma decisão**

[ECRÃ: **Shall we go for…?** · **I could live with that.** (posso aceitar) · **So, we've agreed on…**]

**PROF:** In the task, the examiner will disagree or ask for something. Don't fight — negotiate, and then close: "Shall we go for…?", "I could live with that", and "So, we've agreed on…". An exam task without a decision is an unfinished task.
[PAUSA 4 s]
(PT: Na tarefa, a nota depende de levar a conversa até uma decisão. Use o que aprendeu na unidade 3: condições, concessões e um resumo no fim.)

[ECRÃ: já conhece — **That's an interesting question.** · **It depends on…** · **On the one hand… On the other hand…** · **So, overall…** · **I see your point, but…** · **What I mean is…**]
[PAUSA 3 s]

### 5. Um exame de demonstração (5 min)

**PROF:** Now watch Lurdes — her first B2 exam.

[ECRÃ: "Practice exam B2.1 — Lurdes"; ícone do examinador]

**EXAMINADOR:** Hello, Lurdes. Welcome to your B2.1 speaking exam. Tell me about a team you work with.
**LURDES:** I work in the accounts team at the bank's office in Benguela. There are five of us. My manager, who's been at the bank for twenty years, is very calm, which helps a lot at the end of the month. And Joaquim, whose desk is next to mine, is the IT person for the whole floor.
**EXAMINADOR:** What will you have achieved by the end of this year?
**LURDES:** By December, I'll have finished my master's in finance, and I'll have been working at the bank for eight years. I hope I'll have become a team leader, too.
**EXAMINADOR:** Thank you. Now, Part 2. Your bank wants to reduce the queues in its branches. Option one: more staff at the busiest hours. Option two: an app where clients book a time. Compare them for about one and a half minutes, and say which you'd choose.
**LURDES:** OK. The first option, more staff at peak hours, is simple, and clients like talking to a person. But it's expensive, and the queues will come back if the branch gets busier. The second, on the other hand, costs less in the long run, and clients don't waste time waiting. But many of our older clients don't use apps, so they're unlikely to book anything. The main difference is who it helps: staff help everyone, the app helps clients with smartphones. If I had to choose, I'd go for the app — provided that we keep one counter without appointments for older clients.
**EXAMINADOR:** What could go wrong with the app?
**LURDES:** Clients may well book a time and not come, and the system might fail on busy days. So it should be tested in one branch first.
**EXAMINADOR:** And how would you know it had worked?
**LURDES:** By the numbers: the average waiting time, and the number of complaints. If the waiting time falls sharply in the first three months, we roll it out.
**EXAMINADOR:** Now Part 3. I'm the sales manager of a hotel in Benguela. Your bank wants to rent our conference room for a two-day training in March. Our price is six hundred thousand kwanzas a day.
**LURDES:** Thank you. To be honest, that's more than we were hoping for. We'll need the room for two days, with coffee for forty people. Would you consider five hundred thousand a day?
**EXAMINADOR:** I'm afraid that's too low. Five hundred and seventy.
**LURDES:** I see. If you could do five hundred and thirty, we could book the room for our June training too.
**EXAMINADOR:** Hmm. Five hundred and fifty, with the coffee included. That's as far as I can go.
**LURDES:** I could live with that — provided that the deposit is twenty percent, not fifty.
**EXAMINADOR:** Twenty-five.
**LURDES:** Shall we go for twenty-five? So, we've agreed on five hundred and fifty thousand a day, with coffee, for March and June, and a twenty-five percent deposit. I'll send it to you in writing today.
**EXAMINADOR:** Agreed. Now a question for discussion. Should salaries in a company be public?
**LURDES:** That's an interesting question. On the one hand, public salaries are fair: everybody knows the rules, and it's harder to pay women less for the same job. On the other hand, people may well feel uncomfortable, and colleagues might start comparing all the time. So, overall, I think salary ranges should be public, but not each person's salary.
**EXAMINADOR:** But some people say it's private information, like your health.
**LURDES:** I see your point. But a salary isn't like health — it's paid by the company, and fairness matters. That's why I'd publish the ranges, not the names.
**EXAMINADOR:** Others say that public salaries make people leave for better-paid jobs.
**LURDES:** That's possible. But if people leave because they discover they're paid less than others, the problem isn't the information — it's the salary.
**EXAMINADOR:** Thank you, Lurdes. The exam is finished. You can press the finish button now.

**PROF:** Look at what Lurdes does well.
[ECRÃ: lista com vistos]
**PROF:** In part one, she uses relative clauses, the future perfect and the future perfect continuous. In the long turn, she compares the two options, gives the main difference and chooses — with a condition. In the task, she uses tentative language, gives something in exchange, and closes with "Shall we go for…?" and a summary. In the discussion, she organises her opinion and answers both counter-arguments with a reason.
(PT: Compara as opções e escolhe; negoceia com concessões em troca; fecha a tarefa com uma decisão e um resumo; responde aos dois contra-argumentos.)
[PAUSA 4 s]
